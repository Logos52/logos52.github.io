# Argument Validation

An argument is a set of facts that infer a conclusion. Validating an argument means checking its facts are true and that its conclusion follows. An argument is valid when its conclusion follows from its facts, whether or not the facts are true. It's closely tied to soundness. Logic gives each check its own name. An argument is sound when it is valid and its facts are true.

The page doesn't care more about validity. Validating, as defined above, checks both, and an argument that passes both is sound. The reason the linked pages spend most of their time on the validity half is that it's the half a reader can finish alone.

**Why that is**

- Checking validity needs nothing but the argument. You look at the facts as given and ask whether the conclusion follows. Anyone who can follow the reasoning gets the same answer, so it can be settled without agreeing on politics or trusting a source.
- Checking the facts needs the world. It means going outside the argument to sources, studies, and people you have to decide whether to trust. It depends on the subject, so no single method page can teach it.
- Validity comes first in practice. If the conclusion doesn't follow, the argument is finished, and checking the facts would have been wasted work. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]] puts it this way: a premise stands unless something specific overturns it, because you can't test whether a conclusion follows while you're also pulling the facts apart.
- In political argument the facts are usually true. The earnings gap is real, the crime rate is real. The failure is almost always in the step from the fact to the conclusion, which is the validity check.

What the page can't do, and says so: [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]] is clear that the method can show an argument works or where it breaks, and cannot show a position is false. Soundness is the finish line. Validity is the part of the run that happens on this site.

Each page linked below gives a test that works on any argument, whatever the argument is about. The pages that test one particular belief are in [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Worldviews & the Political Order|Worldviews & the Political Order]].

## When true facts lead to a wrong conclusion

- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]]: An argument can fail because a fact under it is false, or because the conclusion does not follow even though every fact is true. Whether a conclusion follows is the same for every reader, so it can be checked without agreeing on anything else. A conclusion stops following when a word changes its meaning between the facts and the conclusion. A general rule applied to a case that lacks the conditions the rule needs is another failure. So is an argument that would work just as well against the person making it.

## Numbers and words that mislead

- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Per Capita|Per Capita]]: Per capita means per head, a count divided by the number of people the count came from. A larger group has more of nearly everything, so a larger total on its own shows only that the group is larger. Dividing each total by its population says how common the thing is inside each group. That division often reverses which group looks worse. A rate says how common something is and says nothing about why, so it is where an explanation starts. Someone who uses rates when rates favour them and totals when totals favour them is not confused about the arithmetic.
- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Who Gets Called Racist|Who Gets Called Racist]]: The word racist covers a guess about a stranger, made from the group the stranger belongs to when nothing else is known about them. It also covers hostility toward a person that stays in place after that person is known as an individual. Arguments about the word usually treat the guess and the hostility as one thing. They come apart when you watch whether the judgment changes once information about the individual arrives.
- [[wiki/Concepts/Bias and Framing|Bias and Framing]]: Bias is an error that leans the same way every time, and it reaches a reader through framing. The facts stay the same while the words carrying them are chosen for their moral charge. That charge decides which conclusion feels right before any conclusion has been weighed. Putting a flat word back in place of the loaded word is the test. If no fact changes, the loaded word was doing the persuading.
- [[wiki/Red Team/The Twitter Test|The Twitter Test]]: A writer under a tight length limit keeps only the words that serve the writer's goal, so each word left in a short text was chosen over a plainer one. The test reads such a text one word at a time. For each word it asks why the writer chose that word and what feeling the word is meant to produce. Then it asks who gains from that feeling. The answers show what the text is doing to you before you take on its view.

## The person making the argument

- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Moral Language as Leverage|Moral Language as Leverage]]: A moral demand works on the people who hold the morals behind it, whether or not the person making the demand holds them. That is why a principle can be demanded in one direction and never applied in the other. It is also why pointing that out changes nothing. The test for a principle being used as a tool is whether the person applies it when applying it would cost them. Failing that test does not make the person's argument false. Whether an argument holds does not depend on who is using it.

## Your own judgment

- [[wiki/Red Team/Applied Critical Thinking - Testing Frames|Applied Critical Thinking - Testing Frames]]: A story can fit together and still be false. Applied critical thinking is a short pause between meeting a claim and accepting it. The pause is spent finding the assumption the conclusion depends on, and it ends with a decision. It applies to a claim from a news outlet and to a fluent answer from an AI model alike. Rejecting whatever a news outlet says, out of habit, is the same failure as accepting it out of habit.
- [[wiki/Red Team/Epistemic Exceptionalism|Epistemic Exceptionalism]]: Epistemic exceptionalism is treating your own reasoning as the standard that everything else is measured against. When people disagree with you, the explanation becomes that they are corrupt or slow. Once that is the explanation every time, no disagreement can count as evidence that you are wrong. Confidence that has been earned can name the evidence that would change its mind. If you cannot name that evidence, your own judgment is the one thing that has never been tested. The same holds if you have a reading ready whichever way the facts turn out.
- [[wiki/Concepts/Levels of Thinking - The Step Back|Levels of Thinking - The Step Back]]: A reaction to a claim arrives before any thinking does. Asking why the reaction is there, and then why that matters, leads back to where the reaction came from. Knowing that lets you judge the claim instead of the feeling it set off. Each step back is a level. A higher level means more awareness. It does not mean a better person.

## Your own arguments

- [[wiki/Concepts/Five Thinking Habits - Conclusion First|Five Thinking Habits - Conclusion First]]: A listener judges your thinking by how you present it, so sound reasoning delivered as a long chain gets heard as poor reasoning. Saying the conclusion before the reasons also checks your own understanding. If the conclusion will not come, there is a gap in it. Saying what would change your mind, along with the decision, is what earns the listener's trust.
- [[wiki/Concepts/How to Communicate Truth Into Someone Else's Frame|How to Communicate Truth Into Someone Else's Frame]]: Explaining something you believe to a person who sees the matter differently goes better in their terms than in yours. It starts from the problem they are trying to solve and from the part of their view that is right. That right part is said out loud before your own view is added. When their view is wrong, that is said plainly. When what you are offering does not fit them, you leave.
