# Red Team Training (UFMCS)

A red team is a group that plays the enemy in a wargame or in intelligence analysis and challenges a plan with the reasoning the opponent would use. The US Army trains its red teamers at the University of Foreign Military and Cultural Studies, known as UFMCS, which sits under the Army's Training and Doctrine Command. The training has uses outside the military because it finds a plan's mistakes before money, people or time are committed to it.

## Core takeaways

- A red team argues against a plan from the opponent's side.
- Good red teaming finds the flaw before resources are spent.
- Naming a bias is the first step to catching it in yourself.
- Cultural empathy means taking another group's reasoning as it is.
- The training spends more time on understanding people than on weapons.
- The Red Team Handbook is public and unclassified.

## What the training teaches

The UFMCS course is built from a few groups of techniques. Each group pushes the student out of the habits of thought they arrived with, including their own ideology and beliefs. Groupthink and bias get long discussion, because a person who can name a bias is more likely to notice it. The course is made uncomfortable on purpose, and it pushes each student to the edge of what they can do.

- Self-awareness: self-aware people speak with more authority.
- Applied critical thinking: spotting bias and groupthink in a team.
- Perspective modeling: seeing a situation from several sides.
- Cultural empathy: reading another culture without your own filter.

## Two tools

Two tools from the course show how the ideas turn into steps. Four Ways of Seeing takes a conflict between two parties, X and Y, and writes down four views: how X sees itself, how Y sees itself, how X sees Y, and how Y sees X. The gaps between those four views are where plans go wrong. PMESII-PT is a checklist for describing a place before acting in it, so that no part of local life gets skipped.

- Write out all four views before planning against the other side.
- PMESII-PT: political, military, economic, social.
- Then: information, infrastructure, physical environment, time.

## What it changes

The idea from the course with the widest use is that there is no normal position in cultural matters. Another group's patterns of thought have reasons behind them, even when those reasons cannot be seen from outside. Looked at that way, many problems that had been framed as an enemy can be settled by negotiation and other peaceful means. The handbook is published unclassified for the same reason, since people who understand each other work together better.

- Ask why a practice exists before judging it.
- Look for a peaceful option before a forceful one.
