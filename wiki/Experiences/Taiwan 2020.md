# Taiwan 2020

In late 2019 the owner began a stay in Taiwan to study Mandarin, and in February 2020 COVID forced a short trip to Japan. The observations from that time compare everyday life in Taiwan with everyday life in the United States. Two of them apply beyond travel: how widely manners vary within one country, and what a public rail system does for a city.

- Average friendliness in Taiwan is close to the United States.
- The United States has a much wider range, from very polite to rude.
- The Taipei MRT, the city's metro rail system, was the standout.
- The study goal was everyday conversation and getting around.
- COVID closed the planned trips, and only Japan stayed open.

## Manners: average and spread

Visitors often describe one country as friendlier than another. In Taiwan the typical level of friendliness is not much different from the United States. The difference is in the spread. In the United States politeness and rudeness cover a much wider range, most likely because of the range of cultures living there, while people in Taiwan cluster closer to the middle.

- Compare the range of behaviour as well as the average.
- A wide range means more very good and more very bad encounters.
- A narrow range makes daily dealings more predictable.

## Public transit

When someone asks for the favourite part of Taiwan, they expect to hear Taipei 101, the Yangmingshan mountains or the food. The answer from this stay is the MRT, the Mass Rapid Transit rail network in Taipei. A rail system that people can use for most of their daily trips is rare in American cities, and many Americans do not expect to see one at home.

- Landmarks are the expected answer to the favourite-part question.
- The daily rail network was the answer from this stay.
- Most American cities have no rail network on that scale.

## The study plan and COVID

The plan was a Mandarin school for conversation and practical travel Chinese, with short trips to other countries in Asia near the end of the first stay, and then a return to keep studying. The first trip was to Hong Kong. When COVID reached Hong Kong, Japan was the only place that could be visited without quarantine. An eight-day trip to Japan in February 2020 replaced the rest of the plan.

- Late 2019: arrival and school.
- Early 2020: Hong Kong, then COVID.
- February 2020: eight days in Japan.
