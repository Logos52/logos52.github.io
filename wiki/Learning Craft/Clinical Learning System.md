# Clinical Learning System

The clinical learning system is a way for doctors, residents and clinical students to decide what to study each evening, using the patients they saw that day. Clinical learners face a huge syllabus, very little free time, and exams that do not match what the ward teaches. Letting each day's patients set the order turns daily work into both the starting point for study and its review.

## Core takeaways

- The patients you saw today decide what you study tonight.
- Map one patient's journey, then branch it into variations.
- Study one branch a day, plus the basic science it touches.
- Pick the next patient to overlap an existing branch.
- Common cases come back, so review happens during normal work.
- Sweep the curriculum for small details near the exam.

## Why it works

Three features of clinical work make patient-led study possible. A patient is a learning resource, because examining one gives a context that a textbook cannot. The amount to learn is so large that nearly anything linked to the current patients will be needed later, so the order of study can follow the patients without much loss. Common conditions come back dozens of times in one placement, which gives spaced and mixed review of earlier study at no extra cost.

- Patients give context that makes study stick.
- Almost any related topic will be needed eventually.
- Recurring cases review what was studied before.
- Mapping three or four variations stretches one patient's value.

## The steps

The work happens after the shift, at home. It begins with a brain dump of everything the learner knows about one patient's main problem, laid out as that patient's path through care. A branch point goes wherever a finding changes the path, and each branch becomes a small topic to study. After two or three days on one patient, the next patient is chosen because the case shares a branch with the last map.

```
patient -> history -> exam -> tests -> treatment
                                |
                  CT positive   |   CT negative
                       |
          infection?  or  resistant organism?
                       |
          study: inflammation, antibiotics
```

- Pick the highest-yield learning point from one key patient.
- Map history, examination, tests, differential and management.
- Mark branch points where a result changes the path.
- Fill important gaps in the main path first.
- Study one branch a day, sometimes only half of one.
- Add linked basic science, such as cytokines or antibiotic action.
- Stay two or three days, then take an overlapping patient.
- Expect about two new patients a week.

## What to expect

The first weeks are overwhelming, because every map is full of unknowns and mapping the obvious variations can take a couple of hours. Learners who use the system tend to fall a little behind their peers at first and catch up by the third to fifth week. After three or four weeks some areas have come up so often that they are strong, and each new patient adds fewer new variables. The system says what to study and when, and the study session itself still uses ordinary encoding techniques.

- Prefer common conditions to rare ones.
- Prime tonight for tomorrow's cases, such as a listed operation.
- An unprimed ward round wastes much of its teaching.
- Best suited to learners who already think in relations.
