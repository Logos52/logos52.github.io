# Noticing Game

The noticing game is a way to pay attention to content in a language you cannot yet understand. You pick a set of words to listen or look for, then spot them as they come up. It gives a beginner something specific to do with a show or video they cannot yet follow, and each word spotted is a word heard in real speech, which is how it starts to become familiar.

- Pick targets before you start, then hunt for them.
- Full attention the whole time.
- Listening in the background does not count.
- Start with words you already know or can guess.
- Later targets: newly studied words, then a grammar point.
- A checked version uses subtitles to confirm what you heard.
- Nothing stands out: make the content easier.

## How to play

Attention needs a task, and following the story is not yet possible for a beginner. The game gives you a small, clear job while you listen: catch these words. Early on, the easiest targets are words that sound like words in your own language, words you already know, and the most common words from a frequency list. A bingo card of target words turns the list into something to fill in.

- Choose a list of target words.
- Watch or listen with full attention.
- Mark each target you catch.
- Out of targets: add a list or use words you just studied.

## The checked version

Once you have content with audio and matching subtitles, you can check each catch. When you think you heard a target, look at the subtitle line, or look the word up, to confirm it was there. The check turns a guess into a confirmed match. It also trains you to separate the word from the sounds around it.

- Hear a target, then read the line to confirm it.
- Use a pop-up dictionary to check a word you half recognise.

## Moving the targets up

The targets grow as you study. Words you studied with flashcards that day make good targets, since spotting them in real speech is the step after the flashcard. Once you start on grammar, a single grammar point becomes the target, and you look for it in real content.

```
words you know or that sound familiar
              |
              v
common words from a frequency list
              |
              v
words studied today
              |
              v
one grammar point
```

## When it fails

Two problems stop the game. If the content is at the right difficulty and you still drift off, the content is too dull, and the fix is a separate video account that recommends content you like in the language. If nothing stands out at all, the content is too hard, and the fix is an easier choice or a comprehension aid such as subtitles or slower playback.
