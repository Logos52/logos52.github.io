# Noticing Game

Early immersion becomes less opaque when the learner deliberately hunts for recognizable pieces. The Noticing Game is that hunt. The content stays real; the win condition shrinks to finding those pieces.

## One target set, then the pass

The basic version is five steps.

1. Pick short, tolerable content, so the session does not slide into background.
2. Choose **one** target set. Several sets at once become the over-checking failure later.
3. Watch with high attention. [[wiki/Language/Attention is Important|Attention is Important]] is why the game exists: attention given a concrete object.
4. Mark or mentally register what appeared. Marking produces a record; whether it outperforms mental registration is unverified.
5. Stop before the hour turns into passive background exposure.

The target set is a menu, not a curriculum. Cognates are legal for a beginner. Words from a frequency list are a reasonable early hunt — high-frequency vocabulary covers a large share of running text. A short written list of targets brought into the session is enough; ten already known, or ten from any free frequency list, can be made in a minute. Primed vocabulary is a valid target, and so are repeated sounds (useful while the writing system is still opaque), familiar characters or subtitles, and grammar forms recently reviewed in a primer.

## The basic version is complete

The technique gives attention a concrete object: a specific surface element, registered on purpose. Instead of passively watching incomprehensible content, the session searches for partial familiarity. That makes immersion usable earlier, and it gives repeated chances to connect priming with real input. That last clause is transfer of priming into input, not a spaced-repetition finding.

Freeflow noticing — no confirmation — is still the game. Confirmation is a variant, not an upgrade. Confirmation is also what makes the game a member of [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]; the basic version remains complete without that membership.

## Confirmation, and the four stops

The confirmation version adds a tool layer to check whether the noticed item was real. The class is subtitles, a popup dictionary, a transcript, or another metalayer. [[wiki/Language/Immersion Metalayers|Immersion Metalayers]] is that class, not a product review.

Confirmation earns its place in four situations:

- Target-language subtitles are on screen.
- Sound is being connected to written form.
- A word or grammar pattern is being tested for whether it was actually present.
- The session is Interactive Immersion rather than pure freeflow.

| Failure | Signal | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Passive watching | Nothing noticed can be named | A smaller target list, or shorter content |
| Over-checking | Every second becomes a lookup | Limit checks to likely targets |
| Content too hard | Nothing stands out | Easier, more visual, or previously watched content — [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu\|Hacking Comprehension Menu]] owns the lever list |
| Content too boring | Attention drifts even when difficulty is right | [[wiki/Language/YouTube Immersion Account\|YouTube Immersion Account]] |

The case against the game is a silently restored win condition: understand the whole thing. Confirmation fails when it turns every second into a lookup. The price is high attention on short content. Two sessions that named nothing, or two that were all lookups, are the quit signal. The checkable expectation is that something noticed can be named at the end.

A session that named nothing has left the hunt. A session that checked every second has left it the other way. The smaller win is still the whole: a few recognizable pieces, in real language, while the rest of the video stays allowed to be opaque.
