# Interactive Immersion

Interactive immersion is working through real content in the language you are learning with tools, such as a pop-up dictionary, pausing and rewinding, to understand lines you could not follow on your own. It suits content that is a little too hard to follow straight through and close enough to understand with help. It is where new words and grammar get worked out in real sentences.

- Real content plus tools, used on purpose.
- Use it when a line is understandable with some help.
- Work on sentences with one or two unknown words.
- Skip sentences with three or more unknown words.
- Ten minutes of work may cover two minutes of content.
- Balance it with watching without tools.

## How it works

Interactive immersion covers several activities. What they share is slow, close attention to a short stretch of content, checking what each line says. The most common form is intensive reading: play a line with subtitles in the language you are learning, try to understand it, and look up only what blocks you. Content with audio and matching subtitles works best, because you read and hear the same words.

- The noticing game: listen for chosen words, then check the subtitles.
- Intensive reading: line by line, with lookups.
- Intensive listening: replaying a short stretch of audio.
- Transcription: writing down what you hear.
- Sentence mining: turning found sentences into flashcards.

## Choosing which lines to work on

The useful lines are the ones you can almost understand. A sentence with one or two unknown words can be worked out with a lookup or two, and understanding it teaches those words in context. A sentence with three or more unknowns costs many lookups and may still not make sense, so move past it. Some sentences use only words you know and still do not make sense, and there the work is sorting out how the words fit together.

```
unknown words in the line
  0, but unclear  -> work out the grammar
  1 or 2          -> look up and understand
  3 or more       -> move on
```

- Count the known and unknown words in a sample sentence.
- No sentence with one or two unknowns: play the noticing game.
- Too many lines need lookups: use easier content.

## Balancing it with freeflow

Tool work is slow, so it covers little content per hour. Watching without tools, called freeflow, covers up to five times as much in the same time and trains quick understanding. Content worked through with tools becomes easy content to watch again without them. When you follow the gist without help, switch to freeflow.
