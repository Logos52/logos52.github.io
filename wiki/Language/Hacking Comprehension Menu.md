# Hacking Comprehension Menu

The hacking comprehension menu is a list of changes a language learner can make when a video, show or text in the language they are learning is too hard to follow. The changes come in three lists: tools, techniques and choices of content. Picking one item from each list usually brings hard content back to a level where the learner follows it and learns from it.

## Core takeaways

- Use it when a session fails because you understand too little.
- Pick at least one tool, one technique and one content change.
- Change one small thing, then check whether it helped.
- Keep the real content and make it easier to follow.
- Go back to watching without tools once you follow the gist.

## How to use it

Content you learn from is easy enough to follow and interesting enough to hold your attention, and a lot of content has one without the other. When a session stops making sense, adjust the content or the way you meet it before reaching for a textbook. Try one change, watch a few minutes, and see whether you now follow what is happening. Add a second change only if the first was not enough.

## Tools

Tools are software that sits on top of a video or text while you watch or read. They give you a way to see and check what is being said without leaving the content. Most of them come together in one browser extension for video sites.

- Subtitles in the language you are learning, matching the audio.
- A popup dictionary for looking up a word on the spot.
- Automatic pausing after each line, to give time to read.
- A blurred translation in your own language, to check yourself.

## Techniques

Techniques change how you go through the content. Each one gives you more time or more information for the same stretch of language. Priming and spoiling the plot are done before you watch, and the rest are done while you watch.

- Priming: study some of its words or grammar beforehand.
- Slow the playback, but not below 80 percent speed.
- Rewind or reread a line to get more tries at it.
- Look up a word, a sentence or a grammar point.
- Spoil the plot: read a summary first, then guess from context.

## Content

The last list changes what you watch or read. When you already know the story or the topic, or can see what is happening on screen, you can work out from context the words you miss. Rewatching something you have already seen is the easiest of these, because you already know what happens.

- Content you already watched in your own language.
- Content you already worked through with tools.
- Topics you know well, such as cooking, business or science.
- Simple content, such as children's shows or simple plots.
- Content with a lot of acting, pictures or diagrams.
