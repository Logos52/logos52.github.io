# Character Primer

A character primer is a short orientation that treats characters as reusable parts met in real words, not as a radical list or as isolated handwriting. Character-based writing systems become easier to enter once that layer is in place — components, recognition, repeated exposure — so the drawings stop looking arbitrary during immersion.

## What a character is

Chinese characters are reusable written units. Some function as words by themselves. Some only appear inside larger words. Many modern words are built from two or more characters. Familiar characters do not guarantee that a compound is obvious. Two characters can be one word meaning "immediately," two words meaning "above the horse," or one word meaning "on horseback." Only the surrounding sentence tells which. Some compounds do compose — fire plus mountain as volcano, cow plus meat as beef — so the claim is "not guaranteed," not "never."

Characters can have multiple meanings. They can carry pronunciation clues and meaning clues, often on both sides, never as a promise. A few still look a bit like the thing they name. Most do not. Context is what resolves the ambiguity. When context still fails — every character known, the line still opaque — a best attempt, then move on. More meetings, not a longer dwell, is the repair.

This is an orientation layer, not the mechanics course. Mechanics live on [[wiki/Language/Chinese/How Chinese Characters Work|How Chinese Characters Work]].

## Meeting them

Characters become easier to recognize when they stop looking like arbitrary shapes. A semantic component often hints at meaning. A sound component often hints at pronunciation. Leftover strokes that are neither still make the written form easier to hold. The goal is not to memorize every radical. The 214 is a dictionary index, not a study list. Recurring sound-parts are something to stay on the lookout for. A phonetic-component list is not.

The goal is enough pattern recognition that characters become less opaque during immersion — the same success test [[wiki/Language/Preparation|Preparation]] already uses. The work ties to real words, real subtitles, audio, awareness of simplified and traditional, a popup-dictionary as a tool class, and repeated encounters in immersion. Isolated handwriting memorization is not the default, unless handwriting itself becomes a goal.

For Mandarin the worked case is Traditional as the target and frequent Simplified in the available video. A lookup environment can convert Simplified to Traditional and make missing subtitles inspectable. That pair of jobs is most useful when the target script and the available content do not match. [[wiki/Language/Immersion Metalayers|Immersion Metalayers]] is script-conversion and lookup as a tool class, not a product.

Characters also do grammar work — a common completed-action particle is the stock case. [[wiki/Language/Refold Grammar Primers|Grammar primer]] holds those particle and pattern uses.

They stop looking like drawings once they are met as parts in real words. The cluster is where the mechanics live.
