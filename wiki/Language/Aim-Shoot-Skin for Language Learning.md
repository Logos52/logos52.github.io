# Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning

## Links into the knowledge base

- [[Aim]]: the general Aim, which is questions the material must answer.
- [[Shoot]]: the general Shoot, which is building the map against those questions.
- [[Skin]]: the general Skin, which is cutting the material down to what remains useful without the source.
- [[Bear Hunter System]]: the four-pass encode workflow; this loop is its language version.
- [[Inquiry-Based Learning]]: the related method in which questions are written and then answered.
- [[Refold Language Learning System]]: the three-mode immersion routine this loop is added to.
- [[wiki/Language/Preparation|Preparation]]: the small amount of study that makes a passage possible to start, which is not list-first study.
- [[wiki/Language/Three Pillars of Language Learning|Three Pillars of Language Learning]]: the same three-mode immersion routine.
- [[Interactive Immersion]]: where lookups happen; this loop limits them.
- [[Rote Learning and Memorisation|Flashcards]]: used here only to fill gaps.

## Sources

- Hu, M., & Nation, I. S. P. (2000). Unknown vocabulary density and reading comprehension. *Reading in a Foreign Language*, 13(1), 403–430. Unassisted reading comprehension near 98% known words; 80% already frustrating.
- Nation, I. S. P. (2006). How large a vocabulary is needed for reading and listening? *The Canadian Modern Language Review*, 63(1), 59–82. The range of 2% unknown and 98% known words for unassisted reading.
- Craik, F. I. M., & Tulving, E. (1975). Depth of processing and the retention of words in episodic memory. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 104(3), 268–294. Elaborative encoding; supports the direction of the claim that words learned to solve an immediate problem are remembered better.
- Bjork, R. A. (1994). Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings. In J. Metcalfe & A. P. Shimamura (Eds.), *Metacognition: Knowing about knowing* (pp. 185–205). MIT Press. Desirable difficulties; generation and effortful retrieval support the direction of the claim and are not a strict comparison.
- Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011). Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping. *Science*, 331(6018), 772–775. Retrieval and generation can produce more learning than elaborative study; this is still not a trial of a dialogue against a frequency list.
