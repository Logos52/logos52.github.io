# Vietnamese Grammar Primer

Vietnamese grammar for a beginner comes down to a short list of patterns, read once and then looked for in real speech and text. Vietnamese words never change their form, so most of the grammar is word order and a small set of helper words. A learner who knows the handful of patterns below can follow the shape of most everyday sentences.

## Core takeaways

- Words never change form for tense, number or person.
- The basic order is subject, verb, object.
- Describing words come after the noun they describe.
- A counting word sits between a number and a noun.
- Question words stay where the answer would go.
- People address each other with family words instead of fixed pronouns.

## How it works

Vietnamese is an analytic language, which means its words do not change form. The meaning that English puts into word endings is carried by separate words or left to context. Time is marked by an optional word before the verb, and is often not marked at all when the situation makes it clear. Many sentences open with a topic and then say something about it. Mood and politeness are carried by small words at the end of the sentence.

```
Tôi   đã     ăn    cơm.
I     [past] eat   rice      = I ate.

Anh   ăn     gì?
you   eat    what            = What are you eating?
```

## The patterns

Each item below is a pattern to notice in input, with its most common example. The list is incomplete on purpose. When a sentence in input fits none of these, the learner moves on and comes back to it after more hours of listening.

- Order: Tôi ăn cơm, I eat rice.
- Time words: đã past, đang ongoing, sẽ future.
- Negation: không before the verb, as in tôi không ăn.
- Describing words follow: nhà đẹp, house beautiful.
- Plural: các or những before the noun, when needed.
- Counting words: một con mèo, one [animal] cat.
- Con marks animals, and cái marks many objects.
- Yes-no questions: có before the verb, không at the end.
- Question words in place: anh ăn gì, you eat what.
- Topic first: a topic, then a comment about it.

## Pronouns

Vietnamese speakers mostly refer to themselves and each other with family words, chosen by age and relationship. The same person can be anh, older brother, to one listener and em, younger sibling, to another. A learner has to hear who is speaking to whom to know who anh or em means in a sentence. Getting the choice wrong sounds rude or odd.

- Anh: an older man near your age.
- Chị: an older woman near your age.
- Em: someone younger.
- Cô, chú: a woman or man around a parent's age.
- Ông, bà: a man or woman around a grandparent's age.
