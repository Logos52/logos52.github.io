# Vietnamese Grammar Primer

The Vietnamese grammar primer is a short noticing catalog of the extra words and word order that carry meaning, used in real speech rather than memorized for output. Meaning rides on separate words and order — analytic, in the field sense — not on changing the verb's shape. The catalog is not a comprehensive grammar.

## How this catalog is used

The sentences on this page are writer-invented and still need a native check against references and real immersion. They are good enough to hunt with; they are not yet a finished authority.

A small base comes first: some listening or video already running; basic tones and pronunciation; a small word set; a popup dictionary as a class, not a named app; a dialect decision. Dialect is a decision, not an optional extra. Pronouns, particles, and some question words shift. Listed forms default to the textbook-aligned Northern set so the tables have one shape. Southern variants are marked only where they actually differ. No diet is picked for the reader.

The catalog is a comprehension aid. Patterns get recognized in subtitles, speech, comments, captions, and learner material. Every structure does not get memorized before immersing. One small section is read, content resumes, the pattern is noticed. That loop belongs to the [[wiki/Language/Refold Grammar Primers|grammar primer]] sibling — the stance and loop this catalog serves. Five steps for tomorrow: read one section; choose one pattern; watch or read; mark examples; return when the same confusion repeats. ## Extra words that do grammar's job

Word order, particles, classifiers, aspect markers, pronouns, and context carry a lot of meaning. The basic sentence is often SVO: `Tôi học.` / `Tôi học tiếng Việt.` Topic-fronting is common — a topic can sit in front of that skeleton — so "always SVO" is the wrong law. Serialization is real and is not a section here.

The verb keeps one shape. Person and time do not bend it. Time words and the surrounding situation do the job English puts on verb endings.

Four aspect markers, not tenses. They are optional and they can stack. They are not one-to-one English tense equivalents.

| Marker | What it often signals | Where it usually sits | Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `đã` | past, or completed | before the verb | `Tôi đã ăn cơm.` |
| `đang` | ongoing | before the verb | `Tôi đang học tiếng Việt.` |
| `sẽ` | upcoming | before the verb | `Tôi sẽ đi Hà Nội.` |
| `rồi` | completion or a change of state | after the phrase, or at the end | `Tôi ăn rồi.` |

The noticing hook is position: `đã` before the verb, `rồi` after. Same completed feel, different seat.

Negation is early and frequent. `không` plus a verb or adjective is "not" / "no": `Tôi không biết.` `chưa` plus a verb is "not yet," and it implies the thing still has not happened: `Tôi chưa ăn.`

Question words stay in place — no movement to the front. The set to recognize: `ai`, `gì`, `ở đâu`, `khi nào` (Southern and general) / `bao giờ` (Northern), `tại sao` / `vì sao`, `như thế nào`. `Bạn tên gì?` is asking for a name; `gì` has not left its seat. The goal is to recognize when a sentence is asking for missing information, not to produce a question on command.

Yes/no has a frame: `có ... không`. `Bạn có khỏe không?` `chưa` can also end a question, "yet?": `Ăn cơm chưa?`

## The part English will not give you

Address in Vietnamese is a social act. Family-style words and relationship terms stand in for the people in the room, in place of a single neutral I/you. That system is kinship address.

Choice depends on age, relationship, politeness, intimacy, and context. The same English "you" may appear as many different Vietnamese forms in a single afternoon. Immersion is essential because pronouns are social behavior, not just vocabulary. A rulebook flattens that. The catalog exists to make the flattening visible, so the next conversation can be marked instead of translated into a single English pair.

## Classifiers, modifiers, particles

A counted noun takes a small word that sorts it by type. That word is a classifier. The pattern is number + classifier + noun. Three show up constantly: `cái` for inanimates (`một cái bàn`), `con` for animals or things in motion (`hai con chó`), `người` for people (`một người bạn`). This is not a classifier inventory.

Descriptive modifiers often follow the noun. `nhà đẹp` is "beautiful house" — the highest-value contrast with English, because the adjective sits where English does not put it.

Possession can be expressed with `của`: noun + `của` + person or noun. `nhà của tôi`. The same relation may also be understood from context, with no `của` at all.

Location words earn their keep in visual content, because the scene can confirm them: `ở`, `trong`, `ngoài`, `trên`, `dưới`, `trước`, `sau`, `bên cạnh`.

Particles shape tone, politeness, confirmation, or emphasis: `à` (surprise or confirm), `ạ` (polite), `nhé` (Northern softener) / `nha` (Southern softener), `mà` (contrast), `thì` (topic). `Cảm ơn ạ.` `Đi đi nhé.` The list is not a course. Particles are not mastered from a list. They get noticed in context and connected to the social situation that produced them.

## Quick reference and hunts

| Area | Early noticing target |
|---|---|
| Extra words | Meaning rides on separate words and order, not verb endings |
| Word order | SVO as the default skeleton; a topic can come first |
| Aspect | `đã` / `đang` / `sẽ` before the verb; `rồi` after |
| Negation | `không` not; `chưa` not yet |
| Questions | words stay in place; `có ... không`; `chưa` as "yet?" |
| Address | kinship and relationship terms standing in for I/you |
| Classifiers | number + `cái` / `con` / `người` + noun |
| Modifiers | noun then adjective: `nhà đẹp` |
| Possession | noun + `của` + person, or context alone |
| Particles | `à` `ạ` `nhé`/`nha` `mà` `thì` — in the situation, not from this row |

Five hunts, each one pattern and one stretch of input:

- Mark every `không`, `đã`, `đang`, or `sẽ` in a short video.
- Identify question words in one learner video.
- List pronouns and address terms in one conversation.
- Check one `của` phrase in a dictionary.
- Find three noun + adjective phrases in subtitles.

A ten-row catalog will miss usages and serialization. A reader who wants conjugation tables will bounce. The price is one section plus one video, not a grammar course. The quit signal is tables being memorized, input postponed until the catalog is "finished," or particles being learned from the six-item list above. The checkable expectation: the chosen pattern is markable in the next short video, still incomplete.

The same short list, after a pattern has a name: walk into a video with one row, mark what matches, and come back when the same confusion repeats.
