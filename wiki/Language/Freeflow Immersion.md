# Freeflow Immersion

Freeflow immersion is watching, listening to or reading content in the language you are learning straight through, with no lookups, no pausing and no rewinding. It is one of three study modes, next to preparation, which is studying the language itself with flashcards and grammar notes, and interactive immersion, which is working through content with tools. Freeflow is the mode that turns what you have studied into instinct, and it gets through far more of the language in an hour than the other two.

## Takeaways

- No tools: no dictionary, no pausing, no rewinding.
- Full attention the whole time.
- Listening in the background does not count.
- Up to five times more exposure than working with tools.
- The content must interest you and be easy enough to follow.
- The test is whether you follow the gist.
- It adds to tool-based study and does not replace it.

## How it works

Studying a word or a grammar point makes it something you know consciously. Meeting it again and again in content you understand, at normal speed and without stopping, makes it something you use without thinking. Freeflow is also practice for real conversations and real shows, where no tools are available. Because nothing stops the content, an hour of freeflow gets through up to five times as much language as an hour with tools.

- Pick content you enjoy and mostly follow.
- Watch or listen once through, attending the whole time.
- Missing details is fine if the gist is there.
- Afterwards, note how much you understood.
- Note whether it was interesting, fun and easy to focus on.

## Making it easier

Freeflow fails when the content is too hard to follow or too dull to hold attention, and in both cases little is learned. The simplest fix is content you already know. A show you have already worked through with tools, or already watched in your own language, is much easier to follow the second time. If you get the gist and still miss much of it, keep going, since understanding grows with the hours.

- Rewatch content you already worked through with tools.
- Watch content you have already seen in your own language.
- Choose topics you already know well.
- Still lost after that: go back to interactive immersion.
- Reaching for tools often: call the session interactive.

## Getting used to not understanding

Adults are used to understanding their own language completely, so not understanding feels uncomfortable. A short session with hard content and no tools, with subtitles in the language you are learning allowed, trains the habit of staying with it and noticing what is familiar. The same habit keeps a freeflow session going through the parts you miss.
