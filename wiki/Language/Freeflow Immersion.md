# Freeflow Immersion

Freeflow immersion is a way to spend time in the language that lets the learner unwind and enjoy the material while remaining attentive. It builds self-sufficiency by relying on subconscious understanding. It also prepares the learner for real-world situations where language tools will not be at hand.

That mix of ease and attention is also why it can give so much more exposure. One advantage is up to five times more contact with the language. If the learner is not engaged with the content, nothing is gained from it. The content has to match both interest and skill level.

Freeflow does not take the place of intensive immersion, and it remains high-attention work. Without attention it has no value.

## How a session looks

Because the point is to rely on understanding that is already forming, the session reuses content rather than hunting for something new. Reuse content that has already been used for interactive immersion. Watch it without lookups or rewinding. Afterward, note how much was understood, whether it was interesting, whether it was fun, and whether focus held.

If the gist comes through and some parts stay unclear, keep immersing. Understanding comes with time. Re-watching content that was already done intensively, or that was already seen in a first language, makes freeflow easier.

A session around this work is 45 minutes in total. Twenty minutes of that session are time in the language spent this way.

## Open questions
