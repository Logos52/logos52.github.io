# Freeflow Immersion

Freeflow is relaxed but attentive time with real language that the learner can already follow for the gist. The learner almost never pauses, rewinds, or looks things up. Attention is still required. A session with zero attention does not count.

## Core takeaways

- Freeflow is relaxed, attentive time with real language the learner can already follow for the gist, with almost no pausing, rewinding, or looking things up.
- Attention is still required. A session with zero attention does not count.
- The mode fits when the gist already holds, the content is interesting enough to hold attention, and the learner needs practice understanding without tools.
- Fewer interruptions give more minutes of input per hour. There is no multiplier on top of those minutes.
- Freeflow does not replace Interactive Immersion. It converts conscious knowledge into faster instinct.
- Freeflow fails in three ways: attention elsewhere, content too hard to follow for the gist, or constant pausing and lookup.

## When freeflow is the right mode

Exposure needs a mode with less friction, where attention stays active without constant tool use. Freeflow is that mode. It converts conscious knowledge into faster instinct. It is one of three modes, and the set of three is held at [[wiki/Language/Three Pillars of Language Learning|Three Pillars of Language Learning]].

Freeflow fits when the gist already holds. Gist means the main events or the point, without every sentence. The content also has to be interesting enough to hold attention. Freeflow also fits when the learner needs practice understanding without tools.

Fewer interruptions give more minutes of input per hour. The gain is those minutes. There is no multiplier on top of them.

## Two ways to start

The default way is content already used in [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]. The second way is a rewatch of something already seen in the learner's native language. A beginner who has done no Interactive Immersion yet can use the second way today.

## What a good session looks like

Good freeflow has four parts: high attention, low interruption, content close enough to enjoy, and a willingness to tolerate gaps. [[wiki/Language/Attention is Important|Attention is Important]] holds the zero-attention rule that freeflow depends on. If the gist holds and some sentences do not, the learner keeps going. That allowance covers gaps in understanding. It does not cover a session where attention was elsewhere.

## Notes after the session

After the session the learner writes short notes on four questions. How much was understood? Was it interesting? Was it fun? Was focus possible? These four notes are the check on the session. The focus note is the most important one.

## How freeflow fails

Freeflow does not replace interactive work. It is always a high-attention activity. It fails in three ways.

| Failure | Signal | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Passive exposure | The content played but attention was elsewhere | Shorten the session or choose better content. Improve content selection before adding harder techniques. |
| Too difficult | The gist cannot be followed | Use [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu|Hacking Comprehension Menu]], or return to [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]. |
| Over-tooling | Constant pausing and lookup | Rename the session as interactive immersion. Guilt is not needed. |

When the gist is gone, freeflow is the wrong mode. When attention is gone, the session has only the form of freeflow, and it does not count. Not looking things up is intended, so it is not one of the failures. If the gist is still gone after one repair attempt, the learner switches mode or content. The check is the four notes, and the focus note most of all.

After a repair, the mode is the same as before: relaxed and attentive. Zero attention still does not count. Interactive Immersion is still available as the harder mode.

## How to practice this

1. Pick content whose gist you can already follow: the main events or point, without every sentence. The default choice is content you already used in [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]. Notice whether the content is interesting enough to hold your attention.
2. If you have no Interactive Immersion content yet, rewatch something you have already seen in your native language. Notice whether the gist holds and the content still holds your attention.
3. Watch or listen without pausing, rewinding, or looking things up. When the gist holds but a sentence does not, keep going. Notice the gaps you leave alone. Tolerating them is part of good freeflow.
4. Keep your attention on the content for the whole session. Notice when your attention goes elsewhere. A session with zero attention does not count.
5. Afterward, write four short notes. Record how much was understood, whether it was interesting, whether it was fun, and whether focus was possible. The focus note is the one to read most closely.
6. If attention was elsewhere, shorten the next session or choose better content. If the gist could not be followed, use [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu|Hacking Comprehension Menu]] or return to Interactive Immersion. If the gist is still gone after one repair attempt, switch mode or content. If you paused and looked things up constantly, call the session interactive immersion.
