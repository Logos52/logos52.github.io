# YouTube Immersion Account

A YouTube Immersion Account is a second YouTube identity used only for the language being learned. Its watch-history holds only that language, so YouTube's recommendations move toward that language instead of toward the mixed English viewing of a shared account.

The account does not depend on more discipline on a shared account. It depends on a separate watch-history, which trains the recommendations. The feed exists to supply compelling content: material that would be watched even without a study goal.

## Core takeaways

- A separate identity gives a separate watch-history. Clicks, searches, and subscriptions on it are all in the language being learned, so the signals do not mix with English.
- The feed is for compelling content, meaning material that would be watched even without a study goal. Language immersion depends on repeated access to material that can hold attention.
- Early on, the videos need to be beginner-friendly or visually rich, so the gist is reachable.
- The routine has four moves: seed the recommendations, subscribe, keep unrelated content off the identity, and prune. Within a few sessions the homepage should be mostly the target language.
- The trained feed is supply for immersion practice. It is not a second study method. The quit signal is the account being used for English, or turning into a folder of learner videos nobody would watch.

## What the feed should hold

Target language first. Clicks, searches, and subscriptions on this identity are in the language being learned. A mixed history mixes the signals. A separate watch-history keeps them apart.

Dialect or writing system, when the difference matters. The feed can be trained toward one variety of the language or toward one script. No single variety or script is prescribed.

Beginner-friendly or visually rich videos, early on. The gist has to be reachable. [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu|Hacking Comprehension Menu]] is how to keep a found video in the zone.

Compelling channels, worth returning to. Language immersion depends on repeated access to material that can hold attention. [[wiki/Language/Attention is Important|Attention is Important]] is why compelling content is not optional. Channels worth revisiting keep the account an immersion tool. Without them it becomes a homework folder: a set of learner videos nobody would watch anyway.

## Setting up the identity

Use a YouTube identity that does not share history with the English one. Create a new one, or switch to one that already exists. The function that matters is a separate watch-history. The instruction is that separate history, not whatever name YouTube's menu gives the option this year.

## Finding channels

Search in the target language first, then use the catalogs. [[wiki/Resources/Vietnamese Language Learning Resources|Vietnamese Language Learning Resources]] is the catalog for search and for the dialect case. [[wiki/Resources/Mandarin Chinese Language Learning Resources|Mandarin Chinese Language Learning Resources]] is the catalog for search and for the script and subtitle case. Channel lists belong in those catalogs.

## Training the feed

The routine has four moves.

Seed. Watch enough to seed the recommendations. How much counts as enough has not been measured.

Subscribe. Subscribe to channels that are tolerable or actually compelling.

Keep unrelated content off. Unrelated native-language content stays off this identity.

Prune. Periodically click more of what works.

## Two worked cases: dialect and writing system

Vietnamese is the worked case for dialect. Tag channels Southern, Northern, mixed, or unknown when the difference is audible. No variety is assigned.

Mandarin is the worked case for writing system. Tag a channel for simplified subtitles, traditional subtitles, audio-only listening, or character reading. Traditional is the vault target. The four tags stand on their own, with no personal note attached.

## Costs, quit signal, and what to expect

A second identity is one more login. A feed with too little history is worse than a mixed one for the first week. The price is a free identity plus the refusal to watch English on it. No paid YouTube feature is required.

The quit signal: the account is being used for English, or it has become a homework folder.

The checkable expectation: within a few sessions the homepage is mostly the target language, and it includes something that would be watched anyway.

## Where the content goes

The trained feed is supply for [[wiki/Language/Noticing Game|Noticing Game]], [[wiki/Language/Freeflow Immersion|Freeflow Immersion]], and [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]. Those three cover what to do with the content once the feed is producing it: the Noticing Game for what to do with a piece of content, Freeflow Immersion for use at volume, and Interactive Immersion for use with tools on. The account is not a second study method.

## How to practice this

1. Create a YouTube identity that does not share history with your English one, or switch to one. Notice that for the first week the feed is worse than a mixed one. That is expected.
2. Search in the target language, then look in the Vietnamese or Mandarin catalog. Notice which found videos let you reach the gist.
3. Watch enough to seed the recommendations, then subscribe to channels that are tolerable or actually compelling. Notice whether any channel is one you would watch without a study goal.
4. Tag each channel. For Vietnamese, notice whether the difference is audible, then tag Southern, Northern, mixed, or unknown. For Mandarin, tag simplified subtitles, traditional subtitles, audio-only listening, or character reading.
5. Keep unrelated native-language content off this identity, and periodically click more of what works. Notice whether, within a few sessions, the homepage is mostly the target language. Notice whether it includes something you would watch anyway.
6. Watch for the quit signal. Notice whether the account is being used for English, or whether it has become a homework folder. Either one means stop.
