# Attention is Important

Building an instinct for a new language takes a great deal of time spent immersing in it. The hard part, early on, is how to pay attention and learn when the language is not yet understood. The way through that stretch is to pay attention even before the language makes sense, and to have fun doing it.

That is why time spent in the language is treated as a high-attention activity. Background listening or passive listening does not count.

## What to look for

The practice that makes that attention concrete is the noticing game. While spending time in the language, look for cognates, words already known, or words from a word list or bingo card. If those lists run out, notice things from priming, meaning earlier study done to raise comprehension during later immersion, or use additional word lists.

The first session that introduces this work is short on purpose, so the habit can start before the language is understood. It is 20 minutes in total. Ten minutes go to learning the writing system. Once the phonetic writing system is comfortable, that same block becomes more time with the language. The other ten minutes are time in the language spent on the noticing work above.

## Open questions

- How does looking for a known word change what a learner does with a stretch of speech they cannot yet follow?
- How does background listening change whether time in the language still does any work?
