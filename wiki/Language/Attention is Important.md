# Attention is Important

A barely-understood language hour counts as a session when the learner registers a word, a sound, a subtitle, or a pattern as present. Early language content is often hard to understand, and the learner still needs the exposure. The hour counts because the learner noticed something.

Immersion here means time with real language: shows, videos, talk, text. Time spent only on lessons about the language is not immersion.

## Core takeaways

- A barely-understood hour counts as a session when the learner registers one word, sound, subtitle, or pattern as present.
- Immersion is time with real language: shows, videos, talk, text. Lessons about the language are not enough on their own.
- The list of targets to notice is small: ten items, ten visual minutes, and a mark on each item that appeared.
- Attention keeps the hour from turning into passive listening. It builds familiarity with sounds, words, subtitles, visual cues, and recurring patterns.
- An hour with zero attention does not count as a session. Background listening does not count either.
- The learner does not need to understand everything. Enough engagement to look, listen, predict, compare, or confirm is what counts.

## What the learner does with attention

During the hour the learner uses attention for one task: notice something on purpose. The thing noticed is a word, a sound, a subtitle, or a pattern, and the learner registers that item as present. [[wiki/Language/Noticing Game|Noticing Game]] describes this task as a procedure.

## The target list

The learner does not need a student-only sheet to build the target list. The list is made of cognates, words already known, and words primed yesterday. The procedure is ten items, ten visual minutes, and a mark on each item that appeared. Nothing more is needed. When comprehension is low, the target list is what fixes the hour: the learner still has items to find.

## What attention does for the hour

Attention keeps the time from becoming passive listening. It builds familiarity with sounds, words, subtitles, visual cues, and recurring patterns. Difficult content is less flat to sit through when there is a game to play. Attention also produces data the learner can use to regulate the next session: what was easy to notice, what was never noticed, and what broke focus.

Active attention gives immersion a series of small targets. Without it, immersion is vague exposure. Attention with a target is [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]] at the level of a single word or sound. The split between active attention and vague exposure is the split in [[wiki/Concepts/Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming|Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming?]], applied to language input.

## When the hour stops counting

If attention drops to zero, the session is no longer a useful language-learning session. Background listening does not count. Passive listening does not count. [[wiki/Language/Freeflow Immersion|Freeflow Immersion]] is the lower-tool mode of immersion, and the zero-attention rule applies there too.

The rule does not require understanding everything. It requires enough engagement to look, listen, predict, compare, or confirm.

So the barely-understood hour has one job, to notice something, and one fail condition, attention at zero. An hour with zero attention does not count. An hour where much of the content goes ununderstood counts, as long as something was noticed.

## How to practice this

1. Before the hour, pick ten target items: cognates, words you already know, or words primed yesterday. You do not need a student-only sheet for this. Notice that every item on the list is something you already partly hold.
2. Watch ten visual minutes of real language: a show, a video, talk, or text. Mark each target item that appears. Notice that the marking gives you something to do even when you understand little.
3. While watching, look, listen, predict, compare, or confirm. Notice that you do not need to understand everything for the marking to work.
4. After the session, write down what was easy to notice, what you never noticed, and what broke your focus. This record is your data for regulating the next session.
5. Check whether your attention dropped to zero at any point. If it did, that stretch was background listening and does not count as a session.

## Open questions

Whether a session with partial attention, some noticing and some drift, should count as a fraction of the hour or as a failed hour.
