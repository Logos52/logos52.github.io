# Sound Components

A sound component is the part of a Chinese character that hints at how the character is pronounced. Most Chinese characters have one, paired with a part that hints at the meaning. A sound component rarely gives the exact reading, but it narrows the reading to a small range, and that is enough to make a new character easier to learn and an old one easier to recall.

## Takeaways

- A sound component narrows the reading to a small range.
- Tones almost never carry from the part to the character.
- Odd-looking matches usually sounded close in ancient Chinese.
- Some first consonants are related, such as zh and d.
- Guess the meaning and the sound before opening the dictionary.

## How it works

Pictures run out for words like north, so early writers borrowed a character that already existed for a word that sounded similar. English could do the same thing with a picture of an ear standing in for the word here. 北 was a drawing of a person's back, and the word for back sounded like the word for north, so 北 wrote both. Later, to tell back from north, 月, a body part, was added underneath to write back, giving 背, while 北 kept north.

```
  北  a drawing of a person's back
   |  borrowed for "north", which sounded alike
   v
  北  north
  背  back  =  北 (sound) + 月 (body)
```

- 北 is both sound and meaning in 背.
- Ancient Chinese most likely had no tones, so tones do not match.
- Small old sound differences grew into large modern ones.
  - 監 jiān is the sound part of 藍 lán.
  - 尚 shàng is the sound part of 堂 táng and 黨 dǎng.
  - 占 zhàn is the sound part of 店 diàn.

## The range drill

A common complaint is that sound components are useless because they do not give the exact reading. English spelling does not give it either, and a reader who meets archetype in print can guess its sound and still get it wrong. A fairer test is whether the part narrows the choice. The rare character 樘 shows what the parts give and what they leave to the dictionary.

- 木, tree, says the meaning has to do with trees or wood.
- 尚 says the reading is shang, chang, zhang, tang or dang.
- The dictionary gives the exact reading, táng, a kind of pear tree.

## How to use it

Make the guess a habit on every unknown character: name the likely meaning part and the likely sound part, guess the meaning and the reading, then check. Guessing and then checking is what fixes the character in memory. When a sound part looks wrong, look for the older story behind it, such as a borrowing, a reading that split in two, or a later character that took over a sense.

- 背 has two readings, bèi and bēi, from one root.
- Some senses moved to newer characters: 寺 to 持, 兌 to 悅, 各 to 格.
- Tones come from listening, never from the sound part.
