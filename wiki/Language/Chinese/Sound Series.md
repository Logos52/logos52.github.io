# Sound Series

A sound series is the set of Chinese characters that share one sound component, the part that hints at pronunciation. The readings inside a series often look unrelated in modern Mandarin, because the links were made in Old Chinese and sounds have changed since. A learner who knows which sounds are related can see the family resemblance, trust the sound part again, and guess better about characters never studied.

- Few series have identical readings, and most vary in a regular way.
- Tones do not count when matching a character to its series.
- Related first consonants are made in the same part of the mouth.
- Seven groups of related first consonants cover most variation.
- Learn the seven groups before the places where groups mix.
- Compare a new character with known ones that share its sound part.

## How it works

A rare few series match exactly, so 府 fǔ gives 俯, 腐 and 腑, all read fǔ. More often the tones differ, as in 侯 hóu, 候 hòu and 猴 hóu. Most series go further and change the first consonant, but only to a related one. To test whether one consonant is related to another, say both aloud and notice where in the mouth each is made, since g, k and h are all made at the back and d is made at the front.

| Group | Example series |
| --- | --- |
| t, d | 同 tóng: 洞 dòng, 筒 tǒng |
| zh, ch, sh | 成 chéng: 城 chéng, 盛 shèng |
| j, q, x | 僉 qiān: 險 xiǎn, 劍 jiàn |
| b, p, f | 非 fēi: 排 pái, 悲 bēi |
| g, k, h | 舌 guā: 括 kuò, 話 huà |
| z, c, s | 曾 zēng: 增 zēng, 僧 sēng |
| m, w | 門 mén: 問 wèn, 們 men |

- The vowels and endings can shift too.
- 勁 jìn has an irregular -n ending, a labelled exception.
- The 舌 in 話 is short for an old character read guā.
- That old character is a different one from 舌 shé, tongue.

## Crossings between groups

Three pairs of groups also mix, because of sound changes over time. English shows the same kind of change: the t in -tion is said as sh, and what you becomes watcha. The name George has two soft g sounds where German Georg keeps the hard g. Learn the seven groups first, and the crossings then become easy to notice without memorising them.

- g, k, h with j, q, x: 古 gǔ gives 苦 kǔ, 胡 hú and 居 jū.
- d, t with zh, ch, sh: 單 dān gives 彈 tán, 戰 zhàn and 闡 chǎn.
- g, k, h with l: 各 gè gives 客 kè, 路 lù and 落 luò.

## How to use it

Learners often reject a real link because the letters differ, such as 比 bǐ as the sound part of 批 pī. Once someone they trust says the sounds are related, they accept the link and start seeing others. Study a series when it comes up: when a new character has a sound part, compare it with the known characters that share it. A short rhyme can hold a group, such as one pairing the surnames Zhāng, Shàng and Chén with Tāng and Dèng.

- Score series membership without tones, and speech with them.
- Two readings of one character are usually related: 盛 chéng and shèng.
- When two old characters merged into one shape, treat them as separate.
