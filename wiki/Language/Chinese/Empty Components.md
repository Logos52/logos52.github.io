# Empty Components

An empty component is a part of a Chinese character that gives neither its sound nor its meaning. Some were added on purpose as small marks to tell two characters apart, and others are what is left of older parts whose shapes wore down over the centuries. A learner who knows a part is empty, and why, will not build a memory story on it as if it carried meaning.

- An empty part gives no sound and no meaning in that character.
- Call a part empty only after ruling out picture, meaning and sound.
- A distinguishing mark was added to separate two words.
- A worn-down part is an old part that changed shape over time.
- Every empty part has a reason, and the reason is worth learning.
- Picture an empty part as see-through glass in the memory image.

## How it works

Early writers often used one character for two words and relied on context to tell them apart. When context stopped being enough, one fix was to add a small mark whenever the character was written for one word and leave it off for the other. The mark carries no sound or meaning of its own, and its only job is to show that this is the other word. When the mark was added early, each character then changed shape on its own, so the two look less alike today than they once did.

- 夫, a man, is 大 with a mark added.
- 少, few, is 小, small, with a mark added.
- 否 is 不 with a mark added.
- 高, tall, is 京 with 口 added.
  - 京 drew a tall building.
  - Tall buildings stood in cities, so 京 came to mean the capital.

## Worn-down parts

Over time the shape of a character can change until the way it was first built no longer shows. A part that now looks like a known character may then be what is left of something else. 射, to shoot, began as a bow and an arrow with a hand added. The bow later came to look like 身, the body, and an old explanation said the body is there because an archer pulls the string toward it.

```
  射 then:  bow + arrow + hand
  射 now:   身 (looks like "body") + 寸 (hand)
            身 is the worn-down bow: empty
```

- 春, spring, had grass, a sun and 屯 tún for the sound.
- The grass and 屯 merged into the top of modern 春.
- The same top in 泰 comes from two hands and another sound part.
- 夕 in 外 was once 月 yuè and gave the sound.

## How to remember them

Empty parts are the hardest parts to remember, because nothing in them points to the sound or the meaning. A memory story helps, as long as the rest of the story follows the parts that do give sound or meaning. An old folk explanation, such as the body in 射, is fair material once it is labelled as memorable and wrong. Invent your own story before reading anyone else's, since a story you make holds better.

- Name the reason each empty part is there.
- Draw the empty part as see-through glass in the memory image.
- Label a folk explanation as folk.
