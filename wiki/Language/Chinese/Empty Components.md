# Empty Components

An empty component does not relate to the sound or the meaning of the word being written. It gives neither a sound nor a meaning. In this character, it is empty. That status is not a fixed property of the shape. The same piece can be empty here and functional somewhere else.

There are two main kinds. Distinguishing marks were added to keep two words apart. Corrupted components are what remain after a character's original internal structure has been distorted. A piece can also be empty because it was added as decoration, to regularize a shape.

## Distinguishing marks

In early writing, one character was often used for more than one word, and context usually told them apart. One later fix was to add a semantic component, a meaning-bearing piece, as with 背. Another fix was to add a mark whose only job was to say that this writing is word A, while the original character stays with word B.

夫 shows a standing person from the front, just as 大 does. A mark is added so that the word being written is not 大. 少 takes 小. Here 小 works as a meaning component and as a sound component at once. A mark is added to keep the two characters apart. 否 and 不 are another such pair.

京 depicts a tall building and, by extension, a capital city, because such buildings appeared in cities. 高 is the same depiction, meaning "tall," with 口 added to distinguish it from 京. The two resembled each other more closely in the old script. After the mark was added they evolved differently, so the modern shapes look less alike.

## Corruption

Sometimes a character's form changes until the original internal structure is no longer intact. That is called corruption. 射 began as a drawing of a bow pulled with an arrow. A hand was later added to emphasize the drawing of the bow. Over time the bow was distorted until it resembled 身, "body," and was reread as that component. The traditional story, that body is there because a person draws the string toward the body, is not the historical structure. The body-shaped piece is empty, a leftover of the bow.

春 originally had vegetation and sun as semantic pieces, which fit spring. It also had 屯 as a sound component. 屯 sits in a sound group that can interact with zh, ch, and sh, so it fits 春. Over time 屯 and the vegetation piece merged into the top of 春. That top shape shows up in other characters too, always as a corruption, and not always from the same original pieces. In 泰, the top is a corruption of two hands plus the original sound piece.

外 has a 夕 that was once 月, yuè. The top of 春, written 𡗗, was 屯, tún.

## What to do with an empty piece

Empty components are hard to remember because they do not point at sound or meaning. A memory story can help, and it should rest on the character's functional components, not on a breakdown into pieces that do no work. The story of 偷 as someone slipping into a building after dark, knife in hand, is the kind to avoid.

Two workable sources for a story are the traditional explanation, used as a memorable account even when it is not historical, and the learner's own imagination. Folk explanations are fine when they are labeled as memorable rather than historical. The learner should invent a story before reading someone else's. In the image itself, an empty piece can be pictured as glass or transparent, so that its empty status is part of the picture, as with the glass "body" in 射.

Corruption is a graduate-level topic in paleography. It is not necessary to know every detail before learning a character that contains a corrupted piece. Sometimes the whole story helps. Often it is enough to know that the piece is empty and to make a memory story for it.
