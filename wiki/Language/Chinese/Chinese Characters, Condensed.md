# Chinese Characters, Condensed

Chinese characters write the words of spoken Chinese, and each character carries a shape, a meaning and a sound. Most characters are built from a part that hints at the meaning and a part that hints at the sound, so a learner who reads each part for the job it does has far less to memorise.

## Takeaways

- Learn the spoken word first and the written shape on a later day.
- Divide a character only down to the parts that do a job.
- A part gives meaning, sound, an old picture, or nothing.
- A part's job is decided in each character separately.
- The radical is only the heading a dictionary files a character under.
- The first meaning explains the shape, and later senses grew from it.
- Guess the meaning and sound before looking a character up.

## How it works

The parts of a character point back to its sound and to the sense it was first made for. A sound part gives a range of likely readings, because pronunciation has changed since ancient Chinese and tones came later. A later sense either grew out of the first meaning or was borrowed for its sound, and a borrowed sense has no link to the shape. A memory story built on a part has to work for every character that shares the part.

- 大 plays a different part in each character.
  - In 美, beautiful, it is a picture of a person.
  - In 尖, sharp, it gives the sense big.
  - In 达 it gives the sound.
- A sound part covers first consonants made at one place in the mouth.
  - t goes with d, zh with ch and sh, b with p and f.
- About 18% of characters carry a borrowed sense.
- The story fades, and the sound and meaning stay.
