# Form Components

A form component uses its pictorial quality to express meaning. "Depicts" does not mean "still resembles." 光 once depicted a person holding a torch, and the modern shape no longer looks like that scene. The old form should be looked at the first time the character is met, before a mistaken visual reading takes hold. Direct corrections overwrite folk readings. The top of 美 is a headdress, not 羊. 儿 is not "legs."

When a component is talked about as depicting something, the claim is about the original form, not about the modern printed shape still looking like that thing.

## One identity, several printed shapes

Some components keep one identity across several printed shapes. 犬 and 犭 are the same component. 心, 忄, and 㣺 are the same component as well. So are 手 and 扌, and 刀 and 刂. 肉 and the 月-shaped form of flesh are also one component. Each set is one memory, with a position rule for which shape appears where.

A bound component, one that does not appear as its own character, is shown inside a host character while the rest of that character is dimmed. Components added later in history are timeline beats, as when 糸 was added to 羅.

Same-looking families should not be treated as one origin. 𡗗 in 奏, 泰, 奉, 秦, and 舂 has a different origin in each character.

Components also have spoken names, such as 儿字底, or in Taiwan the phrase that points at the bottom of 儿 in 儿. A pronounceable name is a handle for remembering the piece and a way to talk about characters aloud.

A character and the same shape used as a component can keep two lists of meanings. That split is taken up with meaning trees.

## Open questions

- How does seeing the ancient form of 光 at the first meeting change the later picture a learner builds from the modern shape?
- How does storing 心 and 忄 as one identity change later recall of either shape?
