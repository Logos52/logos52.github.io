# Form Components

A form component is a part serving its host by depiction once the modern strokes have stopped resembling the object. A meaning component serves by what it means. Those two are the subtypes of a semantic component: any part that feeds the host's sense. "Depicts" is a claim about origin, not about whether the printed shape still resembles the object.

## Scenes, then the decay

Early characters drew the things they named: 犬 a dog, 山 peaks, 耳 an ear, 网 a mesh. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Three Attributes of a Character|Three Attributes of a Character]] is the form attribute those drawings join — the written half of a spoken word.

The usual link between a semantic part and the host's meaning is loose. It tightens when the character is built from more than one form component, because the scene itself is the meaning.

取 is 又 seizing 耳. Armies counted casualties in ears. The senses extend to take, receive, select. The ear-count is the scene; the later senses ride on it.

美 is 大 — an adult seen from the front — crowned with a feather headdress. The modern top has decayed into empty 𦍌; [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]] is where that leftover headdress lands. Parsing the top as 羊 misreads the scene. The sheep reading is the folk error this page exists to prevent. Live debate exists (headdress, goat-horns, "big sheep"). This page's analysis is the headdress.

羅 is net 罒 plus bird 隹, with 糸 added later to stress threading. Components accrete to sharpen a meaning. The character is not a single snapshot.

然 originally meant "to burn": 灬 depicts fire, 肰 gives the sound. 狗 is 犭 plus the sound 句. The form component points at the original meaning, not at the loan sense that later rode in.

光 depicts a person — originally kneeling 卩, now written 儿 — holding a torch. The corrupted top no longer resembles 火. The scene remains the correct parse after resemblance dies. That is the opening definition made checkable.

大 depicts a standing adult and means "big." As a component it almost always depicts a person. Only a few characters use its meaning; 尖 is the named exception. Three Attributes keeps the three-job tour of 大. This page keeps the person-reading and that one meaning exception.

木 depicts a tree, and "tree" survives among its senses, so depiction and meaning coincide. Classify it as semantic and move on. The murky-case rule stops over-classification.

The ancient form converts the stroke pattern back into a picturable scene. Visualization and Association can hold a scene; Understanding arrives with it. Consult that older drawing once, at first encounter. Without it, a folk parse of the modern shape fills the gap — 美 as a big sheep is the type case. [[wiki/Learning Craft/Rules of Effective Memorization|Rules of Effective Memorization]] owns those three named rules. This page adds one habit: look the ancient form up once, then put it away.

## Inventories and identities

A character and the same shape used as a component keep two inventories. 心 as a word runs to mind and center; as a component it runs to thought and feeling. 頁 inside other characters keeps only "head." 山 widens to rock; 貝 to money; 犭 from dog to animals generally. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]] owns the trees. The rule here is only that the two inventories are not the same list.

Extension chains start at the picture. 刀 runs through knife-shaped bronze currency to the modern PRC slang for "US dollar" — slang, not the character's core. 車 runs from war chariot through cart and train to vehicles.

Loan senses stand outside the scene. This cluster marks them 〇: 然 "like this," 足 "sufficient," 頁 "page." Deriving those from the picture fabricates etymology. Meaning Trees owns the 〇 system; the refusal lives here.

Unify positional variants into one mental entry plus a position rule: 犬→犭, 肉→月, 心→忄/⺗, 手→扌, 刀→刂. 月-as-肉 and 月-as-moon are the same printed shape in two hosts, not two words.

网 is the function-conditioned case: 罓 when it is a sound component, 罒 when it is semantic. Whether any other component does this is open.

儿 as a component — including in 兒 — is a variant of 人 and reads rén. The native name is 儿字底. Standalone simplified 儿 is 兒 with the top deleted. "Legs" misparses a person. Three Attributes points here for those two histories.

攵 simplified from 攴, "to strike" — a hand holding a whip — and is easily confused with 夂 and 夊, both variants of 止 "foot." 攴/攵 occur essentially only inside other characters.

广 keeps its own identity in traditional script. Only simplified uses it to write 廣. Traditional is the target here.

虫 originally wrote 虺, a venomous snake, and has also written "insect" (蟲) since Qin. Simplification kept the first of 蟲's three 虫.

The ancient form is consulted once so a folk parse of the modern shape never becomes the memory. 美 is a person in a headdress. After that one look, the modern strokes are derivable and the sheep reading has nothing to attach to.

## Open Questions

- Which characters besides 尖 use 大 for its meaning rather than its picture.
- Whether any component besides 网 changes allograph by function.
- A lookup workflow for ancient forms that survives real reading volume. Without one, the once-at-first-encounter habit dies at scale. None is invented here.
