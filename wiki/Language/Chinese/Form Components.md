# Form Components

A form component is a part of a Chinese character that adds meaning through the picture it once was. Early Chinese writing drew things, so the character for a dog looked like a dog and the character for a mountain looked like a mountain, and many of those drawings live on as parts of other characters. A learner who knows what a part first showed can picture a scene where the modern shape looks like random strokes.

- A form component works through its original picture.
- The picture may no longer show in the modern shape.
- Look at the ancient form the first time you meet the character.
- Form and meaning can differ: 大 draws a person and means big.
- The same shape can be form in one character, meaning in another.
- A story from the pictured scene holds better than one from definitions.

## How it works

A part is a form component when it helps a character through what it was drawn as. 取, to take, shows a hand holding an ear, because soldiers once counted enemies by the ears they cut off. 美, beautiful, shows a person, 大, wearing a headdress. 光, bright, shows a person with fire held above the head, and none of the three still looks like its scene.

```
  ancient drawing          modern shape
  hand + ear          ->   取  take
  person + headdress  ->   美  beautiful
  person + fire       ->   光  bright
```

- Centuries of stylised writing wore the pictures down.
- The top of 美 looks like 羊, sheep, and was never a sheep.
- The 儿 under 光 is a person, though it looks like legs.
- A part added later sits on top of the old picture, as 糸 does in 羅.

## Variant shapes

Many form components change shape depending on where they sit in a character. A squeezed version and a full version are the same part with the same meaning, and learning them together saves memory. Shapes that look the same in modern writing can also come from different drawings in different characters. The ancient form is the way to tell them apart.

- 犬 and 犭 are both the dog.
- 心 and 忄 are both the heart.
- 手 and 扌 are both the hand.
- 刀 and 刂 are both the knife.
- The tops of 奏, 泰, 奉, 秦 and 舂 look alike and come from different drawings.

## How to use it

Look up the ancient form once, the first time you meet the character, before a wrong reading of the shape sets in. Picture the thing it drew, then build any memory story on that scene. Many popular explanations treat form components as meaning components and join dictionary definitions into abstract stories, and those stories hold less well than the actual scene. Parts also have spoken names in Chinese, such as 儿字底 for the 儿 at the bottom of a character, so a part can be talked about aloud.

- Check the ancient form once, then work from the modern shape.
- Say out loud what a part depicts in this character.
- Learn the spoken names of common parts.
