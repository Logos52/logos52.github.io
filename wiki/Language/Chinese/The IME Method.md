# The IME Method

On a computer, an input method lets a person type the sound of a word and then pick the right character from a list. The IME method is that same move, done from memory, when no computer is offering the list.

To recall a forgotten written form, list the sound components that can carry the syllable, list the semantic components related to the meaning, and cross-match those two lists until a pairing feels familiar. The method can work with as few as about 50 known characters. If the arrangement of the pieces will not come, write both candidate layouts and let recognition choose. Recognition outlasts recall. That choice is the mental version of picking from an input-method window.

Keep a reverse index from syllables to sound components, loosened by the related-initial groups such as b, p, and f. If that index does not help, recall other characters with the same sound and mine their components.

The method also runs backwards for reading. Classify which piece is likely giving the sound and which is likely giving the meaning. Then let the combination narrow the answer. Some pieces have stable habits. 各 is almost always sound. 穴 is almost always meaning. 立 swings. It gives meaning in 站, 端, and 豎, and sound in 拉, 粒, and 垃. In 位 it does both.

## Guessing in context, then looking up

In running text, a semantic component plus the context can give the category of meaning, and a sound component plus group knowledge can give a pronunciation range. Commit to a guess, then check it. The moment of committing and then checking is the encoding. 臉盆 is the worked example of that guided guess.

Lookups have a threshold. Circle an unknown character at most once per page, and look it up only after about three circles. Write the definition in the margin, and keep an index at the back of the book. Read slightly above the present level.

Memory stories are scaffolding. In the end they fall away. What remains is the sound and the meaning of the words the learner wants to read and write.

Heard tones sometimes differ from dictionary tones. Map the surface tone that was heard back to the citation tone in the dictionary, so the audio cue and the dictionary cue stay connected.
