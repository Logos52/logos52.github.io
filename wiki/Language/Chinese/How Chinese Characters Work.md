# How Chinese Characters Work

A Chinese character writes a spoken word or a part of a word, and it has three things attached: a shape, a meaning and a sound. Most characters are built from smaller parts, and each part does a job, such as hinting at the meaning or at the sound. A learner who reads each part for its job can remember a character from how it works, where copying strokes only stores a shape.

## Takeaways

- A character writes a spoken word or a word part.
- Every character has a shape, a meaning and a sound.
- Shape to sound and shape to meaning are tested separately.
- A part's job is decided in each character separately.
- Divide a character only down to the parts that do a job.
- Most characters pair a meaning part with a sound part.
- Learn the spoken word first and the written shape later.

## How it works

Speech came before writing, and writing was made to record it, so a character stands for a word of speech and does not stand for an idea apart from speech. Early Chinese writing drew pictures of things, and pictures run out for words like "north" or "I", so people borrowed existing characters for their sound and later added parts to tell the borrowed uses apart. The result is a system where the parts of a character point at its sound, its meaning or its original picture. About 85% of the 9,641 characters in one standard dictionary are built from a meaning part plus a sound part.

- A part's role is form, meaning, sound or empty.
  - A form part helps through the picture it once was.
  - A meaning part adds a sense it gained over time.
  - A sound part hints at the pronunciation.
  - An empty part gives no sound or meaning, for a known reason.
- The same part changes role between characters.
  - 大 is a picture of a person in 美, beautiful.
  - 大 adds the sense big in 尖, sharp.
  - 大 gives the sound in 达.
- The radical, a dictionary filing heading, says little about the parts.
- Readers take in parts as well as whole characters.

## How to use it

When a new character appears, name the role of each part in that character before reading anyone's explanation. Guess the meaning and the sound from the parts, then check. A memory story helps early on, and it should be built on the parts that do a job, because the knowledge of sound and meaning is what stays once the story fades.

- Guess meaning and sound first, then look the character up.
- Build any story on parts that do a job.
- Test shape to sound and shape to meaning separately.
