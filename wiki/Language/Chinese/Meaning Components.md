# Meaning Components

A meaning component carries an abstract extension of a concrete original sense. 力 began as a plow and extends to effort and strength. Form components, by contrast, carry concrete pictures. Very few characters are built only from meaning components.

Components are learned through tables of siblings that share the same piece. Each table lists the member characters and the role the component plays in each one. 木 can mean "part of a tree" in 根 and 標, "a type of tree" in 机, and "made of wood" in 架 and 機.

## The consistency test

A memory story holds up only if it still works for every sibling that shares the component. Reading 偷 as "roof, night, and knife" fails against 輸, 愈, 愉, and 喻. Reading 亼 as a roof breaks 合, 命, and 令. Stories anchored in the component's real function reinforce the whole family. Folk decompositions damage it.

One true component meaning can open a whole cluster. 亼 as "a mouth speaking downward from above" holds 合, 命, and 令 together. The component should be taught just before its members.

Transparent characters such as 想 encode almost for free. Explanation time should go to opaque ones such as 藍, where stylization, corruption, and sound drift hide the logic. The older language is a tool for explaining those links, not material to memorize. 監, 籃, and 藍 belonged to one sound family.

Empty pieces inside a meaning analysis still get a one-line reason. The 冖 in 勞 was once 衣, a corruption. The extra 口 in 命 was added to keep 命 apart from 令, and that reason also records the pair that learners mix up.

An English phrase such as "plowed through his work" sits close to the way 力 extends from a plow to effort. 合 originally meant "to answer" and is now written 答. That link turns an old sense into a live connection between two characters.

## Open questions

- How does checking a story against every sibling that shares the component change which stories a learner keeps?
- How does spending explanation time on opaque characters, and leaving transparent ones almost unexplained, change what gets learned?
