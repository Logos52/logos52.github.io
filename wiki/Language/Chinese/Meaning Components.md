# Meaning Components

A meaning component is a part of a Chinese character that adds a sense it has picked up over time, usually a wider and more abstract sense than the picture it started as. 力 began as a drawing of a plow and, as a part, now stands for strength and effort. A learner who knows the sense a part has gained can see why a group of characters that share it mean related things, and can spot a memory story that will break.

## Takeaways

- A meaning component adds an acquired, abstract sense.
- A form component does a different job and adds a concrete picture.
- Very few characters are made only of meaning parts.
- Most characters pair a meaning part with a sound part.
- A memory story must work for every character sharing the part.
- Spend effort on characters whose logic is hidden.

## How it works

Over centuries, a part first used for its picture gathers senses. 力, the plow, came to mean the effort of plowing and then effort and strength in general, much as English says someone plowed through their work. The sense a part adds can also vary a little from character to character. The useful unit of study is a table of the characters that share a part, with what the part contributes in each one.

- 力 as effort or strength sits in 功, 助 and 努.
  - 功 is achievement.
  - 助 is to help.
  - 努 is to exert oneself.
- 木, the tree, carries a different sense in different characters.
  - In 根, root, and 標, treetop, it is part of a tree.
  - In 架, a frame, it means made of wood.
  - In 松, pine, it means a kind of tree.
- A part's sense can differ from the same shape used as a word.
  - 又 as a word means again.
  - 又 as a part means a hand.
  - 氵 as a part means an action involving liquid.

## The sibling test

A memory story built on the wrong parts can help with one character and damage several others. A common story reads 偷, to steal, as a person climbing onto a roof at night with a knife. The same right-hand part appears in 輸, 愈, 愉 and 喻, where there is no roof, night or knife, so the story fails there. A story is safe only if the part means the same thing in every character that shares it.

- 亼, the top of 合, 命 and 令, is often read as a roof.
- 亼 is a mouth speaking downward from above.
- That one sense explains 合, 命 and 令.
- 口 was added to 命 only to tell it apart from 令.
- Learn a part just before the characters that use it.

## Transparent and opaque characters

Some characters show their logic in their parts. 想, to think, has 心, the heart, and a sound part close to its modern sound, so it takes little effort to learn. Other characters hide their logic, and 藍, blue, is the usual example, because its sound part 監, jiān, sounds nothing like lán. The logic hides when shapes were stylised, when sounds changed, when meanings changed, or when shapes wore down over time.

- 監, 籃 and 藍 sounded alike in Old Chinese.
- Old Chinese explains a link and is never memorised.
- 合 first meant to answer, and that sense is now written 答.
