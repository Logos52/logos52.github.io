# Meaning Components

A meaning component is a character part that carries an abstract extension of its own original, concrete sense. It does not draw the modern meaning of the character that contains it. 力 first named a concrete object. Which object does not matter for this use. Inside 勢 it contributes the sense of power, inside 功 the sense of effort, inside 勞 the sense of toil. None of those three characters has anything to do with farming. Next to 田, the same strokes draw the people who work the field. The role of a part is decided one character at a time. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Form Components|Form Components]] describes the same split from the form side.

## Core takeaways

- Most characters are sound-meaning compounds, 形聲字: one meaning part paired with one sound part. Characters built only from meanings are rare and late.
- A meaning component carries an abstract extension of its original sense. It does not draw the modern meaning of the character it sits in.
- The unit of study is the family of characters that share one component.
- Test any story about a component against every character that contains it. One character the story does not fit is enough to reject the story.
- Spend research time on characters whose parts no longer show their sound or meaning. Skip it for characters whose parts still do.
- Build the mnemonic on the real analysis first. Add decoration after that.

## Where meaning components sit

Characters were rewritten many times over many generations. The rewriting removed most of their picture quality. Modern native readers now read nearly every character as if it were assembled from meanings. Very few characters are. Meaning components live mostly inside sound-meaning characters, 形聲字, where a semantic part is paired with a sound part. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]] covers the sound part of that pair, including the syllable-range cycle. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/How Chinese Characters Work|How Chinese Characters Work]] is the hub page for the whole set.

Because readers see meanings where there are none, they invent breakdowns that assign a job to parts that contribute nothing.

## The family is the unit

A semantic series is the family of characters that share one meaning component, studied together. Characters in the same series are siblings. Three habits follow from this. Learn each component through its series. Test every reading across the sibling characters. Let the degree of hidden structure decide where research effort goes.

The learning unit is the family table. Each row is one sibling with its pronunciation, its original meaning, and the role the component plays in it. The 力 series runs 勢 功 勞 辦 助 努. The 土 series runs 地 社 場 基. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]] holds the original senses those tables start from.

## How far a component's sense reaches

木 is a picture in 本: a tree plus a stroke at the root. As a meaning component it covers a range. In 根, 標, and 樣 it means a part of a tree. In 機 and 架 it means a thing made of wood. The same spread appears again and again as three relations: part of, type of, made of. That three-way cut narrows the plausible roles of a component in an unfamiliar character.

One part can hold more than one job at once. 工 in 功 gives the sound and part of the meaning. 土 in 社 and 基 is partly meaning and partly form, and the two cannot be cleanly separated. The role is settled one character at a time. 土 in 基 stays a mixed case. No test settles it. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Surface vs Deep Structure|Surface vs Deep Structure]] covers the per-character role and the point where analysis stops.

## Characters built only from meanings

Characters made only of meaning parts are rare, late, and never the default reading. 歪 (不 over 正) and 尖 (小 over 大) show the reason. The pictures of the parts do not draw "crooked" or "pointed." Only the senses of the parts produce those meanings. 林, 森, and 炎 exist. They do not make the pattern the majority type.

## Test each story against the siblings

The test is an action. Take the mnemonic and try it against every known character that contains the same part. If one sibling cannot take the story, the story is wrong. This is the sibling-falsifies test. The unit of study is the whole family. The extension of a concrete sense into other characters holds only for the characters where the extended sense fits.

偷 has a common story: person plus rooftop plus night plus knife. That story fails against 輸, 愈, 愉, and 喻. All four contain 俞. None of them has a roof, a night, or a knife in its meaning. 俞 gives the sound. 亻 gives the meaning. The original sense of 偷 was "degenerate, insincere." It extended through "secretly" into "steal." The rooftop story was a folk assembly of leftover strokes. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]] describes the residual role those invented pieces were made to fill.

A false reading damages other characters too. 亼 is a mouth speaking downward. That reading explains 合, 命, and 令. Read 亼 as a roof and all three get harder to learn.

Build the mnemonic on the real analysis first. Add decoration after that. Treat character breakdowns found on the internet as suspect until checked. [[wiki/Learning Craft/Rules of Effective Memorization|Rules of Effective Memorization]] explains why a grounded mnemonic beats an invented story: a story that fails against the siblings adds nothing useful to memory.

## Deciding where research goes

Four processes hide a character's structure: stylization, senses that components picked up over time, pronunciation change, and shape corruption. A character is transparent when its parts still show its sound and meaning. It is opaque when they do not. The more of the four processes have acted on a character, the more research it needs.

想 is transparent. 相 sounds nearly the same as 想. 心 marks thoughts and feelings. No research is needed.

藍 is opaque. ⺾ is the meaning part. 籃 has the same structure with ⺮, so 監 is the sound part. In modern Mandarin that pairing is hard to believe. [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]] may treat the same pair as a reconstructed chain.

Old Chinese reconstruction restores the link: 監 *kram, 籃 *k.ram, 藍 *g.ram. The asterisk marks a reconstructed form. Reconstructions explain a link. They never go on review cards.

Mark drift so that a sound part which no longer sounds right is still read as sound: 且 in 助, 也 in 地, 土 in 社. The mark keeps the role from turning into a folk meaning.

[[wiki/Language/Chinese/The IME Method|The IME Method]] gives the meaning-side list of candidates when a character's form has to be rebuilt.

## How to practice this

1. Pick one meaning component. List every character you know that contains it. Notice that the list, not one character, is now what you study.
2. Make a family table from the list. Give each character its pronunciation, its original meaning, and the component's role. Notice which rows share one extended sense: part of, type of, or made of.
3. Take any mnemonic you use for one character. Try it on every sibling in the table. If one sibling cannot take the story, drop the story.
4. For each character, count how many of the four processes have acted on it. Spend research time on the ones with many. Notice that a character like 想 needs none.
5. When a sound part no longer sounds right, mark it as sound in your notes. Notice that the mark stops you reading it as a meaning.
6. When you find a character breakdown on the internet, test it against the siblings before you use it. Notice whether it survives.
