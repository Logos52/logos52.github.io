# Three Attributes of a Character

Every Chinese character has three attributes: its form, which is how it looks, its sound, which is how it is said, and its meaning. A spoken word already carries the sound and the meaning, and writing adds the form to record that word. A learner can therefore learn the word by ear first and then test each link to the written form on its own.

## Core takeaways

- A character writes down a spoken word.
- Speech carries sound and meaning, and writing adds form.
- Learn the word by ear before its written form.
- Test form to sound and form to meaning as separate links.
- A part of a character can give form, meaning or sound.
- A part's job can change from one character to the next.

## Why speech comes first

Writing was invented to record speech, so a character points to a word, and the word carries the sound and the meaning. English shows the same order: a reader who has only seen a word in print often says it wrong the first time. A learner who already knows a word by ear only has to attach a shape to it, which costs less than learning a shape, a sound and a meaning together. A word heard first also makes its character easier to remember.

- Anchor each new character to a word you already know by ear.
- The three attributes are one thing to remember.
- The links between them fail one at a time, so test each.
- Sound is a memory channel of its own, so rhyme helps.

```
   spoken word
  sound + meaning
        |
        v
  + form (writing)
        |
  test form -> sound
  test form -> meaning
```

## What each part contributes

Most characters are built from smaller parts, and in a given character each part does a job. A form part contributes a picture, a meaning part contributes a sense, and a sound part contributes a hint at how the character is read. The same part can do different jobs in different characters, so the question to ask is what the part does in this character. Many popular breakdowns treat picture parts as meaning parts and build memory stories on the wrong job.

- 大 pictures a person, and the word means big.
- 大 is form in 美, meaning in 尖, sound in 达.
- A part can do two jobs at once, as 大 does in 达.
- The top of 美 pictures a headdress, though it looks like 羊, sheep.
- As a part, 氵 means actions with liquids, which explains 游.
- 阝 on the left means hills, on the right a city.

Where the picture and the meaning differ, as with 大, work out the link yourself before reading someone else's explanation.
