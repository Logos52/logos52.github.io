# The Pipelining Strategy

The pipelining strategy is a schedule for learning new Chinese characters that spreads a character's sound, its meaning and its written form over three days. Learning all three on one day means holding three unfamiliar things at once. Spread out, each day holds one new task and two that are already half known, so each character costs less effort.

- Never learn a character's sound, meaning and form on the same day.
- Learn the spoken word first and the written form last.
- Work in small groups of three to five characters.
- Start a new group each day so the groups overlap.
- Keep the daily flashcard reviews going alongside it.

## How it works

A native speaker who learns to write a character already knows the word by ear, so the writing lesson only adds a shape to a word they already know. The schedule gives a learner the same start by making each word known by ear before its shape appears. On day one the learner hears the Chinese word and recalls its meaning. On day two the learner goes the other way, from the meaning to the spoken word, which is much easier after day one, and on day three the written form is attached to a word that is by then well known.

- Day 1: hear the word, recall the meaning.
- Day 2: see the meaning, recall the spoken word.
- Day 3: learn the written form of a word you now know.
- Adding a shape to a known word beats learning all three together.

## The overlap

A new group of characters starts every day, so the groups overlap the way laundry does with a washer and a dryer, where the second load goes into the washer while the first is in the dryer. On any given day the learner does day one for the newest group, day two for yesterday's group and day three for the group from two days ago. Only the newest group is unfamiliar.

```
         day 1    day 2    day 3    day 4
group A  hear     say      write
group B           hear     say      write
group C                    hear     say
group D                             hear
```

## Setting it up

The schedule only decides when new items come in. It sits on top of an ordinary spaced repetition deck, the flashcard system that brings old cards back at growing intervals, and the reviews in that deck still happen every day. Most of the day's items are common words built from the new characters and characters already known.

- Three to five new characters a day, about twenty items in all.
- Fill the rest with common words pairing new and known characters.
- One card per sound and meaning pair: 道 dào and dǎo get two.
- Merging those cards is allowed if it works better for you.
- A slower variant waits a month or two before the written form.

## Sources

Share, David L. "Phonological recoding and self-teaching: sine qua non of reading acquisition." *Cognition* 55 (1995): 151–218. Phonological-primary, orthographic-secondary: a new written form attaches more cheaply to a word already held by sound.

L2 character work showing oral associations developing before form associations, for example the open-access study at PMC10313221. That study supports spoken-first as the cheaper order rather than a house preference.

Paired-associate and incremental vocabulary research (ERIC ED550749 and similar studies): a new link onto an existing pair costs less than three new nodes at once. Backward recall after a forward link is the step this schedule uses in session 2.
