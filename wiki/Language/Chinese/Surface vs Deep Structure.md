# Surface vs Deep Structure

A Chinese character can be looked at for how it is written or for why it is built the way it is. Deep structure means the parts that give the character its sound or its meaning, and surface structure means the strokes, the stroke order, the dictionary heading called the radical, and the pieces that do no job. Knowing which is which tells a learner where to stop dividing a character, so memory work goes into the parts that explain it.

- Deep structure explains why a character looks the way it does.
- Surface structure tells you how to write it.
- Both matter, at different moments.
- Divide a character only down to the parts that do a job.
- A part's job is decided in each character separately.
- The radical only tells you where a dictionary files a character.

## How it works

A working part, often called a functional component, is a part that does a direct job in that character, usually giving a sound or a meaning. Its own smaller pieces are only there to form its shape. 部 originally meant area: 咅 gives the sound and 阝, a city, gives the meaning. The 立 and 口 inside 咅 do nothing in 部, so dividing 部 down to 立 and 口 produces stories about pieces that explain nothing.

```
          部
        /    \
      咅      阝      <- stop here: sound + meaning
     /  \
    立   口           <- surface only: writing pieces
```

- 立 plays a different role in each character.
  - In 端, stand up straight, it gives meaning.
  - In 拉 and 粒 it gives sound.
  - In 位 it gives sound and meaning at once.
  - In 部 and 章 it does no job.
- A shape can change identity with its position.
  - 阝 on the left means hills.
  - 阝 on the right means city.
- A part can mean one thing inside a character and another alone.
  - 口 as a part often means sound or speech.
  - 耳 as a part often means hearing or perceiving.

## The radical

The radical is the part a dictionary files a character under. The usual list has 214 radicals, set by a dictionary of 1716 that took them from one of 1615, and the Chinese name 部首 means section head. A part on that list is the radical in some characters and a plain part in others, and even where it is the radical it may give meaning, sound or nothing. Use the radical to find a character in a paper dictionary, and look at the working parts for how the character works.

- 立 is the radical of 端, where it gives meaning.
- 立 is the radical of 章, where it does no job.
- 立 gives sound in 拉, where it is not the radical.

## Writing standards

Surface structure is also where regional writing standards differ. Taiwan, Hong Kong and mainland China each set their own stroke order and stroke shapes, and the standards overlap without being identical. When one book shows a stroke order and another book shows a different one, each usually follows a different standard, and neither is wrong. Printed fonts do not always follow their own government's standard.

- Learn one standard and write it consistently.
- Treat a different stroke order as a different standard.
