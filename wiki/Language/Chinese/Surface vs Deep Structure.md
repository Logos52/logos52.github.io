# Surface vs Deep Structure

Deep structure is the hidden logic of the writing system. It includes functional components, internal logic, and the connections of sound and meaning. It shows how the characters actually work. Surface structure is how a character is supposed to look at a given moment in history, or how it looks today. It includes radicals, strokes, stroke order, and non-functional components. It shows how to write a character, and what the character is supposed to look like in a given setting. The People's Republic of China, Taiwan, and Hong Kong each have their own standards. Those standards overlap a great deal and are not exactly the same.

Both layers matter, and they apply at different times. Deep structure explains why a character looks the way it does. Surface structure explains how to write it.

## Functional components

To understand a given character, see how it divides into functional components, what job each one is doing, and how those jobs connect to the sound and the original meaning. A functional component is a part that is serving a direct function in that character, usually by giving a pronunciation or a meaning. Functional components belong to deep structure.

Decompose only to the functional level. 部 breaks into 咅 plus 阝. Cutting deeper produces noise. Over-splitting, and parsing by radical, build memory stories on parts that explain nothing. The radical of 錦 tells the reader where to find the character in a dictionary. It does not say what the character is.

The same component can take on different jobs in different characters. 立 is a meaning component in 端, a sound component in 拉, and an empty component in 音. In 部 it is a non-functional fragment. 口 as a component means sound or speech. 耳 as a component means perception. 而 as a component means beard.

A radical is a role a component can play, not part of the component's nature. Appearing on the list of radicals does not make a shape a radical in every character that contains it, and knowing which part is the radical does not reliably say what that part is doing. Radicals belong to surface structure.

There are four types of functional component. Form components use their pictorial quality to express meaning. Meaning components use an acquired meaning to express the character's meaning. Those two types together are called semantic components. Sound components give a sound, and empty components give neither a sound nor a meaning.

Some sound loans need to be marked honestly, including 而, 需, 成, and 要. Some sound links are not obvious in modern Mandarin, including 位, 成, and 定. The semantic part alone can separate a pair such as 問 and 聞, both built on 門.
