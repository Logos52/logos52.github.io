# Immersion Metalayers

A metalayer is a tool that sits on top of immersion and makes the session easier to work with. It can let the learner look up words in a show, pause automatically after each line of dialogue, and jump between subtitle lines.

Those functions show up together in one working setup that uses a browser tool, and that tool behaves the same way on YouTube and on Netflix. After it is added and the page is reloaded, the learner sets a translation language, sets the subtitle language to the language being learned, and hides the translations so they are not visible yet. Autopause is turned on. Playback then stops after each line on its own. The learner can hover a word to look it up, click a line in the side list to jump back to it, and hover the hidden translation when a check of the meaning is needed.

The same tool also has catalogs of YouTube and Netflix titles by language, which can be used to see what exists. Once the layer is in place, the content to use is dual-channel input with matching subtitles, meaning the learner reads and hears the language at the same time. One search mentioned for finding that kind of content on Netflix is a global title search. After setup, the noticing game is played on that read-and-listen content.

Learners of Chinese or Japanese are pointed at ready-made sources for that content on the tenth-day page of the resource sheet. Everyone else is pointed at the ninth-day page of the sheet. A session around this work is 35 minutes in total, including ten minutes of time in the language on that read-and-listen content.

## Open questions

- How does leaving the translation hidden change what the learner tries to understand before checking?
