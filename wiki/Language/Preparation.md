# Preparation

Preparation is a short orientation to sounds, words, characters, grammar, and tools that makes the next stretch of immersion noticeable without replacing it. Immersion becomes usable once those items can be registered in running speech or text. That refusal to substitute names the thing: time in the language stays the main event.

The forms are a list, not a syllabus. Sound and writing-system work so a phonetic spelling can be heard and recognized — enough of a system like pinyin that Mandarin sounds and spellings stop being decoration. A few high-frequency Vietnamese words, reviewed before beginner video, so the session is not a wall of unknowns; "a few" is the size of the job, not a deck-building afternoon. An SRS is the class for that review; Anki is one free instance, never a requirement. Character orientation for Mandarin or Japanese lives at [[wiki/Language/Character Primer|Character Primer]], the character-orientation form. A [[wiki/Language/Refold Grammar Primers|grammar primer]] is a short, deliberately incomplete tour of common patterns, used so those patterns can be noticed in input — a short grammar point, then a hunt for that pattern in real speech or text. The [[wiki/Language/Vietnamese Grammar Primer|Vietnamese Grammar Primer]] is the worked primer, rebuilt around Vietnamese. Lookup and subtitle setup sits with [[wiki/Language/Immersion Metalayers|Immersion Metalayers]], the class of popup and subtitle tools, and the sibling of the tool-fiddling row: a metalayer in place before a session so lookups stop fighting the video. Choosing catalogs and content is the last form — knowing where the next session's language will come from. All of this is one mode of [[wiki/Language/Three Pillars of Language Learning|Three Pillars of Language Learning]], the three-mode system this orientation belongs to.

## The test, and the three failures

The success test is whether the following session of real language is easier to walk into. That is how the week is read.

| Failure | Signal | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation replaces immersion | Most of the week is tools, decks, or grammar about the language | Cap prep and force input time |
| Too little preparation | Every session feels opaque and demoralizing | Add targeted sound, vocab, or grammar priming |
| Tool fiddling | Setup expands without more language exposure | Define the exact friction the tool must remove |

The house cap: preparation ends when the next session is enterable, or after a short fixed slice, whichever comes first. There is no verified optimal ratio. The price is that slice, not a second hobby. Two weeks where most time went to decks, tools, or grammar is the quit signal: the next session was never the point. The named pillar is house taxonomy, not a finding.

The same short orientation, judged after the week: the next stretch was noticeable, or the week was preparation.
