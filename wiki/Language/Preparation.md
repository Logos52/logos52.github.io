# Preparation

Preparation is the part of language study spent on the language itself, through flashcards, grammar notes and the writing system, so that you recognise more of what you meet in real content. It is one of three study modes, next to interactive immersion, which is working through content with tools, and freeflow, which is watching content without them. Preparation is short and daily, and its job is to make the hours spent with real content more useful.

## Takeaways

- Preparation makes words and patterns familiar before you meet them.
- Keep it short: about 15 minutes of words, 5 to 10 of grammar.
- Ten new words a day at most.
- Any extra time goes to real content.
- Finish your flashcard reviews every day.
- Read grammar notes to understand patterns, without drills.
- Spot what you prepared in real content the same day.

## How it works

A word studied on a flashcard is weakly known. Meeting it again in a show or a book, in a real sentence, is what makes it familiar. Preparation gives you a supply of half-known words and patterns to spot, and the most common words come first because a small set of common words covers a large share of any text. The flashcard system, whether an app or a box of paper cards, brings each card back at growing intervals, just before you would forget it, which is called spaced repetition.

- Vocabulary: 10 new words a day, 10 to 20 minutes.
- Over 20 minutes most days: add fewer new words.
- Never raise new words above 10.
- Spend spare time on content.
- Any spaced repetition app or paper method works.

## Grammar and characters

Grammar notes are a short tour of the common patterns, incomplete on purpose. Start them after 10 to 15 hours of time with real content, so the patterns have something to attach to. Read one point a day, then look for it in what you watch, and come back to the notes when a sentence confuses you. For Chinese or Japanese, a short introduction to how characters are built helps you see a character as familiar parts instead of random strokes.

- One grammar point a day, 5 to 10 minutes.
- No memorising and no drills.
- You do not need to produce the pattern yet.
- Characters are learned through vocabulary cards plus lots of reading.

## Where it fails

Preparation is easy to overdo. Too many new words make the reviews long, and long reviews take time away from content. Tools and card setups can also turn into the activity itself, so give the setup a fixed number of minutes and then start.
