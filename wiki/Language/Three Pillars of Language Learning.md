# Three Pillars of Language Learning

Language learning in this model rests on three pillars. They are preparation, interactive immersion, and freeflow. Preparation is learning about the language and priming for acquisition, meaning studying ahead of time so later immersion is easier to understand. Interactive immersion expands understanding through hands-on experiences and tools. Freeflow hones instincts, turning conscious knowledge into subconscious abilities.

Maximum progress comes from balancing all three pillars, because each one does a different job. Preparation is not only one flashcard program. Any spaced-repetition software works, and some pen-and-paper methods work as well, including the Gold List method.

The session that introduces the three pillars keeps the other daily work running, so the balance is practiced rather than only named. That work includes tracking daily minimums, time on the writing system, and vocabulary priming, meaning study of words ahead of immersion to raise comprehension later. It also includes 20 minutes of dual-channel input, content the learner reads and hears at the same time, spent on intensive reading.

## Open questions

- How does a learner tell that time is collecting in one pillar and thinning out in the others?
- How does progress change when preparation and interactive immersion run for a stretch with no freeflow?
