# Three Pillars of Language Learning

Language learning in this model rests on three pillars: **preparation**, **interactive immersion**, and **freeflow**. Maximum progress comes from balancing all three, because each one does a different job. Preparation is learning about the language so later immersion is easier to understand. Interactive immersion expands understanding through hands-on experiences and tools. Freeflow turns conscious knowledge into subconscious ability by spending relaxed, attentive time with material you can mostly follow.

Preparation is not only one flashcard program. Any spaced-repetition software works, and some pen-and-paper methods work as well. None of the pillars replaces the others. Crowding out immersion with cards defeats preparation's purpose. Skipping preparation leaves immersion as noise. Doing only interactive work without freeflow keeps knowledge conscious and slow.

## What each pillar is for

**Preparation** means studying on purpose before or beside immersion: learning the writing system, priming frequent vocabulary, and reading grammar primers for comprehension, not for drilling rules into speech. The test is whether prepared words and patterns show up and make sense during real content. Short daily limits keep it in proportion.

**Interactive immersion** is a family of high-attention activities that use tools on input you read and hear at the same time. The core practice is the noticing game: look for cognates, words you already know, or words you primed today. Intensive reading adds a two-target threshold: look up only when two unknown pieces block a sentence, not every word.

**Freeflow immersion** reuses content you already worked through interactively. Watch or read without lookups or rewinding, stay attentive, and note how much you understood and whether focus held. The point is to rely on understanding that is already forming and to get more exposure per hour when the content matches both interest and level. Background listening does not count.

## How to tell the balance is off

Time collecting in one pillar usually shows up in the others thinning out. Too much preparation feels productive but starves acquisition, which happens in immersion. Too much interactive work without freeflow leaves you tired and tool-dependent. Too much freeflow on content that is still too hard becomes passive staring.

The session that introduces the three pillars in the course keeps the other daily work running: writing-system time, vocabulary priming, and about twenty minutes of dual-channel input spent on intensive reading or the noticing game. That balance is practiced, not only named.

Session lengths in the course are guidelines, not ceilings. Homework assignments are a baseline; extra time belongs in activities where you are actively understanding the language, even if only a little. Someone who is not a total beginner should move through the first week quickly, with a recap within a day or two rather than a full week of review on material already known elsewhere.
