## Sources

The claim, grounds, and warrant analysis is Stephen Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (1958). The warrant is the usually unstated assumption licensing the step from one to the other. The standard for stating an opposing position is Rapoport's rules as set out by Daniel Dennett in *Intuition Pumps and Other Tools for Thinking* (2013): re-express the target's position so clearly and fairly that they would thank you for the phrasing. The objections-first form is the medieval *articulus*, most fully worked in the *Summa Theologiae*, where producing the objections was the students' task and each had to be a valid argument resting on a recognized authority.
