# Financial Slavery

Financial slavery is lending set up so that the lender earns most when the borrower never clears the debt. The same design runs from a small buy now pay later purchase up to a government's budget, with the state holding it in place. A reader who knows the pattern can spot a loan whose terms reward the lender for the borrower's failure.

- An honest loan shares risk between lender and borrower.
- A predatory loan pays the lender more when the borrower falls behind.
- The state makes a predatory loan hold, by blocking bankruptcy.
- The pattern scales to a country that borrows every year.
- A central bank lets the borrowing continue without limit.
- The inflation that follows falls on everyone who holds the currency.

## Honest lending

An investor puts money into a company now for a chance at a larger return later, and if the company fails, the investor loses too. An honest loan works the same way. The lender wants the borrower to succeed, because a failed borrower cannot repay. Predatory lending removes that shared risk, first for one borrower and then for a whole country.

## One borrower

The lender starts by lending to someone likely to make bad decisions, such as a $100,000 loan for a degree with poor earning prospects. The ideal borrower keeps paying and never clears the balance, because interest charged on unpaid interest grows the debt faster than the payments shrink it. Then the lender gets the state on its side. In the United States, student loans cannot be discharged in bankruptcy, on the reasoning that an education cannot be handed back, and while a contract alone cannot force payment, a law that blocks bankruptcy can.

- Buy now pay later firms use the same design at small scale.
- A purchase carries no interest until a payment is missed.
- Then late fees start.
- The firm also charges fees to the shop.
- Its income depends on people spending more than they have.

## A country

Lenders lobby politicians to spend more than tax brings in, so the government borrows every year to cover the gap and the interest on earlier borrowing. Part of the profit goes back to the politicians, and the lending runs through enough middlemen that voters cannot see who holds the debt. A population that does not know its creditor, and whose politicians blame the wrong people, has no one to push against. A central bank that can expand the money supply lets the borrowing go on without limit, and the inflation it causes works as a tax on everyone holding the currency.

- Loans must be repaid in a currency the lender creates.
- Paying off one loan then requires taking out another.
- Breaking free would mean breaking the whole monetary system.
- A slave in ancient Egypt owed a fifth of the harvest.
- Most modern taxpayers hand over a larger share.

```
one borrower               a country
------------               ---------
lend to a weak borrower    lobby for deficit spending
         |                          |
state blocks bankruptcy    middlemen hide the creditor
         |                          |
interest on interest       central bank expands money
         |                          |
never clears the debt      repays in lender's currency
```

## Where it fails

The country-scale version needs lobbying to cause the deficits, and a second cause is enough without it, since a politician facing an election within a few years has a reason to spend now and borrow. It also needs a small group of lenders to beat the whole voting public, which the pattern takes as given. These gaps leave the country-scale version unproven, and it can still be true.

- No creditor is named.
- Holders of most countries' government debt are published.
- Large shares are usually held by the country's own citizens and institutions.

## How to apply it

Before signing a loan, check what the lender earns if the borrower falls behind. If that is more than the lender earns from repayment on time, the loan is set up against the borrower. A debt that cannot be discharged in bankruptcy has no exit.

- Avoid debt with no exit through bankruptcy.
- Treat a zero-interest offer as a fee product.
- Its price appears when a payment is missed.
