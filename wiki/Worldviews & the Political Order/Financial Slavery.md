# Financial Slavery

Financial slavery is a term for lending set up so that the lender earns most when the borrower never clears the debt. Knowing the pattern lets a reader recognise a loan whose terms reward the lender for the borrower's failure, from a small buy now pay later purchase up to a government budget. The argument behind the term says one lending design runs from consumer credit up to national debt, with the state holding it in place.

## Core takeaways

- An honest loan shares risk: the lender gets paid only if the borrower does well.
- A predatory loan removes the lender's risk and adds income when the borrower falls behind: late fees, interest on unpaid interest, and no exit through bankruptcy.
- The state is what makes a predatory loan hold. A contract alone cannot force payment; a law that blocks bankruptcy on student loans can.
- The same pattern scales to a country. A government that spends more than it taxes must borrow every year, and its interest bill grows.
- A central bank that expands the money supply lets the borrowing continue without limit, and the resulting inflation falls on everyone who holds the currency.
- The argument describes a mechanism and gives no evidence for it. It names no creditor, and deficits have other causes that are enough on their own.

## The argument

- Honest finance
  - An investor puts money into a company now for a chance at a larger return later. If the company fails, the investor loses too.
  - An honest loan works the same way. The lender wants the borrower to succeed, because a failed borrower cannot repay.
- Removing the risk, one borrower at a time
  - Lend to someone likely to make bad decisions. An example is a $100,000 loan for a degree with poor earning prospects.
  - Aim for a borrower who keeps paying and never clears the balance. Interest charged on unpaid interest grows the debt faster than the payments shrink it.
  - Get the state on the lender's side. In the United States, student loans cannot be discharged in bankruptcy, on the reasoning that an education cannot be handed back.
  - Buy now pay later firms use the same design at small scale. A purchase carries no interest until a payment is missed, then late fees start. The firm earns from those fees and from fees charged to the shop, so its income depends on people spending more than they have.
- Removing the risk, a country at a time
  - Lobby politicians to spend more than tax revenue. The government then borrows every year to cover the gap and the interest on earlier borrowing.
  - Hand part of the profit back to the politicians, and route the lending through enough middlemen that voters cannot see who holds the debt.
  - A population that does not know who its creditor is, and whose politicians blame the wrong people, has no target to push against.
  - Back the creation of a central bank that can expand the money supply. Borrowing can then continue without limit, and the inflation it causes works as a tax on everyone holding the currency.
  - Once loans must be repaid in a currency the lender creates, paying off one loan requires taking out another. Breaking free would mean breaking the whole monetary system.
- The comparison the argument closes on: a slave in ancient Egypt owed a fifth of the harvest. The point is that most modern taxpayers hand over a larger share than that.

```
one borrower               a country
------------               ---------
lend to a weak borrower    lobby for deficit spending
         |                          |
state blocks bankruptcy    middlemen hide the creditor
         |                          |
interest on interest       central bank expands money
         |                          |
never clears the debt      repays in lender's currency
```

## Where it fails

- It does not show that lobbying causes deficits. A politician facing an election within a few years has a reason to spend now and borrow, and that reason alone produces deficits.
- It assumes that a small group of lenders can beat the whole voting public, and never argues the point.
- It names no creditor. Who holds a country's government debt is published for most countries, and large shares are usually held by that country's own citizens, pension funds, banks and central bank.
- A gap in the argument means the conclusion is unproven. It does not mean the conclusion is false.

## How to apply it

- Before signing a loan, check what the lender earns if the borrower falls behind. If that is more than the lender earns from repayment on time, the loan is set up against the borrower.
- Check whether the debt can be discharged in bankruptcy. A debt with no exit is the one to avoid.
- Treat a zero-interest offer as a fee product. The price appears when a payment is missed.
