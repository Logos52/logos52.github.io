# Financial Slavery

Owning a person is illegal everywhere it was once legal. What the ownership produced is not illegal, because the prohibition attaches to the title and not to the labour, and the two come apart. That leaves a problem with a definite solution: obtain the output of a person who cannot decline to supply it, using only instruments a court will enforce.

The solution is financial and it has two parts. The first part is revenue that rises when the borrower fails. The second part is an obligation that no failure ends. Together they produce a claim against a person's future earnings rather than against any asset. That much holds. Most of the steps taken from there to the word slavery do not.

## Core takeaways

- A lender who can lose money screens borrowers and prices the loan. Both behaviours come from the possibility of loss and disappear without it.
- Credit that charges no interest and collects late fees earns more from buyers who miss payments. The holder then has reason to seek out the borrowers a risk-bearing lender would refuse.
- Bankruptcy is what makes a loan risky. Withholding discharge from one class of debt removes the risk from that class without changing any other term of the contract.
- A payment set below the monthly interest never reduces the balance. On $100,000 at 7% with a $500 payment, the borrower has paid $120,000 after twenty years and owes about $143,000.
- A signature settles whether an obligation was accepted at entry. It says nothing about whether the borrower can exit, and unfree labour has always been defined by exit.
- The test that survives asks three things: whether the holder gains when the borrower fails, what event ends the obligation, and whether the claim runs against a pledged asset or against future earnings.

## How ordinary lending works

An investor puts money into a company and is paid out of what the company earns. The money is given up now against a return that exists only if the venture works. The investor wants the venture to succeed, because there is no other route to the return. If it fails, the investor absorbs the loss. That absorption is what makes the position an investment rather than a fee.

Lending on the same principle behaves the same way. The lender's return depends on the borrower earning enough to repay. So the lender has reason to check whether the borrower will repay, and to decline when the answer is no. When a lender declines, the screening is doing its work. Capital gets withheld from ventures unlikely to produce anything, by people with their own money at stake in the judgment.

Both the screening and the pricing come from the lender's ability to lose money. Remove that possibility and both stop.

## Getting paid when the borrower fails

The first part changes where the revenue comes from. A lender paid out of the borrower's success can be replaced by a lender paid out of the borrower's failure. Once that change is made, the screening reverses. A holder who profits from default has reason to seek out exactly the borrowers a risk-bearing lender would turn away.

The clearest example is short-term consumer credit that charges no interest and collects on missed payments. Revenue arrives as late fees from the buyer and as fees from the merchant. Both scale with the number of transactions rather than with the quality of any of them. Extending the facility to a fast-food order is not a lending decision that came out wrong. There is no repayment prospect to assess at that size and none needs assessing, because the position is profitable across the whole distribution of buyers and most profitable at the tail that misses. The instrument is built to make small commitments easy for people who will not meet them. That is the opposite of the screening function, performed deliberately and at scale.

This produces a bad product and not servitude. The amounts are small and the obligation ends. The buyer can stop borrowing. A balance that becomes unpayable can be written off, discharged, or sold at a loss that lands on whoever holds it. Up to this point the buyer can recover. The second part removes the recovery.

## Removing the way out

Bankruptcy is what makes a loan risky. It is the point at which an unpayable debt stops being the borrower's problem and becomes the lender's loss, and its availability is the reason a lender screens at all. Removing it for a class of debt removes the risk from that class without altering any other term of the contract, and without the contract having to say anything unusual.

A payment set below the monthly interest never reduces the balance.

```text
Principal $100,000 at 7% annual interest.
Monthly interest at the outset: $583.
Monthly payment: $500.

The balance grows in the first month and grows faster in every month after.
After twenty years the borrower has paid $120,000 and owes about $143,000.
```

Nothing in that case is exotic and no term in it is concealed. The payment falls short of the interest, and a borrower with a fixed income may have no way to change that. Once it holds, the balance is not an amount owed that gets smaller. The borrower transfers earnings at a steady rate for as long as there are earnings.

The justification given for withholding discharge from student debt is that an education cannot be handed back, so there is nothing to surrender in settlement. Most consumer debt buys things that cannot be handed back. A hospital stay, a funeral, a year of rent, a holiday, a restaurant meal, a repair to a car that has since been scrapped: none of them can be returned, and all of them discharge. Being unrecoverable is the ordinary condition of unsecured lending rather than a feature distinguishing one class of it. Applied to every debt that shares the feature it names, the stated reason would end discharge for nearly all consumer credit. What separates this class from the others is that a rule was written for it.

Student debt is also the instance that has drawn scrutiny and partial correction. It is the version of the structure that is easiest to see.

## What the two parts add up to

Revenue that rises with borrower failure, and an obligation that no failure terminates. Together they describe a position whose value does not depend on the outcome of the thing the money was borrowed for, and whose payment stream is drawn from the borrower's earnings for as long as earnings exist. That is not a claim against an asset. Nothing was pledged and nothing can be seized in satisfaction. What answers for the debt is the future output of a person. The claim cannot be released by the failure of the venture it financed, by the person's insolvency, or by any event other than the person paying until it stops.

An arrangement with those features is called indenture. Indenture is entered by contract rather than by capture, runs against labour rather than against property, and is exited on terms set by the instrument rather than by the person bound. Indentures were bought and sold in their own time, which is what happens to a defaulted balance now. The features separating indenture from chattel slavery are how a person enters it, and whether the person, rather than a claim against the person, is the thing owned. Both distinctions are real, and neither is affected by how heavy the obligation gets.

The modern obligation has one feature the historical arrangement did not have. An indenture ran for a term, and the term is what ended it. An obligation serviced below its interest rate has no term at all. That supports a stronger claim than the comparison with indenture does, and the claim goes unmade.

## The objection that the borrower signed

Every arrangement described was entered by an adult who signed. Nobody is compelled to borrow, the terms are printed, and the alternative on offer is no loan rather than a gentler one. Unsecured credit reaches a borrower with no assets and no earnings record precisely because the holder's recovery does not depend on the borrower's assets or earnings record. Restore discharge to that class and the class stops being lent to. The person who signed weighed whether the degree, the treatment, or the equipment was worth those terms, and calling the result servitude takes away that person's standing to make the judgment. A term is not coercive because it is onerous. An obligation is coercive when it was imposed, and a signature is what separates an imposed obligation from an accepted one.

The exit half of the objection fails. Whether an obligation was accepted at signing and whether it can be exited afterward are separate properties of the same instrument, and unfree labour has always been defined by the second. A person who agreed to a term of service was in unfree labour for the duration of the term, and the agreement is why the term was enforceable. Voluntariness at entry does not answer a question about exit.

The pricing half is different. **Nothing in the mechanism addresses the claim that removing discharge is what makes the credit exist at all, and that claim goes unanswered.** If it holds, the harsh term is the price of the loan being available, and the comparison that matters runs between the arrangement and its absence rather than between the arrangement and a milder version of itself. Which of those is the real alternative is a question about what lending markets do when the term is removed. The mechanism does not settle it.

## The same structure at government scale

The individual version has a ceiling, which is the size of one person's earnings. A sovereign borrower removes the ceiling, and the counterparty becomes the whole tax base.

A government spending beyond its revenue borrows the difference, and the following year borrows the difference plus the service on what it already owes. Past a point the borrowing is no longer for anything. It covers interest on prior borrowing, which is the individual case with the payment set below the interest rate, run at national scale. Nothing pays it down and no event ends it. The holders are paid out of taxation, collected from a population that signed nothing and cannot decline.

The step added on top is that the creditors produce this condition, through influence on the politicians who authorise the spending. The condition is real. The influence is not the only thing that would produce it. An elected government's horizon ends with its term, the benefit of spending lands inside the term, and the service falls due outside it. That is enough on its own to generate persistent deficits, and it operates whether or not any lender does anything. A cause that is already sufficient stays sufficient when a second one is available, and nothing in the mechanism distinguishes a system where both operate from one where only the first does.

The larger difficulty is the exit, which the individual case depended on. Sovereigns have exits that individuals do not. They default, and have defaulted, repeatedly and under their own names, and the same sovereignty supports outright repudiation and the inflating away of any obligation written in a currency they issue. The party unable to leave in the individual case was the borrower. At national scale the borrower holds the largest set of ways out, and the creditor holds a claim it cannot enforce against a debtor with an army.

The monetary reply meets this. Where the debt is denominated in a currency the borrower does not issue, obtaining that currency requires either earning it or borrowing it again, and the exits above are closed. That case is exactly as described, and it is the case for every state that borrows in someone else's money or in a currency it shares without controlling. The version naming the borrower's own central bank as the instrument does not reach it. A state whose debt is written in the currency its own central bank issues can settle that debt by issuing, and the cost of doing so falls across everyone holding the currency. That cost is inflation, and it means the borrower discharges the obligation at a third party's expense. A creditor who cannot lose and a debtor inflating the debt away cannot both be true of the same arrangement.

A stronger version of the point is available here and goes unused. The extraction occurs on either path, and the only variable is who absorbs it: through taxation when the debt is serviced, through the currency when it is inflated. The payer in both cases is the population, which contracted for nothing and cannot exit. Its position has the two properties the individual case identified: no event discharges it, and the burden does not depend on the outcome of whatever the money bought. Stated that way, the mechanism survives the monetary objection.

What it loses is the counterparty. The individual case had a name on a document and someone who collected. The relocated version describes a transfer with no identifiable holder of the claim. The beneficiaries of deficit spending are diffuse and change with each budget, and the paper sits with pension funds, foreign central banks, and institutions of the issuing state itself. Indenture requires a party that owns the claim and can be identified. In the version that survives at national scale, no party occupies that position.

## The creditor nobody can name

The obscurity is offered as a feature of the design: layers of intermediaries, a share of the revenue returned to the politicians, and a population that cannot say who holds the claim against it and therefore cannot organise against the holder.

Opacity is a real property of extraction systems, and the absence of a visible beneficiary is not evidence that no beneficiary exists. The difficulty is what the claim can be checked against. Put this way, a visible creditor confirms the arrangement and an invisible one confirms it more strongly, since invisibility is what competent execution would produce. Nothing counts against it. The same construction is available at equal strength to any claim about a concealed party, including claims incompatible with this one.

## Comparing tax rates to a slave's share of the harvest

One comparison in circulation sets the burden of a slave in ancient Egypt at a fifth of the harvest and leaves the modern rate unstated. Taken as given, the figures are what they are, and on the single dimension of what fraction of output is taken, the modern figure is higher across most developed countries.

That dimension is the one on which the two arrangements are most alike. What was at issue in the ancient case was whether the person could be sold, whether the condition passed to their children, whether they chose their work, and whether leaving was possible. A rate comparison sets all four aside. Applied consistently it also produces results nobody advances. A citizen of any polity taking more than a fifth comes out worse off than a slave, including a slave in a polity that taxed slaves on top of owning them. A lightly taxed free population comes out equivalent to a lightly taxed enslaved one.

## What holds up, and the test it gives

The core holds. Revenue that rises with borrower failure, combined with an obligation that no failure terminates, describes a claim against a person's future output rather than against any asset, and no appeal to the borrower's signature reaches the second condition.

The steps built on top vary. The stated reason for withholding discharge from one class of debt does not survive being applied to the debts sharing the feature it names. The national version identifies a real condition and asserts a cause for it where a sufficient cause is already present. Its monetary half describes a creditor immune to loss and a debtor inflating the debt away, and both cannot hold at once. The form that does survive at that scale has nobody occupying the position indenture requires. The closing comparison holds one variable and drops the ones that defined the case. The label financial slavery claims more than the mechanism delivers, and nothing the mechanism does deliver depends on the label.

The portable part is a test. Three properties separate a risk-bearing arrangement from an extractive one, and none of them requires knowing anyone's intentions.

```text
Whether the holder of the claim is better off when the borrower fails.
What event terminates the obligation, and on whom the loss falls when it does.
Whether the claim runs against a pledged asset or against future earnings.
```

An arrangement where the holder gains from failure, no event terminates the claim, and the claim runs against earnings is an extraction, whatever it is called and whatever anyone building it intended. The test does not establish that any particular instance was designed for the purpose, and it does not settle whether such arrangements should be permitted, which turns on grounds it does not supply. It establishes what the instrument does once it exists.

## How to practice this

1. Take a credit agreement you hold and ask whether the holder is better off when you fail to pay. Fees charged on missed payments are the sign. Notice whether the holder had any reason to check that you could repay.
2. Compare your monthly payment to the monthly interest on the balance. On $100,000 at 7%, the interest is $583 a month. A payment below that figure makes the balance grow every month.
3. Ask what event ends the obligation and who takes the loss when it does. If no event ends it, the claim runs against your earnings for as long as you earn.
4. Ask whether the claim runs against a pledged asset or against your future earnings. Unsecured credit has nothing to seize, so the earnings answer for it.
5. When someone defends a term by saying you signed, separate the two questions. A signature settles entry. Ask separately whether you can exit.
6. Run the three tests before asking who built the arrangement or why. The test does not require knowing anyone's intentions.

## Open questions

If what converts a claim on capital into a claim on labour is that no failure terminates it, a tax liability meets the description as fully as a non-dischargeable debt does. Is there anything in the structure that separates them, or is the separation only that one of them was signed?

Where the party without an exit is the currency's users rather than the borrowing state, what would identify the holder of the claim against them, and does the mechanism still describe anything if no holder can be named?
