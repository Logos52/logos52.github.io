# Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim

A [Basic Logic video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWS59L52h1M) argues that human groups differ genetically enough to build different kinds of society, so no single political system suits every people. The video avoids naming real groups. It calls them circles, squares and triangles, and says it uses these stand-ins because biology at the scale of whole groups is a sensitive topic. Its chain runs from geography and isolation, to measured genetic distance, to differences in the nervous system, to the kind of society each group builds.

## Geography and distance

The video starts with geography. In its made-up world the three groups live far apart, and a large desert has cut the squares off from everyone else for nearly 40,000 years. It sets that against domestic dogs, which it says became a distinct species 15,000 to 30,000 years ago. Its reasoning is that a long isolation with little mixing lets a group keep adapting to its own conditions.

To measure the distance, the video uses the fixation index, which it describes as a measure of allele frequencies between two groups, where more matching alleles means a closer relation. In its account, a value below 0.15 means moderate to low difference, the range of closely related subspecies, such as different North American gray wolves. Above an index of 0.15 it places greatly differentiated organisms. It puts the domestic dog and the gray wolf at an index of about 0.17. The video then gives its groups index values. Circles and squares sit at 0.15, triangles and squares at 0.19, and circles and triangles at 0.11. By its own scale, triangles and squares sit further apart than dogs and wolves. The transcript gives no source for these values.

## From genes to societies

The video rejects the idea that differences between groups are only skin deep. It says a 2011 study found that skin-related differences make up only a small part of total genetic differentiation, and that genes affecting the nervous system showed some of the greatest differentiation. It names neither the study nor its authors. Its next step is its own inference. It says similar nervous systems logically lead to similar intellect, personality and temperament, and that those traits matter most for a cohesive society.

A separate 2011 paper by [Wu and Zhang](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2148-11-16) is a related source, though nothing ties it to the video. It found higher population differentiation in genes for bone development, hair follicles, pigmentation, sperm cells, the nervous system, organ development and some metabolic pathways, with few immune-system genes in that group. The paper reports differences by gene group. It does not discuss intellect, temperament or societies.

On history, the video says circles generally built individualistic, decentralised civilisations, triangles built collectivist, centralised ones, and squares for the most part built no comparable civilisation. It asks whether that pattern is random and answers that peoples more likely shape their societies to fit their own neurology. The video then argues that politics should not be treated as a god with one system for all, and that a group's long-working ways deserve respect. Its analogy is that a Chihuahua and a German Shepherd belong to one species and still do not suit the same working roles.

## Mixed marriage

The video's last section asks what system suits the children of very different parents. On its chart, a circle's child with a triangle sits further from the circle parent than a random circle from another country, and the same holds from the other parent's side. The video says neither parent is suited to the setting the child would need. It compares this to crossing a husky with an Ibiza Hound, whose pups would be only partly suited to snow or desert. It adds that social order is more complex than climate.

For circle and triangle families the video is hopeful. It says both groups have shown some ability to adapt to the other's order, and because they were never fully separate, a middle population with its own settings already exists. For square mixes it says the genetic midpoint is nearly empty, square history gives few clues, and parents would be guessing. It says the circle share of a square person's ancestry predicts success, that mixed square children struggle, and that nobody knows a fix that would not make the society worse for its main group. It ends by noting that triangles mostly keep their societies built for themselves and says the results make that hard to disagree with.

## Open questions

Where do the fixation-index values of 0.11, 0.15 and 0.19 come from? Neither the video nor the notes give a source.

Which 2011 study does the video mean? It names none.

What evidence supports the step from nervous-system genes to intellect, temperament and the type of society? The video calls it logical and cites nothing.
