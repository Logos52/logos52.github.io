# Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim

The distance claim says that peoples who lived apart for tens of thousands of years differ in their genes enough that one form of society cannot fit them all, and that a child of two distant peoples fits no society that exists. It turns up in debate over immigration and unequal outcomes, so a reader who knows its steps can tell which parts are measured and which parts are only asserted.

The argument is told with three made-up peoples called circles, squares and triangles. It names no real people, and it says any real group a listener matches to a shape is the listener's own doing. The shapes let the steps be tested without a fight over any real group.

## Core takeaways

- The claim is a chain: long isolation, then a measured gap in genes, then a gap in nervous-system genes, then a gap in temperament, then a form of society built to fit each people.
- The one measured link is a number for how far apart two populations sit in the frequencies of their gene variants, called the fixation index; the claim puts the line between the same kind and distinct kinds at 0.15.
- On the claim's own numbers, triangles and squares sit farther apart than a domestic dog from a wolf.
- From this the claim concludes that a people doing badly under another people's rules needs no discrimination to explain it.
- It also concludes that a child of two distant peoples does well in neither parent's society, and that no society exists that fits the child.
- Every link after the measured number is asserted, so the chain can hold together in form and still rest on premises no one has checked.
- The owner of this knowledge base has taken no position on the claim.

## The argument

- Isolation. A desert cut the squares off from everyone else for almost 40,000 years. Domestic dogs became separate from wolves in 15,000 to 30,000 years, so the span is long enough for a people to change.
- Measurement. The fixation index reads as follows.
  - Below 0.15: close, the range of subspecies or breeds of one species. Gray wolf subspecies in North America sit here.
  - Above 0.15: strongly separated, very different subspecies or different species. Domestic dog against gray wolf is about 0.17.
  - Circles against triangles 0.11. Circles against squares 0.15. Triangles against squares 0.19.
- What differs. A 2011 comparison of the three found that surface traits such as skin and build are a small part of the total genetic difference, and that genes acting on the nervous system are among the most different.
- Nervous system to temperament. Similar nervous systems give similar intelligence, personality and temperament, and shared temperament is what holds a society together.
- Temperament to society. Circles built individualist, decentralized states. Triangles built collectivist, centralized ones. Squares for the most part built nothing comparable. The claim reads each as a people building the society that fit it.
- Conclusion. No single political system makes every people prosper. A circle telling a triangle its way of life is wrong is as mistaken as the reverse. A Chihuahua and a German Shepherd are one species and not suited to the same work.

```
fixation index, 0 = same, higher = farther apart

0.11  circles / triangles    same kind, close
0.15  circles / squares      the line the claim draws
0.17  dog / wolf
0.19  triangles / squares    farther than dog from wolf
```

## The mixing step

- On a plot of genetic distance, a circle's child by a triangle sits farther from the circle parent than a random circle from another country does. The same holds from the triangle side.
- So neither parent has lived in the society the child would do best in. A husky crossed with an Ibizan Hound does well in neither snow nor desert, and a social order is a narrower fit than a climate.
- Circles and triangles were never fully apart. An in-between group already exists with conditions fitted to it, and circle-triangle people have mostly done well.
- With squares there is almost no in-between group, and square history gives few clues about what worked. A square-circle or square-triangle child has no past community like it, so the parent builds the child's world without a guide.
- The claim says square-circle and square-triangle people do badly in societies fitted to circles or triangles, that no fix is known, and that any fix would make those societies fit circles or triangles less well.
- Circles have tried to fit squares in, with poor results. Triangles have not tried and keep their societies fitted to themselves.

## Where it fails

- The fixation index was built to compare frequencies of gene variants. It has no rule for sorting species, and the claim concedes this. The 0.15 line is a reading convention, so a value above the dog and wolf figure is a larger number on the same scale and does not make the peoples compared separate kinds.
- The 2011 comparison has no title and no author on record, so the finding about nervous-system genes cannot be checked.
- The step from nervous-system genes to temperament, and from temperament to a form of state, is asserted. Who built which kind of state has other well-known causes: climate, disease, trade routes, conquest.
- The link between circle administration and square success is a correlation with nothing held constant.
- Any check against real populations would run on rates per head, and the claim gives none.
