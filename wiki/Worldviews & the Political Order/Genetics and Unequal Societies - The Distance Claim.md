# Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim

The distance claim says that peoples who lived apart for tens of thousands of years differ in their genes enough that one form of society cannot fit them all, and that a child of two distant peoples fits no society that exists. It accounts for unequal outcomes between peoples without needing discrimination as a cause, which is why it turns up in debate over immigration. The argument uses three made-up peoples, circles, squares and triangles, names no real group, and says any real group a listener matches to a shape is the listener's own doing.

- The claim runs from isolation to a society fitted to each people.
- Its measured link is the fixation index, a number for genetic distance.
- The claim draws the line between kinds at 0.15.
- Triangles and squares sit farther apart than a dog from a wolf.
- A people failing under another's rules needs no discrimination to explain.
- A child of two distant peoples fits neither parent's society.

## The argument

A desert cut the squares off from everyone else for almost 40,000 years. Domestic dogs became separate from wolves in 15,000 to 30,000 years, so that span is long enough for a people to change. The fixation index measures how far apart two populations sit in the frequencies of their gene variants, where a value below 0.15 is the range of subspecies or breeds of one species and a value above it means strongly separated subspecies or different species. A 2011 comparison of the three peoples found that surface traits such as skin and build are a small part of the genetic difference, and that genes acting on the nervous system are among the most different.

- Gray wolf subspecies in North America sit below 0.15.
- Domestic dog against gray wolf is about 0.17.
- Similar nervous systems give similar intelligence, personality and temperament.
- Shared temperament holds a society together.
- Circles built individualist, decentralised states.
- Triangles built collectivist, centralised ones.
- Squares for the most part built nothing comparable.
- Each people built the society that fit it.

```
fixation index, 0 = same, higher = farther apart

0.11  circles / triangles    same kind, close
0.15  circles / squares      the line the claim draws
0.17  dog / wolf
0.19  triangles / squares    farther than dog from wolf
```

No single political system makes every people prosper. A circle telling a triangle its way of life is wrong is as mistaken as the reverse, in the way a Chihuahua and a German Shepherd are one species and are not suited to the same work.

## The mixing step

On a plot of genetic distance, a circle's child by a triangle sits farther from the circle parent than a random circle from another country does, and the same holds from the triangle side. So neither parent has lived in the society the child would do best in. A husky crossed with an Ibizan Hound does well in neither snow nor desert, and a social order is a narrower fit than a climate. Circles and triangles were never fully apart, so an in-between group already exists with conditions fitted to it, and circle-triangle people have mostly done well.

- Squares have almost no in-between group with anyone.
- Square history gives few clues about what worked.
- A square-circle child's parent builds the child's world without a guide.
- Square-circle and square-triangle people do badly in circle or triangle societies.
- No fix is known.
- Any fix would make those societies fit circles or triangles less well.
- Circles have tried to fit squares in, with poor results.
- Triangles have not tried, and keep their societies fitted to themselves.

## Where it fails

The fixation index was built to compare frequencies of gene variants and has no rule for sorting species, which the claim itself concedes. The 0.15 line is a reading convention, so a value above the dog and wolf figure is a larger number on the same scale and does not by itself make two peoples separate kinds. The steps from nervous-system genes to temperament, and from temperament to a form of state, are stated without a measurement behind them.

- The 2011 comparison has no title or author on record.
- Squares under circle rule: a correlation with nothing held constant.
- A real-population check needs rates per head, and none are given.
- The owner has taken no position on the claim.
