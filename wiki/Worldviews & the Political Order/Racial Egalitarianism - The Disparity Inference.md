# Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference

Racial egalitarianism is the belief that all racial groups are the same in every trait that affects how their members live. From that belief comes an inference: if groups differ in income, crime or schooling, discrimination must be the cause. Policy across the West rests on the inference, and the inference holds only if the belief is true.

## Takeaways

- The inference needs every group to be innately the same.
- Intelligence and temperament are partly inherited.
- Small genetic differences can matter a great deal.
- Some traits are near universal in one group and absent in others.
- Culture is made by a people, so it cannot be the first cause.
- Disparities alone do not show discrimination.

## The three explanations

Three answers are given for why racial groups have different average outcomes. The mainstream left says all groups are equal, so any gap is discrimination. The mainstream right says culture explains the gaps, and any group could adopt a culture that has worked for another. Both leave out the plainest answer, that groups differ innately and would produce different outcomes with no outside pressure at all.

- Discrimination: every gap is caused by other people.
- Culture: every gap is caused by habits a group happened to adopt.
- Innate difference: groups adapted to different conditions and differ on average.
- The question concerns group averages.
- Exceptional individuals exist in every group and do not move the average.

## The six defenses

Six arguments are used to defend the belief that all groups are the same. Each one either states something false or states something true that does not lead where it is taken. The table gives the six and the answer to each.

| Defense | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Intelligence is not genetic | Twin and adoption studies show it is heritable |
| Humans are 99.9% identical | Wolves and dogs are also 99.9% identical |
| More variation within groups | A few genes matter far more than most |
| Race is a social construct | Naming a real thing does not make it unreal |
| IQ tests are unreliable | IQ is the best predictor of success available |
| Culture explains the gaps | Culture comes from the people who make it |

## How small differences matter

The 99.9% figure counts how much genetic material people share. It says nothing about which differences carry weight. Humans share about half their genes with a banana and about 90% of their DNA with a cat. Gray wolves and domestic dogs split 20,000 to 30,000 years ago, more recently than some human groups did, and nobody blames a wolf's greater aggression on how it was raised.

- Most variants between two people have no effect.
- A few variants spread through a whole population because they mattered.
- The Duffy-negative blood type: 95 to 100% of West and Central Africans.
- It is near zero elsewhere and protects against vivax malaria.
- The gene EPAS1 lets about 90% of Tibetans live at altitude.
- They get by on 40% less oxygen.
- The gene ABCC11: 80 to 95% of East Asians have no sweat odour.
- Intelligence and personality depend on many genes, not all of them identified yet.
- Border Collies and Chihuahuas are one species with very different trainability.

## IQ and culture

IQ tests have faults, and they still predict success better than being born rich does. One reply says IQ only measures fitness for Western society. If that were so, groups with lower scores would do better in other societies, yet migration runs toward the West, because high intelligence helps everywhere. Culture and genes shape each other, and genes came first, so culture is a product of a people, and a culture that works for one group may fail for another.

- Aggression and impulse control are also partly inherited.
- Moving a people to a new country does not produce identical results.
- Belief in equal starting points rests on fear of being called racist.
