# Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference

Groups differ measurably in income, education, and crime. All of those differences are averages. An average reaches no individual, who can sit anywhere in a distribution.

One inference organises most public argument about those differences. It begins from a premise: the groups themselves are equivalent in every relevant respect. If that premise is true, something outside the groups must be producing the gaps, and discrimination is the proposed cause. The conclusion follows from the premise without difficulty, so the whole argument depends on whether the premise is true. The premise is rarely examined.

Six defenses are offered for the equivalence premise. They vary considerably in quality. One of them is answered with a policy argument rather than with a reply. What produces the gaps, once the inference is set aside, is a separate question and is not answered here.

## Core takeaways

- The inference runs in three steps: the groups are equivalent, so the cause of the gaps is external, so the cause is discrimination. Only the first step is in dispute, and the first step is the one rarely argued for.
- Four of the six defenses fail as stated. Two of those four cite a figure that is true and draw a conclusion the figure cannot reach.
- The claim that intelligence is not heritable is false. Twin and adoption studies show substantial heritability within populations. That defeats the defense without showing that the gaps between groups are genetic.
- The objection that IQ tests are unreliable is granted and then never answered. The reply offered says the position is inconvenient for the people holding it, which leaves the position where it was.
- The claim that any group could adopt another group's culture assumes the transfer would work. That assumption restates the equivalence premise instead of showing it.
- Showing that the equivalence premise is assumed rather than demonstrated removes the licence for concluding discrimination from disparity alone. It does not say what causes the gaps.

## First defense: intelligence is not heritable

The first defense says cognitive ability is not genetically influenced at all. If that were true, differences between groups could not be genetic, and the inference would go through by default.

The claim is false. Twin and adoption studies show substantial heritability within populations, and any account of how the trait arose at all requires that it was selectable. Variation within a species in cognitive and behavioural traits is uncontroversial elsewhere in biology. Dogs bred for work differ from dogs bred for company in how trainable they are, and nobody attributes that to circumstance.

Heritability within a population does not by itself show that a difference between two populations is genetic. Those are two separate measurements, and the second does not follow from the first. What the reply establishes is weaker: a genetic contribution is possible and cannot be ruled out on the grounds this defense offers. That is enough to defeat a defense that claimed impossibility. It is short of showing that the gaps are genetic.

## Second defense: humans are 99.9% genetically identical

The figure is correct. The conclusion drawn from it is not.

Similarity in the quantity of shared genetic material does not mean equivalence in outcomes. Roughly the same order of similarity separates domestic dogs from grey wolves. Those two populations diverged more recently than some human groups did, and they differ substantially in temperament. The traits under discussion are carried in the fraction that varies. A statistic that reports the size of the shared fraction reports nothing about those traits.

## Third defense: there is more variation within groups than between them

The premise is true and is granted. Individual variation within any population exceeds the difference between population averages.

An average taken across all locations in the genetic material hides places where the difference is close to fixed. Some variants are near universal in one population and near absent in others.

- The Duffy-negative variant, which confers resistance to a form of malaria, is carried by nearly all West and Central Africans and by effectively nobody without African ancestry.
- The EPAS1 variant, which supports function at low oxygen, is carried by about nine in ten Tibetans and by no Africans or Europeans.
- A variant governing body odour is carried by most East Asians and by few others.

A statistic that averages across all locations cannot see a location where one group is at one hundred per cent and another is at zero.

The defense fails as stated. The average does not show that the groups are equivalent, and it says nothing about any trait it did not measure.

## Fourth defense: race is a social construct

That a category is socially constructed does not make what it tracks unreal. Every category is socially constructed in the sense of being named and agreed on, including the categories for planets and molecules. The naming comes after the thing and does not create it. Categories get built around distinctions because somebody noticed the distinctions first.

A narrower version of this defense would carry weight: that the particular boundaries drawn are arbitrary, or that they track ancestry poorly. As it is used, it says the thing being referred to is invented. That is a stronger claim, and this defense does not support it.

## Fifth defense: IQ tests are unreliable

Here the objection is granted and the subject is changed.

That the measure has limitations is granted outright. The reply offered is that the measure remains the best available predictor of success, ahead of parental wealth. That answers a claim that the measure is useless. It does not answer a claim that the measure is unreliable. Those are two different objections.

The rest of this defense takes up a different claim: that the measure predicts success in Western societies rather than intelligence itself. One real reply exists to that. No society exists where high cognitive ability is not an advantage, so what is being measured is not local to the West.

What comes after that is not a reply. It is an argument that this second claim carries policy consequences its own advocates would reject. That may be true, and it changes nothing about the defense. The reliability objection is not answered. Showing that a position is inconvenient for the people who hold it leaves the position where it was.

## Sixth defense: any culture would transfer

This defense says any group could adopt a culture that has served another group. If that is so, the groups are equivalent in the respects that matter, and the remaining gaps must be imposed from outside.

The transfer is the step that has to hold. That a set of practices works for the population that generated it gives no guarantee that the practices transfer. A claim that they would transfer restates the equivalence premise instead of showing it.

A separate question is whether practices explain the gaps in the first place. That is a different claim, and it is not a defense of equivalence. It is not taken up here.

## What survives of the six

Four defenses fail as stated. Two of those four fail cleanly: the identity statistic and the within-group variance statistic both report something true, and both are used to license a conclusion the numbers cannot reach.

One defense is untouched. The reliability objection is conceded on its own terms and then answered with a consequence instead of a rebuttal.

The sixth fails as a defense of equivalence, and only as that. A transfer that has not been shown cannot support the premise.

Together the six establish less than either side wants. The equivalence premise is assumed rather than demonstrated, which removes the licence for concluding discrimination from disparity alone. That does not replace the inference with another account of the gaps, and it does not leave some other cause standing by default. The inference fails. The cause of the gaps is not settled here.

## How to practice this

1. When discrimination is named as the cause of a gap, find the equivalence premise. Ask whether the speaker showed it or assumed it. Notice that the conclusion follows only if the premise holds.
2. When a statistic is offered as proof of equivalence, ask what the statistic measured. The 99.9% figure measures the shared fraction of genetic material. Notice that it reports nothing about the fraction that varies.
3. When an average is offered, check whether it could hide a near-fixed difference. An average across all locations cannot see one location at one hundred per cent and another at zero.
4. When a defense grants an objection, check whether what follows answers it. An argument that a position is inconvenient for its holders answers nothing.
5. When someone says one group could adopt another group's culture, ask what shows the transfer would work. Notice that an unshown transfer restates the equivalence premise instead of supporting it.
6. Read every group claim here as a claim about averages. Notice that no average tells you where a particular person sits.
