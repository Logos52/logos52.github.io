# Worldviews & the Political Order

Seven question groups map arguments about power, prosperity, group differences, guilt, immigration, and sex, with each group collecting the related debates under one question while two pages on guilt and DEI stay separate. The reading order starts with how to check an argument, then walks the seven questions in sequence.

## How the section is built

Most arguments come from credited public sources, especially Basic Logic videos. Each group is written for a reader who already holds a view and wants to test whether an opposing line of reasoning survives the checks in [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 1 - How to read these arguments|Group 1]]. Ideas spread when they pay the person holding them, and the costliest ideas to test are the ones that travel fastest (explored in [[wiki/Books/The Parasitic Mind|The Parasitic Mind]]). Alongside the political groups sit cross-cutting patterns on technology and pace.

## Reading order

1. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 1 - How to read these arguments|Group 1 - How to read these arguments]] (Validity, soundness, hidden premises, and [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Per Capita|per capita]] rates.)
2. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 2 - Who rules, and on what terms|Group 2 - Who rules, and on what terms]] (Democracy as a tool, organized minorities, faith and the state, proportionality.)
3. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 3 - Markets, states and prosperity|Group 3 - Markets, states and prosperity]] (Planning, prices, country cases, and industrial signals.)
4. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 4 - Do group gaps prove discrimination|Group 4 - Do group gaps prove discrimination]] (From outcome gaps to discrimination claims, with locked companion [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/DEI as Anti-Discrimination|DEI as Anti-Discrimination]].)
5. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 5 - The racism charge and Western guilt|Group 5 - The racism charge and Western guilt]] (Moral language as leverage, with locked companion [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/White Guilt|White Guilt]].)
6. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 6 - Immigration and the electorate|Group 6 - Immigration and the electorate]] (Cohesion, accountability, and who votes.)
7. [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Group 7 - Sex, gender and family|Group 7 - Sex, gender and family]] (Identity claims, feminism, schooling, dating markets.)

Read [[wiki/Books/Suicidal Empathy|Suicidal Empathy]] when a moral feeling is doing the work that evidence and proportion should do. On any topic here, state the steelman, name what would change your mind, then read the group that matches the question.

## Using the groups

Start with Group 1 whenever a clip feels conclusive. Name the warrant, divide raw totals, and only then open the group that matches the fight you are in. Pair the groups with the two book pages when an idea spreads because it pays the holder or when feeling overrules evidence, and turn to the technology-pace pages when a debate turns on who gets to ship.
