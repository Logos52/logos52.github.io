# Worldviews & the Political Order

# Worldviews & the Political Order

Each page in this section takes one disputed political belief, such as that democracy is sacred or that a racial gap proves racism, and tests whether it holds. The pages check whether a conclusion follows from its facts, so a reader can apply the same check to beliefs these pages do not cover.

- Read [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]] first, since it is the method.
- Each page states an argument at its full strength.
- The facts are taken as given unless something checkable overturns them.

## Methods

These pages set out tools of reasoning that apply to any subject. They were worked out on the hardest subjects, where a wrong step costs the most. [[wiki/Argument Validation/Argument Validation|Argument Validation]] gathers the same checks without a political subject.

- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]]: when a conclusion follows, and the common ways it fails.
- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Per Capita|Per Capita]]: divide a count by group size before comparing.
- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Moral Language as Leverage|Moral Language as Leverage]]: shame words work only on those who feel shame.
- [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Who Gets Called Racist|Who Gets Called Racist]]: using group judgments and answering the label.
