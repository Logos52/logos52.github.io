# The Organized Minority

# The Organized Minority

In every society big enough to need running, a small organized group governs a large unorganized one. The idea comes from the Italian school of elite theory, the writers Gaetano Mosca, Vilfredo Pareto and Robert Michels, and it explains why voters hold little power in practice. Applied to the West today, it explains why the ruling group favours policies that weaken the population it rules.

## Core takeaways

- A hundred people who act together beat a hundred thousand who cannot.
- A ruling group must use its advantages or a rival group will.
- Ruling ability runs in families through who marries whom.
- Revolutions are one elite replacing another, using the masses.
- Europe replaced its elites faster than other societies did.
- Europe's mixed population makes its people a threat to today's elite.
- Welfare and mass immigration serve that elite by removing rivals.

## How it works

Running a state is specialised, full-time work, so most people hand it to the few who can do it. The few are small enough to act as one, and acting as one gives them a monopoly over authority and resources. They have to use that monopoly to protect their position, because a rival group of the same kind is always forming and will use it if they do not. A monarchy, a republic and a one-party state each have a ruling few and differ only in how the few are chosen.

- Ruling needs intellect, charisma, cunning and courage in a fight.
- Many of these traits are inherited.
- People who have them marry each other.
- Their children inherit the traits.
- Over generations this produces noble lines bred to rule.
- A line decays when its traits weaken or stop being useful.
- New capable lines then rise to challenge the old.

```
ruling few --> traits weaken --> rival few forms
    ^                                  |
    |        masses used as tool       |
    +------- new ruling few <----------+
```

## Europe's fast turnover

European noble lines collapsed, and new ones rose, more often than anywhere else. Europe built more ways for talent to move up, so that a failing elite could be cut off and a new one raised before a revolution did the same by force. The Industrial Revolution broke this system of fast replacement. Business sense, mathematics and marketing became the skills that ruled, court politics and skill in war stopped counting, and more noble lines lost their standing than at any time before.

- Poorer noble families married into rising business families.
- Many married into the common population.
- Traits bred over centuries spread through the common population.
- The decades that followed were the age of revolutions.

## The West today

Europeans now have no fixed biological castes, and a capable person can come from almost any family. The skills of the current elite are finance, money printing, insider trading and bureaucracy, none of them hard, and they depend on connections more than ability. When the gap between rulers and ruled is small, the rulers are at risk of being overthrown. So Europeans as a people are a threat to any elite that wants to rule for long.

- The elite hides behind elected politicians and anonymous finance.
- Welfare takes from middle-class savers and pays those living off the state.
- Capable earners say they cannot afford children and have fewer.
- Mass immigration imports the masses of low-IQ societies with castes.
- Those newcomers depend on the state and produce no rival elite.
- The result is a new caste system with a loyal foreign underclass.

None of this needs a conspiracy. A ruling group only has to keep the policies that happen to remove its challengers, and it has every reason to keep them.
