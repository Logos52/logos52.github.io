# Sex, gender and family


The last group asks what the sources claim about men, women, identity and family.

## Gender Ideology - The Override Claim

# Gender Ideology - The Override Claim Gender ideology holds that a person's inner identity can override biological sex. The Basic Logic video ranks the defenses of that idea and argues they fail for a shared reason. Each one either defines a term by itself, borrows biology only when it helps, or puts feelings in place of facts that can be checked. Two defenses keep a valid premise, which is that gender dysphoria is a real condition and that adults make their own choices, and neither of them supports overriding biology.

## LGBT - Permission vs Promotion

# LGBT - Permission vs Promotion The Basic Logic video argues that the best argument for LGBT is that what two consenting adults do in private is none of anyone else's business. That argument supports leaving people alone. It cannot support pride months, rainbow logos and school lessons, because those make the matter everyone's business. The movement dropped its strongest argument when it moved from asking for permission to promoting itself, and the other defenses in circulation are weaker or fail outright.

## Late Stage Feminism

# Late Stage Feminism A [Basic Logic video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3SOWFC2SvFY) asks how a society reached the point where, by its account, people online raised over $1 million in support of a mother who murdered her three children. According to its own description, the fundraiser was for her parents. By the video's figures, a woman who rescued six children from a fire, with burns to 93% of her body, received about $42,000. The video says the crime is less shocking than the public reaction to it. It offers two answers, a short one through the abortion debate and a deeper one in which feminism, by rejecting patriarchy, ends up rejecting biology itself. In a [reaction video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtli77ZzhT0), Asmongold accepts part of this and gives a simpler cause.

## Single-Sex Spaces - The Asymmetry

# Single-Sex Spaces - The Asymmetry The asymmetry, as the Front Page video describes it, is that American law spent about 40 years opening men-only clubs while leaving women-only spaces legal, and nothing replaced what men lost. The video, [The Hypocrisy Of Male VS Women Spaces](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kcy9rQjMglU) traces how courts used public accommodation laws to integrate men's clubs and why carve-outs protect women's spaces. It then follows the gap into men's friendships, work and deaths. The video says it is not taking a side, and it frames its conclusion as measured by outcomes: the balance between equal access and privacy, safety and past disadvantage went further than almost anyone intended.

## Schooling - The Format Filter

# Schooling - The Format Filter The format filter is the claim that the way schools run the day, with children sitting still while an adult lectures for hours, screens out boys and then blames them for failing. The Basic Logic video [How Misandry Destroyed Gen Z's Education](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bnq5feQzbK4) argues that this format, together with three later pressures, taught girls that boys are less capable and taught boys that institutions and women are hostile to them. The video links that split to Gen Z's trouble forming relationships. The three pressures are teacher bias, MeToo-era suspicion and pay-gap campaigns.

## The Gen Z Gender War - The Split

# The Gen Z Gender War - The Split The split is the growing gap between young women and young men in politics, schooling, dating and marriage. The Front Page video [The Gen Z Gender War](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVPpfKOqXY0) cites a 2025 King's College London survey of 30 countries. It says young women there are about 30 percentage points more progressive than young men, up from a gap of 6 points in 2000. The video's view is that each side's grievances are real, and that the two sides now live in separate media worlds that feed those grievances back to them.

## Dating Apps - The Gini Coefficient

# Dating Apps - The Gini Coefficient The Gini coefficient of a dating app is one number between 0 and 1 for how unevenly the likes that women send land on the men. On Hinge in 2017 it came out at 0.542 for the men. On the list economists keep for national incomes, 0.542 sits near apartheid-era South Africa. One of Hinge's own engineers ran the sum on the app's own record of every like and published the result. He kept his job, the post stayed up, and the company changed nothing about the product. The number gives a measurement of something a man on the app otherwise only experiences, an inbox that stays empty however much he swipes. A measurement can be compared across apps and over years. The number is also narrower than the way it gets quoted. It measures how the likes are shared out among the men. It does not measure how few women are there to send them. Part of the number is made by the swipe itself, because on a swipe app rating a man and writing to him are one motion.
