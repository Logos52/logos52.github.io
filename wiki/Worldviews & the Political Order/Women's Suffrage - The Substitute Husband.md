# Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband

# Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband

Women in Western democracies outnumber men and vote at higher rates, so their average preferences set much of public policy. On average they vote for a larger state that protects and provides, which are the jobs a husband once did. The state pays for that role by taxing men, and the result weakens the family.

## Core takeaways

- Women vote well left of men on welfare, guns and immigration.
- Many use the state as a substitute husband.
- The state produces nothing, so it takes the provision from men.
- Women track whatever the institutions of the day say.
- Married couples vote alike, so a wife's separate vote adds little.
- Parties that court single women gain from delayed family formation.

## The voting gap

The gap between the sexes is large on the issues that decide the size of the state. Women back more welfare, more gun control and more immigration than men do. A play by Aristophanes, Assembly of Women, written around 391 BC, imagined women taking over Athens and predicted the same programme: more welfare, freeloading on the treasury, hostility to private property, and the state as provider.

| Issue | Women's lead over men |
| --- | --- |
| More welfare | 8 to 15 points |
| More gun control | 20 points |
| More immigration | up to 15 points |
| Abortion for any reason | 15 points, 56% in favour |

- Women also outnumber men in schools and HR departments.
- Family courts favour women in divorce and custody.
- Men keep only production and force, and they no longer use force.

## The substitute husband

A woman without a husband to protect and provide for her has a reason to vote for a state that does both. The state funds that by taking resources from men, which strips men of the leverage they once had when choosing a wife. What men have left to compete with is their looks, so they invest in appearance and drift toward traits women once used. Women in lasting marriages do not vote this way, because they have no wish to share their husband's provision with the state.

- The less a woman succeeds in marriage, the more she backs redistribution.
- Casual dating and non-commitment act as socialism for less desired women.
- The state can replace a husband's provision.
- It cannot replace a wife.
- So only women's suffrage creates this imbalance between the sexes.

## The agreeable voter

Women score higher than men on agreeableness, a trait that includes a fear of going against the perceived consensus. So on average they vote with whatever the dominant institutions teach. Decades ago, when those institutions were conservative, women voted more conservatively than men. Now that the institutions are liberal, women vote more liberally, and a bloc that follows the institutions protects them from scrutiny.

## The household

In a family that holds together, husband and wife vote the same way, so the second vote changes little. Universal suffrage lets parties appeal to individuals where before they appealed to families. A platform that suits single women more than men gains from policies that delay marriage or set wives against their husbands. The female vote consistently backs the policies behind today's results.

- Record-low family formation.
- Record government spending.
- Lonelier men and women.
- Entrenched institutions.
