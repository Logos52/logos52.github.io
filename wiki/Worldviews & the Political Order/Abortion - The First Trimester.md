# Abortion - The First Trimester

A stop after the first trimester is a different position from a ban at conception. Most arguments in circulation are built for one of those two poles, and they miss the line.

The inventory below is the common defense of permission. Some of it is aimed at a total ban and does not touch a later stop. Some of it is aimed at erasing any stop. One exception survives on any line.

## What the later-stop does not need

A first-trimester line does not require a theory that moral worth begins at a heartbeat, at viability, or at neurological activity. Those are competing lines. Picking one of them is a different page. This one picks twelve weeks and leaves the metaphysics of week one where they are.

It also does not require treating a total ban as the only honest prohibition. A partial stop is a stop. Calling it a legislated remainder is a complaint from the camp that will accept nothing short of conception. The complaint names a fact about the line. It does not show the line is empty.

## Defenses that erase every line

**Fine up until birth.** Birth does not invent a morally relevant difference that was absent a minute earlier. The claim is not a line. It is a refusal of one.

**My body, my choice.** From conception there is a second organism, genetically distinct. A slogan about one body does not reach two. Aimed at a total ban it still has to answer the second body. Aimed at a later stop it answers even less: the later the act, the less the second party can be treated as an organ.

**A clump of cells.** So is every organism. The phrase subtracts the developmental fact without replacing it. It licenses no particular week and forbids no particular week.

**Consent to sex is not consent to pregnancy.** Every voluntary act carries risks that are not separately signed. The cut does not establish a right to end a third party. It also does not tell you where a line should sit.

**Men should not have a say.** The sex of the speaker is not a property of the argument. The claim is an attack on who is talking.

**If you ban it, people will do it illegally.** Theft and fraud stay illegal for the same reason. The prediction is not a reason to have no line. It is a reason to ask what a line can actually do.

## Defenses that pick a different line

**Heartbeat. Viability. Neurological activity.** Each of these is a proposed stop, not a case against having one. Heartbeat treats an operational organ as the start of worth, which fails the cardiac-arrest test for anyone already born. Viability treats dependence as the test, which fails for newborns. Neurological activity is the strongest of the three, because brain death is already used as the end of a person — but a trajectory toward consciousness is not the same fact as a loss of it, and the moment of onset is not clean. None of these is an argument that the first trimester is the wrong stop. They are arguments that another stop is better. They do not carry themselves.

## Defenses that treat the child as a cost

**Disability. Equality. Overpopulation.** Each makes the act a tool for a social goal. A disabled child, a career, a population target. The goal may be real. The tool is the ending of the pregnancy. A line after twelve weeks still refuses those as reasons for a later abortion. It does not need the source's eugenics verdict to do that work. Instrumental justifications do not pick a week. They pick an outcome.

## What about rape

The case removes the woman's agency in the conception. It is also the case most often used to carry a much wider permission. Those are different jobs. A traumatic conception does not make the later child the offender. It also does not force a conception-ban on anyone who draws a first-trimester line. The exception, if it exists, is a ruling about early abortion after a crime. It is not a ruling that there is no line.

## Medical necessity

This one holds on any line. Where the mother's life is in immediate danger and the child's death is the side effect of saving her, the intent is not the ending. The phrase gets stretched to cover what is not immediate. The stretch is the abuse. The narrow case is not.

## What the inventory comes to

The defenses that erase every line fail against a later stop the same way they fail against an earlier one. The defenses that nominate a different week are a debate among lines, not a case that twelve weeks is empty. Instrumental reasons do not pick a week. Rape is a possible early exception and not a solvent. Medical necessity survives.

What remains is the line itself. After the first trimester the act is the one being stopped. A ban from conception is a different prohibition, and it is not this page.

## Open questions

If the prohibition is on the act after twelve weeks, who is the law pointed at — the provider, the woman, or both?

A first-trimester line is a count of weeks. What would move the count, and what would show the count had been wrong?
