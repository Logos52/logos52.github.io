# The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association

Freedom of association is the right to choose whom you keep company and do business with, free from coercion. The Basic Logic video [An Honest Review of Civil Rights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iw8YDdx8mhY) argues that the Civil Rights Act of 1964 was right to end Jim Crow laws, which forced private owners to segregate. It says the Act was wrong to replace them with laws forcing owners to integrate. The video calls that the same violation of liberty in reverse and gives the Act one out of 10.

## Title II and business owners

The video says economists have long argued the market would end segregation by itself, because an owner who turns customers away for no reason loses business to a rival who does not. Defenders of the Act reply that racist business practices still dominated large parts of America even without Jim Crow. The video says both are right, and resolves the puzzle by asking whether the owner might not be irrational at all. Suppose a group were on average more likely to refuse to pay, steal or harass staff. Then the honest customers from that group might not be worth the extra risk and security cost, and the video says that would explain the situation perfectly.

The video also takes up the objection that the owner only refused because his other customers were racist. Its answer is to ask the same question one step further back, namely why those customers were racist, and whether they were irrational too.

## Title VII and jobs

Title VII banned discrimination in hiring, and the video says it was worse than Title II because discrimination in hiring is hard to prove. An employer could always say a candidate fell short in some way. The video says the answer that came later was disparate outcomes, where a court compares identity groups and treats any gap as proof of discrimination, which the video calls a legal mandate for blank slate theory.

The video's reasoning about consequences runs through the enforcement system. In the video's account, a person could report a company to the EEOC, which would investigate and demand compensation, and companies usually just paid. If a company refused, the EEOC could sue, and the only fair way to prove innocence was equal representation across protected groups. That, the video says, is how race quotas and affirmative action arose, and why they spread into modern DEI as protected groups grew to include gender and orientation. It also notes the paying-out gave people a financial incentive to cry racism with no consequences.

## Schools under Titles IV and VI

The video says Title IV focused on desegregating public schools, building on Brown v. Board of Education from 10 years before. The video says Title VI pressured private schools by withholding funding, and that a 1976 Supreme Court decision effectively extended Title IV to all education. The video says many parents turned to homeschooling or local community schools, and that the official explanation was racism. The video suggests some parents instead feared delinquency and lower school quality, and says the federal government answered with armed troops and threats of fines, jail or loss of custody.

## The video's verdict

The video's closing argument is that a group needing the threat of violence to make others associate with it may be the problem, since people who offer value attract association without force. It scores the Act one out of 10, saying it caused the racism it set out to end and pointing to black family data from before and after the 1960s.
