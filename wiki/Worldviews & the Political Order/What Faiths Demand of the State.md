# What Faiths Demand of the State

# What Faiths Demand of the State

A religion that teaches how people should act ends up with positions on law, because law is where conduct is enforced. Christianity and Islam both make demands of the state, and those demands reach people outside the faith. Each demand can be set out and judged from outside without first settling whether the faith is true.

## Core takeaways

- Christianity requires opposing abortion as the killing of a human being.
- Obedience to rulers is conditional, and tyranny can be lawfully resisted.
- Charity is for those unable to work, which the state does badly.
- Foreigners present get equal law, and nations belong to their people.
- No passage commands democracy, which is a tool to judge by results.
- A nation needs one religion to hold its people together.
- Islam's own rule makes its later verses of war override earlier peace.

## Six Christian demands

Christian morality maps onto the main political divides of the day. A Christian owes no politician or party unconditional support, and what the faith asks is harder than party loyalty. The six demands cover life, resistance, welfare, immigration, sexuality and the form of government. Life, welfare, immigration and sexuality are set out here, and resistance and the form of government have the two sections that follow.

- Abortion: about 2.5 billion in 50 years, and the number is rising.
- Pro-lifers back any law that cuts abortions.
- Abolitionists refuse partial laws.
- At that scale, drop whatever fails and try whatever works.
- Welfare: "the one who is unwilling to work shall not eat."
- Women able to marry and people with family are not recipients.
- The state buys votes with welfare, so Christians give personally instead.
- Immigration: the Bible names nations by descent, the Canaanites from Canaan.
- It warns against Israel importing foreign cultures.
- Homosexuality: the old civil penalty bound ancient Israel only.
- The moral verdict still binds, and the state now promotes the conduct.

## Just war and resistance

The command to give Caesar what is Caesar's and God what is God's divides authority, so obedience to the state has limits. Augustine set three conditions for a just war: a valid cause, a right intention aimed at lasting peace, and a legitimate authority. The third condition stops a private person from starting a war of his own. Thomas Aquinas answered that condition for the case of tyranny: a tyrant's rule is violence with no standing as law, so resisting it is not sedition, and legitimate authority passes to lesser officials who keep to God's law and to the community as a body.

- Valid cause: self-defence, recovering what was taken, correcting a grave wrong.
- The overthrow must not cause a greater evil than the tyranny.
- Every legal channel must already have failed.

## One people, one faith

Christian nationalism comes in two forms. The newer, cultural form makes every professing Christian a countryman, and it breaks down fast: an English atheist teenager stops being English, a devout Nigerian becomes English, and every Christian nation merges into one. In the older form a nation is its people, joined by family ties, and the people and their rulers must be Christian. Under the older form a countryman who fights the faith becomes a traitor and stays a countryman.

- The United States is an empire ruling several nations.
- Nations inside it move, merge and separate over time.
- Shared descent alone does not unite a people.
- Unity comes from common values, and values come from a religion.
- A people split among competing faiths cannot keep one order.
- Every state carries some religion, admitted or not.
- What remains is which religion, and the answer is the true one.

## What Islam asks

The claim that Islam is a religion of peace rests on early verses such as Surah 5:32, which says that killing one man is killing all humanity. The tradition's own rule for verses that conflict, called abrogation, says that a later revelation overrides an earlier one, and every Muslim sect accepts the rule. The later verses from Medina, such as Surah 9:5 and 9:29, command war on unbelievers and tribute from Jews and Christians. A state that takes the text as law takes the later position.

- Peace was preached while the movement was small and weak.
- The message changed once the movement had an army.
- Rapid conquest as proof of God's backing fails: Christians later reversed it.
- Islam improved women's condition over pre-Islamic Arabia.
- Against a fixed moral standard, it permits wife-beating, several wives and concubines.
- It gives women lesser standing in divorce, law and testimony.
