# What Faiths Demand of the State

A Christian owes no automatic loyalty to any politician, party or nation that appeals to his faith, so anyone who makes that appeal is simply manipulating him. What the faith demands is far greater, because it sets the standard by which every law and every ruler is judged. A government is acceptable only if it actually is what it claims to be and is Christian.

Authority comes from God, so a law that sets itself against God loses its force, and abortion tests that limit hardest. Rebellion against a tyrant can be just when lesser officials committed to God's law lead it, when it causes no greater evil than the tyranny and when every legal channel has failed. Charity belongs to the church, and Christians should oppose current state welfare because giving to the able robs the truly desperate. A nation is its people, held together by shared religious values, and since no passage commands democracy, a Christian only has to judge whether it suits his own society.

## Authority and its limits

"Give unto Caesar what is Caesar's, and give unto God what is God's" is the standard for how a believer treats the state. The authority behind the rule of law is granted by God, which means a law that sets itself against God loses its claim. When obeying both becomes impossible, a Christian must choose God.

Abortion tests that limit hardest, because it kills a human being. It has happened 2.5 billion times worldwide over 50 years. Christians agree on the wrong and differ only on strategy. A pro-lifer backs any law that reduces abortions, while an abolitionist accepts nothing short of ending it.

The question that follows is when a believer may resist. A just war needs a valid cause, a right intention aimed at lasting peace, and a legitimate authority. Rebellion against a tyrant can still meet that last test, because "Tyranny is not law, but rather a species of violence." Legitimacy then passes to lesser officials committed to God's law and to the community as a whole. Even then the overthrow must not cause a greater evil than the tyranny, and every legal channel must already have failed.


## Charity belongs to the church

God's standard also decides who should run charity, and the answer is the church. Christian charity goes to those who cannot provide for themselves, since "the one who is unwilling to work shall not eat." Giving to the able rewards parasitism and robs the truly desperate, because resources are limited. The modern welfare state does exactly that, and it has proven unable to manage charity while staying prone to corruption. Christians should therefore oppose current state welfare and give personally and as a community, so that welfare and education fall to the church.

## Nation, law and system of rule

A faith also shapes what a nation is. Through most of Christendom's history a nation was its people, and the people and their rulers had to be Christian. Blood alone cannot hold such a nation together, because all nations need the unity that shared religious values give, and those values underpin law and culture. Separation of church and state, read as laws detached from the nation's values, only clears the way for secular religions such as Marxism.

The same view of a nation as its people shapes immigration. Scripture asks believers to treat sojourners as equals under the law, which is a command to fairness and gives no warrant for limitless immigration.

On the system of rule, no passage commands democracy. Democracy is a tool that works for some societies and fails for others, so a Christian's only duty is to judge wisely whether it suits his own. Democracy, monarchy and aristocracy can each work in the right conditions.

## Open questions

- How does a believer recognise the point where a ruler has crossed the line?
- Why does the abolitionist reject the strategy the pro-lifer accepts?
- How does charity given by a church shape the people who receive it differently from welfare given by a state?
