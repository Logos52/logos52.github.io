# The Grooming Gang Cover-Up

The grooming gang cover-up is the claim that British institutions knew about organized sexual abuse of young white girls for decades and chose not to act. The Basic Logic video [Why Did The UK Cover Up The Grooming Gangs?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ML76HE86wfk) reviews Rupert Lowe's independent gang inquiry report, which the video says cites a conservative minimum of 250,000 victims. The video argues that fear of being called racist outweighed fear for the country's children, and it ends by calling remigration Britain's last moderate option.

## Where the 250,000 figure comes from

The video admits that 250,000 is not a count of confirmed cases. It is an extrapolation, first made in 2018 to 2019, that scales detailed local inquiries such as Rotherham's 1,400 victims to the whole country. The scaling allows for underreporting and the spread across dozens of towns, and it is checked against witness and victim testimony. The video expects people to dismiss the figure because of the word extrapolation, and its defense is that this is the standard way to estimate victims of large crimes when cover-ups make full records impossible. Anyone who rejects it on method alone, it says, would have to reject other recognized atrocities estimated the same way.

## Who was targeted

The video rejects the view that these were random individual crimes. Its evidence is that several victims testified they were picked because they were white and Christian, and that offenders justified the abuse by saying such girls had less moral value. It adds a population comparison. The video says Pakistani Muslims are about 2.3% of the UK population, half of them women and a third under age, yet form the overwhelming majority of perpetrators, as confirmed, it says, by official inquiries.

## What the state did

According to the video, victims say some police officers were among the abusers. It says that when scandals broke in Rotherham in 2010 and Oxford in 2013, the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) dismissed or played them down, which is why most of what is known comes from private efforts like Lowe's.

The video focuses on Keir Starmer, who ran the CPS at the time and is now Prime Minister. It says he was told of 13,000 cases and responded with child abduction warning notices. Such a notice, the video says, contacts the suspect and warns him to stop, which the video calls a lighter outcome than a speeding fine. The video says he later admitted the CPS failed to take the allegations seriously. Its explanation for the choice is offered as the narrator's belief: that prosecutions would have put the abuse in the public eye and made calls for remigration louder, which threatened Starmer's ideology.

## The conclusion it draws

The video argues Europe made a mistake with mass immigration and has not corrected it because, as the inquiry repeatedly shows, the terror of being called racist was greater than fear for the children. It calls remigration the last moderate option. The video closes with victim testimony, which this page leaves out.
