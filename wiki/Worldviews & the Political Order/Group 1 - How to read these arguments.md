# How to read these arguments

The two pages on reasoning tools come first in the section's reading order, and both give tools for checking an argument before reading the pages that make one.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Validity and Truth|Validity and Truth]] reports the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's rule that an argument can be valid without being sound. The encyclopedia calls an argument valid when its form rules out true premises alongside a false conclusion, and sound only when it is valid and all its premises are true. The page gathers three tools for checking an argument.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Per Capita|Per Capita]] explains that per capita means "per person", the division that turns a raw count into a rate. The Basic Logic video it covers names three reasons people leave the rate out: the sum is hard for them, bias makes the raw count seem enough, or other variables still need controlling for. It counts only the third as a fair objection. The page also notes the limit of the measure, which tells you how common something is and not why it happens.
