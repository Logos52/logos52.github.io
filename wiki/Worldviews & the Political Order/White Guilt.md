# White Guilt

# White Guilt

White guilt is the claim that people of European descent alive today carry a debt for their history and for a system that still favours them. Six arguments are usually made for it: the slave trade, colonialism, the killing of native peoples, systemic oppression, inherent white racism, and the Holocaust. Each one needs European conduct to have been uniquely bad, none of them shows it, and a true charge would still not pass to people born later.

- Guilt belongs to the person who acted, and it cannot be inherited.
- Slavery, conquest and mass killing were practised almost everywhere.
- Europe was the first civilisation to call slavery evil and end it.
- Europe gave back the territory it conquered.
- No current law in Western countries favours whites.
- White people show the lowest preference for their own group.

## The structure

A debt owed by one people alone requires that its conduct was unique. A practice common to every society cannot single one people out. Each of the six arguments fails that test, and several turn around, because the part that was unique to Europe runs against the charge. Separately, a debt for an act can fall only on the person who did it.

| Argument | What was universal | What was European |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slave trade | Slavery | Abolition |
| Colonialism | Conquest | Giving it back |
| Wiping out natives | Killing in war | Intermarriage |
| The Holocaust | Mass killing | Accountability |

## The historical charges

The Atlantic slave trade was not the largest in history, or even the largest of its own time. Europeans bought people already enslaved by African kings, and then became the first civilisation to declare slavery a moral evil, abolish it at home and suppress it abroad at their own cost. Every society tried to conquer its neighbours, and Europe differed only in succeeding on a world scale and then handing the territory back. The governments that took over often failed to keep up the roads, power and water they inherited.

- In Latin America, native and European populations mixed into today's Hispanics.
- About 500 conquistadors toppled the Aztecs with local allies.
- Most native deaths came from disease.
- Europeans committed specific atrocities, and none was unique to Europe.

## The present-day charges

The systemic oppression argument says Western societies are built to exploit non-whites for the benefit of whites. Nobody who makes it can name a current law that advantages whites over non-whites. Immigrants from every continent work hard to move into these societies, and people do not work to reach a place that oppresses them. The claim that whites are inherently racist runs against the studies of in-group preference, in which white people show the lowest preference for their own group of any population.

- The Holocaust argument assigns collective racial guilt for one atrocity.
- Most of its victims were white Europeans.
- It was not the largest mass killing of its own time.
- Genghis Khan's conquests killed 10 to 20% of the world's population.
- Europe built institutions to prevent a repeat.
- Other peoples deny their crimes.
- These charges are built to demoralise a high-trust, guilt-based culture.
