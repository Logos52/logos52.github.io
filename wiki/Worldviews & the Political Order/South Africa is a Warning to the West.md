# South Africa is a Warning to the West

The Moon video [South Africa Is a Warning To The World](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YKGGUexEhUw) argues that South Africa went from Africa's strongest economy to a place of crime and blackouts in about 20 years. Its explanation is that the rulers captured the state and drained it. When public services failed, the rich bought private replacements, and the video says the West is starting down the same path more slowly. Its closing line is that the West "just has to keep on following in its footsteps, one failure at a time."

## How the state was captured

The video dates the turn to 2009, when Jacob Zuma took power. According to the video, the ANC had run the country for years, and a party committee decided who ran the institutions, so Zuma's first step was to give the top jobs to his friends. The video says the best-known of them were the Guptas, three Indian-born brothers who built a business empire inside the state. The video says a judicial inquiry that began after the ANC forced Zuma out in 2018 put the total at $65 billion and gave the practice a name, state capture.

The power company shows the choice the video says Zuma made. Eskom used to export cheap power across the region. When the country needed more electricity, Zuma could build power stations, which the video calls complicated and expensive. The other option was to turn the company into a cash cow. The video says he took the easier option. The video says Eskom paid 80,000 rand, about $4,500, for one knee pad, and that someone tried to kill a new CEO with cyanide after he brought in investigators. South Africans now live with load shedding, planned power cuts that can last 2 hours, 6 hours or even 12 hours a day.

The video says the railways went the same way. The commuter network carried 600,000 people a day in 2008 and fell to just under 14,000, because gangs kept stealing cables and signaling parts and no one stopped them.

## Who pays and who escapes

The video describes a split where the rich opt out of failing services. The video says the police are underfunded, citing an area (Usuthu) of over a million people with a single working rapid response vehicle, so private guards filled the gap. A news clip in the video gives more than 2.7 million registered private security officers against fewer than 150,000 police for 62 million people. The video's summary is that the rich no longer need trains, police, the grid or public space, because they have cars, guards, generators and walls.

The video says the people at the top faced little cost. The video says Zuma was jailed and released after two months, and that his imprisonment set off riots in which more than 350 people died. It adds that two Gupta brothers escaped to Dubai and were never sent back. McKinsey, which the video says joined a bribery scheme to win Eskom and rail contracts, made around $85 million and later paid more than $122 million to settle a US criminal investigation.

## Dividing the public

The video says the elites used divide and conquer to manage public anger. After apartheid, when the economy was almost entirely white-owned, the government set out to change that, and the video accepts the aim but says it created some 145 racial laws. Because the racial mix varies so much by place, the video argues, the formula even discriminates against black South Africans whose local area does not match the national figures. The video says around 1 million people have emigrated, many of them doctors, engineers and other taxpayers. It says cheap labor from Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Malawi and Lesotho filled the gap and puts estimates of the number of foreigners at 4 to 14 million, and some natives have answered with threats and violence.

## Parallels in the West

The video's examples of the same pattern in the West are:

1. Visible decay of streets and buildings, which it ties to someone saving on repairs or taking a bribe.
2. US healthcare, where calling an ambulance is a financial gamble, and the UK's NHS, where people pay to go private to be seen in time.
3. Police failure, with 14% of UK people trusting police to investigate a minor crime and 72% of burglary cases closed without anyone identified, answered by private cameras and private police.
4. Equity over merit, as when the Cheshire police declared all 127 successful candidates equal so it could favor protected groups, and a tribunal found a white, male, heterosexual applicant had been directly discriminated against.
