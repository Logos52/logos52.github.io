# South Africa is a Warning to the West

South Africa after apartheid had the strongest economy in Africa, world-class companies, precious metals and gems, and safe streets. Within about a decade of 2009 its power grid, railways, police and inner cities had failed, and its murder rate reached about ten times America's. Britain and the United States show the same steps at an earlier stage.

- A ruling party placed loyalists at the top of every institution.
- A connected family ran a business empire from inside the state.
- About $65 billion was taken, a process named state capture.
- The rich bought private services and stopped needing the state.
- About a million skilled South Africans emigrated.
- Rhodesia fell the same way a generation earlier.
- Western countries show the same failures in milder form.

## How it failed

Jacob Zuma took power in 2009 under the one-party rule of the ANC, the African National Congress. The party committee placed allies at the head of state institutions, and the Gupta brothers, a business family with ties to Zuma, built a business empire inside the state. The first public sign was the lights going out. Eskom, the state power company, had once exported cheap electricity, and Zuma's circle treated it as a source of cash instead of building new plants.

- Planned power cuts ran 2 to 12 hours a day.
- Eskom paid about 80,000 rand, around $4,500, for one knee pad.
- A new Eskom chief who brought in investigators was poisoned with cyanide.
- Daily rail riders fell from about 600,000 in 2008 to under 14,000.
- Gangs stole the cable and signals, and nobody stopped them.
- Zuma flew about 200 Gupta wedding guests into a military base.
- In 2015 he fired a respected finance minister, and the currency crashed.

## The money

The ANC forced Zuma out in 2018, and a judicial inquiry afterwards put the total taken at about $65 billion. The name given to it, state capture, describes a system in which corruption has become legal. Foreign firms took part, and almost nobody was punished.

- McKinsey made about $85 million from bribes for power and rail contracts.
- McKinsey later paid over $122 million in a US criminal settlement.
- Zuma was jailed and released after about two months.
- Riots over his jailing killed more than 350 people.
- Two Gupta brothers fled to Dubai, and extradition failed.
- Robbers found about $580,000 hidden in the new president's furniture.
- He said it was payment for 20 buffalo.

## What replaced the state

Those who could afford it bought their way out: cars instead of trains, guards instead of police, generators instead of the grid, walls instead of public space. The poor were left with the failing public services. Johannesburg's inner city shows the result, and a Johannesburg tower once called Africa's Manhattan held a pile of rubbish nearly 13 storeys high.

- About 2.7 million registered private guards, against under 150,000 police.
- For about 62 million people, private guards outnumber army and police combined.
- Nelson Mandela Bay, about a million people, had one working rapid-response vehicle.
- A restoration of the tower found 23 bodies in the rubbish.
- Hundreds of buildings have been taken over by gangs.
- Six South African cities rank among the world's 50 most violent.
- Machete mobs, mass shootings, armed robbery and kidnapping are routine.

## Race law and migration

After apartheid South Africa passed about 145 laws that classify people by race, and the country stayed permanently divided by them. White pensioners on farms are attacked, and armed vigilantes often reach them before the police do. The national racial hiring formula can also discriminate against black South Africans where the local population differs from the national one.

- About a million people left: doctors, engineers and taxpaying business owners.
- Cheap labour from Zimbabwe, Mozambique, Malawi and Lesotho filled the gaps.
- Estimates of foreigners in the country range from 4 to 14 million.
- South Africans answered with hospital ID checks and burn-the-foreigner songs.

## Rhodesia first

Before British settlers arrived, the tribes of what is now Zimbabwe fought constant wars, and families had as many children as possible so that two might survive. Settlers negotiated mining rights and founded Rhodesia, which stopped the tribal wars and became the breadbasket of Africa. Only literate property owners could vote, because democracy needs institutions and trust built first, and the government invested heavily in educating the native population. Children now survived, the population exploded, and welfare paid for almost entirely by the small white population could not keep up.

- A third of Rhodesia's black population came from neighbouring states.
- Two Marxist terrorist groups rose: ZIPRA under Nkomo, ZANLA under Mugabe.
- They targeted women, children, missionaries and Red Cross workers.
- Most of the Rhodesian army was black.
- Rhodesia won every major battle and lost to sanctions and foreign funding.
- Mugabe took power in 1979 and carried out genocide against Nkomo's tribe.
- His loyalists took the farms, could not run them, and people starved.
- Black Zimbabweans now say life was better under Rhodesia.
- Zimbabwe offers whites reparations for the atrocities, hoping they return.

## The warning

South Africa shows Western decline at full speed. Britain and the United States already have the potholes, rising taxes, parks turned into offices, ambulance bills and hospital waits that push people into private care. As trust in the police falls, a market grows to replace them, and taxpayers pay twice, once for the public service and again for the private one. The end state:

```
wealthy      -> behind walls
middle class -> watching cameras
poor         -> keeping their own order
elites       -> in bunkers
```

- British police close 72% of burglaries without identifying anyone.
- Only 14% of Britons trust the police to handle petty crime.
- American confidence in police collapsed after 2020.
- Ring, Flock, Palantir and the Citizen app sell private replacements.
- Cheshire police declared 127 candidates of equal merit to favour protected groups.
- Matthew Furlong, a white heterosexual man, won his discrimination case.
- Silicon Valley billionaires are building bunkers.
