# Racial Fatigue

The Basic Logic video [This YouTuber Gave Me Black Fatigue](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7TSJkkY-D5c) describes "black fatigue" as tiredness from a culture it sees as hostile and hypocritical, more than from any crime done to the people who feel it. This page files it as racial fatigue. The video is a reply to a commentator who called its earlier video racist, and it argues that he ended up agreeing with every one of its claims. In the end the video puts the cause somewhere other than race, saying "it's not black that we're fatigued by. It's societal fatherlessness."

## The three claims

The earlier video made three claims, which the reply restates:

1. The murder rate among black Americans is very high, which the video calls simply a fact.
2. Fatherlessness contributes to that murder rate.
3. Welfare that paid out only when no father was in the home encouraged fatherlessness.

The reply says the commentator accepted the second and third, which is why it asks what he actually debunked.

## How the figures are argued

The video's case uses time comparisons rather than comparisons with other groups, because it wants to compare modern black America with black America before civil rights, a population it says suffered far more discrimination. Black male homicide victimization, which it uses as a stand-in for offending, was about 42 per 100,000 around 1960 and nearly doubled by 1970. It gives today's rate as about 50 per 100,000. On family structure, it says about 78% of black children lived with both parents before civil rights, against fewer than half today, and 70 to 78% are now born to unmarried mothers.

The video ties the change to the war on poverty. After civil rights, it says, Democrats courted the growing black vote with federal welfare that depended largely on whether a father was present, so the state in effect paid mothers to leave their husbands. It adds that the scheme was justified to other Americans by saying historical injustice caused the poverty, so fixing it was white people's job.

## The statistical errors it points out

Several of the video's corrections are about method, and each comes with a worked example:

- The commentator said white people are the biggest users of EBT. The video replies that white people are the majority, and in proportion black people are three times as likely to be on SNAP.
- The commentator called the "13% of the population, 50% of crime" figure arrest-only, then a minute later cited a figure that 55% of 13,000 murder victims were killed by black people.
- He divided 7,660 black murders a year by 22 million black men to get 0.035%. The video says that is a yearly rate, and over an average 72-year life it comes to about 2.52%. It adds that even this ignores age brackets and repeat offenders, and that its own figures use the standard per-100,000 format.

## Why the video calls it fatigue

The video's reasoning is that the commentator says he hates the claim that black people call everything racist, then says everything, including suburbs and tipping, is rooted in racism. It calls this an unfalsifiable, all-explaining ghost. It also says the commentator painted the narrator as an outsider with no say, then cut the ending of the original video, which credits the argument to Thomas Sowell, a black American. The video concludes that the welfare that both sides agree is harmful could simply be abolished, and says the commentator will not accept that because he wants the payments without the condition.
