# Single-Sex Spaces - The Asymmetry

Over about 40 years, American courts and laws opened almost every men-only club, lodge and school to women, while women-only colleges, clubs and gyms kept legal protection and new ones were funded. The men who lost those places now make up most of the country's suicides, overdoses and loneliness figures. The same anti-discrimination law is applied in one direction.

## Core takeaways

- Men's lodges once enrolled about one in five adult American men.
- A 1984 Supreme Court ruling ended the legal all-male civic club.
- Women's colleges, clubs and gyms stay legal under exemptions.
- No exemption allows a men-only equivalent.
- Men's close friendships halved between 1990 and 2021.
- About 39,000 American men killed themselves in 2023.
- New men's groups work, at a tiny fraction of the old scale.

## What men had

Before Social Security in 1935 and Medicare in 1965, fraternal orders were how working men insured themselves. A lodge paid rent when a member was injured, paid for his funeral and a widow's allowance, and ran orphanages. In 1897 about 5.4 million American men, one in five adults, belonged to a secret fraternal order, and the Freemasons alone had about 4 million members at their 1950s peak. Black men, shut out of white orders and insurers, built their own, starting with Prince Hall Freemasonry in 1784.

- Orders included the Masons, Odd Fellows, Elks, Moose and Knights of Columbus.
- Some survive as insurance companies, such as Modern Woodmen.
- Rotary, Lions, unions, churches and corner bars filled the same role.
- Their decline caused about half the fall in civic life after 1960.

## How they were opened

The decisive case came in 1984. Two Minnesota chapters of the Jaycees, a young men's civic club with about 235,000 members, admitted women, and when the national body moved against them they sued under a state law on public accommodations. The Supreme Court ruled 7 to 0 that only intimate groups such as families, and expressive groups such as parties and churches, keep strong protection for choosing their members, and the Jaycees were neither. Every large men's club was open to the same suit from then on.

- 1987: Rotary's men-only rule fell, 7 to 0.
- 1988: a New York City law opened private clubs over 400 members.
- 1990: New Jersey's top court opened Princeton's eating clubs.
- 1996: Virginia Military Institute ordered to admit women, 7 to 1.
- 2012: Augusta National's first two women members, after a decade of pressure.
- 2024: the Boy Scouts finished renaming themselves as a mixed organisation.
- The Girl Scouts sued over the name and stayed girls-only.

## What women kept and built

In 1960 America had more than 250 women's colleges, and about 30 remain women-only, including most of the Seven Sisters, the old elite women's colleges of the Northeast. Three all-male four-year colleges remain. New women-only businesses raised large sums: Chief, an executive club charging $5,800 to $8,900 a year, reached a $1.1 billion valuation in 2022 with about 20,000 members. Women-only gyms operate under state exemptions in Massachusetts, New York and Connecticut, and no state has an exemption for a men-only gym.

- The Wing, a women's coworking club, was made to admit men.
- Such cases are rare.

## The stated reasons

The law protects women-only spaces on three grounds. Privacy covers locker rooms, changing rooms and restrooms, and it applies to men in the same settings. Safety covers shelters, since women are 70 to 80% of domestic violence victims. Religion covers Muslim and Orthodox Jewish women who cannot exercise with men.

- None of the three covers a billion-dollar executive club.
- None covers a coworking space for networking.
- That is the kind of club the Jaycees ruling took from men.
- A 2025 UK Supreme Court ruling defined woman by biological sex.
- The US has made no equivalent ruling.

## The men in the gap

The loss of male institutions landed on men already falling behind in school, work and family life. Men earned 57% of bachelor's degrees in 1970 and 42% in 2021. Real median male wages fell 7.6% between 1979 and 2012 while productivity rose nearly 70%. In 2022, 63% of men aged 18 to 29 were single, against 34% of women.

- Men with six or more close friends: 55% in 1990, 27% in 2021.
- Men with no close friend rose from 3% to 15%.
- Loneliness carries the death risk of 15 cigarettes a day.
- Suicides: almost 39,000 men in 2023, about 80% of the total.
- Male overdose deaths: 32.2 per 100,000, against 14.1 for women.
- Boys trail girls by about a grade level in reading.
- 145 boys are held back in kindergarten for every 100 girls.
- Men were 30% of school teachers in 1988 and 23% in 2021.
- Young men swung about 16 points toward Trump in 2024.
- South Korea shows the same split and a fertility rate near 0.72.

## What is being rebuilt

Some men's groups have grown again by building around a shared activity. Men's sheds, started in Australia in the early 1990s, give older and isolated men a workshop to build things in and talk while they work. F3 Nation, started in Charlotte in 2011, runs free dawn workouts for men in parks. Both are local, volunteer-run and repeat every week, like the old lodges.

- About 3,000 men's sheds across 12 countries by 2026.
- Regulars show lower loneliness and depression scores.
- F3 had over 50,000 men at over 3,500 sites in 2024.
- The Masons alone once had about 4 million members.
- The groups that survive are presented as health, fatherhood, faith or sport.
