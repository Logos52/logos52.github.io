# The racism charge and Western guilt


The guilt group asks what the charge of racism does in an argument and whether European history carries a special guilt. All four pages here report one channel's argument (Basic Logic), and no opposing source is included.

## Who Gets Called Racist

# Who Gets Called Racist Three videos by [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYIITZs0C8A) argue that people use the word "racist" to end an argument rather than answer it, and work out what follows from that. The first says judging by group averages is a tool that is sometimes the only option when information is short. The second says the right response to the accusation is to ask whether you are right. The third turns the charge around and asks why the group it says is quickest to use it scores highest on in-group preference, as the studies it cites claim.

## Moral Language as Leverage

# Moral Language as Leverage The Basic Logic video [Why Did the Third World Learn to Cry Racism?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Tg_gnb7KJo) argues that words like "racism" and "discrimination" get aimed at the West by people who do not apply them at home, and that they do it because the words reliably win resources and better treatment. It explains this through three ways a society keeps order (guilt, shame and fear) and through the incentive to take what is hard to make. Its conclusion is that asking these people to be consistent achieves nothing, so the only useful response is to stop being moved by the words.

## The Indictment of Europe

# The Indictment of Europe Critics accuse Europe of three historical crimes, which are slavery, colonialism and mass killing. [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yZJoK0Jz7qY) says those charges are used in support of white guilt and of policies like DEI, reparations and open-border immigration. He answers each charge with one test, which is that a people can be singled out for punishment only if what it did was unique. He argues that on every charge Europe did what other civilisations did, and that its only unique act was to end slavery.
