# Socialism as Moral Correction

Socialism is usually argued for as a moral repair: a few people own most things, workers are paid less than they produce, medicine is too expensive, and the economy crashes. Some of those faults are real, and in each case the cause is the state and big business working together. Socialism answers by making the state bigger, which enlarges the cause.

- Arguments about ownership, medicine and crashes name real faults.
- Each of those faults comes from state power joined to wealth.
- Arguments about inequality and exploitation misread people and work.
- Arguments about Nordic success, computers, scarcity and subsidies fail on prices.
- Every socialist state ends up ruled by a small group too.
- The larger problem is a corrupt financial system.

## The real faults

Rich people buy political power, then use the state to shut out competitors and grant themselves monopolies. Supporters of socialism call this late-stage capitalism. The drift is real, and a market where the state hands out monopolies is no longer a free market. American healthcare and education squeeze their customers, and state rules produce that squeeze. Crashes are real, and the big ones followed years of central banks holding interest rates down and printing money.

| Fault | Real cause |
| --- | --- |
| A few own everything | Wealth buys the state, the state grants monopolies |
| Healthcare prices | Price secrecy rules, drug monopolies, licensing |
| Crashes | Cheap central-bank money, then bad investment |

- Rule by a few appears in every society, socialist ones included.
- This pattern is called the iron law of oligarchy.
- Hospitals were not allowed to publish prices in advance.
- Until 2018 pharmacies could be penalised for mentioning cheaper payment options.
- Cheap essential drugs such as insulin carry state-granted monopolies.
- Certificate-of-need laws block new clinics and hospitals.
- The Great Depression followed decades of central-bank tinkering with rates.

## The moral arguments

The inequality argument needs unequal outcomes to be bad and to come from the system. People differ in ability, so no system raises everyone to the top, and equalising means cutting the able down. The exploitation argument says profit is the part of a worker's output the owner keeps. It leaves out that someone supplies the tools, organises the work, finds buyers and carries the risk of loss, and all of that is work.

- A socialist state still ranks people, by closeness to the officials.
- Capable people get safe posts, the rest do menial work.
- A state monopoly makes nepotism more likely.
- Where wages are held down by force, the fault is the force.
- Aligning the interests of high and low performers works better.

## The practical arguments

Supporters point to Denmark, Sweden and Norway. Those are market economies with large welfare systems, and they are also highly homogeneous, bound by shared faith, ethnicity and culture. An economy is a system of incentives, and when everyone's incentives already line up, as in a family, almost any system works. Socialists reject the shared identities that make this possible, and only a free market coordinates groups that compete.

- Computers cannot plan an economy without the information prices carry.
- Only prices reveal what people will pay and accept.
- Redistribution adds no resources, only moves them.
- Socialist states have a long record of shortages.
- Taxing producers to fund consumers creates no wealth.
- Prices rise, or where fixed, production falls.

## What stays

Socialism has been tried many times, and it ends the same way, with bureaucracies living off the productive. The argument over which economic model to use distracts from a financial system that is corrupt, and no model works while that corruption continues. The faults socialists name are worth keeping, and the remedy is less state power granted to the wealthy.

- Concentrated ownership comes from wealth buying the state.
- Extraction in medicine comes from licensed cartels.
- Crashes come from credit set by central banks.
