# Who rules, and on what terms

The power group follows the reasoning tools and asks who holds power in a society and what claim that power rests on.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Democracy as Sacred Cow|Democracy as Sacred Cow]] covers a Basic Logic video that treats democracy as a tool and not as a god. Of the common defenses, the video says only one holds, and only while votes still stand for real strength.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Organized Minority|The Organized Minority]] covers a Basic Logic video arguing that in every complex society a small organized group rules a large unorganized one, because running a society is full-time work that most people hand over to a few. In his account, fast turnover among Europe's ruling families left today's Western elites with skills ordinary people can match, so they treat their own population as the rival to fear and rule through politicians and anonymous finance.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband|Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband]] reports Basic Logic's claim that women in the West vote differently from men on welfare, guns, immigration and abortion. He gives two reasons, which are that many women vote to make the state a substitute husband and that women, being more agreeable on average, follow whatever the dominant institutions believe.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/What Faiths Demand of the State|What Faiths Demand of the State]] covers Basic Logic's argument that a Christian owes no automatic loyalty to any party or nation that invokes his faith, though the faith itself asks far more of him. The page adds that a nation, in this view, is its people bound by shared religious values, and that because no passage of scripture commands democracy, a Christian need only judge whether it suits his own society.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Woke Mind Virus|The Woke Mind Virus]] traces the phrase, which according to Wikipedia was popularized by Elon Musk, who called it "the mortal threat to civilization". It also reports one peer-reviewed study that found a gap in depression between American teenagers of different political beliefs, and the page notes that the authors only suggest a possible reason for it. It is a reference page built on Wikipedia, Musk's own posts and one study, not on a worldview video.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Virtue of a Proportionate Response|The Virtue of a Proportionate Response]] takes its question from a scene in *The West Wing* that asks what is good about matching an attack with one of the same size. The page answers from the law of war, where Protocol I's test weighs civilian harm against military advantage, and from RAND's warning about escalation. Its sources are a television drama and legal and reference texts, not a worldview video, so it may move to another section later.
