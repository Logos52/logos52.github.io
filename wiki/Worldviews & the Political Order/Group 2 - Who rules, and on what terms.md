# Who rules, and on what terms


The power group follows the reasoning tools and asks who holds power in a society and what claim that power rests on.

## Democracy as Sacred Cow

# Democracy as Sacred Cow Democracy is a tool and not a god. Modern societies treat it as an unquestionable good that every people would prosper under and that should be imposed by force wherever it is missing. Like any tool, though, it works for some peoples some of the time, and where it does not fit the job, a better-suited tool should take its place. Most of the common defenses of democracy fail, and the one that holds depends on votes still standing for real strength. The consent defense fails because a democracy is still organized coercion, and the wisdom-of-crowds defense fails because most voters stay ignorant for a rational reason. On its own terms the fairness defense fails too, since one person, one vote is equal without being fair. The defense that holds is that elections replace violent fights for power, because a vote works as a promise of force. That logic breaks once an armed, motivated minority faces an apathetic majority. Democracies are richer because a competent, self-reliant people would prosper anyway, which is also why democracy cannot be imposed from outside.

## The Organized Minority

# The Organized Minority In every complex society, a minority that is organized rules a majority that is not. The reason is specialization, because ruling and running a society is complex full-time work that most people have to hand to a few. That hand-over creates a natural monopoly over authority and resources, so the question of who rules is mostly a question of which minority holds that monopoly and who is trying to take it. The ruling minority cannot let go, because a rival minority would use the same tools against it. The same logic explains revolutions, since a newer elite that has been shut out uses the masses to overthrow an older one. That danger grows when a society trains more people for leadership than it has places to give them. A society holds together when it can absorb that trained class, as the Caribbean did. Today's Western elites are exposed because their skills are within reach of ordinary people, so they rule from behind politicians and anonymous finance.

## Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband

# Women's Suffrage - The Substitute Husband Women in the West vote differently from men, and the gap comes from two habits. Many women vote to make the state a substitute husband that protects and provides for them, and many follow whatever the dominant institutions believe. At best, then, women's suffrage adds little that is needed, and at worst it gives parties a reason to undermine the family. Against men, women show swings of 8 to 20 percentage points on welfare, guns, immigration and abortion, and among the young the gap has grown from six percentage points in 2000 to 30 percentage points. The state takes over the husband's job of protecting and providing, which leaves men with little but their looks and splits married women from unmarried ones. Women also back whatever the institutions currently teach, because agreeableness brings a fear of going against the consensus. Once votes belong to individuals, a party that appeals to single women has a reason to delay family formation or to set wives against husbands.

## What Faiths Demand of the State

# What Faiths Demand of the State A Christian owes no automatic loyalty to any politician, party or nation that appeals to his faith, so anyone who makes that appeal is simply manipulating him. What the faith demands is far greater, because it sets the standard by which every law and every ruler is judged. A government is acceptable only if it truly matches what it claims to be, and is Christian. Authority comes from God, so a law that sets itself against God loses its force, and abortion tests that limit hardest. Rebellion against a tyrant can be just when lesser officials committed to God's law lead it, when it causes no greater evil than the tyranny and when every legal channel has failed. Charity belongs to the church, and Christians should oppose current state welfare because giving to the able robs the truly desperate. A nation is its people, held together by shared religious values, and since no passage commands democracy, a Christian only has to judge whether it suits his own society.

## The Woke Mind Virus

# The Woke Mind Virus The woke mind virus is a phrase, popularized by Elon Musk, for "woke" ideas treated as an existential threat to American society. The phrase often travels with a claim that the left and right differ in mental health. A large study of American teenagers found that depressive symptoms rose for everyone after 2010 but fastest among liberal girls. The phrase belongs to a wider attack on "woke" ideas from the alt-right, the religious right, moderate liberals and libertarians, and Musk called it "the mortal threat to civilization." A study of 86,138 American 12th-grade students from 2005 to 2018 found that the shares identifying as liberal and conservative barely moved. Depressive symptoms rose for every group after 2010, but liberal girls whose parents had less education scored highest of all. Rising exposure to politicized events may have contributed, which remains possible and not proven.

## The Virtue of a Proportionate Response

# The Virtue of a Proportionate Response A proportionate response matches an attack with one of roughly the same size, and its weakness is that the enemy can see it coming. "They hit an airplane, we hit a transmitter." The law of war asks something different, because its test weighs harm to civilians against the military advantage expected and says nothing about matching the enemy's last strike. A harder answer avoids the first problem, but it carries the risk of escalation. A matched response gets priced in, as when the planned targets had stood empty for three days, and the gradual bombing of North Vietnam gave the enemy time to build up its anti-aircraft defences. The legal test in Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions bans any attack whose civilian harm is excessive against the military advantage expected. One strategy, the Dahiya doctrine, rejects proportion outright by destroying civilian infrastructure so that the population turns against the militants. The case for restraint rests on escalation, which leaders need to understand and dampen.
