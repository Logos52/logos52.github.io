# The Gen Z Gender War - The Split

Young men and young women of Gen Z, born roughly 1997 to 2012, now vote, study, date and think about family in opposite directions, by margins no earlier generation showed. In 2000 women aged 18 to 30 were about 6 points more progressive than men the same age, and by 2025 the gap was about 30 points across 30 countries. The split runs through school, the dating apps, debt and the social media feeds each sex sees.

## Core takeaways

- Young women moved left and young men moved right after 2016.
- Women now earn most college degrees, and men fall behind.
- Dating apps give men constant rejection and women overload.
- Gen Z carries more debt than earlier generations at the same age.
- Each sex gets a separate feed built on what keeps it watching.
- Fewer marriages and fewer births follow from all of it.
- Influencers, algorithms and campaigns profit from keeping the fight going.

## The gap in numbers

In the 2024 US election young women under 30 backed Harris by about 24 points and young men backed Trump by about 16, a gap of 40 points. Young men had swung about 24 points since 2020. The same split shows in Germany, Britain, Scandinavia, southern Europe and South Korea, where people began calling it a gender war around 2019.

- 40% of young American women call themselves liberal, against 25% of young men.
- One in four German men aged 18 to 24 voted AfD in 2025.
- 76% of Gen Z women want abortion legal, against 59% of men.
- Trump-voting Gen Z men rank having children as the top sign of success.
- Harris-voting Gen Z women rank it second to last.

## Education

In 1970 men were 58% of American college students. By 2015 women were about 60%, and today there are 2.4 million more women than men on campus. When women trailed, a 1972 federal law, Title IX, and federal money closed the gap in about 20 years, and no such effort exists for men. Boys develop verbal and planning skills 12 to 18 months later than girls, and classrooms that reward sitting still punish them for it.

- Aged 25 to 34 with a degree: women 47%, men 37%.
- Four-year graduation: women 54%, men 43%.
- Boys are diagnosed with ADHD at twice the rate of girls.
- An electrician or plumber earns $50,000 to $80,000 without tuition debt.
- 42% of Gen Z adults were in or heading for trades in 2025.
- People marry within their education level, so both sexes' pool shrinks.

## Dating

The apps concentrate women's attention on a small share of men. Women respond positively to about 14% of male profiles and men to about 46% of female ones, so men meet steady rejection and women meet more messages than they can handle. Many young men have stopped trying in person, and politics now decides matches that it once did not.

- 24% of adults 18 to 29 had no sex last year, double 2010.
- 44% of Gen Z men have never asked anyone out in person.
- Tinder's paying users fell from 10.9 million in 2021 to 9.2 million.
- Most Gen Z women now list political agreement among their top three needs.
- One in four men under 35 is chronically lonely.

## The two exits

Some on each side have stepped away from the other sex altogether. 4B, a movement from South Korea around 2015, means no dating men, no sex with men, no marriage and no childbirth, and American interest in it spiked after the 2024 election. On the male side, men going their own way reject marriage to avoid the legal and financial risk of divorce, and passport bros move abroad in search of more traditional wives. A 2024 online question, whether a woman alone would rather meet a man or a bear, drew hundreds of millions of views.

- 33% of Gen Z men say a wife should obey her husband.
- 18% of Gen Z women agree, across 29 countries.
- So do 13% of boomer men.
- Women heard the man-or-bear question as a safety judgment.
- Men heard it as a verdict on all men.

## Money

Gen Z aged 18 to 29 carries $1.12 trillion in debt, an average of about $94,000 a person, against $59,000 for millennials at the same age. The median American home cost over $420,000 in 2025, and only 7.6% of Gen Z hold a mortgage. Women hold about two-thirds of student debt because more of them went to college, and the pay gap they are taught shrinks to about 1 cent in equivalent jobs.

- 41% of Gen Z run out of money before the month ends.
- 55% name housing costs as a barrier to marriage.
- 64% name student debt.
- National debt: about $39 trillion, with $10 billion a week in interest.
- Where the woman earns more, divorce runs about three times as high.

## The feeds

Social media shows each sex a different world. In 2024, test TikTok accounts set up as lonely teenage boys went from 13% to 56% misogynistic content within seven days, and male test accounts reached that content after about 15 minutes of normal browsing. Young women's feeds fill with empowerment, breakup and body-image content and with videos showing men as dangerous or incompetent. Neither feed matches the real population, and both match what holds attention.

- 61.5% of YouTube Shorts recommendations to test boys met a study's toxicity test.
- Nearly two-thirds of men 16 to 25 regularly watch masculinity influencers.
- Nearly half of Gen Z men say they face gender discrimination.

## Minds and families

Both sexes are unwell, in different ways. 53% of high-school girls report persistent sadness against 28% of boys, while men die by suicide at nearly four times the rate of women. Young women are leaving religion faster than young men, and a share of young men is moving toward strict traditions.

- 61% of Gen Z have been diagnosed with anxiety.
- Male suicide: 22.7 per 100,000, against 5.9 for women.
- Weekly time with friends fell from 12.8 hours in 2012 to 5.1.
- US births hit a record low of 1.6 per woman in 2025.
- South Korea's rate was 0.72, and cash incentives did not raise it.
- Gen Z wants marriage more than millennials did, but cannot afford it.
