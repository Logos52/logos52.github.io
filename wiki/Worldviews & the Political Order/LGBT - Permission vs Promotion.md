# LGBT - Permission vs Promotion

Letting adults do a thing and having schools, companies and governments push the thing are two different acts. Most arguments made for LGBT causes show that adults should be left alone, and they are then used to ask for school lessons, public money, parades and required affirmation. Keeping the two acts apart shows which of those requests the arguments support.

## Core takeaways

- Permission means the law and other people leave the conduct alone.
- Promotion means institutions teach it, fund it, celebrate it or require affirmation.
- The privacy argument is the strongest, and it supports permission only.
- The other arguments also stop at permission.
- Promotion needs a benefit to people who do not take part.
- The privacy principle also protects people who decline to join in.
- The arguments support conduct that is allowed and left unmentioned in public.

## The distinction

A society can decline to interfere with a practice and at the same time decline to teach it, pay for it, celebrate it or make anyone speak in its favour. Those are two separate postures toward the same conduct. Leaving people alone means nobody acts, while teaching, funding and celebrating mean schools, companies and governments act, so a reason for the first does not carry over to the second. Public debate runs the two together, and a case made for permission gets used to ask for promotion.

- Permission: no law, fine or penalty against the conduct.
- Promotion: school lessons, public money, company campaigns, parades, required speech.
- Each step toward promotion needs an argument of its own.

## The privacy argument

The strongest defense says that what consenting adults do in private is nobody else's business. It needs no claim about what causes orientation, whether the conduct is natural, or what history allowed. It rests on a principle most people already hold: whoever wants to interfere carries the burden of proof, and without a shown harm to others that burden is not met. LGBT campaigners dropped this argument when they asked for a dedicated month, rainbow company logos, children's media, parades and school lessons, since each of those makes the matter everyone's business.

- It holds on one condition: no shown harm to third parties.
- A shown harm to others is grounds the principle itself accepts.
- It delivers non-interference and nothing more.
- It gives no ground for public visibility campaigns.

## The other defenses

Several other defenses are in common use besides privacy. Birth, animal behaviour, history and scripture each reach permission by a different route and stop there. The claim of harmlessness decides whether permission holds at all. Overpopulation and treatment claim a benefit to the public or to patients, and neither gives a school, a company or a government a reason to advance the practice.

| Defense | What it reaches |
| --- | --- |
| Born this way | Against punishment, nothing on promotion |
| Animals do it | Answers "unnatural" only |
| It's historical | Answers "new" only |
| The Bible was misread | A religious rule only |
| It's harmless | Whether permission holds at all |
| We're overpopulated | A public benefit, now reversed |
| Affirmation saves lives | Treatment for a patient who asks |

- No gene for orientation has been found.
- Childhood environment shows the strongest correlation.
- Many unchosen dispositions exist, and nobody proposes celebrating them.
- Cannibalism occurs in animals and in history, and nobody permits it.
- Same-sex relationships show higher disease rates and more mental illness.
- Children raised in these homes show more behavioural problems.
- Taxpayers carry part of the cost of treating those illnesses.
- Overpopulation was the common forecast 20 to 30 years ago.
- Countries now face falling births and import migrants to fill the gap.

## The treatment defense

The claim that gender affirmation saves lives is the only defense that asks institutions to act, by providing a treatment, paying for it and making it the default. Its force came from studies that reported drops of 60 to 73% in self-harm among treated young people. In 2024 the Cass Review, a review written for England's public health service, the NHS, found that evidence remarkably weak, with poor study quality, no proper controls, and other mental health conditions ignored. Even a proven medical benefit would license offering a treatment to a patient who asks for it, and would settle nothing about what a school teaches or what a bystander must say.

- The NHS stopped routine puberty blockers for minors after the review.
- A Swedish long-term study found self-harm at 19 times the normal rate.
- Patients after surgery did no better than those without it.
- Cases among teenage girls rose about 4,000% in recent years.
- A healthy body and a troubled mind call for treating the mind.

## What promotion would need

An argument for promotion has to show a benefit to people other than the participants, and the benefit has to be large enough to justify schools, companies and governments spending money and attention on it. Overpopulation was the only defense that tried this, and falling birth rates reversed it. Any other public-benefit claim gives up the privacy premise, because a practice offered as good for the public is open to public examination. Campaigners who switch between the two positions keep the debate from ever settling.

- Declining to celebrate uses the same liberty as celebrating.
- Compelled affirmation is interference of the kind privacy forbids.
- Whoever applies the principle in one direction only is stating a preference.
- If the conduct is nobody's business, nobody owes it support.
