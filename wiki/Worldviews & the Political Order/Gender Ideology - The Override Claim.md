# Gender Ideology - The Override Claim

The position under examination holds that internal identity takes precedence over biological sex where the two disagree. Which category someone belongs to follows from that. What institutions must recognise follows from it. What counts as a harm follows from it.

Ten defenses are offered for the position, and they are of several kinds: definitional, biological, developmental, and two that work by raising the cost of disagreeing instead of answering the question.

Two of the ten hold. The strongest form of the position is not one of the ten, and none of the ten addresses it.

## Core takeaways

- The claim is that internal identity decides the category wherever internal identity and biological sex disagree.
- Eight of the ten defenses fail. Six fail in ordinary ways. The other two do not argue at all, and instead raise the cost of disagreeing.
- The two that hold are that dysphoria is real and that adults may act on it. Both are narrower than the position they were recruited for.
- The strongest form of the position says the category names a social role rather than a biological kind. None of the ten defenses is built on it.
- The objection that the definition borrows the word it defines does not apply to the role version, because the role can be described on its own.
- The override claim is contested. It is not settled.

## What the category means

**The category claim.** Asked what a woman is, the available answer is: someone who identifies as one. That answer uses the term in its own definition and explains nothing. A definition that cannot be given without the word it defines has not fixed a referent. Every downstream claim about membership carries the same problem.

**What this objection does not reach.** The strongest version of the position does not rely on that definition. It holds that the category names a social role rather than a biological kind: a cluster of expectations, treatment, and standing. On that account, role membership can be self-ascribed or socially conferred, with no claim about bodies at all. The definition then no longer borrows the word it defines, because the referent is the role and the role can be described independently. The objection above does not touch that version. It is the most defensible form of the position, and none of the ten defenses answers it.

**Identity as primary.** Identification requires something to identify with. If the standard is itself constituted by the act of identifying, the operation has no content. That objection holds against the version that makes identity self-grounding. It does not hold against the role version, because the role supplies the external standard the objection says is missing.

## Gender as a social construction

The claim is that what manhood and womanhood consist of, meaning dress, work, bearing, and expected temperament, is set by culture rather than by anatomy. The variation across cultures is the evidence offered for it. Cultures are not independent of biology. They emerge from populations with particular dispositions, and the observable differences in instinct, preference, and response are the subject matter of a research field the brain defense depends on. The construction claim is correct that the elaboration is cultural and varies. It does not establish that the substrate is cultural.

## The two defenses from biology

**A brain of one sex in a body of the other.** The literature shows overlapping distributions and shifted averages. It does not show inversions. A male brain with more traits typical of females remains identifiable as male, and the shift does not guarantee identification. This defense also conflicts with the construction claim, because it requires neurobiology to be determinative in exactly the place where the other defense requires it not to be.

**Intersex conditions prove a spectrum.** The conditions are real and uncommon, and most people with them are unambiguously assignable. Edge cases at a boundary do not dissolve the categories the boundary separates. The deeper problem is that this defense argues for biology deciding the question. If biology decides, the relevant population is people with those conditions, and most people making the identity claim do not have them.

## The defenses about children, death, and harm

**Children know.** The claim underwrites intervention on the basis that the child's report is authoritative. Most gender-nonconforming children do not persist. The intervention would need that fact to be false.

**A living daughter or a dead son.** This is not an argument. It converts disagreement into responsibility for a death, and it is deployed at people in no condition to examine it. It rests on the claim that intervention changes the outcome. That claim is what the argument would need to establish, and it does not.

**Self-identification harms nobody.** The cases where it is contested are all cases of a resource allocated by sex: competitive placement and single-sex facilities. Those cases falsify the claim. Whether the costs are outweighed is a real argument. The claim that the costs are zero is false.

## The two defenses that hold

**Dysphoria is real.** This is granted without qualification. It is a documented condition and it causes serious suffering. Any position that denies this is not worth defending. The disagreement is entirely about treatment, and it is a genuine disagreement. One course is irreversible, and the satisfaction reported afterward does not support it. Psychological treatment in the young is the alternative on offer. That is a dispute about evidence. It is not a dispute about whether the condition exists.

**Adult autonomy.** This is granted. An adult who intends to do this will, and prohibition would require force. The wish behind the defense is granted with it. Dissatisfaction with one's lot is ordinary, sex included, and there are cases where life as the other sex would genuinely have gone better. Autonomy secures the choice. The procedure it authorises removes the advantages of the sex a person has, and it does not confer the advantages of the other. The same reasoning also protects the person who declines to participate, because compelling recognition requires force too. That symmetry is usually left out, and the honest version of the defense concedes it.

## What survives of the ten

Eight of the ten fail. Six fail in ordinary ways: a definition that borrows the word it defines, a claim about brains that the brain literature does not support, an edge case doing work it cannot do, and a developmental claim contradicted by persistence rates. The other two do not argue at all. They raise the cost of disagreeing and leave the question where it stood.

Two hold, and both are narrower than the position they were recruited for. A real condition, and an adult's right to act on it, are compatible with almost any view of what the categories are.

The account left standing is the role account, and none of the ten defenses is built on it. The strongest form of the position goes unanswered, which leaves the override claim contested rather than settled.

## How to practice this

1. When a category is defined for you, check whether the definition uses the word it defines. Notice that "someone who identifies as one" fixes no referent.
2. Ask whether the version in front of you makes identity self-grounding or names a social role. Notice that the circularity objection only reaches the first one.
3. When a brain claim is offered, ask whether the literature shows an inversion or a shifted average. Notice that a shifted average does not guarantee identification.
4. When intersex conditions are offered as evidence, ask who the relevant population is. Notice that most people making the identity claim do not have those conditions.
5. When a child's report is called authoritative, ask about persistence. Notice that most gender-nonconforming children do not persist.
6. When disagreement is answered with responsibility for a death, check what was established. Notice whether the claim that intervention changes the outcome was ever supported.

## Open questions

If the category names a social role rather than a biological kind, what settles membership disputes when self-ascription and social conferral disagree?

Where a resource is allocated by sex and the allocation is contested, what principle decides the case other than counting the costs on each side?
