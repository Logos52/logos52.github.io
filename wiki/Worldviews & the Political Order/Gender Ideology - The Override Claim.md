# Gender Ideology - The Override Claim

The position under examination holds that internal identity takes precedence over biological sex where the two disagree. Everything else follows from that: which category someone belongs to, what institutions must recognise, and what counts as a harm. Ten defenses are offered for it, and they are unusually varied — definitional, biological, developmental, and two that work by raising the cost of disagreeing rather than by answering it.

Two of the ten hold. The strongest form of the position is not one of the ten, and it goes unaddressed.

## The definitional core

**The category claim.** Asked what a woman is, the available answer is someone who identifies as one, which uses the term in its own definition and explains nothing. A definition that cannot be given without the word it defines has not fixed a referent, and every downstream claim about membership inherits the problem.

**What this does not reach.** The strongest version of the position does not rely on that definition. It holds that the category names a social role rather than a biological kind — a cluster of expectations, treatment, and standing — and that role membership can be self-ascribed or socially conferred without any claim about bodies at all. Stated that way the definition no longer borrows the word it defines, because the referent is the role and the role is independently describable. **The objection above does not touch it, and this page does not claim otherwise.** It is the most defensible form of the position and it goes unanswered here.

**Identity as primary.** Identification requires something to identify with, and if the standard is itself constituted by the act of identifying, the operation has no content. This one holds against the version that makes identity self-grounding. Against the role version it does not, since the role supplies the external standard the objection says is missing.

## Gender as social construction

The claim is that what manhood and womanhood consist of — dress, work, bearing, expected temperament — is set by culture rather than by anatomy, and the variation across cultures is the evidence for it. Cultures do not float free of biology; they emerge from populations with particular dispositions, and the observable differences in instinct, preference, and response are the subject matter of a research field the brain defense depends on. What the construction claim gets right is that the elaboration is cultural and variable. What it does not establish is that the substrate is.

## The biological defenses

**A brain of one sex in a body of the other.** The literature shows overlapping distributions and shifted averages, not inversions. A male brain with more traits typical of females remains identifiable as male, and the shift does not guarantee identification. The defense also sits awkwardly beside the construction claim, since it requires neurobiology to be determinative in exactly the place where the other defense requires it not to be.

**Intersex conditions prove a spectrum.** The conditions are real and uncommon, and most people with them are unambiguously assignable. Edge cases at a boundary do not dissolve the categories the boundary separates. The deeper problem is that this defense argues for biology deciding the question, which would make the relevant population those with the conditions — and most people making the identity claim do not have them.

## The developmental and pressure defenses

**Children know.** The claim underwrites intervention on the basis that the child's report is authoritative. Most gender-nonconforming children do not persist, which is what the intervention would need to be false.

**A living daughter or a dead son.** This is not an argument. It converts disagreement into responsibility for a death, and it is deployed at people in no condition to examine it. The claim it rests on — that intervention changes the outcome — is what the argument would need to establish and does not.

**Self-identification harms nobody.** The claim is falsified by the cases where it is contested, which are all cases of a resource allocated by sex: competitive placement and single-sex facilities. Whether those costs are outweighed is a real argument. That they are zero is not.

## The two that hold

**Dysphoria is real.** Granted without qualification. It is a documented condition and it causes serious suffering, and any position that denies this is not worth defending. The disagreement is entirely about treatment, and it is a genuine disagreement: one course is irreversible and the satisfaction reported afterward does not support it, while psychological treatment in the young is the alternative on offer. That is a dispute about evidence, not about whether the condition exists.

**Adult autonomy.** Granted. An adult who intends to do this will, and prohibition would require force. The wish behind the defense is granted with it: dissatisfaction with one's lot is ordinary, sex included, and the case where life as the other sex would genuinely have gone better is not always a greener-grass illusion. Autonomy secures the choice; the procedure it authorises removes the advantages of the sex a person has without conferring the other's. The symmetry usually left out is that the same reasoning protects the person who declines to participate, since compelling recognition also requires force, and the honest version of the defense concedes that.

## What the ten come to

Eight of the ten fail. Six fail in ordinary ways — a definition that borrows the word it defines, a claim about brains that the brain literature does not support, an edge case doing work it cannot do, a developmental claim contradicted by persistence rates. The other two do not argue at all; they raise the cost of disagreeing and leave the question where it stood.

Two hold, and both are narrower than the position they were recruited for. A real condition and an adult's right to act on it are compatible with almost any view of what the categories are.

The one left standing is the role account, and none of the ten defenses is built on it. A page examining this position honestly has to record that its strongest form goes unanswered here, which leaves the override claim contested rather than settled.

## Open questions

If the category names a social role rather than a biological kind, what settles membership disputes when self-ascription and social conferral disagree?

Where a resource is allocated by sex and the allocation is contested, what principle decides the case other than counting the costs on each side?
