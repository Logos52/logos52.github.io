# Markets, states and prosperity

The markets group asks why some economies grow and others fail, and it reads from the general argument about planning to the country cases.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Socialism - Why Planned Economies Fail|Socialism - Why Planned Economies Fail]] sets out the argument that a planned economy cannot work because, once the state owns the means of production, there are no prices to tell planners what anything is worth. The page draws on a Front Page video, a Basic Logic video and other sources, and uses Soviet quotas and the Great Leap Forward to show what happens when planners have no prices to steer by.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Socialism as Moral Correction|Socialism as Moral Correction]] covers a Basic Logic video on the case for socialism as a moral fix for real wrongs, such as exploitation, inequality, market crashes and predatory industries. The video finds that several of those wrongs are real but trace mostly to the state's abuse of markets, and that the remaining arguments treat wealth as a fixed pie.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/China - The Temple and the Monks|China - The Temple and the Monks]] reports a Triggernometry interview with the historian Frank Dikötter, summed up by the saying "the temple is rich, but the monks are poor". In the interview's account, the wealth people produce flows to the state and not to households, so visitors see bullet trains that most ordinary people cannot afford to ride.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Financial Slavery|Financial Slavery]] covers a Basic Logic satire arguing that some modern lending works like legal slavery, because the lender profits when the borrower fails and relies on the state to keep the borrower in.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Europe - The Slow Agony|Europe - The Slow Agony]] reports Mario Draghi's 2024 report for the European Commission, which says Europe has grown more slowly than the United States since 2000 and blames the gap mainly on weaker productivity. A Front Page video on the same page tells the story as choices: Europe gave up output for leisure, tied its industry to Russian gas, kept 27 separate national markets and wrote rules faster than it built.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Britain - Poorer Than Mississippi|Britain - Poorer Than Mississippi]] covers a Front Page video arguing that Britain is a large economy with a falling place on income per person, a measure [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Per Capita|Per Capita]] explains. A poll for the Institute of Economic Affairs found that UK voters on average placed Britain 7th among US states on GDP per person, and the IEA reports, using IMF data, that it ranks last.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/South Africa is a Warning to the West|South Africa is a Warning to the West]] covers a Moon video arguing that South Africa fell from Africa's strongest economy to crime and blackouts in about 20 years. The video blames rulers who captured the state and drained it. The video says the West is starting down the same path more slowly.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/East Asian Exams - The Arms Race|East Asian Exams - The Arms Race]] covers a video by The Invisible Hand about South Korea and China, where one exam taken by teenagers decides which university a student attends. Because the exam ranks students against each other, the video argues, extra effort by one family forces every other family to match it. It uses that arms race to explain the cram schools, the cost of raising a child and the low birth rate.
