# Do group gaps prove discrimination

The gaps group asks whether a gap in outcomes between groups proves discrimination, and its opening page names the step the others test. All five pages here report one channel's argument (Basic Logic), and no opposing source is included. The pages give different explanations: the Distance Claim video points to genetic distance, while the Racial Fatigue video blames fatherlessness rather than race.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference|Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference]] defines the disparity inference as the step from "groups differ in outcomes" to "so discrimination caused it." The Basic Logic video it covers argues that the step only works if all racial groups are the same underneath, a belief it calls racial egalitarianism. It ranks six defenses of that belief on a tier list.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim|Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim]] covers a Basic Logic video arguing that human groups differ genetically enough to build different kinds of society, so no single political system suits every people. The video avoids naming real groups and uses circles, squares and triangles as stand-ins.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/DEI as Anti-Discrimination|DEI as Anti-Discrimination]] covers a Basic Logic video that splits ten defenses of diversity, equity and inclusion into six about performance, which it checks against measured results, and four about justice. It argues that the justice defenses rest on one premise, that a gap in outcomes between groups shows some system acting on them.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association|The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association]] covers a Basic Logic video arguing that the 1964 Act rightly ended Jim Crow laws but wrongly replaced them with laws forcing owners to integrate. The video calls that the same violation of liberty in reverse and gives the Act one out of 10.

[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Racial Fatigue|Racial Fatigue]] covers a Basic Logic video replying to a commentator who called an earlier video racist. The video puts the cause of the fatigue somewhere other than race, saying "it's not black that we're fatigued by. It's societal fatherlessness."
