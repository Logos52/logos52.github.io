# Do group gaps prove discrimination


The gaps group asks whether a gap in outcomes between groups proves discrimination, and its opening page names the step the others test. All five pages here report one channel's argument (Basic Logic). The pages give different explanations: the Distance Claim video points to genetic distance, while the Racial Fatigue video blames fatherlessness rather than race.


## Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference

The disparity inference is the step from "groups differ in outcomes" to "so discrimination caused it." The Basic Logic video [Racial Egalitarianism: All Arguments Ranked & Debunked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVZ2I4lzUlI&t=47s) argues that the step only works if all racial groups are the same underneath, a belief it calls racial egalitarianism. It then ranks six common defenses of that belief on a tier list. The video says it deals only with group averages, so individual exceptions in any group do not change the argument.



## Two answers the video rejects

The video sets the debate up as two camps. Mainstream leftism, as the video describes it, treats all groups and cultures as equal, so any gap in income or crime can only come from discrimination. The mainstream right answers that culture explains the gaps, which implies any group could adopt a culture that worked elsewhere and assimilate anywhere. The video calls both views nonsensical, because discrimination is one factor among several and a culture is produced by the people who sustain it. Its own question is why studies of group gaps do not look at the groups themselves, and its answer is that racial egalitarianism has become a sacred dogma.

## Genetics and Unequal Societies - The Distance Claim

A [Basic Logic video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vWS59L52h1M) argues that human groups differ genetically enough to build different kinds of society, so no single political system suits every people. The video avoids naming real groups. It calls them circles, squares and triangles, and says it uses these stand-ins because biology at the scale of whole groups is a sensitive topic.

Its chain runs from geography and isolation, to measured genetic distance, to differences in the nervous system, to the kind of society each group builds.



## Geography and distance

The video starts with geography. In its made-up world the three groups live far apart, and a large desert has cut the squares off from everyone else for nearly 40,000 years. It sets that against domestic dogs, which it says became a distinct species 15,000 to 30,000 years ago. Its reasoning is that a long isolation with little mixing lets a group keep adapting to its own conditions.

To measure the distance, the video uses the fixation index, which it describes as a measure of allele frequencies between two groups, where more matching alleles means a closer relation. In its account, a value below 0.15 means moderate to low difference, the range of closely related subspecies, such as different North American gray wolves. Above an index of 0.15 it places greatly differentiated organisms. It puts the domestic dog and the gray wolf at an index of about 0.17. The video then gives its groups index values. Circles and squares sit at 0.15, triangles and squares at 0.19, and circles and triangles at 0.11. By its own scale, triangles and squares sit further apart than dogs and wolves. The transcript gives no source for these values.

## The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association

Freedom of association is the right to choose whom you keep company and do business with, free from coercion. The Basic Logic video [An Honest Review of Civil Rights](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iw8YDdx8mhY) argues that the Civil Rights Act of 1964 was right to end Jim Crow laws, which forced private owners to segregate. It says the Act was wrong to replace them with laws forcing owners to integrate.

The video calls that the same violation of liberty in reverse and gives the Act one out of 10.



## Title II and business owners

The video says economists have long argued the market would end segregation by itself, because an owner who turns customers away for no reason loses business to a rival who does not. Defenders of the Act reply that racist business practices still held sway across large parts of America even without Jim Crow. The video says both are right, and resolves the puzzle by asking whether the owner might not be irrational at all. Suppose a group were on average more likely to refuse to pay, steal or harass staff. Then the honest customers from that group might not be worth the extra risk and security cost, and the video says that would explain the situation perfectly.

The video also takes up the objection that the owner only refused because his other customers were racist. Its answer is to ask the same question one step further back, namely why those customers were racist, and whether they were irrational too.

## Racial Fatigue

The Basic Logic video [This YouTuber Gave Me Black Fatigue](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7TSJkkY-D5c) describes "black fatigue" as tiredness from a culture it sees as hostile and hypocritical, more than from any crime done to the people who feel it. This page files it as racial fatigue. The video is a reply to a commentator who called its earlier video racist, and it argues that he ended up agreeing with every one of its claims.

In the end the video puts the cause somewhere other than race, saying "it's not black that we're fatigued by. It's societal fatherlessness."



## The three claims

The earlier video made three claims, which the reply restates:

1. The murder rate among black Americans is very high, which the video calls simply a fact.
2. Fatherlessness contributes to that murder rate.
3. Welfare that paid out only when no father was in the home encouraged fatherlessness.

The reply says the commentator accepted the second and third, which is why it asks what he actually debunked.

## How the figures are argued

The video's case uses time comparisons rather than comparisons with other groups, because it wants to compare modern black America with black America before civil rights, a population it says suffered far more discrimination. Black male homicide victimization, which it uses as a stand-in for offending, was about 42 per 100,000 around 1960 and nearly doubled by 1970. It gives today's rate as about 50 per 100,000. On family structure, it says about 78% of black children lived with both parents before civil rights, against fewer than half today, and 70 to 78% are now born to unmarried mothers.
