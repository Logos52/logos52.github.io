# The Indictment of Europe

# The Indictment of Europe

Three charges are brought against European history: slavery, colonialism and mass killing. Europeans living now are asked to pay reparations, run diversity, equity and inclusion programmes and open their borders on the strength of them. Each charge singles Europe out, so each needs Europe to have done what no other people did, and in all three cases most civilisations did the same.

- Punishing one people alone requires that its conduct was unique.
- Slavery, conquest and mass killing appear in nearly every civilisation.
- Europe alone ran a worldwide campaign to end slavery.
- Colonial rule depended on large numbers of local allies.
- Europe documented its atrocities and apologised.
- Most other peoples deny theirs or celebrate them.
- All three charges fail as selective prosecution.

## The frame

The accusation treats Europeans as one body. The wrong done by some Europeans in the past makes the whole people guilty now and still dangerous. The usual conservative reply is that a son does not answer for his father's crimes, and that reply accepts the charge and argues only about who pays. Since the accusers judge a whole people, the defence can answer for the whole people, and it can also stand on the separate point that guilt belongs to the person who acted.

- A practice found everywhere cannot single one people out.
- Responsibility for an act does not pass to descendants.
- Either point alone ends the case for reparations.

## Charge one: slavery

The Atlantic slave trade was one of the large slave trades in history, and neither the largest nor the most brutal. The Arab trade across the Sahara and the Indian Ocean was larger and crueller. African kingdoms did the capturing and sold the people they had enslaved to European buyers at the coast. Europe stood out only at the end, when Europeans declared slavery a moral evil, abolished it at home, and spent money and lives putting it down abroad.

- Slavery existed in almost every society that left a record.
- Selling a slave was as much a crime as buying one.
- No other people ran a campaign to end slavery worldwide.

## Charge two: colonialism

Every civilisation that was able to expand did expand, and Europe was the one that managed it across oceans. A few thousand soldiers arriving on sailing ships could not take or hold territories nearly the size of Europe by themselves. They held them because local people joined them, often because they judged the newcomers a lesser evil than the rulers already over them. Where local people refused to join, as in Ethiopia, Europe could not colonise.

- About 500 Spaniards took the Aztec empire with 150,000 native allies.
- At most 1,000 Belgians ran a Congo the size of western Europe.
- Millions of Congolese enforced Belgian rule.
- Europe took resources and brought technology, law, security and medicine.
- Wide demand for independence came late.

## Charge three: mass killing

Nearly every people with the means to kill on a large scale has done so, and several did it on a larger scale than Europe. Europeans committed real atrocities, and some of the charges against them were invented for political use, such as a Canadian mass graves story, since debunked. The difference is in what came afterwards: Europeans documented their killings, prosecuted them and apologised, while other peoples deny theirs or celebrate them. A system that assigns guilt by the record a people admits to rewards the deniers and punishes the ones who confessed.

- The Mongols killed about 15% of the world's population.
- Mongolia honours Genghis Khan with giant statues.
- Turkey denies the Armenian genocide.
- Japan still plays down its Second World War atrocities.
- The charge is brought against young whites who did none of it.
- Its purpose is to justify taking their resources.

## The verdict

All three charges fail for the same reason. In each case the conduct was shared across humanity, and a case brought against one people for what all peoples did is selective prosecution. The accusation leaves out the part of the record that was unusual: the abolition, the local allies who ran the colonies, and the confessions afterwards. If the accusers insist on judging a whole people by its history, the same measure applies to them, and by that measure few of them have the standing to bring the case.

| Charge | Conduct | What was unusual |
|---|---|---|
| Slavery | Universal | Europe ended it |
| Colonialism | Universal | Locals ran it with Europe |
| Mass killing | Universal | Europe confessed |
