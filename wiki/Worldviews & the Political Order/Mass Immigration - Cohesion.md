# Mass Immigration - Cohesion

Mass immigration here means large inflows of people from places whose way of life differs enough that most arrivals will not take on the local one. The question that settles the policy is whether an admitted person can support himself and will join the host society. That question is decided one person at a time, at the point of admission.

## Core takeaways

- Select on the person: capacity, skill, self-sufficiency.
- Assimilation follows from selecting well.
- Cohesion is the real concern, wages and tax the public argument.
- Cheap imported labour is cheap because the public pays the rest.
- Total output rises with more people, and output per person falls.
- Skilled arrivals pay in, and low-skill and asylum arrivals cost.
- Nobody publishes a measure of cohesion.

## How the transfer works

An employer hires a foreign worker at a low wage, and the worker accepts it because housing support, public services and roads cover the rest of his living. The public pays that part, and it also pays for policing and for repairing trust when the worker does not fit in. The employer books the saving as profit. A cost put on a third party is a negative externality, which free-market economics counts as a market failure.

- A worker has leverage only when he can say no.
- If wages rise, the employer imports more workers.
- If workers demand better conditions, they are replaced.
- Wages fall, and demand for housing and services rises.
- The cost of keeping order rises.
- Add a minimum wage, and locals end up unemployed and dependent.

```
employer pays:  low wage ─────────────┐
public pays:    housing, services,    ├─> worker's full cost
                policing, cohesion ───┘
employer keeps: the gap as profit
```

## The usual defences

The usual arguments in defence of mass immigration each have a short answer. Most rest on a total that hides a split, such as total output against output per person, or on treating all arrivals as one group when skilled and unskilled arrivals differ. The table puts each defence beside the reason it fails.

| Defence | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Diversity is our strength | Unified cultures outperform fragmented ones, and the useful diversity is of ideas |
| Nation of immigrants | Past US inflows came from nations that assimilated easily, and the present ones do not |
| It cannot be stopped | Japan controls its borders with an aging population and a low birth rate |
| Jobs locals will not do | Only wages locals will not accept, so the defence admits wage suppression is the goal |
| They pay more tax than they take | False for low-skill inflows, the majority in Europe |
| They commit fewer crimes | Excludes the second generation, ignores deportations, compares all natives to a self-selected subset |
| Higher GDP | Total rises, GDP per person falls |
| Saves the pension system | Delays the shortfall by decades and makes it larger |
| More entrepreneurial | Counts gig-app sign-ups as business owners |
| Moral obligation | A government's first duty is to its own citizens, and refugees who can assimilate are a separate case |
| No human is illegal | Every nation has borders, and nobody applies the slogan to his own house |
| Vital for technology | 80 percent of H-1B work visas go to entry-level jobs |

## What the fiscal studies show

Every government study that separates arrivals by skill finds the same split. The result turns positive when the arrival is skilled and negative when he is not, so the fiscal case for or against immigration is a result of who gets selected. Across 25 countries in the OECD, the group of mostly rich economies, the net total sits between -1 and +1 percent of GDP, and the sign depends on the mix.

- Netherlands, 2023, lifetime: labour migrants +€125,000.
- Family migrants -€275,000, asylum migrants -€475,000.
- Denmark, 2019: non-Western immigrants and descendants cost 27bn DKK that year.
- Western immigrants contributed 11bn DKK.
- UK, 2024: a low-wage migrant arriving at 25 costs £617,000 by 81.
- A high-wage migrant contributes over £1,000,000.
- US, 2016: an arrival without high school costs $117,000 over 75 years.
- An arrival with some college contributes $261,000 or more.

## What this desk holds

The criterion is selection on the person. When an inflow is too large to assess one person at a time, origin stands in as a guess about likely conduct and net contribution. Assimilation is a spectrum, and a group that does not assimilate is a lesser failure than one that lives off the host.

- The public should not cover part of a foreign worker's wage.
- An employer hiring a foreign worker on a full wage is fine.
- Guilt by descent is rejected.
- The unselected early-2020s US intake was a mistake to reverse.
