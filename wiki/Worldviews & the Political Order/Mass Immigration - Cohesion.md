# Mass Immigration - Cohesion

The case against mass immigration turns on assimilation. The Basic Logic videos argue that immigration from cultures that blend in easily strengthens a country, while large-scale migration from cultures that are unlikely to assimilate weakens it. In the video's words, the difference between immigration and invasion is assimilation. The common defenses of mass immigration are slogans, money arguments that leave out the costs, or moral appeals nobody applies at home. Underneath them sits a transfer, in which companies get cheap labour and the public pays the rest of the bill.

The stance the videos take targets mass migration, legal or illegal, from cultures where assimilation is unlikely. It is not opposition to immigration as such, and the speaker who makes the case is an immigrant himself.

## The slogans

"Diversity is our strength" is no more true than "unity is our strength." Diversity of ideas is useful, though the people chanting the slogan seem allergic to that kind, and the video says unified cultures outperform fragmented ones, pointing to Asia. "Assimilation is our strength" would be closer. "We're a nation of immigrants" is mostly American, and it skips the question of where the immigrants came from. Most came from Western Christian nations that fit easily into a country built by the same civilisation. "No human is illegal" is meaningless, since nobody applies it to their own home, and every nation on Earth has borders.

"Mass immigration is unstoppable" is false. Japan controls its borders despite a collapsing birth rate and an ageing population, and the line exists mainly to demoralise.

## The money arguments

Saying immigrants do jobs locals will not do mixes up jobs and wages. Locals will do any job, but not at the wages that hold once cheap foreign labour floods in. The video says entry-level IT and construction workers stay at $20 an hour in pay because their bosses can hire three undocumented workers for the same price, and that the minimum wage started as a tool against immigration, and that combining it with mass migration produces unemployment and dependency.

The tax argument says immigrants pay in more than they take out. The video says that fails for low-skill immigration, which it says makes up the majority, especially in Europe. It also leaves out schooling, policing, welfare, public facilities and the cost of absorbing a foreign culture. More people do raise GDP, the total size of the economy, and so would importing 10 million houseplants. What counts is GDP per person, and, the video says, that falls. Using immigration to save social security props up a failing scheme for a few more decades while it damages wages and cohesion, and the children of today's voters pay. The entrepreneurship figure counts anyone who signs up to deliver for DoorDash as a business owner. Poaching top talent would make sense, yet, the video says, 80% of H-1B visas go to entry-level jobs, so the program floods the bottom of the market with low-tier specialists.

## The private contract defense

Some libertarians say a company that imports foreign workers has made a private deal that affects nobody else. The company does not pay the full cost, though. The worker accepts subsistence wages because society covers the rest through social services, housing support, infrastructure and public amenities. When a worker does not follow local laws and customs, the public carries the cost of order and policing. That is a negative externality, which libertarian economics says it opposes, and a voluntary exchange counts only when both sides pay the full price. With assimilation priced in, the labour would not be cheap.

Locals pay twice. They subsidise the newcomers and then compete with them. Wages fall while demand for housing, services and policing rises. Workers have bargaining power only when they can say no, and a steady supply of subsidised labour removes it, since an employer can replace any worker who asks for more. A business that cannot pay the full cost of its labour is dependent, not efficient.

## Crime and duty

The statistic that immigrants commit fewer crimes, the video argues, leaves out the second generation, whose crime rate it calls much higher, and ignores deportations that hold repeat offending down. It also compares the whole native population with a self-selected group of migrants, and the gap closes once you separate high-skill and low-skill arrivals. The moral-obligation argument asks a country to do what no one does with their own front door at night. Helping refugees with compatible values is good, and people from very different cultures belong in countries closer to their own. A government's first duty is to its own citizens.
