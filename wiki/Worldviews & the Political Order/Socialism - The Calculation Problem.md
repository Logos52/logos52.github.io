# Socialism - The Calculation Problem

Socialism in the strict sense means the state owns the farms, factories and mines, and a central plan decides what they produce. Without private owners buying and selling those things there are no prices for them, so the planners cannot know what anything is worth. Every country that tried full state ownership ended in shortage, famine or mass emigration, and the countries its supporters now point to are market economies.

## Core takeaways

- A price tells everyone at once how scarce a thing is.
- State ownership removes those prices, so planners guess.
- Every full attempt ended in shortage, famine or flight.
- Denmark and Sweden are market economies with a large welfare state.
- Young Western women back socialism far more than young men do.
- The squeezed American sectors are the ones government already dominates.
- The debt leaves no room for a much larger state.

## The calculation problem

When copper runs short its price rises, and every business that uses copper sees the change and adjusts without anyone giving orders. The price carries knowledge no office could collect, such as a farmer's sense of his soil or a foreman's sense of which machine is about to fail. An essay of 1920 on economic calculation and a paper of 1945 on the use of knowledge set out the problem. When the state owns the means of production, nobody trades them, and the planners set prices by decree.

```
market:  shortage -> price rises -> millions adjust
plan:    shortage -> office guesses -> one order for all
                          |
                          v
               wrong guess stays in place
```

- The Soviet price office set about 200,000 prices a year by 1985.
- A working economy needs about 20 million prices, changing constantly.
- Nails planned by weight came out huge and useless.
- Nails planned by count came out tiny and useless.
- East German buyers waited up to 16 years for a car.
- A market's mistakes are small and many, and customers leaving corrects them.
- A planner's mistake applies to everyone and is hard to admit.

## The record

Each full attempt ran into the same problem at national scale. Grain, food and consumer goods ran short, and the governments used force to hold the plan in place. Every socialist regime built a secret police, because a plan needs enforcement.

- Soviet famine of 1932 to 1933: 3 to 5 million Ukrainians died.
- Their grain was exported to pay for machinery.
- China's famine of 1958 to 1962 killed 36 to 45 million people.
- Villages melted down their tools for steel and killed off sparrows.
- Venezuela, with the largest oil reserves, lost 87% of output per head.
- A quarter of Venezuelans left the country.
- In divided Korea and Germany, the planned halves ended far poorer.

## The market side

China lifted most of its people out of poverty only after it allowed private farming and business. The freest economies are many times richer per person than the least free. When supporters are asked what real socialism would look like, they name Denmark, Sweden or Norway, whose leaders describe them as market economies with a welfare state paid for by a heavy sales tax on everyone.

- Chinese in extreme poverty: about 88% in 1978, under 3% by 2020.
- Freest quarter of economies: about $120,000 output per head.
- Least free quarter: about $10,600.
- Denmark's prime minister called it a market economy in 2015.
- Supporters call each failed socialist state not real socialism.

## Why young women lean left

Young women and young men in the West face the same rents, debts and childcare bills, yet young women favour socialism by a gap of about 30 points. Women spend about five more years than men in schools and colleges whose faculty sit far to the left of the country, and their social media feeds repeat the same framework. The pay gap they are taught shrinks to almost nothing for young childless workers in the same jobs.

- 2018, 51 elite colleges: 12.7 Democrat professors per Republican.
- In anthropology the ratio was 133 to one.
- Women hold about two-thirds of the $1.7 trillion in student debt.
- Raw pay gap: 83 cents on the dollar.
- Same job, young and childless: 94 to 99 cents.

## What the young want

The polls record a protest rather than a wish for state ownership. Only 17% of Americans define socialism as state ownership of production, and 23% define it as equality. The same young people who like the word distrust every party, and asked in other terms, many call themselves pro-market. Housing, healthcare, college and childcare are where they feel squeezed, and government already dominates or distorts all four.

- 2014: 42% of millennials liked socialism, 16% could define it.
- The same poll found 64% wanted a free market.
- 2025: 13% of young Americans thought the country was on the right track.
- Support for socialism among the young fell to 21% in 2025.
- Americans overall preferred capitalism to socialism, 54% to 39%.
- Building limits push coastal house prices over 50% above building cost.
- Government pays or subsidises nearly all American healthcare.
- The 2008 crash followed government-backed mortgages and cheap central-bank money.

## The arithmetic

The American government is about $39 trillion in debt and already pays more in interest than it spends on its military. The Social Security trust fund runs out in 2033, and benefits then fall by law. Taking every billionaire's whole fortune once would cover about 18 months of the deficit. A larger welfare state would need a broad tax on everyone, as in Denmark, and no party proposes one.

- Net interest passed $1 trillion a year in 2025.
- It is projected to double by 2036.
- Benefits fall 23% when the trust fund runs out.
- All billionaire wealth comes to about $5 trillion, taken once.
