# Who Gets Called Racist

Three videos by [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYIITZs0C8A) argue that people use the word "racist" to end an argument rather than answer it, and work out what follows from that. The first says judging by group averages is a tool that is sometimes the only option when information is short. The second says the right response to the accusation is to ask whether you are right. The third turns the charge around and asks why the group it says is quickest to use it scores highest on in-group preference, as the studies it cites claim.

## When a group average is the only thing available

The opening example is a choice of seat on public transport on the way home. In an ideal world you would know each person and judge them as an individual. Split-second choices give you no such chance, and [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGRNJhS2bhI) says stereotypes and generalisations are tools whose worth depends on the user. Overusing them blinds you to nuance and costs you friends, while refusing them leaves you frozen when you lack information.

He sets out three rules for using them well. The first concerns time and sample size. Broad judgments suit a split-second choice or a large group such as an immigration policy. When you can talk to someone, judge them as an individual instead, because individuals depart from averages. The second rule is to get the statistics right, which means using rates per head of population rather than raw totals. His example is two groups that commit 44% and 56% of murders, which looks even until you learn the second group is 13% of the population. The third rule is to learn why a group behaves as it does, which he says helps you read people and can help a group improve. On genetic explanations he says the right method is elimination, so non-genetic theories must be tested first.

## What to do when you are accused

The second video starts at the moment the accusation lands. You were careful, cited only data and said you meant averages, and you were called racist anyway. [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yYIITZs0C8A) gives three possible replies and his reason for each:

1. Deny it by appealing to colour blindness, merit or your intentions. He calls this a trap, because the accuser can simply redefine racism to include colour blindness, so you end up asking for an absolution you will not get.
2. Turn it around and call the accuser the real racist. This is slightly better but still a trap, because it remains an argument over a word. The other side controls the definitions, such as "prejudice plus power".
3. Accept the label and ask whether you are right. Both of the first two replies treat the label as decisive, which gives it its power. The third treats it as empty, so the only labels left are true and false, and the question becomes whether the crime figures are wrong.

His wider reason is that moral accusations avoid a rebuttal rather than supply one. Conservatives once feared the label so much that the fear itself gave it force, and he thinks a generation called racist for wanting a secure border has been immunised by overexposure.

## Whether the charge points the other way

The third video asks the reverse question. It cites studies that, in his account, consistently find black Americans the group with the highest in-group preference and white Americans the lowest. [Basic Logic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WunIYGEYeD0) then weighs three explanations:

1. Historical grievance he rejects as mostly rationalisation. Nobody alive was a slave or a slave owner, grievances usually fade, and the Irish, Italians and Asians moved past their own discrimination.
2. Being taught to hate he finds more convincing. He says schools teach slavery as a uniquely white crime and that media coverage portrayed police as a lethal threat to black people.
3. Being paid to hate is the third, and he gives it the most detail. His example is that corporations gave hundreds of millions to the organisations behind the 2020 riots, which caused billions in damage. Victimhood, he adds, justifies DEI, affirmative action and reparations.

He ends with a challenge to anyone who disagrees. Name a systemic disadvantage black Americans face, but it must be a present-day legal discrimination, not an outcome such as higher imprisonment.

## Open questions

- None of the three videos names the studies on in-group preference or a source for the murder shares. The 44/56/13 split is given as an example, and the notes do not say which country or year it describes.
- The claim that police shootings follow crime involvement rather than race is stated without a source in the notes.
- The notes give no reason, beyond these assertions, why accepting the label wins over onlookers.
