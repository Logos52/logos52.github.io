# Moral Language as Leverage

Western countries are told to take in people from everywhere, share their wealth and stop discriminating, and the people telling them often come from countries that stay homogeneous, keep their wealth and discriminate openly. Pointing out this double standard has never stopped it. Words such as racist and discrimination get results from Westerners whether or not the speaker believes them, so they get used wherever they bring results.

## Core takeaways

- A double standard that survives exposure is a tactic.
- Societies keep order through guilt, shame or fear.
- Guilt only works in a highly homogeneous society.
- Anything valuable and hard to make invites people to take it.
- Accusations of racism bring money, welfare and preferential treatment.
- Asking the people making the demands to be consistent changes nothing.
- The defence is to refuse the demands and warn others.

## The double standard

Orania, a town in South Africa, shows the pattern at small scale. Afrikaners, descendants of mostly Dutch settlers, built it on poor land in the Karoo, a dry inland region, with no welfare, no subsidies and no connection to the national power grid. Once it became a good place to live, other South Africans called it a second apartheid and a town of racists, and demanded a share, with the state to enforce the demand. The West as a whole gets the same demands.

- The West must be diverse, while the accusers' homelands stay homogeneous.
- The West must redistribute, while their homelands do not.
- The West may not discriminate, while they discriminate against Westerners.
- Hypocrisy stops when exposed, because exposure costs the hypocrite.
- This double standard is exposed constantly, costs nothing, and continues.

## Three ways to keep order

A society regulates behaviour through guilt, shame or fear. In a guilt society people feel pain when they break their own principles, and the police step in only when that fails. This is normal in the West and rare everywhere else, because it needs a population whose principles are close enough to match. When a society becomes mixed enough that neither shared principles nor one group's judgement reaches everyone, fear takes over without anyone choosing it.

```
guilt  ->  own principles      needs: advanced, homogeneous
shame  ->  the clan's verdict  needs: homogeneous
fear   ->  expected revenge    needs: nothing
```

- Guilt is the rarest form and depends on a shared religion or culture.
- Shame is the most common form and still needs one clan.
- Fear is the least prosperous form and works anywhere.
- Moral argument has no hold in a society run on fear.

## Why value attracts claims

Anything valuable that is hard to make gives outsiders a choice: learn to make it, or take it. Inside a shared moral order, taking costs guilt or the group's disapproval. Across that boundary those costs are absent, and taking a finished thing is far easier than building the capacity to make one. The same applies to craftsmanship, buildings, natural resources and whole working countries.

- Orania drew no interest while it was poor land.
- Claims arrived once there was something to claim.
- The claims come in the words of belonging and entitlement.
- Takers follow the easier option, as people do with any incentive.

## How the words work

The vocabulary of racism and discrimination was built inside a guilt society and works on anyone who holds its commitments. Whoever uses it gets resources, welfare and preferential treatment from the authorities, without needing to believe the words or understand why they work. A tool used only where it works is not inconsistent, which is why the double standard lasts, and a claim made this way can still be true or false on its own facts. Lecturing the users about consistency fails, because a person without the commitment cannot be argued into acting as if they had it.

- Test: a person who holds a principle applies it against themselves.
- A person using it as a tool applies it in one direction only.
- The test needs no guess about motive.
- The useful response is to keep refusing and to warn others.
- The words are complex and spread fast, so someone taught them.
- Whoever taught them gained something by it.
