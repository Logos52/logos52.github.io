# The Woke Retreat - The New Electorate Claim

Between about 2014 and 2021 a set of cultural positions became standard on the American and British left: rules about pronouns, diversity training at work, men who identify as women competing in women's sport, and disagreement treated as an offence. Since 2024 the same parties and many of the same people have been backing away from those positions in public. The usual explanation is that the right won the argument, and the new electorate claim gives a different one. The left brought in a large population from poor countries, that population does not share the cultural positions, it is now big enough to decide elections, and so the left is dropping the positions to keep its vote while keeping immigration and welfare as they are. On that account the culture war of the 2010s was never the thing that mattered most; it was a disguise for a change in who lives in these countries, and the retreat from the cultural positions is a defeat for the right, because the people who ended those positions will not stop there.

Three of the facts in that account hold: the retreat is real, the population change is real in its direction, and the gap in attitudes between the new population and the old is real in the first generation. The link between them, that the population change caused the retreat, is not shown, and three of the things the claim rests on fail when checked against the numbers.

## The retreat

The retreat has dates and names. On ABC around 9 August 2026, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez repeated a councilman's line that "Woke 1 was crazy," and said the rhetoric of that period is "not rhetoric that we would use today," while calling the discussions underneath it "quite fruitful," so her retreat was from the language and not from the positions. Seth Moulton told the New York Times on 7 November 2024 that he did not want his daughters "getting run over on a playing field by a male or formerly male athlete," and his campaign manager resigned the next day. Gavin Newsom called men in women's sport "deeply unfair" on 6 March 2025, and Rahm Emanuel said on 1 April 2026 that men should not be playing women's sports. Sarah McBride said in June 2025 that the coalition had gone "to Trans 201, Trans 301, when people were still at a very much Trans 101 stage." The strategy papers say the same thing in writing: Third Way's paper of 22 August 2025 lists words for Democrats to drop, including "birthing person," "cisgender," "patriarchy," and "LGBTQIA+," and WelcomePAC's paper of 27 October 2025 recommends shifting on affirmative action and transgender athletes.

The institutions moved less than the people: House votes on the same questions drew two, three, and eight Democratic votes in favour, and Spain, Germany, Denmark, and the Netherlands kept their laws on gender identity in place. In the American off-year elections of November 2025, Democrats who defended protections for transgender people won.

The reversals themselves came from things that can be named, and none of them was a new electorate. Men were kept out of women's sport in the United States by Executive Order 14201 on 5 February 2025, followed within a day by the NCAA and within months by the Olympic committee, and the Supreme Court upheld state bans on 30 June 2026. In Britain the Supreme Court ruled on 16 April 2025 that "woman" in the Equality Act means biological sex. Drag story hours were restricted by Republican state laws in Montana, Tennessee, Florida, and Texas, and the events continue elsewhere. The split between the gay and lesbian organisations and the transgender ones dates to a 2015 petition and the founding of the LGB Alliance in 2019, which is six to eleven years before the point at which, on the claim, the new electorate became big enough to decide elections and caused that split. And nothing in the claim says what would count as the cultural positions being alive, even though pronoun policies, diversity budgets, curricula, hiring practice, and speech rules at work are all measurable and none of them is measured.

## The population change

The change is real, and for two countries it sets records: the foreign-born share of the United States reached 15.8 percent in January 2025, above the previous high of 14.8 percent in 1890, and then fell to 15.4 percent by June, the first fall since the 1960s, and Britain went from 4.2 percent foreign-born in 1951 to 16 percent in 2021. Fertility among the settled population is at or near record lows everywhere the claim looks: 1.41 in England and Wales, 1.56 in France, 1.32 in Germany, 1.18 in Italy, 1.10 in Spain, 1.60 in the United States.

"Unrelenting immigration" describes 2021 to 2023 and not what followed, because British net migration peaked at 944,000 in the year to June 2023 under a Conservative government, fell to 431,000 in 2024, and fell again to 171,000 in the year to December 2025 under Labour, and Denmark granted asylum to 860 people in 2024. The record intake the claim attributes to the left happened under the right.

"Most Western European capitals are already minority-European" fails on every measure, and the reason it fails matters more than the failure itself, because the statistic most countries publish is migration background, which counts anyone born abroad or with a parent born abroad, and that is not the same as non-European. Austria is 27.8 percent migration background and about 10 to 11 percent non-European in origin, because most of its migrants came from the European Union and the former Yugoslavia. Germany is 25.5 percent migration background, of which 52.1 percent is European. Amsterdam is 60.7 percent foreign origin and 56.6 percent European origin. London is 63.2 percent not White British and 53.8 percent White. A claim that moves from a migration-background figure to "minority-European" is counting Poles, Romanians, Italians, Irish, Serbs, and Bosnians as non-European.

On the two-generation foreign-origin measure, six cities have crossed half: Brussels, Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Vienna, London, and Birmingham. On the non-European measure, none has, and the highest is Rotterdam at 44.9 percent. The capitals that disprove "most" are the ones that are far from crossing: Copenhagen is 71.1 percent Danish origin, Stockholm 53.9 percent with both parents Swedish-born, Oslo 35 percent immigrant background, Berlin 24.9 percent foreign nationals of whom 59.5 percent hold European citizenship, and Paris 20.8 percent immigrants.

No official projection supports "Within one to three generations." Britain, Germany, and France do not project by origin at all, and France is barred by its constitutional court from collecting the category. Sweden projects foreign-born rising from 20.8 to 25 percent by 2070, and Austria to 33 percent by 2080. The Netherlands is the one country that projects by origin, and its figure for Dutch origin goes from 72.1 percent in 2024 to 54.4 percent in 2070, with European origin still at 70 percent, and the youngest age group in 2070 still 60.9 percent Dutch origin. The projection that does cross is a private one for Britain, which puts White British below half in 2063, and it assumes a migration rate 110,000 a year above what the statistics office now uses, assumes no migrant is ever White British, and counts every mixed child as non-White. The one official projection that crosses is the American one, around 2045 to 2050 for non-Hispanic whites alone, and that count puts every mixed child on the minority side too, at a time when more than one in ten American babies has one white and one non-white parent. On official numbers, then, the United States crosses first and Europe does not cross, which is the reverse of the claim's "America has a bit longer."

## The attitudes

The gap is real: asked in 2019 whether homosexuality should be accepted by society, 7 percent said yes in Nigeria, 9 in Indonesia and Tunisia, 25 in Turkey, and 37 in India, against 86 to 94 percent in Britain, France, Germany, the Netherlands, and Sweden. The gap survives migration: in 2015, 18 percent of British Muslims agreed that homosexuality should be legal in Britain and 52 percent disagreed, against 73 percent of all adults, in a sample drawn only from areas where Muslims were a fifth of residents or more, and in the Netherlands in 2020, 85 percent of people with no migration background agreed it is good that homosexuals may marry, against 40 percent of Moroccan and Turkish origin and 33 percent of Somali origin.

The gap also moves, as the Dutch figures by generation from the same survey series in 2022 show: they go from 31 to 49 percent for Turkish origin between the first generation and the second, from 35 to 45 for Moroccan, from 28 to 62 for Somali, and from 74 to 92 for Iranian, against 86 for the native Dutch. American Muslims' acceptance of homosexuality went from 27 percent in 2007 to 39 in 2011 to 52 in 2017. Both of those things are true at once: the second generation moves a long way, and it is still 37 to 41 points below the native figure for the two largest groups.

## The bloc

For a group to decide an election it has to be large and it has to vote together, and both conditions have to hold. On size, naturalized citizens were 9 percent of American voters in 2024 and split 51 to 47 between Harris and Trump, and the largest shift inside that group was among white naturalized citizens, who went from 41 to 55 percent for Trump.

On voting together, the category the claim needs splits wherever it is measured. The one group in American politics that does vote as a bloc is Black voters, whose Democratic share across eleven presidential elections from 1976 to 2020 stayed between 83 and 95 percent, and that group is not an immigrant population. The Hispanic share over the same elections ranged from 53 to 73 percent and came close to an even split in 2024. Between 2020 and 2024, Latino men went from 59–36 for Biden to 44–54 for Trump, a swing of 33 points, while Black women went from 90–9 to 92–7, further toward the Democrats. Cuban Americans backed Trump 68 to 23 while the Puerto Rican county of Osceola moved a point and a half. Vietnamese Americans lean Republican 51 to 42 and Indian Americans are 68 percent Democratic. The Pew Research Center declines to use the phrase "the Latino vote" at all, because the phrase implies a single bloc. The Hispanic change in 2024 came mostly from people who had not voted in 2020, and 60 percent of those new voters chose the candidate running on mass deportation.

Britain divides the same way: Labour led 49 to 20 among ethnic minority voters as a whole in 2024, but the Conservatives took about a third of British Hindus against 14 percent of every other minority, and Labour's share fell 28 points among Asian Muslim voters while rising among Black voters. The report that found this said in its headline that Labour cannot rely on ethnic minority voters as a bloc. And on the one cultural question polled by group, the numbers go the opposite way from the claim: 75 percent of white Americans oppose transgender athletes in women's sport, against 67 percent of Hispanic, 68 percent of Black, and 68 percent of Asian Americans. Public opinion on that question moved seven points in four years, and no population change produces seven points in four years.

## The cases offered

The strongest instances for the claim are real. Hamtramck, Michigan elected the first Muslim-majority city council in the United States in 2015, to the applause of local progressives, and on 13 June 2023 that council voted unanimously to ban Pride flags from city property and a month later removed two members of its Human Rights Commission for flying one. The former mayor said: "We supported you when you were threatened, and now our rights are threatened, and you're the one doing the threatening." Hamtramck's mayor endorsed Donald Trump in September 2024. In Dearborn, seven books were pulled from school libraries in 2022 after complaints from mostly Muslim parents. In Birmingham, Muslim parents forced a primary school to suspend its relationships programme in March 2019, and the council needed a permanent court order to keep protesters away from a second school, where 21 teachers had been treated for stress. In the British election of July 2024, five independents campaigning on Gaza beat Labour, one of them unseating a shadow cabinet member by 979 votes, and in the London local elections of May 2026 a party founded on the same issue took 24 of 66 seats in Newham, having held no seats there before.

What those cases show is narrower than what they are used for. The electoral defections were about foreign policy and not about any cultural position, and the programme the winning independents published afterward is about austerity and child benefit. The local fights in Hamtramck and Birmingham were over a flag on city property and a programme in a school, which are fights over what an institution promotes and not over what is permitted, and they stayed local. The half of the claim in which the new population imposes its own positions has no national instance. Against Hamtramck there is New York, which in November 2025 elected a Muslim mayor who campaigned on 65 million dollars for gender-affirming care. And the largest measured retreat from Pride in 2025 was by companies, not by parties.

## What the bloc is said to want

The claim lists what the new electorate wants: more immigration so that relatives can follow, more welfare and public housing, the right to run its own communities without interference, more of its own in positions of power, and no Pride flags or parades. No polling on any of the first four was found. What exists is data on what groups receive, and what a group receives does not show what it wants. On what immigrants cost the state, no study finds immigrants as a class to be a net cost, and every serious one splits the answer by skill, earnings, and age at arrival, with family migration the expensive kind. On representation, the preference for a candidate of one's own ethnicity measures at about ten points among Pakistani-origin voters in Britain and does not appear among Indian, Black Caribbean, or Black African voters, and every party in every country with a concentrated minority runs such candidates.

The claim that judges are more lenient with the new population is contradicted by the sentencing data of every jurisdiction that publishes it. The British guideline usually cited, from March 2025, required a pre-sentence report for listed groups and attached no discount to any of them; it had been welcomed by the Conservative government a year earlier, and it was repealed in June 2025. Average custodial sentences for serious offences in England and Wales run 18.4 months for white offenders, 32.2 for Asian, and 28.6 for Black, and the American figures, after adjusting for other factors, run 13.4 percent longer for Black men and 11.2 percent longer for Hispanic men. The British analysis that adjusts for other factors finds no consistent pattern either way, so the claim that sentencing is unequal is also weaker than its advocates say. What is documented is a different thing: the national audit of group-based child sexual exploitation published in June 2025 has a chapter titled "Denial," and it found that the ethnicity of perpetrators "is still not recorded for two-thirds of perpetrators" and that organisations avoided the subject out of fear. That is an institution suppressing a pattern, and it is the strongest documented item anywhere in the area the claim covers, but it is not lenient sentencing.

## The half the claim needs most

The claim needs the left to be dropping the cultural positions while keeping immigration and welfare as they were, and that is the half the record contradicts. Denmark's Social Democratic prime minister set a goal of zero asylum seekers in January 2021, her government was the first in Europe to move to deport Syrian refugees, it legislated to process asylum claims in third countries, and in May 2025 she led nine prime ministers in a letter seeking to loosen the European human rights convention for deportations. Her government also kept a framework under which social housing is demolished and child benefit withdrawn in areas above a threshold of non-Western residents, until the European court ruled the ethnic criteria unlawful in December 2025. Britain's Labour government made refugee status temporary and reviewable every 30 months, raised the wait for settlement from five years to twenty, and made asylum support discretionary, and the Migration Observatory judged the result more restrictive than Denmark's. Germany's Social Democratic chancellor said in October 2023 that Germany must "finally deport on a large scale." Sweden, Austria, and Finland tightened their immigration rules too. The one centre-left party in Europe that kept immigration open, Spain's, also kept its law on gender identity. The two things the claim needs to move in opposite directions move together.

The name the claim uses for the new politics does not come from where the claim says it does. "Woke 2.0" traces to a games journalist on Bluesky on 21 December 2024, not to a leftist politician, and on the left the term means more cultural confrontation and more defence of immigrants, which is the opposite of what the claim says it means. The path the claim treats as rare and tolerable, left economics with immigration restriction, has a successful national instance in Denmark's governing party, and its purest instance, Germany's Wagenknecht alliance, missed the parliamentary threshold in February 2025 by 9,529 votes.

## The intention step

The last part of the claim is that the leaders of the left knew this would happen when they admitted the new population, and the evidence offered is that the alternative, that some of the most powerful people on the planet were ignorant, is hard to believe, which makes the speaker's confidence the evidence. The nearest document is a former Labour adviser's article of October 2009, which said the immigration policy was "intended – even if this wasn't its main purpose – to rub the Right's nose in diversity," and which he then clarified in the same paper to say the main goal was migrant workers for a labour shortage and the political purpose was secondary. A simpler account explains the record without anyone planning it: institutions and parties acting in their own interest produce coordination without a conspiracy. Sincere advocacy and cynical advocacy leave the same public record, so nothing could separate them, and the claim also needs the same leaders to have been naive about what they were admitting and cynical about their own principles at the same time.

One definition in the claim is wrong, and it matters for the whole claim. Civic nationalism is defined there as the belief that a nation is defined by the state bureaucracy rather than by its people. The term means membership by citizenship, shared institutions, and shared political values, open to anyone who takes them on, as against membership by descent, and no reference source gives the bureaucracy definition.

## What the chain establishes

Three facts hold: the left retreated from its cultural positions, and the retreat is documented in named people and strategy papers, with the institutions moving less; the population changed, in the direction the claim says, at a size and on a timetable the claim gets wrong; and the new population holds different views in its first generation and narrows the gap in its second. Nothing found connects the first fact to the second, because no memo, vote, statement, or internal account shows a left party dropping a cultural position for immigrant-origin voters, and the parties' own stated reason is public opinion.

Six facts contradict the connection. The group that was supposed to force the retreat is 9 percent of the electorate and splits nearly evenly, and its largest component swung 33 points toward the candidate promising deportations while the one group that does vote as a bloc moved the other way. White Americans oppose transgender athletes more than any minority does. Opinion moved seven points in four years, which no population change can produce. The reversals were delivered by an executive order, two supreme courts, state legislatures, and federal pressure on corporate sponsors. And the half of the claim about keeping immigration and welfare intact is contradicted in every European country with a centre-left government except one.

What survives of the claim is smaller, and the record supports it. Who is in an electorate changes what that electorate wants, and the places with the most concentrated immigrant-origin electorates produced local fights over what institutions promote, and one national defection over foreign policy. That is an observation about who makes up an electorate, and it is the claim in its general form with the part about how the change happens left out. What the bloc wants is asserted, and the plan behind the admission is asserted. The end of the cultural positions was carried out by documented means, and those means belong to the right.

## Open questions

What document would show a party dropping a cultural position for a bloc of voters rather than for the public at large, and has anyone looked for one?

A group decides an election only if it votes together. What measure of voting together would the claim accept, given that the census categories it relies on split at every election where they are measured?
