# Grok Bot Galaxy

The workshop ran for three days, from 15 September 2026 through 17 September 2026, as a public livestream with a matching in-person schedule. The first day's replay ran about 8 hours 45 minutes and showed about 1 million views on the player. Schedule pages named guest blocks for engineering, product, founders, sales-shaped teams, support, marketing, and a closing presentation. Faces were not always plated, so some speaker identifications in the notes stay tentative. Later days' exact broadcast addresses were not posted as concrete replay links on the public schedule page that was fetched, and those addresses are not invented here.

The first day mixed table talk, helper overlays, and product slides with a live attempt to stand up a pop-up food business. Chat proposed fry-themed names that were not confirmed as a final brand. A venue-finder prototype appeared, including an access-control prompt and a map-based change listed as the fourteenth pull request. A later screen showed a different event-style name that may have been a one-off label rather than a finished company title. Helper overlays climbed from about 13 helpers in the afternoon samples to about 29 helpers by evening, with message counts moving into the high hundreds in the samples that were captured. Product slides that day introduced four pillars and a colleagues framing, plus an auto-fix claim for failing checks. The stream ended on a branded card after a questions slide and a short wrap.

The second day moved through department-shaped helper packs, including sales-engineering templates. A support lane insisted on public documentation for answers, internal rules that must not be pasted out, and a review before anything was written to an outside channel. A shared docs workspace was treated as the source of truth, and a missing-page error stopped a critique helper in the middle of a demo. A named set of single-duty helpers worked in parallel on a dessert-themed product, covering roles such as lead, bake, glow, critique, pixels, and pings. Near the close, a sound-design helper wrote audio notes, including placeholder listen links, back into that same shared workspace. The player ended earlier than the schedule card's evening slot, at 7:53 in the evening Eastern Time, or 4:53 in the afternoon Pacific Time. A sales block in the middle of the day was only sparsely captured.

The third day opened with a game-studio build and later showed marketing-operations and post-sales sessions. Governed choices were on screen. One prompt offered approve or hold on territory-style planning, and another offered an explicit hold that left a web filter alone. A marketing close stressed writing the job clearly before letting a helper run freely. Helper names again came in packs, including a music-themed set and a contact-form set in which one helper was marked as drafting only. A demo lane described a path from daily bookmarks through an agent and into a checked preview link. Chat asked about templates that other people could copy. The day ended with a final presentation and wrap.

Several figures from audience chat were not treated as facts, including an early claim of hundreds of thousands of viewers melting servers and an unverified note about token spend. Spoken dialogue was only partly captured, because the notes come from timed scrubs of the video rather than from a word-for-word transcript. Replay chat on seek was not trustworthy for late timestamps on the first day.

What the product is, how to run it, and the short rule list each have their own pages. This one is the chronicle of the three days.
