# Agentic Engineering, Condensed

Vibe coding raises the floor. More people can produce working software by asking a model for the next chunk and accepting what comes back. Agentic engineering is a different job. It keeps the quality bar that professional software already had, including the rule that you do not ship vulnerabilities just because a helper wrote the code. It also asks whether you can go faster while remaining responsible for the result. You can go faster. The live question is how to coordinate helpers that are powerful, jumpy, and a bit stochastic without letting that bar slip.

The shift became obvious around December, after a year in which agentic coding tools were already useful in pieces that still needed hand edits. Newer models started returning chunks that did not need correction, trust rose with each clean pass, and the loop turned into true vibe coding. Anyone who had filed the tools away as last year's chat novelty was told to look again. The coherent multi-step workflow had started to work.

A useful name for the new programming style is Software 3.0 generation. You hand the helper a piece of text and let it inspect the machine, take intelligent actions, and debug in the loop. The older style spelled out every setup step in ordinary code. Installation notes that used to balloon into brittle scripts become a prompt the helper can interpret against your actual environment. The details of an interface can recede. What does not recede is an understanding of the underlying pieces, such as whether two views share storage or whether you are copying memory you did not mean to copy. Those fundamentals still have to live in the person directing the work.

The helpers behave like strong interns. They recall fine-grained details well. You remain in charge of taste, judgment, aesthetics, and oversight. You decide that identifiers must be unique and that the design has to make sense, and they fill in blanks. That is why a line that made the rounds matters here. You can outsource your thinking, and you cannot outsource your understanding. Models do not currently excel at understanding. If you cannot direct the work, you cannot be a good director. Building a personal wiki from what you read, then asking questions of it, is described as one way to keep processing information rather than only generating more of it.

Jaggedness is the other property to plan around. These systems are not animals with intrinsic motivation, curiosity, or a drive to play. They are closer to summoned ghosts, shaped by data and by reward. Training with verification rewards makes them spike in domains you can check, such as math and code, and stay rough where a check is harder. An old party trick was failing to count letters in a common word. A newer one is recommending that you walk to a car wash 50 meters away because the distance looks small on paper. When the edges stay this uneven, you need to remain in the loop and treat the system as a tool. Verifiability also hints at where automation will move fastest. If you can build your own checked environments, you may be able to train for a niche the big labs are not focused on. Almost anything can be made more checkable, including writing judged by a panel of models, though some checks are much harder than others.

People used to talk about an engineer who was 10 times as productive as the baseline. The claim here is that people who are already good at directing these helpers peak at a good deal more than that 10 times mark, and that the ceiling on the discipline looks high. Hiring is already chasing that skill. The condensed rule is simple to say and easy to violate. Let the floor rise. Keep the bar. Stay responsible for taste and for understanding, even when the thinking that fills the middle has been handed off.
