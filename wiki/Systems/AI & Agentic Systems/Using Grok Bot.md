# Using Grok Bot

Day-to-day use, as shown across three public workshop days in mid-September 2026, is less about collecting more helpers and more about how you talk to the ones you already have. The orientation page covers what the product is. This page covers the loop you actually run.

Start by giving the helper a single duty and writing the hard limits into its profile, where you and anyone watching can see them. A slides helper on the first day carried rules for clean layout, type no smaller than 20 points, and a fixed set of colors. When those rules live only in a one-off prompt, they drift. When they live on the helper, you can audit them.

Ask in the thread for any job that should repeat. On-screen help said recurring tasks are set up that way. A sample request called for a start-of-day file review that listed who edited what. Memory should make a stored preference come back the next time that kind of choice appears, the way a stored color preference reappeared in a design demo.

When the work hits a fork, do not bury the choice in a paragraph of prose. Lay out labeled options. The stage used a trio of choices. You could lock the present direction, keep iterating, or hold. If you pick lock, name who will build. The same shape appeared when a prototype needed an access gate, with choices that included team-only protection, an allowlist tied to existing accounts, or a password. The point was to make the decision visible, not to pick a vendor.

Build the artifact before you let it act on the world. One helper drafted a product spec and locked the markdown before engineering work began. Another built a form and was marked as not sending it. Support-style guidance on the second day blocked an outside message until a review passed. Treat sending, merging, and flipping infrastructure as separate moves from drafting.

Match the reply to your confidence. High confidence can answer from public material, while medium confidence should show its work. Low confidence, or an ambiguous ask, should come back to you. Public pages can be quoted. Internal policy can decide a question and should not be pasted into a place other people will see.

Keep a shared source of truth and notice when it breaks. Helpers on the second day were told to keep a common docs workspace current. When that workspace returned a missing-page error, the critique helper stalled in public. The honest next line is that the connection failed, not a fabricated stand-in for the missing page.

Watch the fleet while it works. An overlay counted how many helpers existed, how many messages had been sent, how many were working, and how many were active. Those counts climbed through the first day from the low teens into the high twenties. The useful habit is to glance at working versus idle, not to chase a higher total.

Scope the request the way you would write a short job description before you let the helper run. A later session warned that bloated context slows the work. If a job has two duties, it is already two jobs.

This loop is the usage page. The short rule list lives next door. The three-day public build, including what was shipped in front of the audience, lives on the event chronicle.

# Grok Bot Primer

The product, as presented at a three-day public workshop in mid-September 2026, is a fleet of named helpers rather than a single chat box. A slide titled as an introduction laid out four pillars. Helpers can run on their own to complete objectives around the clock, including engineering work. They can connect to the tools a team already uses. They can steer first-party cloud coding agents so a workflow can grow past one thread. They keep memory and routines, which means they recall preferences and can take recurring jobs. One demo offered to update a design choice to a preferred color because that preference had already been stored.

A later slide described helpers as colleagues along four lines. Helpers tie tools together to produce an outcome, rather than leaving each connected app as a session-scoped lookup. Long-running context means prior work is not forgotten at the end of a thread. Independence includes their own computers, access to services, the ability to work while you are away, and those recurring jobs. Messaging no longer has to mean waiting your turn in one shared chat.

Specialization is part of the same picture. Early in the first day, profile-level rules were visible on a slides helper, covering layout, a minimum type size of 20 points, and a fixed palette. Other helpers on stage carried single duties such as data, mail, operations, ordering, or a creative lead. Role packs later in the day grouped engineering, founder, product, design, and release work under separate names. The pattern on display was one helper, one job, with the rules living on the helper.

Recurring jobs are treated as a first-class feature. On-screen copy said you can ask, in the thread, for a recurring job to be set up. An earlier composer draft asked for a morning pass over a file, reporting who changed what. Memory and those scheduled passes sit next to each other in the pitch.

An engineering slide claimed a helper could watch main-line checks and deploy pipelines, then step in on failure, fix the break, prove the fix locally, and merge the change. That was presented as a product claim on a slide, not independently verified from the audience seats.

This page is the orientation. Day-to-day operation, including how to pose a fork and how to keep a draft from going out too early, belongs with the usage page. A short rule list belongs with the condensed page. The three workshop days themselves, including the public build and the rising helper counts, belong with the event chronicle.

## Grok Bot, Condensed

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

# Grok Bot, Condensed

A short set of operating rules kept coming up over the mid-September 2026 public workshop. They are not a catalog of every demo, and they are not a personal desk roster.

Give each helper one named job. Generic assistants blurred together on stage. Named roles with a single duty, from a slides specialist to a bake-and-critique set working on one product, made it clearer who should act.

Put the rules on the helper. Format, cadence, and hard limits belonged in the profile, where they could be inspected, rather than only in a prompt you might forget to paste.

Recurring work is something you ask for in the thread. Memory is supposed to persist across sessions. Once a preference is stored, it should reappear when that sort of choice comes around again.

When a fork appears, name the options. The stage used an explicit trio. You could lock the current direction, keep iterating, or hold. If you lock, say who builds.

Draft before anything leaves the building. A locked markdown spec went to engineering before side effects. A form helper built an artifact and was marked as not sending it. The same discipline showed up as an approval gate before a message could be posted outside the thread.

Default external writes and destructive infrastructure changes to a hold. One day showed approve-or-hold on territory-style decisions. Another showed a deliberate hold that left a web filter untouched rather than flipping it.

Answer from what is public. Use internal policy only to decide, and do not paste it out. When confidence is low or the request is ambiguous, hand the question back rather than inventing a reply.

If a connector fails, say that it failed. A missing page stopped a critique helper mid-demo. Inventing the missing page would have been the worse move.

Scope the job the way you would write a job description before you let a helper run freely. Bloated context was called out as a thing that slows the work.

Keep fleet health visible. A running overlay of how many helpers exist, how many are working, and how many actions are in flight was part of the demonstration, not an afterthought.

This list is the short form. What the product is sits on the primer. How to run those rules day to day sits on the usage page. What happened on each workshop day sits on the event chronicle.

## Grok Bot Galaxy

# Grok Bot Galaxy

The workshop ran for three days, from 15 September 2026 through 17 September 2026, as a public livestream with a matching in-person schedule. The first day's replay ran about 8 hours 45 minutes and showed about 1 million views on the player. Schedule pages named guest blocks for engineering, product, founders, sales-shaped teams, support, marketing, and a closing presentation. Faces were not always plated, so some speaker identifications in the notes stay tentative. Later days' exact broadcast addresses were not posted as concrete replay links on the public schedule page that was fetched, and those addresses are not invented here.

The first day mixed table talk, helper overlays, and product slides with a live attempt to stand up a pop-up food business. Chat proposed fry-themed names that were not confirmed as a final brand. A venue-finder prototype appeared, including an access-control prompt and a map-based change listed as the fourteenth pull request. A later screen showed a different event-style name that may have been a one-off label rather than a finished company title. Helper overlays climbed from about 13 helpers in the afternoon samples to about 29 helpers by evening, with message counts moving into the high hundreds in the samples that were captured. Product slides that day introduced four pillars and a colleagues framing, plus an auto-fix claim for failing checks. The stream ended on a branded card after a questions slide and a short wrap.

The second day moved through department-shaped helper packs, including sales-engineering templates. A support lane insisted on public documentation for answers, internal rules that must not be pasted out, and a review before anything was written to an outside channel. A shared docs workspace was treated as the source of truth, and a missing-page error stopped a critique helper in the middle of a demo. A named set of single-duty helpers worked in parallel on a dessert-themed product, covering roles such as lead, bake, glow, critique, pixels, and pings. Near the close, a sound-design helper wrote audio notes, including placeholder listen links, back into that same shared workspace. The player ended earlier than the schedule card's evening slot, at 7:53 in the evening Eastern Time, or 4:53 in the afternoon Pacific Time. A sales block in the middle of the day was only sparsely captured.

The third day opened with a game-studio build and later showed marketing-operations and post-sales sessions. Governed choices were on screen. One prompt offered approve or hold on territory-style planning, and another offered an explicit hold that left a web filter alone. A marketing close stressed writing the job clearly before letting a helper run freely. Helper names again came in packs, including a music-themed set and a contact-form set in which one helper was marked as drafting only. A demo lane described a path from daily bookmarks through an agent and into a checked preview link. Chat asked about templates that other people could copy. The day ended with a final presentation and wrap.

Several figures from audience chat were not treated as facts, including an early claim of hundreds of thousands of viewers melting servers and an unverified note about token spend. Spoken dialogue was only partly captured, because the notes come from timed scrubs of the video rather than from a word-for-word transcript. Replay chat on seek was not trustworthy for late timestamps on the first day.

What the product is, how to run it, and the short rule list each have their own pages. This one is the chronicle of the three days.
