# Grok Bot Primer

The product, as presented at a three-day public workshop in mid-September 2026, is a fleet of named helpers rather than a single chat box. A slide titled as an introduction laid out four pillars. Helpers can run on their own to complete objectives around the clock, including engineering work. They can connect to the tools a team already uses. They can steer first-party cloud coding agents so a workflow can grow past one thread. They keep memory and routines, which means they recall preferences and can take recurring jobs. One demo offered to update a design choice to a preferred color because that preference had already been stored.

A later slide described helpers as colleagues along four lines. Helpers tie tools together to produce an outcome, rather than leaving each connected app as a session-scoped lookup. Long-running context means prior work is not forgotten at the end of a thread. Independence includes their own computers, access to services, the ability to work while you are away, and those recurring jobs. Messaging no longer has to mean waiting your turn in one shared chat.

Specialization is part of the same picture. Early in the first day, profile-level rules were visible on a slides helper, covering layout, a minimum type size of 20 points, and a fixed palette. Other helpers on stage carried single duties such as data, mail, operations, ordering, or a creative lead. Role packs later in the day grouped engineering, founder, product, design, and release work under separate names. The pattern on display was one helper, one job, with the rules living on the helper.

Recurring jobs are treated as a first-class feature. On-screen copy said you can ask, in the thread, for a recurring job to be set up. An earlier composer draft asked for a morning pass over a file, reporting who changed what. Memory and those scheduled passes sit next to each other in the pitch.

An engineering slide claimed a helper could watch main-line checks and deploy pipelines, then step in on failure, fix the break, prove the fix locally, and merge the change. That was presented as a product claim on a slide, not independently verified from the audience seats.

This page is the orientation. Day-to-day operation, including how to pose a fork and how to keep a draft from going out too early, belongs with the usage page. A short rule list belongs with the condensed page. The three workshop days themselves, including the public build and the rising helper counts, belong with the event chronicle.
