# Glossary

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

The glossary defines the terms the wiki uses most, each in the words of the page that explains it. Most come from the learning system, and the rest come from the pages on how Chinese characters work.

## Active Learning

Active learning is working on material rather than taking it in at low effort, for example reading as if you will have to teach it and field hard questions about it later. Learners need a clear sense of what it looks and feels like, or no list of strategies will help. See [[wiki/Concepts/Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming|Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming]].

## Bear Hunter System

The Bear Hunter System is a learning method built on three steps. In Aim you direct your thinking by asking what matters and how things relate. In Shoot you answer those questions and build the map. In Skin you refine the groups until the structure feels surer. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Bear Hunter System|Bear Hunter System]].

## Deep Processing

Deep processing is the brain's ability to turn information into meaningful learning. That learning comes from the thinking the information sets off. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]].

## Dimensions of Learning

The dimensions of learning are the five capabilities that decide how well a person learns. They are retrieval, mindset, deep processing, self-regulation and self-management. Each can be trained separately, and whichever is weakest holds back the rest. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Dimensions of Learning|Dimensions of Learning]].

## Kolbs Experiential Cycle

Kolb's experiential cycle is a structured look back at an experience to work out how to do better. Marginal gains set the direction of improvement, and the cycle is the means of getting there. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management/Kolbs Experiential Cycle|Kolbs Experiential Cycle]].

## Marginal Gains

Marginal gains treat progress as a chain of small improvements. Small steps are easier to hit and fail less often, and they help you choose the next move toward a larger goal. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset/Marginal Gains|Marginal Gains]].

## Metacognition

Metacognition is your view of what your own mind is doing during learning. It works as a loop in which a cue appears, you notice it, and you judge what the cue means and what to do next. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition - The Control Layer]].

## Mindset

Mindset is the filter through which you see situations and respond to them. It colours what you decide and what you do. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Mindset]].

## Retrieval

Retrieval is using and recalling knowledge from memory, whether that means bringing back a fact, explaining something or working through a problem. Knowledge fades all the time, so retrieval practice is what slows the decay. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval|Retrieval]].

## Self-Management

Self-management is setting up the habits, routines and surroundings that keep action steady. Those systems let you stay focused, manage time, energy and attention, avoid procrastination and set priorities. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]].

## Self-Regulation

Self-regulation is the skill of monitoring, steering and refining how you study. If deep processing is the power of a car's engine, self-regulation is the skill of driving it. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]].

## Spaced Interleaved Retrieval

Spaced interleaved retrieval is recalling material from memory at widening, loosely followed intervals, with later sessions covering several earlier days or weeks of material together. Memory always decays somewhat, so some retrieval is always needed, though stronger encoding means less of it. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]].

## WPW

WPW, or whole-part-whole, is a technique for teaching yourself. You view the topic whole first, split it into its parts, then put it back together and view it whole again. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/WPW|WPW]].

## Passive Learning

Passive learning is absorbing material with little mental effort, such as reading a text only to understand what each line says. For most people it is the default state and fills most study time. See [[wiki/Concepts/Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming|Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming]].

## Working Memory

Working memory is the short workspace where new information lands first and where long-term knowledge is pulled back for use. You have about 15 to 30 seconds to make information meaningful there, and its limit is better pictured as weight than as a count of items. See [[wiki/Concepts/Memory Handling|Memory Handling]].

## Functional Component

A functional component is a part of a Chinese character that does a job in that character by expressing sound or meaning. Many resources split characters into pieces that do no such job. See [[wiki/Learning Craft/Rules of Effective Memorization|Rules of Effective Memorization]].

## Form Component

A form component is a character component that uses its pictorial quality to express meaning. It once depicted something, though the modern shape may no longer look like it. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Form Components|Form Components]].

## Meaning Component

A meaning component brings an abstract sense that grew out of a concrete original one. 力 first showed a plow, and its sense widened to effort and strength. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Components|Meaning Components]].

## Sound Component

A sound component works as a prediction tool. It narrows the pronunciation to a range rather than fixing one reading. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]].

## Empty Component

An empty component supplies no sound and no meaning in the character it sits in. That status belongs to the character, not the shape, so the same piece can be functional elsewhere. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]].

## Sound Series

A sound series is a group of characters built on the same sound component. Their readings vary in patterned ways, with initials varying within seven related groups. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Series|Sound Series]].

## Semantic Series

A semantic series, also called a meaning series, groups characters that share a meaning component. Their meanings do not always match the component's own. See [[wiki/Learning Craft/Rules of Effective Memorization|Rules of Effective Memorization]].

## Meaning Tree

A meaning tree lays out a character's senses with the original meaning as its trunk. Extensions branch from it, sound-loan senses are marked so no false bridge is built to the form, and two readings get two trees. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]].

## Original Meaning

The original meaning is the sole sense bound to a character's form. Every later extension can be traced back to it. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]].

## Fence Principle

A trusted note saying two readings are related works like a fence. Learners often reject a valid sound link when the pinyin differs, and once the fence is up the brain can take the link in. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Series|Sound Series]].

## Rebus Principle

The rebus principle borrows an existing character for its sound so it can write a different word. It was one answer to the limit on how many words could be drawn. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]].

## Pipelining (Character Intake)

Pipelining spreads a character's form, meaning and sound over different days. Characters go through a three-day cycle in groups of three to five, and on the third day the written form is attached to a word that is already overlearned. See [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The Pipelining Strategy|The Pipelining Strategy]].

## Encoding

Encoding is the initial work of making knowledge stick. It is the counterpart of retrieval, and efficiency and accuracy both need the two together. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]].

## Prestudy

Prestudy covers any study done in advance of a main learning event, whether a class or a long dedicated session. It gives new information somewhere to land, which makes later detail easier to hold on to. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Prestudy|Prestudy]].

## Inquiry-Based Learning

Inquiry-based learning is posing problems for yourself and then solving them, letting curiosity steer what you take in and how you think. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Inquiry-Based Learning|Inquiry-Based Learning]].

## Locus of Control

The locus of control covers the actions within your own reach that improve your chances of the result you want. The result itself, such as an exam score, lies outside it. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset/Locus of Control|Locus of Control]].

## Importance-Based Chunking

Importance-based chunking is grouping information and then judging the value of the groups. That judgment is the part of chunking that drives efficiency. See [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Importance-Based Chunking|Importance-Based Chunking]].
