# Thinking About Thinking Bank

Source bank for a page that does not exist yet, on how a person climbs the levels of thinking. The ladder itself is in [[wiki/Research/Levels of Thinking Bank|Levels of Thinking Bank]], which also holds the corpus table and the method note. Kinds: **R** reasoning, **X** the speaker's own example, **W** a claim about the world, **L** the speaker's own life.

**This bank produces facts. It does not draft prose.**

## The subject in plain words

The way up is to think about your own thinking. Sit with a thought or a feeling and ask why it is there, then why that matters, until you reach where it came from. Listen for the lies your own mind tells you. See your mind as something that happens to you, the way hunger does. Then watch it build a story in the moment and choose whether to keep the story.

## Claim ledger

- **M1** (R, X, L, v13, v12). The best way to move up is to sit with your thoughts and figure out where they come from. Three worked runs. Embarrassment at showing scribbly drawings to 200,000 people: why, a negative impact; what impact, people leaving or saying bad things; why bad, lost opportunities; how do you know, no evidence, it has gone fine; so nothing is wrong with it. Choosing partners who will not stay: why, not believing he can support them; why does that matter, they want more support; why, it hurts when they leave; can it change, tried a better job; then it gets fuzzy and the pattern has not been fixed. Making short videos: why, to teach; why, to change behavior; why, bad experiences; what was bad, they made me feel like this; is that a good reason, in his case yes.
- **M2** (R, v1). Thinking about thinking is constantly using the conscious mind to ask what is going on in the rest of the mind: what am I thinking and feeling and why. It is different at each level and always the same act.
- **M3** (R, v1). Listen for your own brain lying to you. Common lies: I am always the good guy; I deserve something more than other people; this fact that makes me uncomfortable must be false, how do I use words to make it look false. Catch yourself and ask why would I tell myself that. Most people write a story with the lower parts of the mind and believe it without questions, and the lower parts are easier to manipulate; then anyone who disagrees is an insane and evil monster.
- **M4** (R, v1). Instead of saying your story is awesome and being done, ask why is my story different from yours, what am I missing, what are you missing. That is how you absorb the perspectives that make a high-level thinker.
- **M5** (R, X, v1). I am not my mind, built from I am not my body. Cut off a finger and you are still you; get fat, thin, old, still you; so you operate the body, you are not it. Forget how to cook eggs or forget third grade and you are still you. When hungry you say I am hungry, not I am hunger; hunger is here now and gone later. Thoughts are the same: you experience libertarianism or fanhood the way you experience hunger, and you never become them however long you feel them. "I am feeling a little libertarian today, maybe it will pass." Once you see your mind happening to you, you see everyone else's mind happening to them, and you remember your mind happening to you over time, which is where levels come from. Then you plan for who people are instead of pretending to change them: you cannot should the moon into being an owl. The speaker's first step into this at 20: reading two opposite political books back to back to see what it did to his mind, saying I am not my opinions.
- **M6** (R, X, v1). Construct aware, in the moment. Looking at a man in a hat: at a low level, people in that hat are jerks, so he is one. At a higher level, I am noticing my own belief that this is a jerk hat, I do not need to engage with it, I can wait for new information and build my picture of him differently. That gives power over how you invent an idea of a person. When you see how you are telling yourself a story you can let it go and ask whether there is a better story to tell from the same bits.
- **M7** (R, X, L, v4). The impulse loop. You feel an impulse that is not good for you. You notice it. You evaluate whether to act. You imagine the feelings of living out that choice. Done enough, this reconditions the impulse so it no longer looks good; the same principle as admitting a problem and then recovering. Feelings tell you what you want, not what you should do. A woman who wants to be attracted to the sensible man and is attracted to the lifted truck is asking the right question, is this something I should want. A partner who felt the pull of a richer man, noticed it, and told him at once. The more you slow down and spend time on it, the higher your thoughts go.
- **M8** (R, X, v10). Where every thought starts. Not with reasoning. First you perceive light, color, shape, sound. Then you develop a feeling about it. Then you think about what you could or should do about the feeling. Then you put that in the context of what people around you do, what is socially appropriate, if you have that skill. Very rarely do you check whether it is a reasonable thing to do. Most of a day is absorbing stimuli, reacting, and acting on the reaction. A woman who saw "men are bad" on her phone, had a feeling about her relationship, and then stepped back to check whether her view was correct. What you watch controls you if you never think about it. The check: should I, and how do I know.
- **M9** (R, L, v1). The question is the answer. Everything you are looking for is already in your mind and you find it by asking: what do I want, why, is that a good reason, if so why am I not doing it, what am I scared of, why. "I don't know what to do" becomes "I want to know what to do," which is the first step. If you want to be there, being on the way there is where you want to be. Accept, surrender, let go does not mean stop trying; it means I am at the part where I am trying, and that is enough. The wise man's sayings sound obvious because you forgot the first step while worrying about the steps not here yet.
- **M10** (R, L, v12). Shadow work is recognizing thoughts you did not know you had. A feeling points to a thought you have all the time. Journaling on a prompt: "I always feel like the weird one," "hiding is how I escape," "being inside an invincible fortress brings me peace."
- **M11** (R, v12, v1). Each level up is one more layer: thinking about them thinking about someone thinking. Moving from level to level is different each time and is always thinking about thinking.
- **M12** (L, v1). The speaker's own transitions: a sudden one at 12; slow growth through high school; the first step into seeing his mind from outside at 20. He remembers the transitions and says the top feels like another one.
