## The subject in plain words

At the sixth level of thinking every view is valid, so the rule becomes include everyone everywhere all the time. When that rule includes people who do not include, the place falls to the bottom. The seventh level's answer is a place for everything, not the same place for everything. Underneath sits a wider claim: people have stopped growing up, meaning stopped taking responsibility for their own actions, and the world they live in was built by people who did.

## Claim ledger

### E. Everybodyism

- **E1** (W, v3, v1). We built a world at a high level of thinking and are no longer growing to meet it. Adults used to be expected at four or five, able to say we disagree, let us find a way around it. The world was built at about four or five; the new generation is stuck at about three.
- **E2** (R, v3). Level six's idea, include and validate everyone, is being abused by people at two, three, and four who do not understand inclusion. What two and three hear is "we can do whatever we want now, there are no rules." What four hears is "you had better be inclusive or else." Six defends them even when they do it wrong, because six hates the idea of levels and wants everyone equal. When told people have different abilities, six says give them more help, which is dysfunctional because it removes the need to grow.
- **E3** (W, v3). People at six are usually smarter than average with more money and power, so they are insulated from the consequences of their actions. They want you to deal with the man smoking in your house; they do not have to. They want total inclusion but do not include anyone who does not want total inclusion.
- **E4** (R, X, v1). Everybodyism: insisting that everyone gets included everywhere. It destroys everything it touches. One way of life up here, another over there; insist both go in the same place and it all collapses to the bottom and turns to mud. A writing group worked on one script for eighteen months because every time it was final someone wanted a change; five men from one neighborhood could not agree on a movie because every part of everyone had to fit. The fix: each writes his own script; on my script I listen to your ideas and if I do not like them we do not do them; on yours you need not take mine.
- **E5** (R, X, v1, v3). The mosque and the gay bar. Level four says one is good and the other bad, everywhere, forever. Level six says they can get along with more understanding, when more understanding is what neither wants, and the outcome is only the one true way, because it all falls down. Level seven says there is a place for each, not the same place; whoever moves into the other's place conforms. Level six hears "everything in its right place" as coming from level four and meaning bomb them back to the stone age. Putting the mosque next to the gay bar and saying now get along causes damage.
- **E6** (R, v1). The contradiction. Anyone can be what they want only if they agree that everyone can, and almost nobody agrees. You are tolerating people who do not tolerate you. You cannot force people to grow; sometimes they do not want to and sometimes they lack what they need. The speaker guesses level six sits in the top 20% of IQ and is trying to build a world for the 80% who do not want it. The Tower of Babel: everyone in one place, then nobody can talk to anyone.
- **E7** (R, X, v3). Level seven's answer: everyone in a place that works for them and where they work for everyone around them. Alignment, harmony, integrate: things work together the way they want to, not the way you want them to. A city where people from everywhere live and work and nobody fights over who owns the country, because you come, follow the rules, and are included. A pastoral people who are doing fine and need no notes, and who also should not move into the speaker's neighborhood and announce this is your life now. Level three says everyone deals with what I want; four says everyone should follow my one true way; five says everyone should figure out their own way like me; six says everyone should embrace every way like me; seven says no more me.
- **E8** (R, X, v3). The man who smokes in your house does not need inclusion. He needs to learn that other people do not think it is cool. If he never does, he cannot come in. Turning it around on the host ("why do you not like smoke, do your parents smoke, you are lucky to have a house, maybe make him feel welcome") is what the inclusion talk amounts to, and it stops him ever growing up. If you are not allowed to say what does not belong in your life, it is unfair to you and he never learns. In some places it is a crime to disagree.
- **E9** (R, v1). A generation of deservers. Providers build the world by putting in work and getting value back from nature. Deservers soak up that value and give back little or nothing. When too many people are stuck at the bottom levels the place becomes undeveloped, unstable, and chaotic, because everyone is focused on winning and being on top.

### M. The maturity crisis

- **E10** (R, W, v6). Immaturity is refusing to accept responsibility for one's own actions. Another way to say it: not being able to use your own intelligence without guidance from outside. Maturity is growing up, contributing value, getting paid, and using your own choices to decide what to do with it. Taking responsibility for how you react, not for what happens to you, grows your inner guidance, your competence, and your confidence.
- **E11** (R, W, v6). Hard times create strong men, strong men create good times, good times create weak men, weak men create hard times; "you are here" at the weak-men point, in the easiest times there have ever been. Times get good when people make and do; times get bad when people want more for doing less: equality of gimme rather than equality of let me contribute. The masculine, creative principle in everyone makes value; the feminine, consuming principle decides who gets it. Consumption cannot be in charge of production or the battery dies. Men earn more, women spend more. Feminine politics pushes redistribution, taxes, handouts, which removes men's incentive to try, so men drop out, so women say men cannot support us and ask for more from the state. Men have to grow up first because women do not have to.
- **E12** (W, X, v6). The responsibility asymmetry (Four Quadrants Bank, Q10). Victim mentality, pronouns, oppressed-group labels in a profile are collective, needs-based orientations that place a person in the group that benefits from redistribution. Anyone seen as an oppressor has responsibility; anyone seen as oppressed has none. Women are told their problems are structural; men are told every problem outside them is theirs to fix.
- **E13** (W, v6). A moral-development finding the speaker cites: the average adolescent female reached stage three, good-boy-nice-girl morality, and the average adolescent male reached stage four, law-and-order morality, and males were more likely to go on to the post-conventional stages. The objection he cites: that there are different scales for males and females, which he says was asserted and never proved. He adds that boys have an easier time with the odd levels and girls with the even ones, and that he knows women at the higher levels, fewer of them. His inference: as women's preferred party keeps winning and women fill more seats, society falls from law-and-order morality to nice-girl morality, and that fall is the cause of every major problem in the last thirty years: homeless camps not cleared, borders not closed, qualifications dropped, bail removed, hiring by niceness.
- **E14** (R, v6). Three ways to be in the crisis: complicit out of fear, checked out and comfortable, or the one telling the truth, uncomfortable and the only way that makes things better. Just say true things even when uncomfortable. Stop asking for inclusion and become something people need. Do not give it away until you get what you want. Nobody is coming to save you.
- **E15** (L, v6). The speaker's own life drawn as a curve: flat stretches dragged along by life, a low point with a worthless degree, a job through a friend from graduate school, a bad breakup he let happen because of self-image problems from a difficult early life, twenty years of consciously taking control of how he thinks and acts, and a life now getting better faster than ever. Value created in yourself cannot be taken away. Peter Pan syndrome: a tall child. Young men at the self-interested stage see only the good-boy stage ahead, which looks bad because society now expects a lot and gives little back; beyond it you learn to see value differently and keep what you make.
- **E16** (R, X, v6). Adding versus subtracting energy. One man chops trees and another pays $100 a tree; they agree and the world gets better. A woman says her presentation is worth $150,000 a year, the boss says no, and she asks why he is attacking her and sues. The second is the welfare gang. You cannot tell the world to love you as you are; you have to offer something.
- **E17** (W, v1, v6). Political claims. People on the right can explain what the left thinks and feels; the left cannot do the same in return and says the right is insane and evil for no reason. A clip of people celebrating the killing of a political speaker as an example of treating a person as not people. Feminine politics as a blue wave. A figure the speaker says he read, $450 billion spent on illegal immigrants in a year. Small improvements add up: a man unhappy on a tiny motorcycle and happy on a slightly larger toy car.
- **E18** (R, X, L, v14). A woman messages a man she hooked up with eight years earlier; he blocks her; she sends him a dollar to ask why; he has a wife and children; she wakes up mortified and asks for worse stories to feel better. The speaker's client from a traditional culture overseas lives by rules the West no longer has. The West's center of gravity has moved into radical relativism, group norms break, and development falls back to egocentric self-protection.
