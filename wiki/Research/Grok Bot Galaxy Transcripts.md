# Grok Bot Galaxy Transcripts

## What the three days were

Day 1. Matt Palmer, Lauren Tan, and Roshan Sadanani started a company from an empty GitHub org, Ship by Thursday. Roman Ugarte's Grok Bot 101 showed several helpers on one computer, Teach a task, and approval gates. Lauren installed Dr Eggbot and made a chief of staff named Steve. The product idea was still a food pop-up.

Day 2. Overnight agents told them the pop-up would not fit the remaining time. They switched to a game studio. Working title Cupcake. Lauren taught `/poteto-mode` from pstack: a prototype playbook, a multi-model swarm, and full autopilot. Comment Sicko, the comment stripper, was explained. Notion was called the source of truth. Slack listened for messages.

Day 3. Overnight autopilot was said to have landed on the order of 100 to 150 pull requests. The public name became Thursday Arena, thursdayarena.com. A play-test helper ran when CI was green, on the Vercel preview, before merge. A bad SQL change from the factory took production down for a stretch. Stripe's Dan Hill showed one-time cards with a spend approval. They did not show a price list for Grok Bot itself.

## What applies here

This account already does the part that matches the shared computer.

- One job per helper, and a line that says what the helper never does. A helper with every job hides a break.
- The product of a helper is a report. Mail, cards, shopping, refunds, and ads stay off the computer. The studio's Email Ethan, refund bots, and ad auction are the opposite of this account.
- Work between helpers goes through a file, not a chat. They used Notion. This account uses files in a repository. The reason is the same: the chat is disposable, and a long chat makes the next scheduled run cost more.
- A method is taught once, saved as a skill, and only then put on a clock. Teach a task records the screen. That is the same order as [[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Using Grok Bot|Using Grok Bot]].
- Code is written on a machine that is not the shared Grok Bot computer. They used Cursor Cloud Agents and pstack. This account uses a Cloud Agent and merges the pull request itself. Their chief of staff merging to main, and "no pull requests, ship to main," is not this account.
- A screen change is checked by opening the running app. Their Play helper play-tested the Vercel preview before merge. That is the job of `/create-verification-skill` and of [[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Cursor Team Kit|Cursor Team Kit]]'s `control-ui`. The harvest reader is the first app here where that check is still done by hand.
- Do not add a research helper, a QA helper, and a marketing helper on day one. Add the next helper when the current reports show a gap. Steward is the report-and-backup helper. It does not route work.

## What does not apply

- A chief of staff that holds every login and hires the other helpers. One shared computer makes that helper a copy of every secret.
- Dialing approval down until the helper sends mail, spends money, or merges. Approval stays on for anything that leaves the account.
- Putting 1Password, a VPN, or a card on the cloud computer.
- An overnight factory of a hundred pull requests. The weekly allowance, and a person reading each change, is the limit here.
- The game, the contest, the free month, and the ad auction. Those were their company, not a way to run this one.
