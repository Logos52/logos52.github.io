## The subject in plain words

Between 2014 and about 2021 a set of cultural positions became standard on the American and British left. Pronoun rules, diversity training, trans women in women's sport, and the treatment of disagreement as an offence. Since 2024 the same parties and the same people have been backing away from those positions in public.

The question is why.

The usual answer is that the right won an argument. The source under examination gives a different one: the left imported a large population from poor countries, that population does not share the cultural positions, it is now big enough to decide elections, and so the left is dropping the positions to keep it. On that account the culture war was never the main event. It was cover for a population change, and the anti-woke turn is a defeat for the right rather than a victory, because the people who ended wokeness will not stop there.

That is the claim. This bank tests it.

The owner already holds the immigration half. His positions note says the 2020 arrivals should be sent back, that people who arrive should not get a vote over local policy, and that belonging depends on how far someone has taken on the way of life here — a matter of degree, not a yes or no (`personal/2026-08-17-positions.md`, Belonging). He does not own the source's vocabulary. *Woke 1.0*, *Woke 2.0*, *the third world* as a single political actor, *dissident right*, and *based* are the source's words and a stranger cannot enter them. Every one needs defining before use, and two of them should probably not be used at all.

**One direct conflict, and it is load-bearing.** At 4:30 the source says that a rehabilitated leftist who advocates civic nationalism is "politically exiled." The owner's own position, in his own words on the same note, is *"Being American comes from what a person holds and does, and it can be taken on by someone whose parents came from somewhere else."* That is civic nationalism. His keystone position — *"Who you are is something you acquire by what you learn to do, not something you are born as"* — is incompatible with the source's descent frame, which sorts a population by where its grandparents came from and predicts its politics from that.

The record of his interviews puts it plainer still: the admission test is capability of giving, met either by coming from a culture known for it or by individual capacity (`01 - Workbench/basic-logic-intake/OWNER-POSITIONS.md`, Immigration). He does estimate at group level when the flow is mass, and he names groups on both sides of the assimilation line. The basis is conduct and net contribution, not ancestry. That distinction is the whole difference between his position and the source's, and a page that blurs it misrepresents him.

**A second divergence, quieter and more useful.** He already holds an account of why immigrant-origin voters lean left, and it is not the source's: *"Those groups vote Democrat because that coalition is tolerant of the conduct."* On his account the causation runs from the party's permissiveness to the vote. On the source's it runs from the vote to the party's positions. Those are opposite arrows on the same correlation, and the page has to pick one or test both.

This is the third page in the lane where the owner's conclusion parts from the source's, and it fits the pattern already recorded: it happens **where the source takes a harder line than he does** (`PAGE-LIST.md`, "The divergence pattern"). On those pages the source's material is input, not the spine. The bank cannot simply ledger the cuts.

**One biographical fact the page's framing should account for.** He was on the far left — he names Bernie Sanders and AOC — and moved (`OWNER-POSITIONS.md`, Personal context). The video's trigger is an AOC statement. He is not a hostile outsider to the position being described in this argument; he held it and left it. That is recorded here as context for the entrance, and it is not an instruction to write about him.

**What this routes.** The page cannot open in the source's vocabulary, cannot adopt descent as the sorting variable, and cannot treat "the third world" as one political actor without arguing for it first — which the evidence below does not support.

## Terminology

Each defined so a stranger can enter it. No term is defined by whose system it belongs to.

| Term | What it means | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Woke 1.0** | The cultural-left politics of roughly 2014–2021: pronoun rules, diversity training, "defund the police", trans inclusion in women's categories, speech treated as an offence. Named retrospectively, mostly by opponents. Adopted on the left in August 2026. | A party programme. Nobody ever ran on it under that name. |
| **Woke 2.0** | The source's name for a successor politics that drops the cultural demands and keeps the economic and immigration ones. | An established term. The label is the source's; the repositioning under it is real and documented separately. |
| **The third world** | The source's term for immigrant-origin populations from Africa, the Middle East, South Asia and Latin America, used as one political actor. Originally a Cold War term for states aligned with neither bloc. | A measured category. No statistical agency publishes it, and the source's argument needs it to behave as one group. |
| **Dissident right** | The online anti-immigration, ethnonationalist right, which defines a nation by descent and treats mainstream conservative parties as part of the problem. | Mainstream conservatism. The distinction is the point of the word. |
| **Civic nationalism** | National membership defined by citizenship, shared political institutions and values, open to anyone who takes them on. The standard contrast is ethnic nationalism, which defines membership by descent. | What the source says at 4:30. The source defines it as "a nation defined not by its people, but rather by the state bureaucracy," which is not the term's meaning in any reference source. |
| **Based** | Online-right approval for someone who states an unpopular position without softening it. | An argument. It marks a stance, not a claim. |
| **Decisive voting bloc** | A group large enough *and* cohesive enough that its vote settles an outcome. Two conditions, and both have to hold. | A large group. Size without cohesion decides nothing. |
| **Minority-European** | People of European descent falling below half of a country's population. | A published statistic. Most European states do not measure descent, so the quantity the source names is largely not counted. |
| **Migration background** | A statistical category used in Germany, the Netherlands, Austria and elsewhere: a person born abroad, or with at least one parent born abroad. | A measure of descent or ethnicity. It counts European-origin migrants in and third-generation descendants out, so it moves in both directions against "minority-European." |
| **Majority-minority** | A population where no single group holds above 50%. | A statement about who holds power, or about any group's behaviour. |
| **Two-tier policing** | The claim that enforcement or sentencing is systematically lighter for one group than another for the same conduct. | Documented disparity in either direction. The word names a claim; the direction has to be shown. |
| **Descriptive representation** | Running a candidate who shares the ethnicity of the voters being courted. | Evidence that the voters vote as a bloc. It is a campaign tactic, and it is used by every party in every country with concentrated minorities. |
| **The Great Replacement** | The claim that the population change is deliberately engineered by political elites for electoral gain. | The demographic change itself. The change is measured; the intention is the separate claim, and the source makes it at 7:11. |

**Verdict: shown, and this is the video's trigger.** On ABC's *This Week*, around 2026-08-09, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez said: *"I have a local city councilman that has this saying: 'Woke 1 was crazy.'"* She added that the rhetoric of that period is *"not rhetoric that we would use today"* while calling the underlying discussions *"quite fruitful."* — <https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/15/nation/aoc-woke-midterms-2026-2028/> · <https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/aoc-woke-democrats>.

#### A.ii The demographic engine


**C11 (1:15).** The left carried out the mass importation of peoples from the third world.
**Verdict: partly shown, and the agent is misattributed.** Large-scale migration from outside Europe into Western Europe and North America is not in dispute. Attributing it to left parties is. The United Kingdom's record intake happened under a Conservative government: net migration reached **860,000** in the year ending December 2023, with the Conservatives in office until July 2024, and fell to 431,000 in the year ending December 2024 under Labour — <https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/bulletins/longterminternationalmigrationprovisional/yearendingdecember2024>. A claim that names one side as the author of a policy both sides ran is a claim about blame, not about the policy.

**C12 (1:19).** These populations, by themselves, tend to be very hostile to woke ideology.
**Verdict: partly shown. The level is real. The persistence the argument needs is contradicted.** This is the most heavily evidenced row in the bank and it splits cleanly in two.

*For — the level.* Origin-country attitudes are far from Western ones. Pew's 34-country survey (13 May to 2 October 2019, n=38,426) on whether homosexuality should be accepted by society: **Nigeria 7%, Indonesia 9%, Tunisia 9%, Turkey 25%, India 37%** — against **Netherlands 92%, Sweden 94%, UK 86%, France 86%, Germany 86** — <https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2020/06/25/global-divide-on-homosexuality-persists/>. Pakistan's last reading was **2%** in 2013 — <https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2013/06/04/the-global-divide-on-homosexuality/>.

The gap survives migration. ICM for Channel 4, fieldwork 25 April to 31 May 2015, 1,081 British Muslims face to face plus a 1,008-adult control: **18% of Muslims agreed homosexuality should be legal in Britain and 52% disagreed**, against **73% of the whole adult public** and 67% of Christians — <https://www.channel4.com/press/news/c4-survey-and-documentary-reveals-what-british-muslims-really-think> · control figure at <https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/publication/documents/2018-03/a-review-of-survey-research-on-muslims-in-great-britain-ipsos_0.pdf>. **Caveat a careful reader needs:** the Muslim sample was drawn only from areas where the census put Muslim density at 20% or above, and academics criticised that at the time — <http://blog.policy.manchester.ac.uk/ethnicity/2016/04/misleading-irresponsible-and-dangerous-why-phillips-and-co-should-apologise-for-what-british-muslims-really-think/>.

Koopmans's six-country survey (Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Sweden; 2008; n≈9,000) found **57% of Muslim respondents against 11% of Christian natives** agreeing "I don't want to have homosexuals as friends," and 44% of Muslims against under 4% of Christian natives endorsing all three fundamentalism items — <https://www.npdata.be/BuG/255-Fundamentalisme/Article-Koopman.pdf>. Koopmans himself states the sample covers only Turkish and Moroccan origin groups and is "not representative for the whole Muslim population in the countries of study."

The Dutch SCP measured it group by group in 2020 (Survey Integratie Migranten, aged 15+). Percent agreeing "it is good that homosexuals may marry": **no migration background 85, Iranian 76, Surinamese 76, Caribbean 73, Polish 51, Moroccan 40, Turkish 40, Somali 33** — <https://www.scp.nl/publicaties/publicaties/2022/05/17/opvattingen-over-seksuele-en-genderdiversiteit-in-nederland-en-europa-2022>.

*Against — the persistence.* Four independent designs find movement toward acceptance across generations.

**The Dutch generational table is the cleanest single test.** SCP, *Gevestigd maar niet thuis*, 11 October 2022, Table 4.7, agreement that homosexuals may marry, first generation → second generation: **Turkish 31 → 49. Moroccan 35 → 45. Somali 28 → 62. Iranian 74 → 92.** Native Dutch 86 — <https://www.scp.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2022/10/11/gevestigd-maar-niet-thuis.-eerste-bevindingen-uit-de-survey-integratie-migranten-sim2020/gevestigd-maar-niet-thuis.pdf>. Both halves of that table are real: the second generation moves substantially, and it remains 37 and 41 points below the native figure.

**US Muslims doubled in ten years.** Acceptance of homosexuality: **27% (2007), 39% (2011), 52% (2017)**; among the foreign-born, 26 → 49; among those saying religion is very important, 19 → 47 — <https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/07/26/political-and-social-views/>. By 2017 US Muslims were **18 points more accepting than white evangelical Protestants** (34%).

**The origin countries are liberalising too.** Between 2013 and 2019, India +22 points, Turkey +16, Nigeria +6, Indonesia +6, Kenya +6 — same Pew source. Arab Barometer Wave VII found agreement that men make better political leaders fell 15 points in Tunisia between 2016 and 2021 — <https://www.arabbarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/ABVII_Gender_Report-ENG.pdf>.

**The Swedish panel shows why the gap stays open anyway.** la Roi and Mood, following 5,434 youths from age 14: *"we find a divergence in attitudes over time, but not because immigrant-background youth become less liberal: Their views do become more liberal, but majority youth see an even stronger change in the same direction"* — <https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/267697597/Attitudes_in_motion.pdf>. Norris and Inglehart reached the same conclusion from pooled World Values Survey data: second-generation status predicts more liberal sexual morality, and *"younger Westerners are adopting modern values even more swiftly than their Muslim peers"* — <http://nrs.harvard.edu/urn-3:HUL.InstRepos:4481625>.

*The defensible version of the claim.* The gap does not close. Attitudes do change. The video asserts the second, and only the first is supported.

*And the category is wrong.* Dutch Iranians — a developing-world, Muslim-majority origin — sit level with native Dutch. Dutch Poles, a European origin, sit below Surinamese and Caribbean Dutch. la Roi and Mood note the same for Balkan and Central/Eastern European origin youth in Sweden and say so explicitly against the MENA framing. **Origin-country religiosity predicts the pattern; "third world" does not.**

*What no dataset measures.* Nothing above measures "hostility to woke ideology." Every figure measures acceptance of homosexuality, same-sex marriage, transgender policy, or women's roles. Those are related and not identical, and in the United States the transgender items behave differently by race than the homosexuality items do.

**C17 (2:15).** High fertility among foreigners, because of their own distinct culture.

*Against.* Welfare recipients respond *less* to money than the general population. The authors: this "speaks against the widely held assumption that the fertility patterns of welfare recipients might be excessively motivated by financial concerns. The opposite seems to be the case." — <https://doku.iab.de/discussionpapers/2022/dp0122.pdf>. In Israel new immigrants were the most responsive group of all, and since 1990s arrivals had *lower* fertility than natives, the allowance moved them toward the native rate. The two largest natural experiments on immigrant welfare generosity, both Danish, never measured fertility at all. **No study shows welfare generosity driving high foreign fertility in Europe, which is what the claim asserts.**

#### A.iv The bloc, and what it is said to want

Within "Hispanic," Cuban Americans backed Trump 68–23 in the 2024 FIU Cuba Poll while Puerto Rican-heavy Osceola County moved only 1.45 points. **Framing by insinuation.** The comparison is made without being made, so there is nothing to test. It also does argumentative work: it discredits the one path in the video's own taxonomy that the source otherwise treats as tolerable.

**F6 (5:29).** Woke 2.0 is the left signalling to the third-world faction that they are allies and will abandon the positions that faction dislikes.
**Unverifiable as a motive, and its factual half fails.** The abandonment of cultural positions is documented (C19, C33). Who it is aimed at is a motive claim with nothing behind it, and the "while keeping immigration intact" half is contradicted by the record in C21.

**F7 (7:11).** For many this was the plan all along. Leftist leaders knew this would happen when they imported the third world, and the alternative — that some of the most powerful people on the planet were ignorant — is hard to believe.
**Unverifiable, and the argument offered for it is a fallacy the method page already names.** "I find the alternative hard to believe" makes the speaker's confidence the evidence. This is the intentional version of the demographic claim, and it is the step that separates an observation about population change from the Great Replacement claim. The closest documentary support is the Neather episode: Andrew Neather, a former Labour adviser, wrote in the Evening Standard in October 2009 that immigration policy was *"intended – even if this wasn't its main purpose – to rub the Right's nose in diversity and render their arguments out of date."* He then clarified in the same paper that *"the main goal was to allow in more migrant workers at a point when…the booming economy was running up against skills shortages"* and that the political purpose was *"a subsidiary political purpose… I was not comfortable with that. But it wasn't the main point at issue."* — <https://spectator.com/article/the-neather-clarification/>. That is the strongest single document in circulation for the intentionality claim, and it is one adviser's recollection, walked back by its author.
**Note for the page.** `The Organized Minority` already argues that coordination without conspiracy explains elite behaviour that looks coordinated, and the lane has refused an intentional-elite claim once before on the same kind of evidence (`banks/Liberalism as Liberation-bank.md`, R2: "Do not smuggle intentional-elite in as proven"). That refusal binds here.

**F8 (7:28).** There was no culture war in the 2010s. There was a culture feint.
**Unverifiable.** A feint requires someone feinting, so this is F7 restated as a metaphor. The observable part — that a large demographic change occurred while public attention was elsewhere — needs nobody to have arranged it.

**F9 (7:57).** Leftist politicians never were Woke 1.0. They took on the facade that would get them elected, "because that's what a politician is."
**Unverifiable, and self-sealing.** Sincere advocacy and cynical advocacy leave the same public record, so no evidence could separate them. It also proves too much: applied consistently it dissolves the video's own claim that the left "believed" these populations would parrot the liberal worldview (F3). A movement cannot be both naive about its imports and cynical about its principles in the same argument.

**F10 (8:14).** A new demographic of voters has taken over the woke crowd's role as the base of leftist political support. The liberal was a useful stepping stone whose services are no longer required.
**Contradicted on the arithmetic.** Naturalized citizens were 9% of the 2024 US electorate and split 51–47 (C22). A group at 9%, splitting nearly evenly, has not replaced anyone as a base. **Not checked here:** the share of the Democratic coalition still held by college-educated white voters. The page should not assert the comparison until someone runs it.

## What the ledger adds up to

A reading of the evidence for whoever writes the page. Not a page thesis.

**The argument has four links and they are not equally strong.**

1. **Woke 1.0 is in retreat.** Largely shown for named individuals and party strategists, contradicted for party institutions, and two of the video's own examples fail — C6 is unbacked and C7 is contradicted.
2. **A large demographic change is underway.** Real in direction and record-setting for these countries (C34). The *category* the video uses is not what the statistics measure, no official European projection forecasts the crossing it names, and the one projection that does (Goodwin) rests on three assumptions any of which moves the date decades (P2).
3. **Immigrant-origin populations hold the cultural positions the video attributes to them.** Shown for the level of first-generation attitudes and contradicted for the persistence the argument needs (C12).
4. **Therefore link 2 caused link 1.** This link fails, and it fails six different ways.

**Why link four fails.**

- **No document connects them.** No strategy memo, vote, or statement by any party official was found (G3).
- **The arithmetic is against it.** Naturalized citizens were 9% of the 2024 US electorate and split 51–47 (C22).
- **The group is not a bloc.** Latino men swung 33 points to Trump while Black women moved 2 points to the Democrats in the same election (C22).
- **The attitude data runs backwards.** On the flagship question, white Americans are *more* opposed to trans athletes than Hispanic, Black or Asian Americans (C22).
- **The timing is wrong.** The opinion shift moved 7 points nationally in four years, driven by Democrats and independents. No demographic change produces that (C33).
- **The instruments were right-wing state power.** An executive order, two supreme courts, a sports federation, and federal pressure on corporate sponsors (C4, C7, C29, C33).

**The Woke 2.0 model fails on its own terms.** Its "keeps immigration and welfare intact" half is contradicted across Europe: Denmark, the UK, Germany, Sweden, Austria and Finland all tightened immigration in law, and Denmark cut immigrant welfare (C21). The one party that kept immigration open — Spain's PSOE — also kept its trans law and sued a regional government to defend it. **The two halves the video needs to move in opposite directions move together.** And the phrase itself was coined by a games journalist on Bluesky, means the opposite of what the video says it means, and has never been used by a left-wing politician (C19).

**The strongest thing in the video is not its thesis.** It is the observation at 1:37 that a party can admit an electorate whose values it does not share and then find itself governed by that choice. Hamtramck, Dearborn, Birmingham, and the five UK seats lost in 2024 are real instances of it (C13). That claim is the composition lever `Democracy as Sacred Cow` already argues, it needs none of the intentionality, none of the single-bloc assumption, and none of the "only two positions matter" frame, and it is defensible in that reduced form.

**The weakest thing is the frame, not any fact.** F1 does the work the facts are asked to do, and nothing could show it false. A page that grants it has conceded the argument before examining anything.

**One correction the page owes its reader.** Both directions of causation are live. The source says the new electorate forced the left to drop its cultural positions. The owner's own stated view is that immigrant-origin voters go to the left because the left tolerates the conduct. Those are opposite arrows on one correlation, and the evidence in this bank settles neither.

## Gaps

Six, and only where the gap changes what a reader would do.

**G1. The source's own evidence is not in the corpus.** At 2:03 the video says *"On screen, you're seeing some of the statistics on this"* — and the vault copy is text. The figures behind the video's central premise were shown, not spoken, so they cannot be checked against what it actually displayed. **What it changes:** the page cannot cite the video's numbers, and any statement about what it got right or wrong on demographics is a statement about the sentence, not the chart. Closing this takes one person watching 1:47–2:10 and writing down what is on screen.

**G2. No measure of cohesion exists for the group the argument needs.** The argument requires immigrant-origin voters to act as one, and the video concedes at 5:48 that they are "not homogeneous" before proceeding as if they were. Nobody publishes a cohesion measure for a category nobody publishes. **What it changes:** the page can test size, which is arithmetic, and cannot test cohesion, which is the other half of "decisive." It has to say so rather than let the reader assume the evidence exists somewhere.

**G3. No document links the two events.** The demographic change is measured. The cultural retreat is documented. Nothing found here shows a left party dropping a cultural position *for* immigrant-origin voters — no memo, no vote, no statement, no internal account. The party's own public explanation is public opinion. **What it changes:** the page cannot assert the mechanism. It can put the two facts side by side and say what would connect them, which is a smaller and defensible claim.

**G4. What the bloc wants was never measured.** Four of the video's claims (C23, C24, C26, C28) are about the preferences of immigrant-origin voters on immigration, welfare, community autonomy, and representation. This bank found polling on none of them. The fiscal literature measures what groups *receive*; the video claims what they *want*. **What it changes:** four claims in the middle of the argument have to be carried as assertions or dropped, and the page cannot substitute receipt data for preference data without committing the substitution itself.

**G5. Sharia councils and parallel societies were not researched.** The lane assigned to the 2018 Siddiqui review, the 2024 Khan review, and the Danish, Swedish and German parallel-society findings produced no output. **What it changes:** C26 has no evidence behind it in either direction and must be marked as such. If the page wants to make the community-autonomy claim, that research has to be run first.

**G6. No measure is offered for "Woke 1.0 is dead."** The claim opens the video and nothing says what would count as its being alive. Pronoun policies, DEI budgets, curriculum, hiring practice and institutional speech rules are all measurable and none is measured. **What it changes:** without a measure, the retreat is being read off a handful of statements by prominent people — and the institutional record (US House votes at 2, 3, and 8 Democratic ayes; Spain, Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands holding) points the other way.
