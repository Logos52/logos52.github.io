# The Cold Open

An operational page opens on its claim, at full strength, in the first sentence. The reader who arrives at directions, a reference, or a structure has come to use the page, and supplies the stakes themselves; prose that raises stakes first is friction wearing importance. This page holds the form — what it is, where it applies, and where it fails.

## The form

**Sentence one carries the whole claim.** No run-up, no scene-setting, no history of how things used to be. Three accepted openers from this wiki's own pages, all from the day the form was ruled:

- "The research here splits into a standing half and a session half."
- "Bots watch; the desk changes things."
- "A language model's default voice is trained self-regard."

The struck counter-sample opened a structure page with a paragraph about the wiki's life before the structure existed — how research once ran only in sessions, what never stopped needing done. Every sentence was true and none was the page's job. The ruling that killed it: smaller stakes, cold thesis, don't overthink.

**The stakes stay small after the open, too.** No before-and-after narrative, no announcing that something matters. The page states what is; a reader using the page knows why it matters or wouldn't be there.

**The second move is application.** Once the claim lands, the parts arrive — the rules, the lanes, the pipeline. A cold open discharges the whole up front, which frees every later sentence from carrying persuasive load. Kin to the abstract rule in the [[wiki/Writing Craft/Opening Moves Catalog|Opening Moves Catalog]]: nothing can hook once the conclusion is already given away, and that is the point.

**The body's scan spine is the bolded operative lead.** A section opens with its rule in bold, mechanism following in the same breath. Numbered lists hold stacked reasons, each number a complete reason. Declaratives throughout — a rule is a statement, never advice aimed at the reader.

**The close returns without ceremony.** The last section lands back in the opening claim, advanced by the body, in one breath. No coda, no replay.

## Where it applies, and where it fails

The form fits pages whose reader arrives to use: directions, references, structures, workflows, position statements. It is the wrong form in two neighboring genres. A concept telling fails cold when its claim's own terms aren't parseable yet — "one word is doing the work of two" means nothing until the reader has felt both experiences the split names, so that page earns its run-up. And a personal page opened cold reads as doctrine — the working-through is that genre's content, and a verdict-first personal note performs certainty the material doesn't have.

The working test: if the first sentence needs a definition before it can be parsed, the page wants the run-up form. If a stranger could act on sentence one alone, open cold.

## The price

A cold open spends the page's one surprise immediately. Nothing downstream can build suspense, land a reveal, or save the best for last — every later section is elaboration of a claim the reader already holds. For an operational page that is purely a gain: suspense in directions is a defect. For an essay it would be a real loss, which is one more way to tell the genres apart.
