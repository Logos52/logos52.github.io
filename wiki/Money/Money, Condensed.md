# Money, Condensed

Personal money comes down to a few habits: spend less than you earn, invest the difference in low-cost index funds, leave it there for decades, and decide in writing how much is enough. These habits matter more than income level or skill at picking stocks for most people's long-run wealth.

## Core takeaways

- The gap between income and spending is the main lever.
- A higher savings rate shortens the working years sharply.
- Time in the market beats trying to time the market.
- Most active funds trail a low-cost index after fees.
- A written target stops the goal from rising with each raise.
- About 25 times yearly spending is a common, dated US target.
- Price purchases in hours of work to see their real cost.
- Owned things keep costing attention and upkeep.

## How it works

Saving works through two effects at once. Each dollar not spent is invested and grows, and lower spending also lowers the amount needed to live on later. Staying invested lets the growth compound, while trading in and out usually means buying high and selling low. A written target tells a person when to stop pushing and start spending on what matters.

```
income - spending = gap
gap -> index funds -> years untouched
target = yearly spending x 25
```

- Fees and frequent trading are the main drags on returns.
- Status spending is a race against others with no finish.
- Wealth is savings and investments, which others cannot see.
