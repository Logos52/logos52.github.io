# Money, Condensed

Personal money comes down to a few habits: spend less than you earn, invest the difference in low-cost index funds, leave it there for decades, and decide in writing how much is enough. For most people's long-run wealth, these habits matter more than income level or skill at picking stocks.

## Core takeaways

- The gap between income and spending is the main lever.
- A higher savings rate shortens the working years sharply.
- Staying invested for years beats trying to time the market.
- Most active funds trail a low-cost index after fees.
- A written target stops the goal from rising with each raise.
- About 25 times yearly spending is a common, dated US target.

## How it works

Each dollar not spent is invested and grows, and lower spending also lowers the amount needed to live on later. Staying invested lets the growth build on itself year after year, while trading in and out usually means buying high and selling low. A written target tells a person when to stop pushing for more and start spending on what matters. A purchase priced in hours of work shows its real cost, and a thing once owned keeps costing attention and upkeep.

```
income - spending = gap
gap -> index funds -> years untouched
target = yearly spending x 25
```

- Fees and frequent trading are the main drags on returns.
- Spending to keep up with others has no level that ends it.
- Wealth is savings and investments, which others cannot see.
