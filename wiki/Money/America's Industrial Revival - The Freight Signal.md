# America's Industrial Revival - The Freight Signal

In 2026, companies in the middle of the United States were paying about twice the old price to hire a flatbed truck, and carriers were turning down about half of the flatbed loads offered to them. A flatbed is the open trailer that carries steel, lumber and heavy machines, so its price tracks factory and building work. Anyone who wants to know whether American factories are growing again can check what is said about it against this spending.

## Takeaways

- What companies pay for is better evidence than what anyone says.
- Flatbeds carry industrial cargo, never shopping deliveries.
- Freight demand moved from the coasts to the middle of the country.
- Driver shortages, diesel and tariffs explain little of the price rise.
- Big Tech data centre spending is pushing cash through the whole economy.
- Cheap, stable natural gas helps US factories compete.
- Early productivity figures since ChatGPT look strong but may be noise.

## How to read the signal

A choice made with money says more about what people want than an opinion they state, and economists call this revealed preference. When Netflix ended password sharing, people said they would cancel, and the company then had its four biggest signup days. A freight price is the same kind of choice. A company that pays double to move its goods has a reason, and the reason can be found by ruling out the easy explanations one at a time.

- About 450,000 flatbeds are active in the US.
- A true shortage clears in a week or two, since trucks move.
- A price that stays high means demand has changed.
- Tighter driver licensing explains only a small part.
- Diesel explains about 8% of the rise.
- The ports were quiet, so imported goods are not the cause.

For about thirty years, freight activity picked up at the ports first. Goods landed at Los Angeles, Savannah or Newark and reached the middle of the country some weeks later. The 2026 map is close to the reverse.

```
before                  2026
coasts   HOT            coasts   quiet
middle   quiet          middle   HOT
imports -> inland       factory -> factory
```

- Demand is hottest along Interstate 35, in the Rust Belt and Texas.
- Loads carry raw material to factories and finished goods on to buyers.
- Rail freight rose at the same time.
- Chemical and grain shipments hit 20-year and record highs.
- Caterpillar, which builds heavy machines, reported a record order backlog.

## What is feeding it

Three things arrived at the same time. The largest tech companies are racing each other to build data centres, and their spending on buildings and equipment, which companies report as capex, lands on builders, power suppliers and equipment makers across the country. Energy costs less in the US than in the other big manufacturing regions. AI may also be raising output per worker, though the figures cover only a few years.

- Big cloud companies spend over $150 billion a year on data centres.
- In July 2026 Google raised its yearly capex forecast.
- The new forecast is $195 to $205 billion.
- Tesla's capex rose 140% to about $25 billion.
- For the first time, Google's capex exceeded the cash its business earned.
- AI users get far more value than they pay for.
- That extra value stays with the users.
- US natural gas costs about one fifteenth of Europe's or Japan's price.
- It costs about a quarter of the price in China.
- Energy is about 40% of the cost of heavy manufacturing.
- A factory plan needs energy prices that stay stable for years.

A related argument holds that once AI makes knowledge work cheap, most of the value left lies in turning raw material into goods with energy. That argument was made about China, which has about eight times the US electricity output and twenty times its factory floor space.

## Black swans

A black swan is an event that nobody expected and that changes what comes after it. The famous ones are disasters, such as the September 11 attacks. Good ones get less attention and can matter more. Penicillin was found by accident in 1928, and by the end of the Second World War infection deaths among soldiers had fallen from one in five to under one in a hundred. US output per worker may turn out to be another good black swan.

- Since ChatGPT, output per worker has risen faster than after the internet.
- A lasting gap would be a positive black swan for the US.
- So far the gap covers a short time and could reverse.

## Limits

The flatbed numbers come from one freight data company and one interview with its founder, given in a video that carried a paid sponsor. More than 60% of Americans hold stocks, but most of that money sits in about ten large companies. A boom in the middle of the country can therefore go unfelt on the coasts and in people's portfolios.

- Data centre spending can stop if returns disappoint.
- A few years of productivity figures are easy to over-read.
