# The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

Wealth is assets that earn while their owner sleeps. A business, a program serving customers at night and money reinvested into other assets all count. The purpose of that wealth is freedom, which here means not having to be in a set place at a set time doing something you don't want to do. A salary cannot get a person there, because hourly pay ties every output to an input. The route runs through equity in a product, a business or some intellectual property instead. Three ingredients decide whether someone gets paid at scale. Specific knowledge answers why the market pays this person and not someone else, and accountability answers why it trusts them. Leverage is any way of multiplying work so that each hour travels farther. Once leverage is large, judgment becomes the skill that matters most. Because returns compound, the people and the field a person sticks with count as much as any single deal. Even then, wealth does not buy a calm mind, a fit body or a loving home.

## Core takeaways

- Wealth is assets that earn while their owner sleeps, and its purpose is freedom.
- Renting out hours caps earnings, so the route runs through owning equity.
- Specific knowledge, accountability and leverage decide whether someone gets paid at scale.
- Code and media are leverage that needs nobody's permission.
- Once leverage is large, judgment is the skill that pays most.
- Returns compound, so long games with long-term people beat any single deal.
- Money solves money problems only, so peace of mind, fitness and a loving home have to be built separately.

## How the podcast says wealth is built

Wealth, money and status name three different goals, and wealth is the productive asset among them. Money is the way wealth moves between people. It works as a social IOU for value someone delivered in the past, and it loses value when IOUs are stolen, printed or broken. Status is a person's rank in a social hierarchy. The difference counts because status is a zero-sum game in which one person can only rise if another falls, while creating wealth can leave everyone better off. People playing the status game will attack people creating wealth, so the advice is to stay out of status games and keep building.

Selling hours caps what a person can earn. A salaried lawyer or doctor still gets paid only for the hours worked, so nothing comes in during sleep, holidays or retirement. A set role can also be taught, which makes the person in it replaceable by someone newer, cheaper or automated. The doctors who become truly rich open a practice that builds a brand or create a device or procedure that someone else pays to use. The better target is work where inputs and outputs come apart, so that one hour can matter enormously and a thousand hours can matter not at all. Software is the example given, while even the best lumberjack working by hand is only about 3 times better than one of the worst.

## Happiness as a second skill

A fit body, a calm mind and a house full of love cannot be bought, so each has to be earned. A person who gets rich is still the same person, so someone unhappy before the money stays unhappy after it. Money solves money problems and buys freedom in the material world, and the rest has to be built separately.

## Specific knowledge

Specific knowledge is knowledge that cannot be trained for. If a class could teach it, others could take the class, and the pay would fall to the cost of training. It is found by following innate talent and real curiosity, and it often sits at the edge of what is known. Building it feels like play to the person doing it while it looks like work to everyone else. It is usually creative or technical, and apprenticeship can pass it on even where a classroom cannot.

## Leverage, with and without permission

Leverage comes in old and new forms. Labor and capital are the old forms. Both need someone's permission, since people have to follow you and investors have to hand over money. Code and media are the new forms. They need no permission and copy at almost no cost, so a program or a podcast keeps working while its maker sleeps. The ideal business adds scale economies, zero marginal cost and network effects on top.

Judgment means foreseeing where a decision leads over the long run. The early part of a career goes into gathering leverage, and once leverage is large, a small edge in judgment is worth a fortune. A leader steering a company worth 100 billion dollars who is on course 10 or 20 percent more often than a rival creates far more value than any pay package.

## Accountability

Accountability means taking business risk under your own name. It cuts both ways, since a name on the work collects the credit when things go well and the blame when they don't. Society rewards that risk with responsibility, equity and leverage.

## Money and freedom

The purpose of wealth is freedom, which here means not having to be in a set place at a set time doing something you don't want to do. It is not about luxury goods, which grow dull fast.

Spending habits decide whether a person ever gets that freedom. People living well below their means have freedom that people upgrading their lifestyles can't picture, because every upgrade raises the bar for what counts as enough. Wealth in creative fields often arrives in lumps, with a decade of nothing and then one large payday.

## What the podcast adds to this knowledge base

All returns in life, whether in money, relationships or knowledge, come from compound interest. Compounding needs a long game, and long games also build trust, because people deal fairly with someone they expect to deal with again. That is the reason to pick a field where the same people stay in the game with you for decades, and to choose partners with intelligence, energy and integrity. The whole argument fits in two words, productize yourself, which means finding what you uniquely are and putting as much leverage behind it as possible.

## How to practice this

- List the things you do that feel like play to you and look like work to others, as candidates for specific knowledge.
- For each hour of paid work this week, note whether the output could keep earning without you.
- Pick one piece of work to put under your own name, accepting both the credit and the blame.
- Check your spending against last year's, and hold your lifestyle steady when income rises.
