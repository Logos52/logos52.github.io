# Human Track — Taste & Judgment Catalog

The Human Track is the catalog of design calls a machine can draft but cannot score. The job is to look, not to memorize a list of named effects. Entries were sorted by [[wiki/Design/Design Two-Track Extraction|Design Two-Track Extraction]]; the mechanical core of anything here lives on [[wiki/Design/Agent Track — Executable UI Technique Catalog|the Agent Track]]. A **seam** is the cut that puts that core on the Agent Track and the leftover judgment here. This is the catalog that is actually read and looked at.

## Developing the eye

**Interfaces worth admiring are rebuilt from scratch, with no peeking at devtools.** The friction of matching the original surfaces the invisible tricks — tighter heading line-height, letter-spaced caps, doubled shadows. The drill is the calibration. Calling it the highest-yield drill is the book's talk, not a measurement. [RUI]

**The hunt is for decisions that would never have been made.** On any design that works, the question is what the designer did that would not have occurred: an inverted datepicker, a button sitting inside the input, a two-color headline. Those get collected. [RUI]

**Elimination trains comparison.** Given a value system, the trainable perception is seeing that two of three options are obviously wrong. The fixed set itself is agent-work. [RUI]

**Type attention is cultivated on purpose.** Sustained looking at typography on good sites converts "I can't say why" into a fast good-or-bad call. [RUI]

**At the finish, the eyes beat the numbers.** Systematic palettes, proportions, and scales get most of the way there. The last saturation or lightness tweak is made by eye. [RUI]

**The aesthetic-usability effect is real, and it is a trap.** Attractive things get rated as more usable, so beauty buys forgiveness — and it can hide genuine faults from the designer. Both get judged, separately. [UPOD]

## When to break the rule

**Dense interfaces are legitimate.** "Start with too much whitespace" is overridden on purpose for a dashboard. The test is whether density is a choice, not a default. [RUI]

**A grid is a tool, not a master.** The break to fixed widths happens the moment fluid columns make something wider on a medium screen than on a large one, or shrink it past a usable minimum. [RUI]

**A line longer than seventy-five characters can sometimes work.** The call is made only while knowing the territory is risky. The safe range lives on the Agent Track. [RUI]

**Justification is for mimicking print** — a magazine or newspaper feel. Even then, left-align is usually fine. [RUI]

**MAYA is Most Advanced Yet Acceptable.** Novelty is pushed exactly as far as the audience will tolerate, and no further. Where that line sits is a read of the room. [UPOD]

**Satisficing is knowing when a solution is good enough to ship, and when it is still worth more polish.** The rules cannot make that call. [UPOD]

## What looking actually is

The last tweak is by eye. Any leftover claim that the systems get "ninety percent" of the way is the book's talk, not a finding. Beauty buys forgiveness, and it can hide faults from the person who made the surface. Judge the look and the use separately. The named effects below are calls with a tell, not a syllabus.

## Aesthetic coherence

**A personality is chosen, and every choice serves it.** Font, color feel, border-radius, and copy tone cohere toward one character — serious, playful, or elegant. Calibration is against sites the audience already uses. Direct competitors are not aped. [RUI]

**Border-radius: consistency is a rule, amount is a feel.** Mixing square and round looks worse. How much round communicates personality. [RUI]

**Color "psychology" explains a choice after the fact. It does not generate one.** What looks right is the practice. [RUI]

**Realism has a stop.** Once light can be simulated, the temptation is to tune toward a photograph. Knowing when to stop is taste. [RUI]

**Hierarchy is what makes something feel designed.** Noticing when it is *off* — noisy, flat, competing — is a perceptual skill, not a checklist. [RUI]

**Form follows function, and faith-follows-function is the warning the inverse fails.** Functional-looking is not functional. The call is whether honest form actually fits, versus mere styling cues of competence. [UPOD]

**Wabi-sabi sits next to gloss bias and contour bias as a stance, not a law.** People are drawn to glossy surfaces and to curved-over-angular forms, and they also find beauty in imperfection and age. Whether to lean into polish, softness, or deliberate roughness is the call. [UPOD]

**Symmetry reads as stable and formal. Asymmetry reads as energetic.** Which one fits the feel is judgment. [UPOD]

**The back of the dresser is finished anyway.** Code, empty states, and edge layouts — a craft standard held, not a checkable rule. [UPOD]

## Visual perception

**Gestalt grouping is perceived, not measured.** Proximity, similarity, closure, common fate, good continuation, figure-ground. An agent can space things. Whether the eye reads the intended groups, and where figure and ground flip ambiguously, still needs a look. [UPOD]

**Surface area drives perceived weight.** Bold type and solid icons feel heavy. The eye is trained to feel imbalance before contrast is used to fix it. [RUI]

**Hue has a perceived brightness independent of HSL lightness.** Yellow reads lighter than blue at equal L. The formula is agent-work. Judging rich versus dull, warm versus muddy, after a hue rotation is the eye. [RUI]

**Where attention lands first is verified by looking.** Focal point, entry point, reading gravity. Only looking tells whether the eye actually went where it was sent. [UPOD]

## Problem-framing

**A feature comes before a layout.** The real problem is framed before any pixels. The shell cannot be designed until a few features exist. [RUI]

**The smallest shippable version is designed pessimistically.** Functionality that cannot be built is not implied. Short build-and-test cycles replace imagining every edge case. [RUI]

**Detail comes later.** Grayscale first forces spacing, contrast, and size to carry the hierarchy, before color can rescue a weak one. [RUI]

**User-centered is not user-driven.** People report problems well and prescribe solutions badly. How much to weight what they ask for against what they need is judgment. [UPOD]

**The mental model is the whole game.** The user's model is built only from what the surface shows. Judging whether that surface actually induces the right model — and surfacing it when "synced" or "cloud" breaks — is interpretive. [UPOD]

**The remembered experience is designed at the peak and the end, not the average.** Which moment is the peak, and how the thing ends, is a taste and empathy call. [UPOD]

**Desire lines tell the truth.** Where people actually go is paved, rather than the path that was drawn. [UPOD]

## Examples

```text
A raised door panel vs an inset cabinet panel.
Light from above: the source of every UI shadow decision.
```

Typography on well-designed sites is inspected. Steal from people who care. [RUI]

Asset sources worth knowing are a class, not a storefront: a free photo library, an SVG pattern library. [RUI]

**tsumugu** is the language-learning reader and dictionary these catalogs are meant to judge. Two pairs from those surfaces, looked at rather than named:

```text
Guess-first vs the answer shown.
Good: the reader has to produce the word before the gloss arrives.
Bad: a peek that hands the answer and calls it practice.

A control vs an emoji standing in for a name.
Good: the action is labeled in words the hand and the reader can both use.
Bad: a lone pictogram carrying the only name the control has.
```

A judgment catalog invites collecting named effects the way a rules catalog invites collecting numbers. Looking at two surfaces beats naming twelve effects. Rebuilding from scratch with no devtools is the expensive drill; the hunt, the elimination, and the type attention are cheaper and still train the same eye. If a pass produces no "decision I would not have made" and no off-hierarchy — noisy, flat, competing — the catalog was read, not used. After one rebuild, one invisible trick can be named that would not have been thought of. After grayscale-first, color is not carrying the hierarchy.

Following the rules is not enough to tell whether the result is good. The eye is the catalog.
