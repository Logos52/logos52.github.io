# Universal Principles & Design Techniques: Master Scorecard

The scored inventory holds 250 rows. Of those, 200 come from a handbook of named design principles and 50 come from a handbook of interface techniques. Each row has a short name, a one-line gloss in the source file, four scores on a 1-to-5 scale, a subject category, a source tag, and a working bucket. This page reports the buckets and the counts. It does not reprint the handbook glosses.

The working buckets split as follows.

- Own holds 95 rows. These are techniques to keep doing yourself.
- Augment holds 20 rows. These are techniques to work with help.
- Delegate holds 84 rows. These are techniques to hand off.
- Low-lev holds 51 rows. These are techniques that, in this scoring, sit further down the list.

The same 250 rows also carry subject categories. Interaction accounts for 82 rows and strategy for 42 rows. Aesthetics account for 38 rows, process for 30 rows, and social for 24 rows. Cognitive accounts for 23 rows and learning for 11 rows. The bucket and the category are stored as separate fields on every row.

The four 1-to-5 scores sit on every row. The export does not spell out what each of those four columns measures. What the export does make plain is the final bucket, which is the useful sort if you want a reading order.

Because the glosses in the export are drawn from the handbooks themselves, reproducing all 250 rows of them here would be a copy of those books. The scorecard's job is the classification. If you need the full definition of a named row, go back to the handbook that originated it and read that entry there.

## Open questions

Which Own-bucket technique would change the next thing you design if you applied it on purpose this week?

When you are tempted to study a Delegate row in depth, what work are you postponing in the Own set?
