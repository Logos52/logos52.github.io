# Memory Handling

Memory handling is what you do with new information in the first seconds after you meet it. Most people have about the same raw memory, and the difference between someone who remembers and someone who forgets is mostly in this handling. Handling can be learned, so a person who thinks they have a bad memory can usually fix it.

## Takeaways

- Raw memory capacity is similar across most people.
- Working memory holds new information for about 15 to 30 seconds.
- What you do in that window decides what is stored.
- Working memory holds only a few items at once.
- Forgetting is often a failure to recall something that was stored.
- Paper takes the load off working memory.

## How it works

New information first lands in working memory, a small space where the brain holds and works on a few things for a short time. If you shape the information while it is there, by linking it, comparing it or putting it in your own words, it passes into long-term memory. If you only reread it or hold it in mind, it fades. Forgetting the top of a page by the time you reach the bottom is long-term memory fading fast, because nothing was done with the first lines.

```
input --> working memory (15-30 s, few items)
              |                 |
         shaped, linked     left alone
              v                 v
        long-term memory     forgotten
              |
         retrieval brings it back to work on
```

- The working-memory limit is in weight as well as count.
- A dense technical term fills more space than a digit.
- Known patterns shrink the load: 1 to 9 in order is easy.
- Something you already know makes two words one item.

## How to do it

The fixes all follow from the working space being small and short. Do more with each piece while it is there, and stop taking in more until you have. Keep the space clear of other words, and use paper so the space does not have to hold everything. Then practise recalling the material in the way you will need it.

- Increase complexity: re-explain, find an analogy, link to known things.
- Ask why it matters and what problem it solves.
- Handle each piece at once instead of reading everything first.
- Pause a video or stop reading while you work on a piece.
- Block out talk and other voices while you think.
- Practise recall at the level and in the setting of real use.
- Write and draw the links on paper as you go.

## Where it fails

The common approach is to take in all the material, understand it, and plan to make sense of it later. Understanding in the moment stores nothing, so days later the reader has to start again. Recall practice also fails when the prompt used in practice differs from the situation where the knowledge is needed, since a flashcard does not prepare you to answer a hard question in a meeting.

- Reading on while still working on a piece causes overload.
- Defining each term on its own is passive handling.
- Comparing and connecting the terms is active handling.
- A messy first map is normal, so clean it up afterwards.
