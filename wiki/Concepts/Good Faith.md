# Good Faith

Good faith is a commitment you carry into every dealing that matters: the other person gets the true picture from you — your real constraints, your real intentions, the downsides you could have sat on — even when telling them costs you something now. The cost is the working part: anyone can say cooperative things, and a disclosure that cost you something is the one the other person can actually believe. Held over time, the practice buys what makes dealings worth having — problems met while they are small, trust that compounds, a reputation for meaning what you say. It is not naive honesty, and it does not mean saying everything to everyone.

The disclosure has three objects. The constraint is what you actually cannot do or accept. The intention is what you are actually trying to get. The downside is the part of your offer, work, or position that the other person is better off knowing and you are more comfortable hiding. Carrying those unsaid has its own running cost — deception, omission, and impression management all take upkeep, and the upkeep never ends. And the difference between disclosure that signals and disclosure that decorates is whether anything was surrendered:

```text
Costs nothing: "In the spirit of transparency — we're not the cheapest option."
        Everyone could already see it. Nothing was given up.
Costs something: "If the timeline matters more than price, their bid fits you better this quarter."
        Leverage, handed over. This is the one that reads as an offer to cooperate.
```

A disclosure like that carries two messages at once: willing to cooperate, and not optimizing at your expense.

Timing multiplies the effect. A problem or a misalignment disclosed early is a plan adjustment; the same thing discovered late is a betrayal, because by then trust has been spent on the version without it. The public-work practice of this stance — showing what is known, what is not, and what the work demands, before anyone commits — lives on [[wiki/Concepts/Anti-Marketing|Anti-Marketing]]; this page keeps the principle and leaves the practice there.

All of it runs through one gate: transparency that costs nothing signals nothing. **Forthrightness is a signal only where it could have cost you.**

The same move wears four uniforms. In negotiation it is naming real constraints instead of bluffing past them — leverage given up, credibility bought. In feedback it is the direct version without unnecessary cruelty — the comfortable version forgone. In close relationships it is the difficult truth said early, while it is still small. In public work it is stating plainly what is known, what is not, and what the work requires of whoever takes it on. Each application pays upfront and collects later, and the collection is checkable in ordinary life: problems start surfacing before trust is spent on them, rather than after. Delivering the costly fact so it can actually be received is its own craft, and [[wiki/Concepts/How to Communicate Truth Into Someone Else's Frame|How to Communicate Truth Into Someone Else's Frame]] owns it. And the payoff scales with the background: in settings where most actors default to strategic vagueness and over-promising, the paid disclosure stands out furthest.

The stance has a limit, and the limit is the counterpart. Held consistently, forthrightness elicits better behaviour from people who can reciprocate it, and over time it shifts a relationship's whole footing away from defensive ground. Extended unconditionally it is exploitable, and it costs most exactly where it is least returned, because an extracting counterpart converts each disclosure into advantage. The quit signals are behavioural: disclosures keep getting used against you, and none come back. What narrows then is the disclosure, never the honesty — you stop volunteering leverage; you do not start lying. Even there, withholding keeps its own price: hidden information is a burden on the person who needed it, the cost [[wiki/Concepts/Meiwaku|Meiwaku]] counts. The stance was always both things at once — strategic, because the trust it builds compounds; ethical, because the other person's clear picture matters on its own.

Which returns to the commitment this page opened on: the true picture, given at your own cost. What the body earned is the aim of it — early rather than late, inside the other person's frame, extended whole toward people who can read the signal and return it, and narrowed without turning false where it cannot be. The commitment itself never becomes conditional. How far it extends does.
