# The Age Of Nonlinear Returns

We live in an age of nonlinear returns. The upside ahead is usually larger than the pie in front of you, and it can be 100 times, 1,000 times, or even 10,000 times larger. Fighting over a small split before that pie is baked is a poor use of time, especially in technology. Returns follow a power-law pattern. The single best outcome is worth more than the rest put together, the second is worth more than everything below it, and so on. Time and optionality are the scarce resources. Give them up only for something that has a chance to return that kind of multiple on your hours or your capital.

## Grow the pie

One habit of good dealmaking is to keep attention on what you can still give, and on how the shared prize can grow, rather than on who owes whom for past help. The emotional pull may be "I have done a lot and received little." The useful conversation is "I can keep helping if we can work this out." The future upside is the thing worth protecting.

In investing, that means looking for the rare outsized outcome rather than celebrating an early sale that will never be the top of the power law. In any deal, it means asking whether the time you are about to give up could produce a return large enough to justify closing other doors.

## When to walk and when to fight

None of this means every split is generous or every partner is fair. When the large prize finally appears, greed often arrives with it, and you do have to defend yourself. Business can turn into a fight by other means. Sometimes you stand up for a principle so you are not an easy mark the next time.

The test is whether the situation will steal your sleep and your peace if you leave it alone. If walking away would leave you unable to rest for a long stretch, you have to fight. If you can recover your time, decline to work with those people again, and see that the stakes are too small to be worth the cost, you walk. People spend too much attention splitting small stakes and too little attention on the large upside, which is where the real result lives.

## Peace as part of the outcome

Chasing every last unit of money is not the same as choosing well. Leaving money on the table can be the right move if it keeps you happy, productive, and open for the next thing. Life is short. Repeating the same climb only to make a larger pile, after the work has gone stale, trades away the chance to live more than one life inside the years you have. An outcome you can sleep with, without a year of grinding stress, leaves you free for whatever comes next. That freedom is part of the return.

## Open questions

How large would the future upside have to be before this fight over a small split stopped making sense?

How can I tell a prize I must defend from a stake too small to spend my peace on?
