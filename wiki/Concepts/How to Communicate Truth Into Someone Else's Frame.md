# How to Communicate Truth Into Someone Else's Frame

Selling something by first working out what the other person wants and then saying what you believe to be true in terms they already hold. Done this way, the people who see through sales tactics will still hear you, and you know when to stop pitching and move on.

## Core takeaways

- Credibility does more than sales technique. People resist being sold to, and the people you most want to work with notice a pitch at once.
- Only sell what you believe in. If it feels like selling, the thing or the fit is wrong.
- Before you speak, find out what the other person wants and why they hold their current view.
- Say yes to the valid part of what they said, then add your own case. Disagree outright only when you fully disagree, and give the reason.
- Honesty comes first, and kindness goes on top of it. Honest without kind gets no hearing.
- If the pitch does not land, move on. Most people will not get it.
- Never pitch from a position you cannot walk away from.

## How to do it

- Check yourself first
  - Believe the thing. Wait until the thing itself, as it stands, excites you past a threshold, then go. Do not go because the calendar says so.
  - Take your ego out. The aim is for the listener to feel they are hearing their own thinking said back more clearly.
- Read their frame
  - Find out what they want. Smart people have a real reason for what they say, and it can usually be found.
  - Reason your way to their position and test whether it holds. If it holds, say so.
- Speak into it
  - Set a larger context first: the problem it solves, a story, some history. The pitch comes after.
  - Explain it simply, with a comparison the listener already has. Nobody is an expert in everything.
  - Keep the truth whole. Do not exaggerate; the people who matter will notice.
  - Deliver it with care for the person, so they can act on it and leave with energy.
- Let go of the outcome
  - If it does not resonate, move on at once and look for the person it will resonate with.

```
believe it  ->  read their frame  ->  say it their way
    |                 |                      |
 not yet:        no reason found:       does not land:
 keep building   ask, then reason       move on
```

Credibility is built by what you refuse to sell. The estate agent who steers you away from bad houses is trusted when the right one comes. A seller who pushes every option has spent that trust before the sale that matters.

## Leading the same way

- Leading means making people want the work. Telling them what to do is management, and it does not get their best.
- The same steps apply: listen to what they want, find the overlap with what needs doing, then point them at it. Motivation that is faked does not hold.
- Keep a team as small as the job allows and high in trust.
- Working together is a stag hunt: each hunter alone can catch a rabbit, but two who commit to hunting together can take a stag and share far more meat. A pitch to a recruit is a pitch to join a stag hunt.
- A recruiting check: let a candidate interview anyone on the team, and tell them to walk away if those people are not brilliant.

## Deals and walking away

- A contract is two parties giving up future options in exchange for working together on a bigger shared result. It narrows your future, so you must be convinced no better path is open to you.
- The harder a deal is to unwind (a board seat, veto rights, preferred stock), the higher the bar for taking it.
- Plan so you are never cornered. Start raising money six to twelve months before it is needed, so a bad deal can be refused.
- Returns follow a power law: the single top outcome is worth more than all the others together. So do not fight over a small pie; keep your time and options free for the chance at a large one.
- Fight only when the stakes are large or on principle. Otherwise walk away and keep your time, reputation and peace.
- A sinking feeling of obligation when signing is information. Act on it.

## Where it fails

- Robert Cialdini's book Influence lists six levers (consistency, liking, authority, scarcity, social proof, reciprocity). Stacking them into a pitch makes the listener feel sold to. Run the list over an email at most.
- A pitch made because the calendar says so, before the fundamentals are ready.
- Kindness that removes the truth, or truth delivered so harshly that nobody acts on it.
- Trying to sell something you do not care about. Find something else to sell.
