# The Accretion Frame

A practice kept for years runs on one of two engines. The push engine acts against a motivational headwind and manages it — defusion, willpower budgeting, environment design, the tooling of [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]] and [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]]. The pull engine removes the headwind at its source: the activity is redesigned until the desire to return is produced by the practice itself. The accretion frame is the design theory of the pull engine. Its claim: wanting follows from structure — a practice that grows a persistent artifact, keeps a visible open edge, and makes absence cost nothing will generate its own return visits, with no daily willpower spend. The design rules are few, the claim is testable within weeks, and the adherence research has backed it for decades while the popular discourse kept prescribing discipline.

## What a maintenance metric measures

A streak or a ring measures consecutive non-absence against a fixed daily bar, so its entire value lives in its unbrokenness — a fragility counter whose headline number mostly states the size of the loss waiting to happen. The frame it installs is loss: once a streak exists, the daily choice rewrites from "add something" into "avoid losing what I have." The emotional economics decay in one direction, because the pleasure of maintaining habituates while the pain of breaking does not, so over the multi-year horizon a body runs on, the metric drifts toward all-downside. The daily binary adds a second failure: everything below the bar reads as zero, and once the bar is missed the cost of continued lapse collapses — the what-the-hell effect, documented in dieting and addiction research and a fair extrapolation to broken streaks (streak-freeze features exist because break-induced churn is a known product problem). Quantification carries a tax even without a streak: across six experiments, measuring an enjoyable activity increased output while reducing enjoyment and later engagement (Etkin, *Journal of Consumer Research* 2016). And a daily bar reports at the wrong timescale for a body — adaptation needs rest and builds over weeks, so a metric that penalizes a rest day penalizes correct training.

## What pull is made of

Project work that pulls daily with no tracker decomposes under a subtractive test. Remove the persistent artifact — each session's output erased overnight — and the pull dies. Remove discovery — sessions made perfectly predictable — and the pull dies even though the pile still grows. Remove the quota and nothing changes, because a quota never generated pull; it only crowded it. Remove the company and sessions start slower, but the pull survives. Two parts are load-bearing and fused: a persistent artifact plus uncertain discovery, collapsed into one operative property — a visible next addition whose result is unknown. A ring shows a bounded shortfall to zero out; a map with blank streets shows an unbounded affordance to add into. The missed-day regret that needs no tracker to manufacture it is counterfactual: it points at a deposit that can be pictured and was skipped.

The adherence research lands on the same side. Enjoyment, competence, and social-connection motives predict long-term maintenance, while health, weight, and appearance motives predict starting and then quitting (Ryan et al. 1997; Teixeira's 2012 systematic review of ~66 studies). How a single session feels predicts activity levels six and twelve months later (Williams 2008). An eight-week randomized trial that prescribed intensity for pleasure instead of fitness targets produced 77% higher attendance (Teixeira & Ekkekakis group, 2024). The competing model — willpower as a depletable muscle — has largely failed to replicate. The cultures that keep people moving for decades reached the same place without the research: climbing, skating, dance, and the martial arts run on skill as an inexhaustible tech tree with the scene as structure, and their word for it is autotelic — the session as its own reward.

## The body keeps the same books

The accretion frame is also the physiologically accurate one. Adaptations sort onto two clocks. The slow tier is the substance of capability and survives absence: motor skill holds for years, tendon and connective tissue remodel over months, bone runs remodeling cycles of three to four months, and a previously trained muscle regrows lost capacity far faster than a novice builds it. The fast tier is weather: glycogen, plasma volume, mitochondrial enzyme density, the acute mood lift — real, valuable, and never banked. A missed day cannot cost anything structural, because the quantity a daily bar tracks cannot change in a day; a missed week is often neutral or better, since fatigue dissipates faster than fitness. The maintenance frame keeps one true claim — a dose tier of mood, insulin sensitivity, and metabolic sharpness wants rhythm — but at a frequency far below daily, honored by a loose rolling window (something breathing-hard every two or three days) rather than a bar. The audit this yields for any number is the layoff test: a metric is honest exactly when it would still mean something after a month completely off. Skills unlocked, cumulative distance, dated personal bests, and named routes pass. Streaks, closed rings, and burned calories evaporate with the states they mirror.

## Design rules

Each rule forecloses a specific relapse.

- **Append-only structures.** The artifact is a set, a tree, or a log — a data shape that cannot represent lost ground — so a quiet week is structurally unable to subtract anything.
- **No denominators.** A tally may exist (47 streets, 9 nodes); a fraction, target, or completion bar may not. A denominator implies a hundred-percent, and a hundred-percent converts absence into falling behind schedule.
- **Check after, never before.** Opened after moving, the artifact is admired growth; opened before, it is an audit deciding whether the day is permitted. Same file, opposite engines.
- **The body stays untracked.** Daily bodily metrics reinstall the maintenance ledger on its noisiest surface; adaptation stays an invisible byproduct.
- **No numbers on the enjoyed part.** Etkin's boundary condition: measurement corrodes activities framed as leisure and spares goal-directed output, so the metric sits on the collection, never on the experience.
- **Rest is correct.** A rest day is a legitimate non-addition, never a miss.
- **Reward the return.** In a 61,000-person megastudy, the single most effective intervention paid a small bonus for coming back after a missed workout (Milkman et al., *Nature* 2021), and a missed day leaves habit formation intact (Lally 2010). The comeback is the event worth designing for; the gap is a non-event.

## Structure without grit

Structure arrives through three levers, none of which spends willpower. Lower the activation energy until sessions need no run-up: submaximal sets scattered through the day, movement snacks, a first move too small to require a decision. Bind something already wanted to the session and ration it to the session — temptation bundling raised gym attendance 51% in its field trial (Milkman 2014) — so the pull is borrowed from a want that already exists. And let a slot decide instead of a person: a standing time, place, class, or partner where showing up is expected — gap-tolerant containers where missing once is nothing and returning is automatic. Consistency behaves as a property of context (Wood): people who look disciplined are mostly people whose defaults do the deciding.

## The case against

Three warnings keep the frame honest. Streaks do deliver cheap structure for many people, and they work for as long as the activity fits the person's identity; their failure mode — the broken-chain collapse — is real, and both facts stand. The in-act experience has no substitute: pleasure during movement collapses past the ventilatory threshold, roughly where conversation stops, and imposed intensity is what people quit (Ekkekakis), so no artifact rescues movement that feels bad while it happens — pace stays self-selected, and sessions end while they still feel good, because remembered pleasure sets the next forecast. And the cultures that live on the pull engine insist on a friction phase: plateaus are long, struggle precedes flow, and the frame promises effortless showing up, never effortless doing. A hard week read as design failure is the misdiagnosis this warning exists to prevent.

## Quit signal

The three-week test: a skipped day should produce a specific regret pointed at an artifact that did not grow. If it does not, the artifact is decoration — swap it for a different artifact; adding discipline instead is the old engine returning. The second diagnostic is a deliberate fallow week: "the pile paused" means the design holds, while "I'm behind" means a rate leaked in, and the repair is finding and deleting the denominator that carried it.

## The two engines, divided

Push tooling keeps a permanent role: domains where nothing accretes, and cold starts inside converted domains, where a single defused session restarts a stalled pull engine — [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/How to shift your brain to be motivated (when you don't feel like it)|DFUZ]] as reserve, never as daily motor. The accretion frame governs everywhere a practice can be rebuilt to grow something: the artifact persistent, the edge visible, absence free, the wanting produced rather than summoned. Its price is design work — an artifact built, rules kept, audits run — where a streak app charges nothing up front and collects later. Movement is the first conversion attempt, running live in [[wiki/Fitness/Movement as Accretion|Movement as Accretion]] with [[wiki/Fitness/The Treadmill Library|The Treadmill Library]] as its listening arm, and the target state has a working name: motivationless movement — a practice that never invokes the motivation machinery at all.
