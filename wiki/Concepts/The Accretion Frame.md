# The Accretion Frame

The accretion frame is design theory for a pull engine: a practice that brings you back without daily willpower. Long practice runs on push (forcing each session) or pull (structure makes the next visit likely). Pull needs three things at once: a persistent artifact that only grows, a next piece visibly unfinished with an open outcome, and a missed day that costs nothing. Streaks, closed rings, and daily body scores are push dressed as pull. They count maintenance, punish rest, and make absence the only interesting event. This frame is for practices you want for years: movement, language on a treadmill, desk work that already feels wasted when skipped. Push tools stay in reserve for cold starts and domains where nothing accumulates.

## What breaks pull

A streak or ring has value only while unbroken. The decision stops being "add a piece" and becomes "protect a number." Keeping the chain feels good less over time; breaking it keeps hurting. One miss often makes the next miss cheap, the same collapse as breaking a diet rule. Tracking enjoyable activity raises output while lowering enjoyment and later participation; put numbers on the collection, not on the hour. Daily denominators score correct rest as failure. Capacity builds across weeks; a body needs rhythm below daily for mood, insulin response, and hard breathing inside a loose two-or-three-day window.

Enjoyment, competence, and belonging predict staying with exercise; appearance and weight motives predict starts that stop. How a session feels while it happens predicts activity six and twelve months later as much as any artifact. Intensity prescribed for pleasure beat fitness-target prescription in a small eight-week trial with much higher attendance. Pleasure drops once conversation becomes hard (that is near ventilatory threshold), so the person doing the session chooses speed and stops while it still feels good; nothing that accumulates saves a session that feels bad in the moment.

## Seven design rules

| Rule | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| Append-only structures | Shrinking piles and the sense of going backward |
| No denominators | Hundred-percent targets that turn absence into falling behind |
| Check after, never before | The log auditing whether the day is permitted |
| Body untracked | Daily body numbers on the noisiest data |
| No numbers on the enjoyed part | Measurement on the experience instead of the collection |
| Rest is correct | Rest scored as failure |
| Reward the return | Punishing the comeback after a skip |

Small starts, temptation bundling, and standing times raise early attendance; effects at the start often fade. Showing up on schedule is mostly surroundings. About half of daily behavior repeats in the same context. After a month completely off, unlocked skills, total distance, dated bests, and named routes still mean something; day-chains, rings, and calories burned do not.

Three weeks in, a skipped day should produce a specific picture of an artifact that did not grow; if not, the artifact is not working. A planned week off should sound like "the pile paused," not "behind schedule."

## Movement as the live conversion

Desk work with an ongoing project produces a specific regret on a skipped day: the project did not advance, not guilt over a broken rule. That motivation has an ongoing thread, a durable artifact per session, and no quota. Long days and twenty-minute days both count.

Movement failed the other way: systems that ate more effort than exercise. The conversion treats movement as a growing project. Three artifact families are worth trying.

**Material.** Walks mark streets on a map; notes from the walk make fieldwork. Unexplored streets add more than familiar ones, which keeps routes fresh.

**Shared data.** Sessions produce something for the desk (routes, times, patterns), so collaboration has material and the desk shapes the next walks.

**Tech tree.** Capacity drawn as nodes that stay after gaps: first hour, first hill, first carry. A training log with dated bests still means something after a month off, unlike a ring count. Combat sport, climbing, dance, and martial arts fit: skill trees plus people around the activity.

Calm cardio (walking, pool walking, paddle boarding) cuts sweat tax (laundry, showering) while staying lean-enough for many goals. Hard sweaty sessions are not required for that outcome.

## Mindsets that keep a body practice alive

Most slow damage is story, not schedule. "Gym is only for gym bros," "too busy," "too hard and boring," "no time to eat well," "start next month," "I can do it alone from videos," "too late". Each ranks something above health or shrinks the ask. Busy people often find three hours a week once the real ask is visible. Waiting for the perfect month is a lifetime of new obstacles.

Give up the rush to bloom. Rose-and-beach physiques are years compressed into a feed thumbnail. After the goal arrives, satisfaction may last weeks; then goalposts move. Train on a horizon you would still want in ten years: rest days, two or three sessions a week, consistency over speed, preemptive mobility and deloads about every six weeks. Diet is default eating, not a costume until summer. Hustle can unstick a couch start but fades; awareness asks why stuckness is there; accountability makes bargains harder.

## Treadmill library

Dense audio needs pause and reread; exercise competes for the same attention. Searching podcasts on a treadmill fails on interest and density. Generated audio fits pace: wiki listen-backs, a condensed news brief, language study with synced text and tap-for-meaning, with lighter content when effort is high and denser when pace is easy.

The library is append-only like the map or tree: each session moves items to a played list; nothing resets; skipped days leave episodes waiting without penalty. Games that worked the same way (story episodes that continue, collections that grow on walks) were accretion before the frame had a name. Build small: one phone, finished audio only, played versus unplayed as the only state that matters. The test matches movement: three weeks in, does a skip make you think of concrete backlog, not discipline failure? If not, change content, not willpower.

## Where push still belongs

Domains with nothing to accumulate still need push. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|DFUZ]] is a one-session unhook from not wanting to start, a reserve, not a daily motor. The price of pull is design work up front.
