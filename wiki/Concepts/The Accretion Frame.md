## Sources

Etkin, J. (2016). "The Hidden Cost of Personal Quantification." *Journal of Consumer Research* 42(6), 967–984. Six experiments: measuring an enjoyable activity raised output and lowered enjoyment and later engagement. Boundary: leisure, not goal-directed output.

Cochran, W., & Tesser, A. (1996). The "what the hell" effect. Herman, C. P., & Mack, D. (1975). Counterregulatory eating. Everything below the bar reads as zero; once missed, the cost of continued lapse collapses.

Ryan, R. M., Frederick, C. M., Lepes, D., Rubio, N., & Sheldon, K. M. (1997). *International Journal of Sport Psychology.* Teixeira, P. J., Carraça, E. V., Markland, D., Silva, M. N., & Ryan, R. M. (2012). *International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity.* Systematic review, 66 studies. Enjoyment, competence, and connection predict maintenance; appearance and weight motives predict starting then quitting.

Williams, D. M., et al. (2008). Affective response during exercise predicts physical activity at 6 and 12 months. Rhodes, R. E., & Kates, A. (2015). Review confirming the direction.

Teixeira, D. S., et al. (2024). *International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity* 21:85. Preregistered pragmatic trial. Intensity prescribed for pleasure vs fitness targets: 14.35 vs 8.13 sessions over eight-week follow-up (77%). N=46 retained.

Hagger, M. S., et al. (2016). *Perspectives on Psychological Science.* 23 labs, N=2,141, ego-depletion effect not significant. Vohs, K. D., et al. (2021). Multilab failure. Willpower-as-muscle as a discarded rival.

Milkman, K. L., et al. (2021). *Nature* 592, 390–394. N>60,000. Top arm: a small bonus for returning after a missed scheduled workout, about $0.09, 27% boost in gym visits.

Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M., Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010). *European Journal of Social Psychology.* Missing one day did not materially affect the automaticity trajectory.

Milkman, K. L., Minson, J. A., & Volpp, K. G. (2014). *Management Science.* Temptation bundling raised gym attendance 51% initially. The effect decayed (Kirgios et al. 2020).

Wood, W., & Neal, D. T. (2007); Wood, W., & Rünger, D. (2016); Neal, D. T., Wood, W., & Quinn, J. M. (2006). About 45% of daily behavior repeated in the same context.

Ekkekakis, P., Hall, E. E., & Petruzzello, S. J. (2004, 2008); Ekkekakis, P., Parfitt, G., & Petruzzello, S. J. (2011). Dual-mode theory: affect turns negative near the ventilatory threshold. Talk-test as the field proxy.

Issurin, V. B. (2010); Mujika, I., & Padilla, S. (2000); Banister impulse-response / fitness-fatigue. A missed day cannot cost anything structural; fatigue's time-constant is shorter than fitness. Motor-skill retention, tendon collagen turnover, bone remodeling (~3–4 months), and muscle memory: Park & Shea; Magnusson / Heinemeier & Kjaer; Frost; Gundersen 2016; Seaborne et al. 2018.
