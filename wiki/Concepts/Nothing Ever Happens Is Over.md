# Nothing Ever Happens Is Over

"Nothing ever happens" is an internet joke that says the big events people predict never arrive. In a podcast episode in May 2026, the investor Naval Ravikant said the joke has stopped being true, because since COVID the world has been changing faster in politics, the economy and technology. The practical use is to check old assumptions more often, since the conditions they rested on are changing.

- Since COVID, change has sped up in politics, economics and technology.
- Investors now fund rockets, drones, hardware and AI.
- Small teams can use AI in place of an intranet.
- AI lets each specialist do some of the others' work.
- Drones change who can use force, down to single people.
- New jobs are harder to imagine than lost ones.

## Why the default broke

One reading is that the world sat in an unstable balance, COVID knocked it over, and things then moved into a new state. Whatever the cause, several areas are moving at once. Money that went to software now goes to hardware, rockets and drones, and people who can build those things are in short supply. The shape of the AI industry is also unsettled.

- Two to four companies lead AI, plus Nvidia on chips.
- Nobody knows whether AI becomes a commodity, a monopoly or a few firms.
- Current models are uneven and weak at mixed media.
- None of them yet has a model of the world.
- A true world model predicts what its own actions will cause.

## How a small team works now

Naval's own company is flat, with one person in the middle who holds the full picture and everyone else talking to each other directly. It uses no chat tool and no project software, only GitHub for code. That only works when every person is highly capable. AI fills the gaps that an intranet, the internal website where a company keeps what its staff know, used to fill.

- Summarise someone else's code or paper.
- Find who in the company knows a topic.
- Build a status report or chart from code, documents or email.

Hardware, software and AI staff each do 20 to 30% of the others' work.

## Force, health and hardware

The same speed-up reaches areas outside software. Each new weapon has reshaped states: the rifle helped nation states replace feudal ones, and nuclear weapons left seven to nine countries truly independent. Cheap attack drones may bring that kind of threat down to single people, and defending against them is hard.

- Access to dangerous biology widens the way access to coding did.
- Medical rules slow the research that would defend against it.
- Cheap software lets hardware makers ship usable devices.
- Chip makers and Chinese manufacturers back open-source models.
- Open models make those companies' products more valuable.

## How to use it

When the world moves faster, plans built on last year's defaults expire sooner. Review your assumptions on a schedule, and expect some predicted events to happen. Doom is easy to picture because the loss is visible, while the new jobs and products that replace the old ones are hard to see in advance.

- Put a review date on each standing assumption.
- Treat each new event as something that can happen.
- Weigh doom forecasts against the gains that are harder to picture.
