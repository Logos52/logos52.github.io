# Charisma

Charisma is the set of signals, in body, voice, timing and words, that make other people want to deal with you. It can be trained, so the useful question is which signals to send and what has to be true underneath for them to hold.

## Core takeaways

- People read two things in you within seconds: whether you are safe to deal with (warmth) and whether dealing with you pays off (competence). Charisma is both signals arriving at once.
- A plainer wording of the same idea: project confidence and care for the other person at the same time.
- The signals have to sit on something real. Real skill, real interest in the other person, a settled view of how to live. Signals without that read as an act.
- Charisma is trainable. Managers taught a small set of speaking and body habits were rated more charismatic by their coworkers three months later (Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti, 2011).
- The fastest way to lose it is to panic when something goes wrong. Staying calm and slow to react in a mishap is the cheapest way to gain it.
- What charms one group can repel another. Charisma that works across groups is the kind worth building.

## How it works

- The signal
  - Body language, tone of voice, timing, shown warmth, shown confidence.
  - Warmth promises the person is low-risk. Competence promises the contact is worth having.
  - Reading these signals is old and automatic. Dogs read the same signals in people.
  - Stance, walk, face, words and tone must all say the same thing. A mismatch is what people call fake.
- Two layers
  - Front end: what shows in a conversation. [[wiki/Concepts/Style|Style]], telling a story, explaining a thing clearly, answering fast. Humour helps but comes second.
  - Back end: what the front end draws on. Temperance, a worked-out view of how to live, experience, relationships. Learning feeds this layer.
  - Front-end habits can be taught in weeks. The back end takes years and is what keeps the front end from being an act.
- Context
  - The same behaviour lands differently by room, by shared values, and by how well the other person knows you.
  - With a close friend, blunt, cutting wit can be charisma, because both sides know how the other thinks. With a stranger it is rudeness.

```
                  competence high
                       |
   respected, not      |   charisma
   trusted             |
                       |
 warmth low -----------+----------- warmth high
                       |
   avoided             |   liked, not relied on
                       |
                  competence low
```

## How to build it

- Genuine interest in the other person, and listening. This is the warmth half and it cannot be faked for long.
- Real competence in some field, treated as normal. This is the competence half.
- Reduce self-consciousness and worry about status ([[wiki/Syntheses/Status, Vulnerability, and the Three Conversations|Status, Vulnerability, and the Three Conversations]]). Both leak into the body and read as low confidence.
- Practise calm on small mishaps: rain with no umbrella, a cancelled taxi, running late. Act as if it is handled.
- Show real emotion when you speak. It signals that you are not afraid of being judged.
- Social calibration: read the room before choosing how formal or blunt to be. Range: have more than one way of speaking to choose from.

## Where it fails

- Narrow charisma. A person can charm one in-group by sharing its contempt for outsiders. That charm ends at the group's edge. Broad charisma, the kind that also avoids being a burden on strangers ([[wiki/Concepts/Meiwaku|Meiwaku]]), is the kind this vault means.
- Warmth without honesty. If only one is possible, honesty comes first, because a kind lie does not help anyone. Honesty without warmth fails too: people do not listen to it. The working question is whether you want to be right or effective.
- Warmth has a cost. A person who hates being unkind finds it very hard to fire anyone, even when the team needs it.
- Charisma without [[wiki/Concepts/Good Faith|good faith]] is manipulation, sent with the same signals.
