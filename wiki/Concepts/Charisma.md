# Charisma

Charisma is the set of signals, in body, voice, timing and words, that make other people want to deal with you. It can be trained, so the useful questions are which signals to send and what has to be true underneath for them to hold.

## Core takeaways

- People judge warmth and competence in you within seconds.
- Charisma is both signals arriving at once.
- Put plainly, it is confidence and care for the other person together.
- The signals need real skill and real interest under them.
- Charisma can be trained within a few months.
- Panicking in a mishap loses it fastest.
- Staying calm in a mishap is the cheapest way to gain it.

## How it works

Warmth tells the other person that you are safe to deal with, and competence tells them that dealing with you will pay off. People read both from body language, tone of voice, timing, and how much warmth and confidence you show. The reading is old and automatic, and dogs read the same signals in people. Stance, walk, face, words and tone all have to say the same thing, and a mismatch between them is what people call fake.

- Charisma has two layers, a front end and a back end.
- The front end is what shows in a conversation.
- It covers [[wiki/Concepts/Style|style]], storytelling, clear explanation and quick answers.
- Humour helps but comes second.
- The back end is what the front end draws on.
- It covers temperance, a view of how to live, experience and relationships.
- Learning feeds the back end.
- Front-end habits take weeks, and the back end takes years.
- The back end keeps the front end from being an act.

The same behaviour lands differently by room, by shared values, and by how well the other person knows you. With a close friend, blunt and cutting wit can be charisma, because both sides know how the other thinks. With a stranger the same wit is rudeness.

```
                competence high
                      |
  respected,          |   charisma
  not trusted         |
warmth low -----------+----------- warmth high
  avoided             |   liked,
                      |   not relied on
                competence low
```

## How to build it

Training works: managers who were taught a small set of speaking and body habits were rated more charismatic by their coworkers three months later, in a 2011 study by Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti. Warmth comes from real interest in the other person, and competence from real skill in some field that you treat as normal. The rest is removing what leaks into the body as low confidence, and practising calm.

- Take genuine interest in the other person, and listen.
- Build real competence in some field.
- Reduce self-consciousness and worry about status.
- Both leak into the body and read as low confidence.
- Practise calm on small mishaps: rain, a cancelled taxi, running late.
- Act as if the mishap is handled.
- Show real emotion when speaking, since it shows no fear of judgment.
- Read the room before choosing how formal or blunt to be.
- Keep several ways of speaking to choose from.

Status and self-consciousness are covered further on [[wiki/Syntheses/Status, Vulnerability, and the Three Conversations|Status, Vulnerability, and the Three Conversations]].

## Where it fails

A person can charm one group by sharing its contempt for outsiders, and that charm stops at the group's edge. The kind meant on this wiki is broad charisma, which also avoids being a burden on strangers ([[wiki/Concepts/Meiwaku|meiwaku]]). Warmth and honesty can also pull apart. When only one is possible, honesty comes first, because a kind lie helps nobody, and honesty without warmth fails too, because people do not listen to it.

- Narrow charisma that works in one group only.
- Ask whether you want to be right or effective.
- A very warm person finds it hard to fire anyone.
- Charisma without [[wiki/Concepts/Good Faith|good faith]] is manipulation.
