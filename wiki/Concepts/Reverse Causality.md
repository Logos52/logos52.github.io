# Reverse Causality

Reverse causality is a closed loop in a set of notes. You explain why idea A is important by pointing at idea B, and B itself is something you still have to memorize. Remembering the "why" then depends on rote learning the reason. The link that should have made the material more logical has added another item to hold. The page is about catching that loop early and rebuilding the reason so that A's importance rests on a wider network you already have, not on a single arrow you have to store by force.

## What the loop looks like

Take a topic with several ideas, call them A, B, C, D, and E. The loop appears when you say that A is important because you need it to create B. That sentence only works if you have already memorized the fact that A creates B. It does not connect A to anything you knew before, or to any idea besides A and B. On a map, the only mark you can draw is an A-to-B arrow, and that arrow has to be remembered. Nothing else in the topic lets you infer why A is important.

## The two-sentence check

Say the sentence out loud: "A is important because B."

Then ask: "Would I know that only if I had memorized that B comes from A?"

If the answer is yes, the importance of A is being justified through rote memory. It is not being justified through prior knowledge or through other relationships already present in the topic.

## When the loop is most likely

Some ways of studying make the loop more common. A simple importance checklist, or a blunt question-and-answer pass, can increase it. A more intuitive way of grouping ideas, where you look for the role each idea plays in the whole topic, can help prevent it.

## The repair

A better reason for A uses more of the topic. A might matter because of how it bears on C and D, and because E gives rise to it. That ties A to C, D, and E. When you later learn that A also creates B, B arrives inside a network that already exists.

On a map, the broken version is a single arrow between two nodes and nothing else. The working version is a small web.
