# Cave Theory

The brain forgets most of what it takes in by design: storage costs energy, and a piece connected to nothing does not earn its keep. School material arrives in the shape the policy discards — isolated, answering to nothing yet. This system names the mismatch Cave Theory.

The mismatch is one of timescales. The machinery is roughly 300,000 years old; writing a few thousand, universal schooling a few hundred — a brain never built for books, notes, and classes. The old brain learned toward problems where failure was fatal — food, shelter — every clue, from sun position to tracks, pulled into a purpose already held, nothing kept apart from its use. That mode still shows — run it on your own material: handed an afternoon to teach a beginner the thing you know best that nobody assigned, nobody's plan is write it all out, hand it over to be read and copied, then quiz it. Yet that is the coursework plan, from a brain that got the hobby without a note.

The same brain ran both; only the frame differed. Information is held as a network: what answers to something — a problem, a pattern — reads as important and stays; what answers to nothing goes. One attachment is enough — the brain builds outward from it on its own. The sweep is aggressive: the organ is expensive, calories were scarce. None of this needs the prehistory; it stands on how material is encoded.

The techniques downstream are not tastes: each hands material something to answer to. [[Survive and Thrive]] builds the network — to what you already know, and among the new pieces — and details the mechanism; [[Memory Handling]] owns what to do inside the short working-memory window. [[Order Control]] follows relevance instead of the source's order — "that was another class" is a brake on connection, and the wall it takes down. [[Inquiry-Based Learning]] is questions written before the source opens, then answered — never open wandering. The frame governs how material is attached, never whether it is taught: for dry material, interest is built — problem first — not waited for.
