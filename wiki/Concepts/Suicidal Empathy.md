# Suicidal Empathy

## Related pages

- [[wiki/Books/Suicidal Empathy|Suicidal Empathy (book page)]]: the chapter-by-chapter treatment. The institutional and civilizational cases are on that page.
- [[wiki/Books/The Parasitic Mind|The Parasitic Mind]]: the cognitive predecessor, how a belief system defends itself against evidence. Suicidal empathy is the emotional half of the same account.
- [[wiki/Red Team/Red Teaming|Red Teaming]]: the parent practice, deliberately attacking a position you hold to find where it fails before it fails in use.
- [[wiki/Red Team/Applied Critical Thinking - Testing Frames|Applied Critical Thinking]]: the check in its general form, testing a frame before adopting it. The check on the compassion impulse applies the same test to your own response.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]]: the skill the pause is made of, holding an impulse long enough to check where it is pointing.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Mindset]]: the standing dispositions that decide which response fires before any deliberation happens.

## Sources

- Gad Saad, *Suicidal Empathy: Dying to Be Kind* (2026): the coinage, the definition, the miscalibration frame, and the repair sequence the check follows.
- Gad Saad, *The Parasitic Mind* (2020): the cognitive predecessor. The 2026 book supplies the emotional mechanism.
- Paul Bloom, *Against Empathy* (2016): the spotlight formulation, its innumeracy, and compassion plus reason as the alternative.
- D. A. Small, G. Loewenstein & P. Slovic (2007), *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 102(2), 143–153: the giving figures, identified victim against statistics, the combined condition, the bias lesson, and the calculation prime.
- C. D. Batson, T. R. Klein, L. Highberger & L. L. Shaw (1995), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 68, 1042–1054: empathy moving a child up an allocation list past a fairness judgment the movers still held.
- P. E. Tetlock (2003), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*: taboo trade-offs, weighing a sacred value as itself the offense, with moral cleansing to follow.
- S. D. Preston & F. B. M. de Waal (2002), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*: the perception-action model, empathy as evolved and proximity-weighted by design.
- J. Tosi & B. Warmke (2016), *Philosophy & Public Affairs*: moral grandstanding, standing purchased by the intensity of displayed positions.
