# Suicidal Empathy

Suicidal empathy is the state of the empathy response in which the feeling of caring has become the decision. Whatever the feeling responds to gets the help. The decision does not take in what is true, who else pays, or whether the care can last. The amount of feeling is ordinary. What fails is the aim: the care goes to the wrong target, at the wrong intensity, in the wrong way.

## Core takeaways

- Suicidal empathy is care in which the feeling is acted on directly, with no decision made apart from it. Whether the target is the right one, who else pays, and whether the care can last do not enter into it.
- The empathy response goes to whoever is near, easy to see, and most like the observer, and it does not count. One identified child with a photograph drew a mean gift of $2.38. Statistics describing millions in need drew $1.14.
- People act on the feeling even when their own fairness judgment says otherwise. People led to feel for one ill child moved her ahead of children in greater need, then rated their own decision as less fair than the allocations they made unmoved.
- Around a target treated as sacred, raising facts becomes costly, the target's actions get explained by its circumstances, and public displays of compassion raise the displayer's standing.
- The repair is one check at the moment the pull is strongest: hold the impulse, name the target's actual behavior and incentives, count who pays and who gains, weigh what remains against what is true and what can last, and act only on what survives.
- The check lowers giving to the visible case and adds nothing to the invisible one. Care that passes the check is calibrated empathy: aimed at the real target, counted, at an intensity that can last.

## Where the response goes

The empathy response works on one target at a time. It gives that target full attention and gives nothing to whatever it is not pointed at. It does not count: one visible person draws more response than a thousand people described as a number. It picks its target by proximity, visibility, and resemblance. The response goes to whoever is near, easy to see, and most like the observer.

In one experiment, people leaving with five dollars in singles could give to a food crisis. The request came in three forms:

```text
Statistics describing millions in need               mean gift  $1.14
One identified child, with a photograph              mean gift  $2.38
The same child, with the statistics beside her       mean gift  $1.43
```

**Showing the statistics next to the child lowered the gift to the child and did not raise giving to the many.**

The response picks targets this way because that is how it was built. It is old, shared with other social mammals, and made for a target at close range whom the observer would meet again. It weights proximity by design. It turns anti-adaptive when it fires automatically, when it fires at scale, or when it fires at an actor who profits from remaining the target of the care. In that last case the care flows in one direction for as long as that actor keeps up the act.

## What gets set aside

The first thing that gets set aside is the person's own judgment of fairness. People led to feel for one seriously ill child moved her up a waiting list, ahead of children who needed the care more. They then rated that decision as less fair and less moral than the allocations they had made without being moved. The fairness judgment was intact the whole time. People acted on the feeling anyway.

Facts get set aside the same way once a target is treated as sacred. Weighing the target against anything is itself the offense. Whoever asks for the weighing is met with outrage. A display of commitment follows, to clean away the offense. Facts that threaten the protected story become costly to pursue. They turn into forbidden knowledge. Nobody decides to suppress them; they get suppressed because pursuing them costs too much.

Around the protected target, cause is assigned in one direction only. Whatever the target does gets explained by its circumstances. The people it harmed get treated as a minor part of its story. The observer does not work out what the target actually gains from its behavior. The observer fills that gap with their own psychology and their own sense of what is sacred. Compassion is visible to others, so showing it in public raises the standing of the person showing it. Because of that reward, people escalate their displays and take positions whose costs fall on someone who is not part of the conversation.

## How it repeats

When nothing checks it, the process repeats in a cycle. Suffering becomes visible, either because it is real or because it has been framed to look that way, and the impulse fires. A target is selected by how well it fits a story and how much status it carries, ahead of how much it needs. Truth, trade-offs, and second-order costs get left out of the decision, because raising them is now the offense. A decision or a norm gets built around the feeling. Its costs pile up on whoever was never in view. When that suffering becomes visible, the response goes to it, and the cycle starts again on a new target, chosen the same way. At the scale of institutions and states the cycle runs the same way, with the people who pay further out of view.

## Checking the impulse before acting

At any scale, the repair is one check, done at the moment the pull to help is strongest:

- Feel the impulse and the hit of moral satisfaction that comes with it, and hold both without acting.
- Name the target by its actual behavior and its actual incentives.
- Map the consequences out to the second and third order. Count everyone who pays and everyone who gains, including whoever is not in the room.
- Weigh what remains against what is true and what can last. Weigh it also against whether the care runs both ways and what it costs in merit.
- Act only on the care that survives the check.

## What the check costs

The check has a cost. People primed to calculate before the same giving request cut the visible child's gift by nearly half, from $2.34 to $1.19. What went to the many did not move, $1.45 to $1.54. People taught about the bias just before the request gave less overall, $2.00 down to $1.31, and the whole drop came out of the identified child's gift.

Thinking it through and learning about the bias both lower the response. Neither one moves it to a better target. So the check has to change what the decision runs on. It cannot work by teaching the feeling about itself. The check will feel like what the numbers show: less for the vivid case, and nothing extra for the invisible one. A check that produces only reasons not to help, and never a better-aimed way to help, has turned into an excuse for not helping. When that happens, give first and audit the next impulse instead.

## Care that passes the check

Care that survives the check is calibrated empathy: care aimed at the real target, counting its consequences, at an intensity that can last. The warmth is the same as before. The difference is that the care has been counted and still holds. Understanding another person's frame sharpens what you see and leaves your judgment intact. It becomes dangerous when understanding turns into giving in. The count of who pays includes the person doing the caring. Caring that uses up its carer does not last. A person who treats their own claims as worthless has no position left to care from.

## How to practice this

1. When you feel the pull to help, feel it and hold it without acting. Notice the hit of moral satisfaction that comes with the pull.
2. Name the target by what it actually does and what it actually gains. Notice whether you had been explaining its actions by its circumstances.
3. List everyone who pays and everyone who gains, out to the second and third order. Include the people who are not in the room and include yourself. Notice who was outside your view before you listed them.
4. Weigh what is left against what is true and what can last. Ask whether the care runs both ways and what it costs in merit. Notice that the vivid case now gets less and the invisible case gets nothing extra.
5. Act only on the care that survived. If the check gave you only reasons not to help, give first and audit the next impulse.
