# Suicidal Empathy

Suicidal empathy is the state of the empathy response in which the feeling of caring has become the decision. Whatever the feeling lights up gets the help; what is true, who else pays, and whether the care can last never get a vote. The amount of feeling is ordinary — what fails is the aim: wrong target, wrong intensity, wrong way.

## How the Aim Is Set

The response aims like a spotlight: one point at full brightness, everything outside the beam dark. It is innumerate — one face outshines a thousand — and it aims by proximity, visibility, and resemblance: the beam goes to whoever is near, easy to see, and most like the observer. People leaving an experiment with five dollars in singles could give to a food crisis; the request came in three forms:

```text
Statistics describing millions in need               mean gift  $1.14
One identified child, with a photograph              mean gift  $2.38
The same child, with the statistics beside her       mean gift  $1.43
```

**Putting the many beside the one subtracts from the one and adds nothing to the many.**

The response aims this way because it was built to — old, shared with other social mammals, made for a target at close range you would meet again, proximity-weighted by design. It turns anti-adaptive when fired automatically, at scale, or at an actor who profits from staying in the beam: the care then flows one way for as long as the act holds.

## What the Aim Overrides

What the beam overrides first is the person's own fairness judgment. People led to feel for one seriously ill child moved her up a waiting list, ahead of children who needed the care more — then rated that decision as less fair and less moral than their unmoved allocations. The fairness judgment stayed intact the whole time; the feeling outranked it. Truth loses the same way once a target is sacred: weighing it at all is the offense, outrage lands on whoever asked, a cleansing display of commitment follows, and facts threatening the protected story turn costly to pursue — forbidden knowledge, suppressed with nobody deciding to.

Around the protected target, cause gets assigned only outward: whatever the target does is traced to circumstances, and the people it harmed become background in its story. Its real incentives go unmodeled, replaced by the observer's own psychology and sense of the sacred. And because compassion is visible, displaying it buys standing, which selects for escalation and for positions whose costs land on someone outside the conversation.

## The Loop

Left running, the parts close into a cycle. Suffering becomes visible — real, or framed to be — and the impulse fires; a target is selected by narrative fit and status value, ahead of need. Truth, trade-offs, and second-order costs drop out, because raising them is now the offense. A decision or a norm gets built around the feeling; its costs accumulate on whoever was never in the beam, until that suffering turns visible, the response redirects, and the loop restarts on a new target with the same aim. At institutional and state scale it runs unchanged, with the people who pay further from view.

## Checking the Impulse

At any scale, the repair is one check, run where the pull is strongest:

- The impulse and its hit of moral satisfaction get felt, and held.
- The target gets named by its actual behavior and actual incentives.
- Consequences get mapped to the second and third order, counting everyone who pays and everyone who gains — including whoever is not in the room.
- What remains gets weighed against what is true and what can last — and against whether the care runs both ways and what it costs in merit.
- Only care that survives the check is acted on.

The check is priced. Primed to calculate before the same giving request, people cut the visible child's gift nearly in half — $2.34 down to $1.19 — and moved what went to the many not at all, $1.45 to $1.54. Taught about the bias just beforehand, they gave less overall, $2.00 down to $1.31, the whole fall out of the identified child's gift. Deliberation and information both turn the response down; neither re-aims it — so the check has to change what the decision runs on, not what the feeling knows about itself, and it will feel like that: less for the vivid case, nothing extra for the invisible one. A check yielding only reasons not to help — never better-aimed help — has become a permission slip; the move then is to give first and audit the next impulse.

## Calibrated Empathy

What survives the check is calibrated empathy: care aimed at the real target, counting its consequences, at an intensity that can last. The warmth is unchanged; what changes is that the care survives being counted. Understanding someone's frame sharpens perception and keeps judgment — it turns dangerous where understanding becomes surrender. And the count of who pays includes the carer: caring that consumes its carer does not last, and a person who treats their own claims as worthless has nothing left to aim from.
