# Higher-Order Generativity vs Higher-Order Judgment

Higher-order thinking is the work of seeing how the facts, people and pressures in one situation change each other, in a case with no single right answer. The owner splits that work into two jobs: generativity, which is producing a draft, a plan or a design that hangs together, and judgment, which is choosing one and answering for the result when it goes wrong. The split tells a worker which job to hand to an AI model and which to keep.

## Core takeaways

- Employers pay for solving future problems nobody has defined yet.
- AI models already produce drafts, plans and designs that hang together.
- A model does not carry the cost when a call turns out wrong.
- The person who pays when a call is wrong holds the judgment.
- Memorising and reciting facts loses value as models do it.
- The split may blur or disappear as models improve.

## The two halves

Generativity is the making of something that hangs together: an essay, a plan, a code change, a set of options. Models now do much of this faster and cheaper than a person. Judgment is choosing between those things in a real situation, where the facts are new and tangled and each one depends on the others, and then standing behind the choice. That job stays with the person who answers for the result.

| | Generativity | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output | a draft, a plan, options | a decision |
| Who does it now | models, cheaply | a person |
| Test | does it hang together | who pays if it is wrong |

- A model can list ten launch plans in a minute.
- Picking one and owning the result is judgment.
- A person who checks a model's work is doing judgment.
- Work that surprises and serves a purpose is still mostly done by people.

## Why lower-order skill loses value

Lower-order thinking takes each fact on its own: read it, remember it, repeat it on a test. An employer will pay only so much for that, and the amount is falling because models do the same work. Higher-order thinking treats a fact as useful only for how it changes the other facts around it. Getting very good at the first kind does not turn into the second kind, because they are separate habits.

- Surveys expect 30 to 50% of job skills to change in five years.
- Doing 40 hours of work in 10 with AI invites replacement.
- A worker's value is in the tangled cases models handle badly.
- Those cases are hard for people too, and few do them well.

## How to build the judgment side

Judgment is trained by working with the links between facts from the first minute. Use each new piece in a real problem straight away, before the topic feels understood, and ask how it changes the pieces already there. Draw the pieces and their links on paper, and redraw the picture when it turns out wrong, since a messy first attempt is part of the work. Judgment becomes reliable only where the situation gives clear signals and the person hears how their calls turned out.

- Apply new material to a real problem on day one.
- Organise ideas by how they affect each other.
- Map on paper, and expect the first map to be wrong.
- Get feedback on your calls, or judgment will not improve.

## How this desk uses the split

The owner applies the split to his own tools. Work where a wrong result is cheap to catch goes to cheaper models, with a check on the output. Work where the call matters, such as writing a story, goes to the strongest model, and the owner reviews the result. He has not yet settled which jobs in his notes belong on each side.

- Hand off generation where a check can catch errors.
- Keep the final call on anything published or paid for.
