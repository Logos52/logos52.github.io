# Bias and Framing

A bias is a lean in someone's judgment that runs the same way every time. Framing is the choice of words and emphasis that carries that lean into a sentence. Knowing how the two connect tells a reader where to look: every fact in a sentence can be true while one word has already set which conclusion feels right.

## Core takeaways

- A bias has a direction, which a random mistake does not have. Correcting a bias means finding that direction and adjusting for it, since fixing one wrong answer at a time leaves the lean in place.
- Framing changes how facts feel without changing the facts, so fact checking cannot catch it.
- The sign of framing is a swapped word: a near-synonym with a different moral charge stands in while everything else stays the same.
- Test a sentence by replacing the loaded word with a plain word for the same thing. If only the feeling changes, the choice of word is what made the conclusion feel right.
- A test of someone's framing counts as a test only when it is also run on your own side. Run against opponents only, it is more framing.
- Naming frames can replace checking whether a claim is true. If weeks of it produce labels and no changed judgment, go back to the claim and the evidence.

## How it works

- Bias
  - It sits under the stated conclusion: in how the question was asked, which evidence was picked, which answers got rewarded.
  - The person holding a bias usually cannot see it, because the place it was learned rewards that view and peers confirm it.
- Framing
  - A frame shows some parts of a case and plays down or leaves out the rest.
  - Every stated fact can be true. What moves is the moral charge, so the same facts read as fair or as cruel depending on the word attached.
  - A frame lands best when it fits what the listener already believes.
  - Leaving a fact out works as well as a loaded word and is harder to catch, because nothing said is false.
  - In a 1981 experiment, the same set of outcomes described as lives saved or as lives lost got opposite choices from comparable groups.
- How the two connect
  - Framing is the usual way a bias travels from one person to another.
  - How a problem is framed decides which answers feel reasonable before any answer is weighed.
  - Once a frame is accepted, evidence against it gets read as fitting it. Changing a mind then runs through the frame, because new facts entering an intact frame are absorbed by it.

| Move | Facts | What changes | How to catch it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lying | one is false | the facts | check them |
| Emphasis | all true | which fact comes first | read the rest |
| Framing | all true | the moral charge on a fact | swap the word |

## An example

In 2026, at a New York City housing hearing, an activist called evictions "the violence of evictions". An eviction is a landlord removing a tenant from a property. Who was removed, from where, and on what grounds stay the same under either wording.

```
plain:   the landlord evicted the tenant
framed:  the landlord did the violence of eviction

same:    who, from where, on what grounds
moved:   the landlord is now an aggressor
result:  taking the property later reads as a remedy
```

- The word sets a moral verdict before any argument starts. Once it is in place, people argue about who is the aggressor instead of about what is owned.
- A panel of investors who discussed the hearing on a podcast said the same thing: moral charge first, argument built on top.
- The same panel called the city's mayor a dictator and a comrade. Those words do the same work the other way, and the panel did not test them.
- One panelist ran an exposure test: ask whether the person holding a belief bears its costs. He said the activists do not ride the bus or use the parks, so the costs of their beliefs fall on others. The test is sound, and it was not turned on the panel itself, investors arguing the property-owner side.

## How to apply it

- Red teaming: rewrite each loaded term as a plain word and read the claim again. If the argument still holds, the framing was decoration. If it falls, the framing was the argument.
- Decisions: before choosing among options, say who wrote the option list and what a third option would be. A narrow frame does its damage by deciding what counts as a choice.
- Writing: when reaching for the stronger word, say what it buys beyond the plain one. If the answer is only that it hits harder, that is framing.
- Reciprocity: before testing someone else's framing, test one belief of your own and say what it found.

## Where it fails

- Every sentence selects, so a frame can be named in any sentence. A reader who names frames can dismiss a claim without reading its evidence, and the naming feels like analysis.
- The claim that most bias travels by framing rests on a few cases and has not been measured.
- Quit signal: two weeks of reading this way with nothing newly believed and nothing newly checked means the method has become a vocabulary.
