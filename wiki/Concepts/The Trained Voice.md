# The Trained Voice

A language model's default voice is trained self-regard. It learned to write from an internet where prose survived by selling, so display is the register it falls back to — and selfhood, one person's judgment left visible, is the one thing an average of everyone can't contain. Working close to the self with a model is a running rejection of its default voice.

## The Corpus Was Paid in Attention

Prose survives on the open internet by winning attention, and attention converts to money — that is the economy the corpus grew in. Writing that converted got published, promoted, linked, and scraped; writing that wanted nothing sat in low-traffic corners. So the sample the machine learned "good writing" from leans toward the register of the pitch, the way a diet of billboards would bend a person's sense of the sentence. The parent page, [[wiki/Concepts/The Two Meanings of Ego|The Two Meanings of Ego]], names the cultural half of this: when the self is run as a small enterprise, self-promotion stops feeling like a vice and starts feeling like compliance. The corpus is that compliance, archived at scale. A later layer of training compounds it — raters comparing two drafts tend to reward the one that sounds finished and confident, and "sounds finished" is mostly cadence: the balanced pair, the punchline landing. The sell survives every stage because every stage voted for it.

## The Tics Are Sales Tics

The evidence sits at line level, in the moves the model reaches for unprompted. The hooky question. The reveal that plants a wrong idea in order to overturn it. The superlative. The balanced clause pair that sounds like a conclusion without containing one. The hedge wrapped around a plain claim — self-regard pointed downward, in the parent page's phrase. The tagline cadence on anything front-facing. This vault's [[02 - System/Writing Standards|writing standards]] ban each of these by name, and that list accreted strike by strike out of model drafts rather than arriving by design. Read the strike history in one sitting and it is a single discovery made repeatedly: left on defaults, the machine writes display. Every banned move fails the same test — it works on the reader instead of transferring something to them — and that test is the parent page's line-level question wearing work clothes.

## What Cannot Be in the Weights

The other half of the split explains the flatness that shows up once the selling is scrubbed. Selfhood, on the parent page's definition, is one particular person's judgment left visible — this cut and no other, this example kept, that claim turned away. A model is an average of many people's judgments, and an average of judgments is the "anyone" voice by construction: whatever only one person would have chosen is exactly what averaging removes. So the machine can carry a selfhood voice — it holds whatever register a person enforces on it — while supplying none of its own. The judgment walks in from outside, from whoever is at the desk, and it has to keep walking in, because the default behaves like a center of gravity: the output falls back toward it whenever the pressure stops.

## The Case Against

Three objections bite, and each sharpens the page rather than sinking it. The machine has no motives, so "works to impress" is loose talk — true, and the repair is the parent page's own: the test runs against the sentence, never the soul. What a line does to a reader is checkable; what the machine meant by it was never a real question. Second, the corpus holds the plain voices too — the writers this vault's craft pages are derived from are in the weights alongside the salesmen — which is why standards work at all: steady pressure moves the output off the default and holds it there. The claim is about the prior, never the ceiling. Third, the rejection can itself curdle into display — conspicuous restraint, taste worn as a badge — the re-denomination the parent page already documents. The fix stays the same: the same question, asked of the rejecting sentence too.

Working close to the self with a model turns out to be a running act of rejection, most of it small — this landing deleted, this pair unbalanced, this hedge cut, this credential left out. The parent page's rule already said what to aim for: selfhood as high as honesty allows, self-regard at zero, held as a direction. What the machine changes is the daily pressure against that direction — the ambient voice now arrives pre-sold, on tap, free. It also makes the split easier to see than it has ever been. The trained voice is self-regard with nobody behind it, display running on momentum alone, and watching it pour out of a machine settles an old suspicion about the substance: the sell belongs to the economy that raised it, and an economy's voice can be declined.
