# Selfhood

Selfhood is the filter a person runs on their own life: a repeated decision, object by object and sentence by sentence, about what earns its place against a real use and what gets cut. What shows on the outside is only what the filter kept. Handwriting identifies its writer without being shaped for recognition; a wardrobe, habit, or page built by this filter works the same way—years of small decisions none of which was made in order to be seen.

Use the filter when you choose what to keep, what to write, what to practice, and what to say. It replaces audience-managed identity with integrated standards and repairs the habit of counting only the cost of your presence while ignoring what your presence gives.

## Which standard the decisions answer to

Whether a standard is private or public is the wrong sort. The better sort is integrated versus introjected: a standard you genuinely endorse versus one you took in whole and now enforce from inside. Introjection still seeks approval from self or others, so removing the audience does not remove it. In unobserved lab tasks, people shifted endurance toward whichever direction they had been told was the healthy sign and reported the change as involuntary.

Early effort does not settle the question. Audience-driven goals and owned goals look identical at week zero; they diverge around weeks two to four. A check that holds has three parts: the standard was specified in advance, it can return a verdict you do not want, and you would still act on it if acting became socially costly.

## The filter on objects and habits

On objects, each keep-or-cut decision should cost something when it is wrong. A mistake packed across repeated moves keeps the filter honest. The clearest sign of an integrated standard is an item kept although it looks wrong—it costs more in appearance than it returns, and you keep it anyway.

On habits, streaks and quotas build a record anyone could have. A map of walked streets or a tree of unlocked capacities is yours because every point was a choice. Tracking raises how much people do an activity and lowers how much they enjoy it with no external incentive. Daily behaviour in a consistent context becomes automatic with no tracking; the question of whose standard is in use is asked once at the choice of practice, not run as a metric forever. Checked after practice, the record is growth you can look at; checked before, it is an audit that decides whether the day is permitted.

## One test for every medium

Remove the part that faces other people and see whether anything left serves your actual week. For prose, delete the first-person clause; lost facts about the subject mean the clause was needed, lost impression of you alone means display. For habits, ask whether the metric would mean anything after a month off. For objects, whether you would acquire them again today. For purchases, name what they signal. For rooms, what behaviour the layout makes automatic.

Display aimed at an audience is cut. Of 153 laws in this vault's writing standard, selfhood is the sole reason for 55 and a second reason for 22; 87 descend from clarity and honesty to the reader and would stand if selfhood vanished tomorrow. Zero display is a direction, not a level. Status changes form—restraint meant to be noticed, taste meant to be noticed, humility as a move.

Outside writing, the test must produce an observation, not introspection: thirty days with no announcement, six weeks with no tracker, two years of daily use with no visible wear. A felt answer to "would I still do this if nobody saw" is worth nothing. The filter is a decision procedure for the moment of choice; it does not answer what a person is. Some acts are for how someone sees you by design—a gift, an introduction, work shown to be hired.

## Meiwaku: counting burden before you act

迷惑 (*meiwaku*) names unnecessary trouble you export to others: noise, space, attention, time, energy. The habit is to count that burden before you act, cut what serves no purpose, and keep what does. Without the keep step, consideration becomes self-removal. The norm is wider than politeness: it covers people who never chose to deal with you. Micro scale is public behaviour; meso and macro extensions in this vault apply the same count to home, work, and life plans.

Four failure modes: self-erasure, quiet resentment without naming the norm, over-policing every inconvenience, and aesthetic minimalism that still exports noise and shifted work. Five questions before you act: space, time, attention beyond need; ongoing work for someone else; communication useful or merely demanding attention; whether quieting this improves the shared environment; burden you could internalize or eliminate.

## Speaking: the ledger with one column missing

Speaking always reaches another person. Meiwaku applied to visible mess works because cost is all there is. Applied to speech, it records what a sentence takes and not what it gives—yet value is the only reason to say one. The wording "consumes attention without returning value" admits value exists and never asks about value again. Each reasonable verdict adds up to silence over years: the slide seen from inside.

[[wiki/Concepts/Good Faith|Good Faith]] prices withholding: a problem revealed early does far less damage than one found after trust is spent, so silence can be the imposition. The repair is bookkeeping, not courage. Judgment made in the room favors silence because cost is vivid and value is not; rules made at the desk fire when stated conditions hold.

**Say the thing only you hold** when it bears on the decision and would not surface from someone else within ten minutes. **Shrink the room, not the sentence** when the public version is impossible—one person, a message, a working group. **Ask the question** when you hold something uncertain; questions often draw out what no one else will say. **Deliver inside their frame** so the hard point costs less to receive. **Retire the after-feeling**; what gets a vote is whether anyone used, quoted, or acted on it—not how you felt you landed. **Decide on Tuesdays**: positions worked out in writing before the room exists.

Both ledger columns meet one bar: name the person and the specific thing gained or lost. [[wiki/Concepts/The Two Meanings of Ego|The Two Meanings of Ego]] swap and deletion tests catch display dressed as contribution. The motive test from wanting less fails on speech—wanting to say a true thing and wanting to be seen saying it feel identical from inside. The strongest safeguard is a witness who will answer "useful or noise" straight.

Quiet stays correct when the listener cannot leave, when the point is already in the room, when the contribution creates recurring obligation for someone else, when the point is standing, when conviction is unsettled, when the room is already loud. Everything else—less space, less noise, leaving things cleaner—stays as before. Count both sides of a sentence and verdicts come back mixed; the person stays careful and stops being silent.

## What is left with no score

Some activity holds attention with no scoreboard at all. Its test is whether the thing still holds attention with no metric. The filters say which standards to refuse; they do not name this state.
