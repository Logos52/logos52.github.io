# Selfhood

## Sources

- Ryan & Deci (2000), *Contemporary Educational Psychology* 25:54–67, doi:10.1006/ceps.1999.1020. The internalisation continuum; introjection as internal regulation that is still controlling, with approval sought from self or others; includes the 1989 finding of effort alongside anxiety and poorer coping.
- Sheldon & Elliot (1999), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 76(3):482–497, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482. Ownership rather than pleasure as the distinguishing feature; controlled and self-owned goals diverge at weeks two to four.
- Etkin (2016), *Journal of Consumer Research* 42(6):967–984, doi:10.1093/jcr/ucv095. Measurement raises output and lowers enjoyment, with no external incentive required.
- Quattrone & Tversky (1984), doi:10.1037/0022-3514.46.2.237. Self-signalling; unobserved endurance shifted toward whichever direction was described as diagnostic.
- Lally et al. (2010), doi:10.1002/ejsp.674. Automaticity from repetition in context with no tracking, reward, or streak; a single missed opportunity did not materially affect the process; the abstract's figure is a range of 18–254 days across 39 well-fitting cases of 96 starters.
- Wood & Rünger (2016), doi:10.1146/annurev-psych-122414-033417. Habits as context–response associations; leverage at situation design, and intentions inferred back from frequency.
- Tangney et al. (1998), doi:10.1037/0022-3514.75.1.256. The direct test that found no support for the discrepancy grid's central hypotheses; the limit on how finely the sorting can be done.
- Nickerson, Schwarz, Diener & Kahneman (2003), doi:10.1046/j.0956-7976.2003.psci_1461.x. The cost of external standards is domain-localised rather than global.
- Crocker & Wolfe (2001), doi:10.1037/0033-295X.108.3.593. Worth staked on domains rather than held at a level, with a non-contingent third state.
- Leary & Baumeister (2000), doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(00)80003-9. Self-esteem as a gauge, which pushes toward behavioural criteria over felt certainty; chapter text unverified, cited only for the behavioural-criterion argument.
- Kasser & Ryan (1996), *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 22(3):280–287, doi:10.1177/0146167296223006. Relative centrality rather than absolute level; appearance and social recognition sit on the extrinsic side, which is what licenses running the test on possessions.
- Tse et al. (2018, 2020), doi:10.1080/00223891.2018.1491855 · doi:10.1080/17439760.2020.1716055. The no-scoreboard state, and why immediate feedback is not the same thing as measurement.

Magnitudes reported only in secondary summaries were left out deliberately. The citations are confirmed, and only the direction of each finding is used above.
