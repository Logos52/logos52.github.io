# Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming

Thinking with material is comparing, predicting, explaining, retrieving, deciding, or building. Consuming material is working with it without doing any of those six things. The same visible act can be either, depending on what the head is doing while it happens. A highlight, a map, or a watched video does not by itself show which of the two happened.

## Core takeaways

- Thinking with material means comparing, predicting, explaining, retrieving, deciding, or building. If none of those six happened, the hour was consumption.
- The same visible act, such as a highlight, a map, or a watched video, can be thinking or consumption. What decides it is what the head is doing.
- Consumption is marked by low engagement. It feels easy and familiar, and that ease is why it passes for thinking.
- Measuring progress by pages, notes, or videos finished is the clearest sign of consumption. Coverage does not measure whether thinking happened.
- Thinking is marked by transformation. The form of the information changes, and it gets connected to something already known or to a use.
- For most people passive is the default. Leaving it is a choice.

## What consumption looks like

Reading, taking notes, marking lines, watching a video, making cards, and drawing a map can all fail as soon as they become a shallow routine. Highlighting and going over the text again are the usual pair. Cards fail when they only trigger recognition. A map fails when it is copied.

Passive work tends to look like this: consuming line by line; going over a text without changing its form; copying or marking without a judgment; following an explanation without generating one; measuring progress by pages, notes, or videos finished. That last sign is the clearest one. Coverage does not measure whether thinking happened.

The signal these have in common is low cognitive engagement.

## Why consumption passes for thinking

The thing that misleads is ease. Consumption feels like thinking because it is easy and familiar. An hour can feel fluent and still leave little that can be used later.

For most people, passive is the default. Switching out of it is a choice. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]] covers making that choice.

## What thinking looks like

The useful test is whether information is being manipulated into meaning. Active work tends to look like this: holding two ideas against each other; judging importance; generating analogies; teaching or preparing to teach; mapping how pieces relate; asking what problem the knowledge solves; testing recall in the setting where it will be needed.

The signal these have in common is transformation. The form of the information changes, and it gets connected to something already known or to a use. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]] covers how that transformation works.

The thing to watch is the thought pattern that the method is causing, whichever method it is. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition: The Control Layer]] covers that watching.

## The same tool can be either

A mind map made by copying nodes and arrows is consumption. A short note written after a hard comparison is thinking. Both are the same kind of artifact. The thinking behind them is opposite.

Retrieval is one of the six verbs. [[wiki/Concepts/Memory Handling|Memory Handling]] covers retrieval in full.

## Checking an hour against the six verbs

The six verbs are the checklist for a session. Compare, predict, explain, retrieve, decide, or build: if none of those happened, the hour was consumption, however finished the artifact looks.

Noticing the slip while it is happening is a separate skill. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Building the Radar]] covers that skill.

The feeling of ease is what misleads. The tool does not mislead. A map made mechanically can feel fluent and still be busywork. The six verbs tell whether the hour was thinking.

## How to practice this

1. At the end of a study hour, go through the six verbs: compare, predict, explain, retrieve, decide, build. Notice whether any of them happened. If none did, the hour was consumption, however finished the notes look.
2. Look at how you measure progress. Notice whether you count pages, notes, or videos finished. Counting those is the clearest sign of consumption.
3. Take a note or map you made and compare it with its source. Notice whether it copies the source's form or changes it. Copied nodes and arrows are consumption; a short note written after a hard comparison is thinking.
4. Answer a set of your own cards. Notice whether each card only triggers recognition of the answer. Cards that only trigger recognition have failed.
5. When an hour feels easy and fluent, check it instead of trusting it. Notice whether it left anything you can use later. Ease is what misleads.
6. While working, watch the thought pattern the method is causing. Notice whether you are holding two ideas against each other, judging importance, or generating an explanation. If you are only following an explanation, you are consuming.
