# Wabi-Sabi

Wabi-sabi is an aesthetic stance. It finds beauty in naturalness, simplicity, and subtle imperfection. The imperfection it values comes from age, from wear, and from work left unfinished. Industrial gloss is its opposite.

In this vault, wabi-sabi is a taste call on the Human track of the [[wiki/Concepts/Human vs AI Capability Lens|Human vs AI Capability Lens]]. The Human track is the half of that lens that scores what a person still has to own. Wabi-sabi is not a learning technique.

## Core takeaways

- Wabi-sabi finds beauty in naturalness, simplicity, and subtle imperfection.
- The imperfection it values comes from age, wear, and work left unfinished. Industrial gloss is the opposite look.
- The wear has to have happened already, through age and use. Wabi-sabi does not permit shipping sloppy work.
- Polish, softness, and deliberate roughness are options a maker chooses between. No rule forces the choice. People are also drawn to gloss and to curved forms.
- In this vault, wabi-sabi is a taste call on the Human track, the half of the capability lens that scores what a person still has to own. It is not a learning technique.

## Which wear counts

The wear wabi-sabi values has already happened. An object got it through age and use. Wabi-sabi does not permit shipping sloppy work.

## A choice among looks

A maker chooses between polish, softness, and deliberate roughness. No rule forces the choice. People are also drawn to gloss and to curved forms. That is why wabi-sabi is called a stance.

## Why this vault has a page for it

Wabi-sabi has a page of its own so the lens can link a named Human-5 standout. The rest of Human-5 stay as rows. The seed stays short until design work needs a dedicated page.

## How to practice this

1. Pick one object you use every day that shows wear. Check whether the wear came from age and use. That kind of wear is the kind wabi-sabi values.
2. Take one piece of your own work that is rough or unfinished. Ask whether you chose the roughness or left it by neglect. Chosen roughness fits the stance. Sloppy work does not.
3. Take one design decision in front of you. Name whether you are leaning toward polish, softness, or deliberate roughness. Notice that no rule forced the pick.
4. Watch what draws your eye across a day. Notice each time it is gloss or a curved form. Those pulls are normal. They are the reason wabi-sabi is called a stance.
5. When you judge a piece by wabi-sabi, note that you are making a taste call. Notice that the call is yours to own. It is not a learning technique.
