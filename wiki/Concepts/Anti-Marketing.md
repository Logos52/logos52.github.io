# Anti-Marketing

Anti-marketing is the practice of showing the flaws, limits and trade-offs of a thing before the other person has spent time or money on it. It changes who stays: the people who would have left later leave now, while leaving is still cheap, and the people who stay know what they chose.

## Core takeaways

- Put the difficult parts first, while walking away costs the other person almost nothing.
- A weakness shown early reads as honesty. The same weakness found after a smooth pitch reads as something hidden.
- Ask who should still want this once they have seen the weak points, rather than how to look good to the most people.
- Show only what changes fit, trust or understanding. A page of caveats is a second way to fail.
- Saying the awkward thing early is not a licence to say everything. The limit is the burden it puts on the listener.

## How it works

- Ordinary marketing orders the facts by how flattering they are. Anti-marketing orders them by how much they change the decision.
- The cost of a mismatch grows with time.
  - Before commitment: a few minutes of reading, one meeting, a free trial.
  - After commitment: a hire to undo, a purchase to refund, months in a relationship.
- Early disclosure changes how the weakness is read.
  - A study of criminal and civil trials found that a side which raised its own damaging fact first suffered less from it than when the other side raised it.
  - A consumer research study found that a claim naming its own downsides is more credible when the listener can use that downside to decide.
- The credibility gain is not automatic. It appears only when the negative fact bears on the listener's choice.

```
ordinary pitch          anti-marketing
---------------         ---------------
strengths -> commit     limits -> some leave
   -> limits found         -> the rest commit
   -> trust drops          -> trust holds
```

## Where it applies

- Public writing and a knowledge base: keep open questions, unfinished thinking, named limits and corrections visible. Hiding them turns the work into a brand.
- Products and services: say what the thing is not for before the trial starts.
- Hiring and dating: state the constraints in the first conversation, when either side can still walk away without loss.
- Sharing research: publish what did not work alongside what did.

## Where it fails

- Vulnerability as a performance. A flaw chosen because it makes the speaker look brave is a pitch of another kind, and readers notice.
- Over-disclosure. Listing every fault puts a load on the reader that they did not ask for. This vault calls that load on others meiwaku, and the Meiwaku page sets the limit.
- Saying nothing. A practice that wants nothing from the audience can slide into never stating what the thing is good for. The strengths still get said, after the limits.
