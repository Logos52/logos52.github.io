# Anti-Marketing

Anti-marketing is showing the flaws, limits and trade-offs of a thing before the other person has spent time or money on it. It changes who stays. People who would have left later leave now, while leaving is still cheap, and the people who stay know what they chose.

- Show the difficult parts while walking away still costs little.
- A weakness shown early reads as honesty.
- The same weakness found later reads as something hidden.
- Ask who should still want this after seeing its weak points.
- Show only what changes fit, trust or understanding.
- A page of caveats is a second way to fail.

## How it works

Ordinary marketing puts the facts about a thing in order of how flattering they are. Anti-marketing puts them in order of how much they change the other person's decision. The reason is that a mismatch gets more expensive the longer it goes unnoticed. A few minutes of reading or a free trial costs little to walk away from, and a hire, a purchase or months in a relationship costs a great deal.

- Before commitment: a few minutes of reading, one meeting, a trial.
- After commitment: a hire to undo, a refund, months lost.
- In court trials, a side that raised its own damaging fact suffered less.
- A claim that names its own downside is more credible to consumers.
- That gain appears only when the downside bears on their choice.

```
ordinary pitch           anti-marketing
---------------          ---------------
strengths -> commit      limits -> some leave
-> limits found          -> the rest commit
-> trust drops           -> trust holds
```

## Where it applies

The practice fits anywhere a person can walk away early at little cost. In public writing, it means leaving the unfinished parts visible, so readers see the work as it stands. In products, hiring and dating, it means naming the constraints in the first conversation.

- Public writing: keep open questions, limits and corrections visible.
- Hiding them turns the work into a brand.
- Products and services: say what the thing is not for.
- Hiring and dating: state constraints in the first conversation.
- Research: publish what did not work alongside what did.

## Where it fails

Showing a flaw can itself turn into a pitch, when the flaw is picked because it makes the speaker look brave, and readers notice that. The opposite failure is listing every fault, which puts a load on the reader that they did not ask for. On this wiki that kind of load is called meiwaku, from the Japanese word for trouble caused to others, and the page under that name sets the limit.

- Vulnerability as a performance.
- Over-disclosure that burdens the reader.
- Never saying what the thing is good for.
- The strengths still get said, after the limits.
