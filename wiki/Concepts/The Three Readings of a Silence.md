# The Three Readings of a Silence

## Sources

- Ryan & Deci (2000), *Contemporary Educational Psychology* 25:54–67, doi:10.1006/ceps.1999.1020: the internalisation continuum; introjection as internal regulation that still runs on pressure, with approval from self or others as its process.
- Ryan & Connell (1989), reported in the above at p. 63: introjected regulation predicted both effort and greater anxiety with poorer coping after failure.
- Sheldon & Elliot (1999), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 76(3):482–497, doi:10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482: the four reasons a goal gets pursued, ownership as the operative distinction, and the warning that a pressured standard and an owned one diverge only at weeks two to four.
- Higgins (1987), *Psychological Review* 94(3):319–340, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.94.3.319: the own-versus-other standpoint grid that the direct test below targets.
- Etkin (2016), *Journal of Consumer Research* 42(6):967–984, doi:10.1093/jcr/ucv095: six experiments in which private measurement converted an activity into work with no audience or incentive present; why the second repair is a question and never a metric.
- Quattrone & Tversky (1984), doi:10.1037/0022-3514.46.2.237: the unobserved cold-water endurance shift; actions as evidence to oneself.
- Tangney et al. (1998), doi:10.1037/0022-3514.75.1.256: the direct test that found no support for the standard-sorting grid's central hypotheses; the discriminant-validity problem the case against rests on.
- Leary & Baumeister (2000), doi:10.1016/S0065-2601(00)80003-9: self-esteem as a gauge of social inclusion, which is why the test prefers facts about a memory over felt certainty. Chapter text unverified; only the mechanism is claimed.

Effect sizes and magnitudes were not re-verified and are omitted. No published literature is cited for the third reading.
