# Levels of Thinking - The Step Back

Levels of thinking is a model of how people grow in awareness, where each level is one more step back from your own reaction. At the bottom you only feel what you want, and higher up you can see how others see you, how your culture looks to a stranger, and at the top how your own mind builds a reaction while it happens. Knowing the steps gives a way to slow down before acting on a feeling.

## Core takeaways

- Each level adds one step back from your own reaction.
- Everyone uses the lower levels every day.
- Higher levels mean more awareness, and nothing about being a good person.
- Nearly everyone reaches about level four by adulthood.
- People develop unevenly, strong in one area and weak in another.
- The way up is asking where a thought came from.

## The nine levels

Everyone starts at level one, and how far a person goes depends on their surroundings, their choices, their culture and the brain they were born with. A level is a way of thinking a person can reach, and nobody lives in only one, since needing the bathroom is a level-one feeling for anyone. Each level sees everything the one below it sees, plus one more layer of other people's minds. Levels one to three are about getting what you want.

```
9 complete    watch where you are looking from
8 aware       watch your mind build a reaction
7 harmonize   see yourself and others as a history
6 individual  who looks changes what is seen
5 achieve     how my culture looks to a stranger
4 belong      how others see me, shared rules
3 control     others relate to each other, work it
2 connect     others have minds and wants
1 survive     what do I need right now
```

- Level three is common in gangs, teams, the police and the military.
- Level four is where right and wrong, and rule-following, begin.
- Level five brought science and is a few hundred years old.
- Level six became mainstream in 1960s America.
- Level seven is the first to understand all the levels below it.
- Level eight is rare, and few people stay there.

## Two examples

A woman sees a post on her phone saying men are bad, then looks at the man in her life and feels he is bad too. At level three she handles the situation to get what she wants. At level four she steps back, sees herself as another person would, and asks whether acting on that feeling is right. At level eight a person looking at a doughnut notices hunger, guilt from a time they ate a whole box, and the memory of a stomach ache, all pulling at once, and then chooses which one to listen to.

- The same decision can be made at any level.
- What changes is the reasoning behind it.
- Seeing a reaction's source lets you choose not to act on it.

## Where it fails

Higher levels are more complex and not always more effective. At level six a person sees so many ways to live that picking one becomes hard and stressful. The model is a simplified reading of several researchers' stage models, and its top levels are the least certain part.

- Higher does not mean nicer or kinder.
- A high level of thinking does not bring a high level of morals.
- Some problems cannot be solved at the level they came from.

## How to climb

The practice is to sit with a thought and ask why it is there, then ask why again about the answer, and to end with how you know. Someone worried about posting rough drawings asks why and finds fear of criticism, then fear of losing opportunities. Asked how they know that, they find no evidence, and the worry goes.

- Why do I feel this?
- Why would that matter?
- What would happen then?
- How do I know?
