# Learning Styles Myth and Multimodal Learning

Running the same material through several channels beats matching it to a declared learning type. The folk model says each person owns one best channel, and people still introduce themselves as visual learners. Preferences stay; the ceiling is set by activities you cannot sleepwalk and by stacking the channels school never trained.

## Where the identity comes from

Matching a declared style to a lesson is the claim that was tested. The design that would prove it almost never appears. The studies built to that design find no matching effect on tests taken immediately, and none on tests taken later. In one follow-up, children who preferred visual material scored higher on both listening and reading. Preferences are real. For some information you would rather see a diagram, hear an explanation, or read the text, and the preference shifts with the material. A preference is comfort. It is not an aptitude, and it does not license matching.

Schooling trains one channel until the preference feels like identity. For more than a decade almost everything arrives as text and is graded as text. Style tests then measure that conditioning and hand it back as a type. The type is not a genetic finding. It is a trained feeling with a quiz name on it.

Being talked at is easy to drift through. Eyes on a page or a diagram make drift harder. That gap is a selection heuristic, not a ranking of channels: a live lecture can hold attention, and silent reading can wander. How engaged the brain is during the activity predicts learning more strongly than which channel carries the material. Inattentive anything encodes nothing. A technique earns its place by being difficult to perform while mentally absent — it forces re-representing, deciding, producing. Getting sleepy over study material when you are not sleep-deprived is the same signal from the other side: the technique has already lost the brain. Zero engagement yields zero retention. That is the attention rule [[Attention is Important]] states for language, applied here to every mode.

Adopting a style label frames the brain as a fixed object and licenses skipping every other channel. A student labelled auditory opts out of writing-heavy work. The label is the trait trap: the same move [[Fixed vs Growth Mindset]] names, applied to learning itself.

## Two rules

Preferences describe comfort and set no ceiling on what the brain can absorb. Two rules do the work matching was supposed to do.

Pick activities by how hard they are to zone out of. Engagement outranks mode. The test is whether you can perform the activity while mentally absent. If you can, it is not doing the encoding.

Stack modes, and weight the channels schooling never trained. After a decade of text, more sentences add little. The starved channel is where cheap gains sit.

## The doodle, and the story that was dropped

The useful direction is toward re-representation, not toward a prettier copy of the page. Reading then writing is the low end. Reading then drawing a picture is already more. An abstract doodle that re-represents the idea sits higher. Teaching the material aloud, hearing it explained back, or applying it as a live skill sits higher still. Drawing beats writing on recall. Teaching and self-explanation are moderate-utility moves. The full ladder is a house ranking, not a measured set of deltas.

After years of text-heavy schooling, notes written as more sentences reuse a channel already at capacity. Doodles, symbols, and diagrams activate one that idles. Turning a read paragraph into an abstract doodle forces three operations: process it critically, re-represent it in a different form, and render the result. The encoding lives in those operations. The drawing itself is almost incidental. An abstract mark that forced the three operations beats an accurate copy that did not. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Non-Linear Note-Making|Non-Linear Note-Making]] owns the craft of routing material through that visual channel.

Problem-heavy subjects supply the application layer through real problem-solving. Discussion supplies the auditory layer in both directions — explaining and hearing the explanation back. Whatever mode carries the material, the processing it must serve is [[Higher-Order Learning]]: relationships, comparison, and use, not isolated recall. Drawing more is not the page.

The mixing advice used to ride on a left-brain / right-brain picture: one hemisphere for critique, the other for abstract and artistic work. That picture did not survive. Personality does not track hemisphere. Nobody has shown a cognitively dominant side that handles information as a whole. Motor and sensory laterality — handedness, visual fields — is real, and it is a different claim. Functions do light up distinct regions, and some of those lights sit on the wrong side of the tidy map. The multimodal benefit cannot be pinned on hemispheres rather than cortices. What the outcome studies actually measured is words plus pictures, and drawing as an encoding act. Dual-channel processing is cognitive — verbal against pictorial — not hemispheric.

The advice to mix modes was tied to those outcome studies. The hemisphere account was only a why-story sitting on top. Pull the story off and the advice remains. A portable habit follows: when a why-story breaks, keep the recommendation if outcome evidence still holds it, treat the explanation as a hypothesis, and throw away any extra claim that existed only because of the broken story.

## What stands after the story

Running the same material through several channels still beats matching a declared type. The story that used to travel with that advice is gone. What remains is the two rules, and the harm a label does when it licenses skipping a channel.

## Sources

- Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 9(3), 105–119. The design required to test matching, and the finding that studies built to it do not support it.
- Rogowsky, B. A., Calhoun, B. M., & Tallal, P. (2015). Matching learning style to instructional method: Effects on comprehension. *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 107(1), 64–78. First study built to that design; no matching effect on immediate or delayed tests.
- Rogowsky, B. A., Calhoun, B. M., & Tallal, P. (2020). Providing instruction based on students' learning style preferences does not improve learning. *Frontiers in Psychology*. Same design in fifth-graders; visual-preference children scored higher on both listening and reading.
- Riener, C., & Willingham, D. (2010). The myth of learning styles. *Change*. Students have preferences; preferences are not styles.
- Newton, P. M., & Salvi, A. (2020). How common is belief in the learning styles neuromyth, and does it matter? A pragmatic systematic review. 37 studies, 18 countries, weighted 89.1% of teachers still endorse matching. The 89% that exists is teacher belief, not a student identity-share.
- Nancekivell, S. E., Shah, P., & Gelman, S. A. (2020). Maybe they're born with it, or maybe it's experience. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*. Many people treat styles as innate.
- Nielsen, J. A., et al. (2013). An evaluation of the left-brain vs. right-brain hypothesis with resting state functional connectivity magnetic resonance imaging. *PLOS ONE*, 8(8), e71275. Lateralization is local; no whole-brain left/right cognitive style.
- Wammes, J. D., Meade, M. E., & Fernandes, M. A. (2016). The drawing effect. *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology*. Drawing at encoding beats writing for free recall.
- Wammes, J. D., Jonker, T. R., & Fernandes, M. A. (2019). Drawing as a tool for memory. *Cognition*. The encoding lives in the multimodal operations, not in the quality of the picture.
- Mayer, R. E. Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. People learn better from words and pictures than from words alone. Dual-channel is cognitive (verbal vs pictorial), not hemispheric.
- VARK official research statistics (vark-learn.com). Single read/write is a few percent; most people are mixed. Used only as the contrast that retired the 80–90% figure.
