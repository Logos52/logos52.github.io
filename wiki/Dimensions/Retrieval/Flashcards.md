# Flashcards

A prompt-and-answer card trains recall only when it tests one leftover fact at a time, and used past leftover facts it becomes the week's work. The leftover pile is names, constants, arbitrary lists — anything with no causal hook. More cards is not more diligence, and cards are not a first encoding system.

## What makes a usable card

A usable card holds a single prompt: a question, a blank to fill, or image occlusion — part of a picture covered, the missing piece asked for. Several facts on one prompt cannot be marked. Five blanks and one miss leave no legal score: the card is neither right nor wrong, so it cannot be scheduled. That scoring bind is why one thing is a rule rather than a slogan.

What belongs on a card is leftover. Relationships should already be encoded. Cards work on whatever they test, including concepts; the restriction is cost and sequencing, not a law of memory. A conceptual deck will feel productive while it eats the week.

## When the deck is the diagnosis

Hundreds or thousands of cards is the signature of a system compensating for shallow encoding, not of diligence. The bloated deck demands constant repeating time and creates overwhelm, crowding out the relational work that would have shrunk it. When [[Layers of Learning]] and [[Higher-Order Learning]] do that work first, most of the material becomes relevant and deeply held without a card, and the leftover pile is what remains.

A summary card states the information; it has no retrieval effect. A flashcard forces the answer before the check. Only the second trains memory — restudy on a card is still restudy.

Reviews run through [[Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]]. This page does not invent intervals. Flashcards sit with [[Method of Loci]] under [[Rote Learning and Memorisation]]: last-resort tools for the outer remainder, after understanding has taken what it can.

They work on whatever they test, so a conceptual deck keeps paying while it steals the week from encoding. The price is repeating time. The quit signal is a deck that is the week's main session, or a pile counted in hundreds. Success is a leftover pile small enough for pockets, not a dedicated block.

A small deck of leftover facts is the sign the rest of the system worked. The cards mop up what has no hook. They are not a second encoding system.
