# Revision

A closed-book miss surfaces a gap — anything struggled with on recall, got wrong, or felt unsure about. Familiarity is the trap: it reads as competence and leaves the holes where they were. The loop is Test, Target, Teach, and it runs across most subjects and levels without waiting for a large method menu.

## Test, then target

The first move applies the knowledge. The answer is produced before the source is checked. Questions come from both sides: ones written for the session, and ones already in circulation.

Writing a question is itself a retrieval event. Building it teaches even when the answer is not yet there, and it trains the shape of a curveball — a question whose form was never practised, built from pieces already known. Premade questions save time, can mirror the assessment's style, and expose gaps the writer would not have thought to probe. Style-matching is useful, not a guarantee: the paper is not bound to last year's form. Over-relying on premade items is one reason a curveball lands as a shock.

Those gaps, once found, do not get equal time. The second move starts at the weakest slice. The areas of least confidence go first, even when that is uncomfortable. Comfort-first revision spends the hour where it is least needed. People prefer the fluent option; the discomfort of the weak slice is the price of the loop.

## Teach, and what counts

The third move is not an explanation of what was just recalled. The material is reordered and reframed for someone who has not learned it. The sharpest version says why a concept matters before any terminology, as if to a ten-year-old.

A gap is anything struggled with on recall, got wrong, or felt unsure about — not only an incorrect answer. Unsure counts. Every gap found gets re-encoded, using the methods that taught it first, connected more deeply or from another angle. [[Higher-Order Learning]] is where that re-encoding should aim.

No gaps found is a warning, not a win. Unless the knowledge is genuinely pristine, retrieval should surface gaps. Finding none usually means the technique was done wrong, or it tested the wrong levels — the ladder in [[Knowledge Mastery - From Recognition to Usable Knowledge]]. Too easy and gap-free is the same signal: the practice missed some orders of learning or types of knowledge. The repair is to widen the kinds of retrieval, not to do more of the same. The menu for that widening lives in the [[Interleaving Table]].

The same loop overlaps the advanced group-study method; the protocol lives on [[Group Study]].

A session that found nothing did not run. This is the first loop; the standing schedule is [[Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]], and the fuller method menu is the table this page exists so a reader does not have to wait for. Gaps found here feed higher-order work and the mastery ladder.
