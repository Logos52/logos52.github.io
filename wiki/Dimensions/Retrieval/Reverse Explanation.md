# Reverse Explanation

Reverse explanation is building the concept in ordinary words until a listener can say I understand this, I just don't know what it's called. The name arrives last, into that slot. The usual handover, term first then a definition, arrives isolated, a new fact with nothing already held to join.

## Building the slot

The explanation that does this walks out from what the listener already holds, until a gap with a shape shows up: something is plainly here, its job is visible, and the only absentee is the name. The label then falls into a place already cut for it.

The talk moves in that order. It opens on the big picture, then why the thing matters, then the detail in lay terms, then a concrete case. The label arrives only after that. The same idea can then be said again in specialised terms, and the talk returns to the whole. Specialised language is not banned. It arrives later, once there is a place for it.

Cells stay alive on one kind of molecule in the air. Converting that molecule into usable energy is cellular respiration. The stuff itself is a pair of atoms of one element, joined, and the name of that element is oxygen. When the word finally shows up, the job, the stakes, and a rough picture of the thing are already in place.

Those stakes need a case in the same breath. Parking the why for later — we'll see why this matters later — snaps the order. A concrete use belongs before the name.

Building one concept in lay terms can take several minutes, and it feels slow. Once that base exists, later terms can land back-to-back, because each one names something already understood. The slow stretch is the setup. The later speed is the payoff.

## Who the move tests

That slow stretch is what the speaker pays for. Take the definition away as a fallback and the idea has to stand on its ties to other ideas alone, which makes every thin join hearable. That is why the move is retrieval for the speaker, not only a teaching trick. Preparing to teach, and actually teaching, beat restudy. The extra cost here is that the word itself is gone as a hiding place.

The evidenced half is that speaker-side test. The listener-slot is the hoped-for side-effect. A session that felt slow may have been the one that actually ran.

If the importance cannot be said before the name, the relationship was not actually held. A definition waiting as backup means the ties never had to be proved, and the hardness it deletes is the hardness that was doing the learning.

A lay-term build that was not needed spends a minute. A term with no attachment spends the explanation. When the level is unsure, the cheaper error is to go lower.

## Hearing it go wrong

Term-then-definition is the tell that becomes audible first. Reading out three names and then defining them one by one feels smooth and economical, and that feeling is the alarm. That habit is terminology crutching: the name stands in for the relationship, so the idea never has to be carried without the word.

Inside [[WPW]] — the reteach this move lives inside — a teaching pass with no reverse explanations means the whole technique is failing, whatever else looks right. Crutching is the most common error of that pass.

A recording of the first attempts makes the order audible. Term-before-relevance is hard to hear live. Listening back builds the radar for it. A swap with a study partner does the same job.

Easy means wrong. A first pass that feels straightforward is a strong sign the move is not happening. Stopping to fix a sentence halfway through is ordinary, and it does no damage.

A pause on "why is this important?" is data. That hesitation separates understanding from memorisation. Persistent trouble explaining importance fluently suggests the underlying chunks were grouped by information instead of shared importance, and those chunks are worth rebuilding before the next pass. A failed reverse explanation is a gap; [[Inquiry-Based Learning]] is the loop that gap opens.

Total avoidance is unrealistic. Crutching cannot be eliminated every time. A trained radar that catches it most of the time is enough. A perfect pass is not the job.

The listener for the pass is an imaginary student: a listener invented in advance, whose prior knowledge gets decided before the explanation starts. Early in a subject, or with any discomfort, that student knows nothing, and everything comes down to lay terms. Genuine confidence at an advanced level earns the right to assume the basics and skip the ground-floor build.

A word can show up ahead of its full idea only if two gates both hold. It is conceptually light — atom, molecule. And it is set inside a story already underway that supplies the setting. Either gate alone is not enough.

The same ordering leaves the reteach. A presentation opens with the situation and the stakes in plain terms, and names the framework or product once the audience already wants a name for it. A page that builds the reason to care before introducing its own subject performs a reverse explanation in prose. First sentences that lead with the mechanism are the written form of label-last. [[Theme-First Text Analysis]] runs the same ordering at essay scale.

A pass, a presentation, or a page that hands over names and then defines them, and feels easy doing it, did not run the move.

A term that arrives into a slot stays attached, in a lesson and in a room that is not a lesson. Connected terms are the ones that survive pruning; [[Survive and Thrive]] is why. The name still comes last. The slot is still the test.
