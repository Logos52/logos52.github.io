# Interleaving Table

The menu is large, and you do not need to read all of it. Pick whether the material you are working on is mostly declarative or mostly procedural, then choose a few methods to try. Declarative knowledge is facts, concepts, and logic, the kind of content you encode as material. Procedural knowledge is the skill of doing the work. Many subjects mix both.

## Techniques that are not worth the time

Some methods offer almost no payoff, and they have no place in a learning system. The same time is better spent on any of the methods further down.

## Using chat tools with these methods

AI can save time on low-effort chores, such as collecting keywords or pulling several sources into one place so you are not splitting attention across many tabs. It can write test questions for you, which saves the time of writing them by hand and can give you an outside check on what you know. It can also answer exploratory questions the way a tutor might.

Do not use it as a judge of truth on a nuanced or complex idea. These tools do not understand truth the way a careful reader hopes they do. Do not use them to skip the thinking that forms a network of knowledge, such as building a model, judging relationships, or working out why something matters. Getting a ready-made "why it matters" can drop you into a passive understanding mode that is much weaker. Until you can tell that passive mode from active work under load, avoid using AI to chunk and organise information. If you do use it that way, challenge what it gives you and look for alternatives.

## Declarative methods

Subjects that lean declarative include the sciences, history, politics, and law. They also include economic or accounting theory, the theory side of mathematics, business and finance, coding logic, and the vocabulary, syntax, grammar, and structure of a language.

The Feynman method, a mind-map brain dump, and the advanced group method for practice questions can sit across more than one order.

Cover-copy-check means covering part of your notes, reproducing it from memory, then checking. It helps short-term retention of basic information, especially labels on diagrams. Image-occlusion flashcards are a more practical version of the same idea.

Teaching an idea you have only just worked through, right after that first encounter, is a useful form of micro-retrieval. Isolated and simple relational teaching work especially well in that slot.

## Procedural methods

Procedural work shows up in skills such as coding, mathematics problem solving, clinical procedures, and using a language in real conversation. It is practised by doing the skill under retrieval, not by restating facts about it.

Edge-case challenges can stand in for evaluative generated questions when you use the advanced group method of practice tests on procedural work. You can also change a variable in a problem you already have, or add a new one, to create a fresh variation.

Flashcards and the Ben system can still support small declarative pieces inside a procedural subject.

## How the menus combine

A mostly procedural subject can use a simple path. Start with lower-order methods and move toward higher-order ones. The skills often build on themselves, which supplies some repetition naturally, and lower-order competence is tested inside the higher-order methods.

A mostly declarative subject needs more structure.

Adapt any part that is not working.

When the mix is even, start from the declarative combination, because declarative knowledge usually takes more time, then swap in procedural methods as that side of the subject grows. After the declarative foundation is solid, extra interleaved sessions can drop away and the work can become mostly procedural, moving from lower to higher order.

## Open questions

- How does dropping one passive method, such as rereading, change which gaps the next interleaved session finds?
- How does moving from simple retrieved execution to an integrative version change the kind of mistakes that show up?
