# Locus of Control

The golden rule of learning is to put attention on the locus of control. In this lesson, that phrase means the actions you can actually direct that put you in a better position to get the result you want. The result itself sits outside that circle. An exam score, a grade, a place in a program, or a dreamed-of outcome cannot be steered directly, no matter how badly it is wanted or how much worry is spent on it.

Accepting that is uncomfortable, and it is also what frees attention for the work that changes the odds. A result follows from the process that produced it, and when those processes are sound, success becomes more likely without ever becoming certain. The leftover "what if" is part of the work, and learning to sit with it is part of the rule.

Fixating on what sits outside that circle has the opposite effect. Grades, competition, and other people's approval scatter attention, waste time, and make the goal feel farther away. Effort still matters. Studying a great deal with a weak method loses to studying a great deal with a strong one. Hard work is a baseline. What separates people who learn well from people who struggle is the kind of work they do. Focus, delay, and distraction also change the odds, and not every one of those factors can be steered.

The same rule applies to old habits. Ask a hundred people how they learn and you will hear a hundred answers, which does not mean the answers work equally well. Years of habit sit underneath those answers, some helpful and some harmful. Knowing what to stop can help more than adding a new technique. Dropping a harmful habit also sits more firmly inside the circle you can direct than training a whole new skill from scratch.

## Open questions

- How does fixing attention on a grade change the work someone actually does?
- How does treating hard work as a necessity, rather than as the thing that decides the result, change which processes get chosen?
