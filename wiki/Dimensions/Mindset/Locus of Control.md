# Locus of Control: The Golden Rule of Learning

A locus of control is the set of things a person can actually do that make a result more likely. The rule of learning built on it says to spend attention on those things and to leave the result alone. A student who follows the rule stops worrying about the grade and starts working on the study methods, the focus and the schedule that produce grades.

- A grade, an admission or a job offer cannot be controlled directly.
- Wanting a result more does not make it more likely.
- Results follow from the process that produced them.
- Good process raises the odds and never guarantees the result.
- Better methods raise grades more than extra hours do.
- Stopping one harmful habit is often easier than learning something new.

## How it works

Many things affect how an exam goes: how much you study, which techniques you use, how well you focus, how much you procrastinate and how easily you get distracted. Some of these you can change and some you cannot. Attention spent on the ones you cannot change, such as the grade, the competition or other people's opinion, produces worry and scattered effort. The same attention spent on your own methods produces more progress for the same hours.

```
outside your control     inside your control
  the grade                which techniques you use
  the competition          your focus and schedule
  others' opinion          habits you stop
         |                         |
      worry                  a better chance
```

- Name the result you want, then accept that you cannot control it.
- List the processes that make that result more likely.
- Put your effort into improving those processes.
- Accept the uncertainty that is left, since it cannot be removed.

## Why method beats hours

Hard work is required, and it still does not separate strong learners from weak ones, because both groups work hard. Someone who studies many hours with poor techniques will usually do worse than someone who studies the same hours with good ones. Research on university students found that total study time predicted grades only weakly once study quality and earlier achievement were taken into account.

- Strong and weak learners both work hard.
- Technique and study quality decide most of the difference between them.
- Removing a harmful habit is fully within your control.

## Terms

The phrase comes from psychology, where Julian Rotter used it in 1966 for a general belief about whether events are caused by your own actions or by outside forces. The use here is narrower and practical. It is a way of sorting one goal into the parts you can act on and the parts you cannot.

## Sources

- Rotter (1966), *Psychological Monographs* 80(1): the term's origin. The construct measured there (a generalised expectancy, internal versus external) is adjacent to, and distinct from, the use on this page.
- Plant, Ericsson, Hill & Asberg (2005), *Contemporary Educational Psychology*: study quantity is a weak predictor of GPA once study quality and prior achievement are controlled.
- Dunlosky et al. (2013): strategy quality over volume.
- The rule itself, the sort, the fixation test, and the do-no-harm corollary are the learning system this wiki organizes, stated as its own doctrine.
