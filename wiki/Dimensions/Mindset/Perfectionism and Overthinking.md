# Perfectionism and Overthinking

Perfectionism is usually treated here as a symptom of anxiety and insecurity. The repair for overthinking is not to think less. It is to think in a more productive and targeted way, using steps that force a wider view of the perfectionist thoughts.

Anxiety is a real condition, and it can be serious. If it affects daily life in a significant way, the recommendation is to see a medical professional such as a family doctor. Medication is described as one way to help start a change when an emotion has become ingrained, because the brain can adapt toward feeling that emotion more readily. Work with a psychologist is described as coaching for how you think, in the same spirit as coaching for how you learn. Trying to train those thinking patterns alone can take years or decades. Help from people who are not trained can send you the wrong way or fail to give the support you need. Mental-health diagnoses are described as confidential. They do not affect almost any university program or career path, aside from work as a commercial pilot and some military roles, and even those paths have leniency.

A four-step framework is offered for reducing overthinking and anxiety-driven perfectionism. First, name the emotions you are feeling, and be specific. Second, make a problem list of the root causes of each feeling, such that you would not feel this way if those problems were gone. Be both specific and complete. Third, break the problems into actionable tasks that would help, either by removing a problem or by changing how you see it or respond to it. Some problems will sit outside what you can control. Marking which are inside that circle and which are outside helps direct energy. Fourth, pick one task from the list you can control, something within your control at this moment, then take that step.

The more specific the list, and the smaller the pieces, the more likely there is something you can actually do. The more you can act, the less overwhelming the pile tends to feel. Even the work of breaking the problems down, before any later action, can reduce how large they feel.

The harmful side of a perfectionist identity is left aside here. That is the pattern where a person pushes toward high achievement under unhealthy pressure and ties perfect results to their worth. If that description fits, the recommendation is to talk with a trained therapist who can help separate worth from achievement.

## Open questions

- How does turning a feeling into a list of root causes change what overthinking is able to hold onto?
- How does acting on one small controllable task change how large the remaining pile feels?
