# Perfectionism and Overthinking

Perfectionism in study usually means a fear of making mistakes, and overthinking is the loop of worried thought that goes with it. Both slow learning, because the learner asks "what if" questions instead of trying the technique and finding out. A short written routine turns that worry into a list of problems and then into one action you can take now.

- Most perfectionism is anxiety about failing.
- Someone aiming for the best result would make mistakes fast.
- Overthinking goes in circles, and the fix is thinking aimed at problems.
- Name the feeling, list its causes, turn the causes into tasks.
- Do one task you control right away.
- Waiting for certainty before acting leaves the result to luck.

## How it shows up

A learner with this kind of perfectionism grows slowly and experiments little. Faced with a new method, they ask more questions about what might go wrong and gather more information before trying it once. Perfectionism mixes high personal standards with worry about mistakes and other people's judgement. The worry part is the one linked to fear of failure and procrastination.

- Many "what if this happens?" questions before a first attempt.
- Little testing of ideas in practice.
- Avoiding even mistakes that cost nothing.
- Staying with what feels safe and familiar.

Thinking hard about an important plan is useful, and overthinking is the version that circles one feeling and makes no progress. Brooding in general terms tends to keep a low mood going, while thinking about concrete problems tends to reduce it.

## How to break the loop

The routine moves from feeling to problem to action. A feeling on its own gives nothing to act on, and the first guess at its cause is often wrong. Writing each step down makes the problems smaller and more specific, and the act of breaking them down already reduces how overwhelming they feel. The routine usually takes less time than the overthinking it replaces.

```
feeling  ->  problem list  ->  tasks  ->  one action now
"anxious"    causes that       steps      the one you
             would end it      to take    control
```

- Name the specific emotion behind the word "perfectionism".
- List the causes, meaning whatever would end the feeling if removed.
- For each cause: solve it, change your reaction, or remove it.
- Mark which causes are inside your control and which are not.
- Pick one task you control and do it now.

## Changing the goal

A person who truly wanted the best possible work would try things early, get feedback and make many small mistakes quickly, since that is the fastest route to a good result. One trick changes how the perfectionist sees themselves: keep the value placed on high-quality work, and make getting there as fast as possible the first priority. Mistakes then become part of the route instead of something to prevent. The trick has not been tested in research.

- Keep the high standard.
- Make speed to a good result the first priority.
- Expect to be wrong on the first attempts at anything hard.

## Where to get help

Anxiety can be a serious condition. If it affects daily life, see a doctor, since medication and a psychologist both help and working it out alone can take years. When self-worth is tied to achievement, a trained therapist is the right person to work on it.
