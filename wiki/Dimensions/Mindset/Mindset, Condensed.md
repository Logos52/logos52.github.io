# Mindset, Condensed

Mindset is how you respond to difficulty and mistakes, and it sets how fast every other learning skill can improve. A learner who fears mistakes avoids the practice that builds skill, so most of the rules below make mistakes cheaper and more useful.

- Skill grows by trying, making mistakes, reflecting and trying again.
- Read a mistake as information about your method.
- Put effort into the study processes you control, since grades follow.
- A familiar method feels safer than it is, so check its cost.
- Improve 1% at a time on two or three skills at most.
- Work next on whatever limits everything else.
- Turn a vague fear into a list of problems, then one action.

## How it works

Every skill improves through a loop of attempts and corrections. Fixed thinking, where ability feels set and results feel like a verdict on the person, makes people avoid the attempts that produce corrections. Loss aversion adds to this by making the weeks needed to learn a better method feel like a loss. Worry and perfectionism slow the loop further by adding more "what if" questions before each attempt.

```
attempt -> mistake -> reflect -> adjust
   ^                              |
   +------------------------------+
fear, loss aversion and worry slow each turn
```

- Make mistakes small and cheap by breaking tasks into steps.
- Schedule practice sessions whose only goal is finding mistakes.
- Track small gains in writing, since they are too slow to feel.
- Once a week, compare what you expected with what happened.
- Redirect attention away from a worry instead of fighting it.
- Expect a change in mindset to take months.
