# Motivation

The feeling of motivation is for the weeks before a habit exists — practice and reflection, unlearning the old ones, clearing the barriers. Improving the feeling and refusing to depend on it look like opposite advice until you see the handoff: the feeling carries the early stretch, then habit takes over.

## Where it sits in the build

Self-development runs a five-step map, not a protocol: name the outcome, name the skills and processes it needs, unlearn the old habits and clear the barriers, learn the new skills, then continue on those new habits. The unlearning step is its own page — [[How to Unlearn Old or Bad Habits Efficiently|How to Unlearn Old or Bad Habits Efficiently]] owns the removal of what is already in the way.

Habit formation is not immediate. You need habits of practice and reflection before other habits will form, time to unlearn the old ones, and effort to clear the barriers sitting in front of the new ones. That early stretch, before any progress is visible, is where the feeling dies. [[Marginal Gains|Marginal Gains]] is the shield for that stretch: small visible upgrades keep the feeling available until the habit exists.

Spend the feeling on breakthroughs — to push through a challenge, and to get a new habit standing. Using it to perform work that should already run on habit is the failure. The longer treatment of that dependency lives on [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/How to shift your brain to be motivated (when you don't feel like it)|How to shift your brain to be motivated (when you don't feel like it)]]; this page owns only the spend-then-handoff. Use the feeling to build the key habits, then let habit take over. The system that takes over is [[Habits, Productive Routines & PEER|Habits, Productive Routines & PEER]].

Visible small progress protects the feeling through the build, which can take weeks or months. The early stretch has no visible progress; that is the price. If the thing still needs a pep talk every day after the habit should have formed, the handoff failed — check the habit page, not this one. A reader can name one habit the feeling is being spent to build, and one behaviour that should already run without it.

Once the habit exists, needing the feeling daily is the failure the weeks were meant to end. The feeling was for the build.

## Sources

- Wood & Neal 2007, *Psychological Review* — habits are context-cued responses that run without the original motivation
- Wood, Quinn & Kashy 2002, *Journal of Personality* — habitual behaviour depends less on current intentions
- Galla & Duckworth 2015, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* — self-control predicts outcomes via beneficial habits, not daily resisting
- Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle 2010, *European Journal of Social Psychology* — automaticity for simple daily behaviours plateaus on a weeks-to-months grain (median 66 days, range 18–254); not a law
- Locke & Latham 2002, *American Psychologist* — feedback on progress sustains effort
- Amabile & Kramer 2011 — workplace diary studies; the popular cousin of visible progress
