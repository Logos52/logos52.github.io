# Compounding vs Additive Gains

A compounding gain is an improvement that changes the learner, so every later gain builds on a higher starting point. An additive gain raises results only while it lasts and leaves the learner the same underneath. The difference decides where limited study time should go, because two gains can look alike this week and end up far apart after a year.

## Takeaways

- A gain compounds only if it changes the learner's own ability.
- An additive gain helps only while it is in place.
- Test a gain by imagining it removed.
- Tutoring and extra study hours are usually additive.
- Better study skills are usually compounding.
- Work on the skills that produce a grade.
- Compounding starts slow and overtakes additive gains later.

## How it works

Compound interest shows the pattern. At 1% a month, 100 becomes 101, then 102.01, then 103.03, because each month's interest is paid on a balance that already includes the last month's interest. If each month's interest were set aside in a separate account, the balance would stay at 100 and each month would add the same 1. In learning the learner is the balance: a gain that raises their real ability makes the next gain larger, and a gain held outside them does not.

```
result
  |                           compounding
  |                        /
  |                     /
  |                 /     _______ additive
  |          __/-----
  |  ____/-----
  +------------------------------ time
```

- 1% a day added up over a year gives 365% more.
- Compounded daily at 1%, a year gives about 37 times the start.
- Both cost the same time and effort each day.

## The removal test

To sort a gain, imagine taking it away and ask whether the learner would be left any better than before. When the answer is no, the gain was additive. Tutoring often fails this test: it supplies teaching, guidance and materials, so results rise, and they fall back when it stops. As the material gets harder the learner needs more and more tutoring, until the limit becomes how much they can take in by themselves.

- More study hours help up to a point, then stress lowers results.
- Stop the extra hours and results return to where they were.
- Improvements from teaching programmes often fade after they end.
- A better way of studying stays with the learner after the course.

## How to apply it

A grade reports how well the skills underneath it work. A higher grade comes from improving a skill that produces it, and a skill gain carries into later work. Some study skills, such as recalling details, apply across subjects, though research finds that transfer to very different tasks is uneven. A technique produces a gain only once it is used in daily study.

- List the skills behind the result.
  - Exam technique, recall of detail, understanding of concepts.
  - Solving unfamiliar problems, working at speed.
  - Managing time, anxiety and procrastination.
- Pick one skill and get slightly better at it each week.
- Spend an hour on how to study before adding a study hour.
- Use additive help for short-term needs, and know it will not last.
