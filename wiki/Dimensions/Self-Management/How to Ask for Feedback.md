# How to Ask for Feedback

## Sources

- Hattie, J. & Timperley, H. 2007. "The Power of Feedback." *Review of Educational Research* 77(1), 81–112. The three questions a reply must answer (where going / how going / where next), and self-level feedback as the weakest of four levels.
- Kluger, A. N. & DeNisi, A. 1996. "The effects of feedback interventions on performance." *Psychological Bulletin* 119(2), 254–284. More than a third of feedback interventions reduced performance.
- Wisniewski, B., Zierer, K. & Hattie, J. 2020. "The Power of Feedback Revisited." *Frontiers in Psychology* 10:3087. Revised effect is medium and highly variable; a minority of effects remain negative.
- Butler, D. L. & Winne, P. H. 1995. "Feedback and self-regulated learning." *Review of Educational Research* 65(3). Internal feedback is the regulator external feedback has to enter.
- Boud, D. 2000. "Sustainable assessment." *Studies in Continuing Education* 22(2). Learning to judge your own work is the long-term purpose.
- Boud, D. & Molloy, E. 2013. "Rethinking models of feedback for learning." *Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education* 38(6). The loop is not complete until a next attempt uses the reply.
- Sadler, D. R. 1989. "Formative assessment and the design of instructional systems." *Instructional Science* 18. Formative assessment as building evaluative knowledge.
- Seijts, G. H. & Latham, G. P. 2005. "Learning versus performance goals." *Academy of Management Executive* 19(1). Learning goals outperform outcome goals on novel and complex work.
- Topping, K. 1998. "Peer assessment between students in colleges and universities." *Review of Educational Research* 68(3). Peer assessment as a learning activity for the giver.
