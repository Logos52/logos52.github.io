# Kolbs Experiential Cycle

Hard goals produce mistakes. What separates people who keep improving is whether they can look back at what happened, name what went wrong, and change the method before the next attempt. Kolb's experiential cycle is the look-back tool for that job. [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Marginal gains]] pick the direction and the next small problem worth fixing. The cycle is how you actually make the fix.

The two tools belong together. Marginal gains keep motivation up when a cycle only deepens understanding and does not yet repair anything. They also choose which problem gets a cycle today, because a beginner may have dozens of possible directions and cannot run reflection on all of them.

## Rumination versus a cycle that moves

Not every backward glance helps. Turning a problem and the feelings around it over and over, without making sense of them or choosing a next step, is rumination. That pattern shows up in anxiety and depression as well as in study. The version taught here is meant to end each pass with something you can try on the next attempt, and to pull as much as possible from each experience. Each pass should teach more about what the problem is, what sits underneath it, and which repairs have a good chance of working. It often takes several cycles to see the real shape of the problem, and more cycles after that to put a repair in place.

A set of questions is taught with the cycle to reduce common biases in that kind of looking-back. Those questions are not listed on this page.

## Pairing the cycle with marginal gains

When a process is weak, repeated cycles can find what is going wrong and eventually repair it. In practice there are many possible problems at once. It is not workable to run the cycle on every one every day. Marginal gains narrow the field. They also make the small wins from early cycles feel like progress even when the first few passes mostly clarify the problem rather than fix it.

## Open questions

- How does turning a problem over without choosing a next step change what that look backward does?
- How does choosing which problem to run the cycle on change whether reflection time is worth spending?
