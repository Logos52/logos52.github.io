# Kolbs Experiential Cycle

Kolb's experiential cycle is a way of looking back at what happened so you can find a way to improve. Used well, it lets you learn from experience quickly. Marginal gains, described on their own page, tell you the direction of improvement. This cycle tells you how to make the improvement. One picture in the notes is that marginal gains are the road, and the cycle is the vehicle that travels it. Another is that marginal gains describe what the journey of progress looked like, and the cycle describes how the progress was made.

Mistakes are part of any hard goal. Being able to learn from them and change the method is what separates people who keep getting better. Not every look backward helps. Turning a problem and the feelings around it over and over, without making sense of them or changing anything, is rumination. That pattern also shows up in anxiety and depression. The version of the cycle taught here is meant to turn each look into a next step you can try, and to get as much as possible from each experience. Each pass should teach more about what the problem actually is, what sits underneath it, and which repairs have a good chance of working. It often takes several cycles to see the real shape of the problem, and more cycles after that to put a repair in place.

A set of questions is taught with the cycle to reduce common biases and mistakes in that kind of looking-back. Those questions are not listed here.

The two tools are meant to be used together. When a process is weak, repeated cycles can find what is going wrong and eventually repair it. In practice there are many possible problems at once, and for a beginner there can be dozens of possible directions, not all of them equally tied to the larger goal. It is not workable to run the cycle on every problem every day. Marginal gains help in two ways. They keep motivation up, because the progress that comes from the cycle is often itself a small step, and the first few cycles more often deepen understanding of the problem than fix it. They also pick which problem is worth the cycle, so reflection time goes where it helps most.

## Open questions

- How does turning a problem over without choosing a next step change what that look backward does?
- How does choosing which problem to run the cycle on change whether reflection time is worth spending?
