# Skills Audit

A skills audit is a sit-down review of how you actually study or work, step by step, checked against a written description of how each technique should be done. It finds errors that came from rushing, skipping steps or time away. Each technique gets a rating, and whichever one or two score lowest become the next things to practise.

## Takeaways

- Map your real process before judging any part of it.
- Rate each technique from 1 to 5 against a written standard.
- Work on anything below 4, one or two at a time.
- Run one every two to three months.
- Beginners rate themselves too high, so test before rating.

## When to run one

An audit helps most when something has gone wrong without an obvious cause. Learning techniques too fast, or skipping ones that seemed irrelevant, leaves mistakes that are hard to trace later. Skills also fade when they go unused. Before starting material that assumes earlier skills are solid, an audit checks that they are.

- After rushing through or skipping techniques.
- After a long break.
- Before moving to harder material.
- When progress has stalled for no clear reason.

## How to do it

Start by writing out every step of your current process, from first contact with new material to review. Then compare each step with the written guidelines for that technique, and give it a score from 1, poor, to 5, excellent. Some techniques cannot be rated from memory, so use them once and watch how you do it. A low score says where to work next, and says nothing about past effort.

1. Map the process you actually follow, step by step.
2. Compare each step with its guidelines and rate it 1 to 5.
3. Fix the lowest-rated steps first, with feedback from yourself or others.
4. Practise the fix, reflect, and adjust over several rounds.

- An advanced learner's full audit can take hours.
- One fix often needs several rounds of experiments.

## Quick screen or full audit

An audit comes in two sizes. A quick screen covers only the main techniques and looks for key parts done wrong, and it is the routine check. A full audit covers every technique and looks for gaps between knowing a technique and doing it. Run the full one when the screen found nothing or when a problem is old.

| | Quick screen | Full audit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Covers | main techniques only | every technique |
| Looks for | key parts done wrong | gaps between knowing and doing |
| Works on | scores below 4 | below 4, or below 5 for basics |
| Use when | routine check | screen found nothing, or old problem |

Improving a skill fastest means isolating its specific weak parts, which is what the rating step does.
