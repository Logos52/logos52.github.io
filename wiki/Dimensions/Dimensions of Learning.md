# Dimensions of Learning

Five capabilities decide how well a person learns: mindset, self-management, self-regulation, deep processing, and retrieval. Each one can be trained on its own. Whichever one is weakest right now limits how much the other four can produce.

The five are what a learner believes about difficulty, how the day is set up, whether the learner notices and adjusts while working, how deeply material is taken in, and whether it can still be produced later.

The five are a way of finding where learning breaks down. They are not a map of how the brain is organised.

## Core takeaways

- The five capabilities are mindset, self-management, self-regulation, deep processing, and retrieval. Each one can be trained separately.
- The weakest of the five limits the other four. There is no fixed ranking of importance among them.
- Self-management and self-regulation are the two that get confused. Self-management builds systems ahead of time. Self-regulation monitors and adjusts during a session.
- Most techniques serve one or two of the five. A technique aimed at a dimension that is not the weak one produces visible activity and flat results.
- A short list of findings holds across people, and retrieval, spacing, and depth of processing are on it. Most of what is taught on top of that list was worked out by one person for one brain.
- The model is working when the bottleneck can be named in one sentence and the next session is different because of it. If two weeks of diagnosis has not changed a session, stop diagnosing and study.

## The five dimensions

Each dimension has a question that shows whether it is the one setting the current limit.

| Dimension | The question | The job |
|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset\|Mindset]] | Do my beliefs support growth through difficulty? | Decides how an experience is read: what gets noticed in it, what gets concluded from it, and what happens next. |
| [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management\|Self-Management]] | Have I built the systems to make consistent action possible? | Habits, routines, and environments that make consistent action possible without spending the day's willpower just getting through it. |
| [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation\|Self-Regulation]] | Can I monitor what is happening and adjust my approach in real time? | Monitoring, guiding, and refining learning while it is happening. |
| [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing\|Deep Processing]] | Am I transforming information into meaningful, usable structure? | Extracting meaningful learning from information. It runs on a range. At the low end it is repeating isolated items. At the high end it is comparing and judging. |
| [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval\|Retrieval]] | Can I reconstruct and use the knowledge when needed? | Using and recalling knowledge from memory: a fact, an explanation, a problem, a discussion. Recall is what keeps knowledge from fading. |

## What each dimension does, and what happens when it is weak

[[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Mindset]] decides how difficulty, effort, and the self get read. Under a growth reading, difficulty is information and a reason to keep working. Under a fixed reading, the same difficulty is taken as evidence of a limit. When mindset is weak, the other four break down more easily. Hard work gets avoided. Techniques get dropped once they become uncomfortable. Setbacks get taken as personal failure.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]] builds the outside conditions: habits, time allocation, environment, and task systems. It builds them so that high-quality work does not use up willpower. When self-management is weak, self-regulation, deep processing, and retrieval become unreliable even when they are strong, because there is no protected space and no consistent rhythm in which to practise them.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]] is the ability to monitor cognitive, emotional, and energetic state while learning, and to adjust in real time. Mid-session that looks like noticing the effort has gone into memorising details rather than understanding the concept, then changing approach there and then. When self-regulation is weak, methods that are not working continue, shallow processing goes unnoticed, and good systems get abandoned the moment conditions dip. The drop shows up where there is no scaffolding: thin guidance, unfamiliar topics, demanding conditions. It does not show up everywhere.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]] is the quality and richness of the mental structures built while learning. When deep processing is weak, knowledge stays shallow and poorly connected, and retrieval becomes unreliable, because there is little high-quality material to reconstruct. It also runs the other way. Attempting a harder form of retrieval, such as complex self-testing rather than recognition, forces processing deeper than habit would go.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval|Retrieval]] is whether knowledge remains usable after time passes and when conditions are imperfect: under pressure, in new contexts, with incomplete cues. When retrieval is weak, even excellent deep processing produces knowledge that feels familiar during study and cannot be reconstructed when it matters.

## Key Distinctions

Self-management and self-regulation are close enough that keeping them apart takes work. Self-management builds external systems over time. Self-regulation runs the internal loop in real time. Strong management with weak regulation looks like good routines that do not adjust when things go wrong inside them. Strong regulation with weak management looks like quick in-the-moment adaptation with no structure that makes anyone show up enough for it to compound.

The field often uses "self-regulation" for the whole cycle, including the day's setup. Environment structuring sits inside that broader cycle, so the confusion comes from the field's own usage rather than from carelessness. The distinction is still worth keeping for diagnosis, because the two failure patterns need different repairs.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition]] is the awareness that self-regulation runs on. It is the moment-to-moment readout that monitoring and adjustment act upon. It stays inside self-regulation rather than becoming a sixth dimension, because awareness only produces results when it drives adjustment. Improving the adjustment without that readout succeeds occasionally and never repeatably.

## How the five work together

The five feed each other in a teaching sequence. The sequence describes how they are taught, not a pipeline through the brain. [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Mindset]] decides whether difficulty is engaged or avoided. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]] sets the conditions. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]] adjusts the approach mid-session. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]] encodes. [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval|Retrieval]] keeps the result usable.

Deep processing sets how much capability there is. Self-regulation sets how well that capability is used while learning. Retrieval is what lets a learner still perform on unfamiliar material and in poor conditions. Consistent performance when conditions vary needs both deep processing and self-regulation. Mindset and self-management are not described in those terms.

**The limit is set by whichever dimension is weakest for this learner right now, not by a fixed ranking of the five.**

No one of the five is the foundation. Weakness in any one creates a bottleneck for the rest.

## What holds for everyone, and what is one person's solution

Not everything in the learning pages rests on statistics. The findings that hold across people are a short list, and retrieval, spacing, and depth of processing are on it. Almost everything taught on top of that list is an n=1 solution. A memory trick, a note layout, a study schedule, or a mnemonic system was worked out by one person for one brain, and is then taught as if it fit every brain. Taken as a science, that layer is close to a pseudoscience, because a solution built for one brain does not fit the different kinds of brains it gets sold to.

Most of the tricks were legitimately derived from a real finding. They still work as a distraction, because the learner ends up working at the trick instead of at the thinking the finding asks for. So every technique page here is read in two layers. The finding underneath is the part that transfers. The trick on top is one person's own version of it. It is kept while it produces the thinking, and dropped when it stops.

## Which dimension each technique serves

Most techniques primarily serve one or two dimensions. Knowing which one lets a technique land on the dimension that is actually limiting, instead of adding activity to a dimension that is already holding.

- [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Bear Hunter System|Bear Hunter System]] is the main encoding workflow, filed under Deep Processing.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]] is the main retrieval system: spacing, interleaving, and method by type. It has a strong second benefit for encoding, because harder retrieval forces processing deeper.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/WPW|WPW]] is teach-back from memory: whole, parts, whole again.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management/Kolbs Experiential Cycle|Kolbs Experiential Cycle]] turns an attempt into what happened, how it felt, what rule that suggests, and what changes next. It is filed here under Self-Management.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management/Skills Audit|Skills Audit]] is capability diagnosis, also filed under Self-Management.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset/Marginal Gains|Marginal Gains]] is small consistent upgrades as mindset practice.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset/Fixed vs Growth Mindset|Fixed vs Growth Mindset]] is the two readings of difficulty, effort, and self, and the work at the level of the trigger.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/How to Maintain Sustainable Energy Under Pressure|Recovery]] is detachment and rest, filed under Self-Regulation.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition]] is the awareness mechanism already named in the [[wiki/Dimensions/Dimensions of Learning#Key Distinctions|distinction above]].
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/The Technique Is Only as Good as the Thinking It Produces|The Technique Is Only as Good as the Thinking It Produces]] cuts across all five. A technique produces only the thinking it produces, and a quiet retreat into a familiar version of the method can leave the notes looking right while the work did not happen.

The stance behind the model treats learning as a designed process that can be seen and adjusted, rather than as more hours. That stance is set out on [[wiki/Syntheses/ICS System|How Top Performers Learn]]. In practice it means watching the process, not the grade.

## Using the model

The model turns "learn better" into a diagnosis. Name the dimension that is currently the bottleneck. Work on it directly. Do not add techniques on top of a dimension that is too weak to support them. A technique aimed at a different dimension from the one that is limiting produces visible activity and flat results.

Someone with unusually strong natural processing performs well on mediocre methods, because raw processing compensates for technique. A learner without that processing copies the same ordinary-looking habits and does not get the same outcome. The compensation also runs out. Once material is hard enough, a strong processor still has to fix the other dimensions. That failure is [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Deep Processing Tanking|Deep Processing Tanking]].

The more common case of activity on the unused dimension is thorough notes, comfortable re-reading, and a blank mind in the exam. That is a retrieval bottleneck. More encoding effort is the usual next move, and it leaves the bottleneck in place. The repair is retrieval practice.

The boundaries between the five are not clean. Metacognition covers both awareness and adjustment, which is why it sits inside self-regulation rather than beside it. The [[wiki/Dimensions/Dimensions of Learning#Key Distinctions|distinction above]] between self-management and self-regulation takes deliberate effort to hold.

The failure mode of believing the model is diagnosis paralysis: categorizing weaknesses becomes a substitute for studying. Diagnostic overhead only pays when the diagnosis changes what happens in the next session. If two weeks of dimension-diagnosis has not changed what happens in a session, stop using the model and go back to studying. The two-week limit is a threshold set here, not a research finding. Use the model again when progress stalls for a reason that cannot be named.

[[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges|Dimension Practice Tracks]] is the next task once a limiter is named, rather than another pass of diagnosis.

When all five are reasonably strong, learning stays consistent and resilient under high load, poor conditions, or low motivation. That is the result of using the model, not the starting point.

The check is a bottleneck that can be named in one sentence, and a next session that is different because of it. What the model produces is a different hour at the desk, not a better theory of how learning works.

## How to practice this

1. Ask the five questions in turn, one for each dimension. Name the single dimension whose answer is weakest right now. You should be able to state that bottleneck in one sentence.
2. Make your next session work on that dimension directly. Do not add a technique aimed at one of the other four. Notice whether the session is actually different from the last one.
3. Take one technique you already use and name the dimension it serves. If that dimension is already strong, expect visible activity and flat results. Notice whether you are busy while your results stay flat.
4. Ask whether your routines adjust when something goes wrong inside them. Routines that hold but never adjust point to weak self-regulation. Quick adjustment with no routine behind it points to weak self-management.
5. Close your notes on material you have re-read and find comfortable. Try to reconstruct it from memory. A blank mind on comfortable material is a retrieval bottleneck, not an encoding one.
6. Split a technique you use into the finding underneath and the version on top. Keep that version while it produces the thinking the finding asks for. Drop it when it stops.
