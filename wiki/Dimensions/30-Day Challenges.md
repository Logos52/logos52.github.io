# 30-Day Challenges

A 30-day challenge is a four-week practice plan aimed at one part of how a person learns, such as how deeply new material gets processed or how the study day is run. Each week has one task, and each week is harder than the one before. The plans are called tracks, and a track settles which habit gets worked on this month and what to do when a week fails.

## Takeaways

- Run one track at a time, on the weakest part of learning.
- Week 1 changes nothing and measures the habit as it is.
- Later weeks make the same task harder each time.
- Every step fits inside study sessions that already happen.
- A failed week means the change was too big, so shrink it.
- End each day with a short note on what happened and why.

## How a track runs

Every track has the same four-week shape. The first week only measures, with a tally, a table or a log, so the later weeks have something to compare against. The middle weeks change one thing on purpose and then add pressure until the weak point shows. The last week cuts the effort back to the smallest set of habits that still keeps the result.

- Week 2 moves one variable, since two at once cannot be read.
- Week 3 adds load, such as a full day planned ahead.
- Week 4 trims tracking to what keeps the result.
- The cost is a log, some interruptions and extra effort.
- Most habits take longer than 30 days, so any week can repeat.

The daily note has four parts: what happened, why it happened, the rule drawn from it, and the change for tomorrow. A track ends when its part of learning stops being the limit, and it runs again when that part slips.

## The five tracks

There are five tracks, one each for deep processing, self-regulation, self-management, mindset and retrieval. Running all five in one month works only when the workload is kept deliberately light. The Self-Management track protects the time the other four need.

- [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges/Deep Processing Challenge|Deep Processing Challenge]]: relate new material, then rank the links.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges/Self-Regulation Challenge|Self-Regulation Challenge]]: map study methods, drop weak ones, change a fifth.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges/Self-Management Challenge|Self-Management Challenge]]: log real days, test one change, trim supports.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges/Mindset Challenge|Mindset Challenge]]: make wrong attempts small and early, one change per miss.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges/Retrieval Challenge|Retrieval Challenge]]: close the book and rebuild ideas, then make recall harder.

## Where it fails

A track fails when its result cannot be read. That happens when week 1 is skipped, or when week 2 changes several things at once. A pressure week with no planned day tests nothing, and a week 4 that never cuts back becomes a permanent project. No track page yet records a week that was actually run, so the plans are untested so far.

- Several changes in week 2: nothing can be read.
- No attempts in the month: no evidence either way.
