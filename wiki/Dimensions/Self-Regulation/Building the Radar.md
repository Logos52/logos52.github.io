# Building the Radar

Building the Radar is a practice of catching the unnoticed slide from thinking into consuming while the session is still live. It feels like noticing that reading has turned into scanning, several pages in, with no idea when it started.

## What is being caught

Active, on this page, means comparing, judging, reconstructing, or preparing to teach. Passive means consuming, scanning, copying, or drifting. The pair is not focused versus distracted.

Thinking leaves no external trace. In a skill where a move goes wrong, something exists that can be pointed at and traced back. In thinking, nothing does. That absence is why a sheet of paper is not a silly instrument.

A book can be read front to back, the words taken in, and several pages later the realisation arrives of having been elsewhere, with no idea when the leave started. The two states feel distinctly different, and the difference that can be felt is how much effort is going in. The easy state is the drowsy one, because the brain is not being asked for much.

That drowsy default is common. Noticing only after the session is over is the untrained starting point, not a personal failure. The first bottleneck in learning to learn is often detecting that the old default has returned. Better methods do not help much if the moment they were left is invisible.

The diagnostic contrast the radar is meant to catch — and where the active moves live — is [[wiki/Concepts/Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming|Are You Thinking, or Just Consuming?]].

The old default that returns is a cue-response habit, and awareness is the first move against it on [[wiki/Concepts/How to Unlearn Old or Bad Habits Efficiently|How to Unlearn Old or Bad Habits Efficiently]].

## The A/P sheet

A normal learning session is enough. The material can be work already on the desk, a topic that was going to be learned anyway, or a reading list assembled from freely available articles. The practice trains the noticing, so the content's relevance is beside the point.

A sheet sits beside the work, a line down the middle, A for active and P for passive. The session starts in A, on purpose, holding an intention. P is written when the leave is noticed.

The intention can be preparing to teach, answering questions, mapping concepts, or explaining implications. Preparing to teach changes the questions in the head: from what the line means, to what someone could ask and what is missing. Expecting to teach organises the reading more than expecting a test does.

When drift, drowsiness, shallow reading, or daydreaming is noticed, a P goes down, and a short note of what just happened goes next to the mark. The after-review runs on the note. Passive asks what this says and reaches for repetition. Active asks how it relates to something else.

Reorienting means re-entering the intention — reframing what is being done with the material — not sitting up straighter. Then the session continues.

After the session the sheet is reviewed for how the two states felt: the effort they took, how engaged they were, what they did for memory and understanding. The size of the tally is not the object.

The sheet yields two things: how much of the session went each way, and a faster notice of the entry into the passive state. Cue recognition and awareness of the passive default are what the practice trains. Faster recovery and a cleaner distinction between real processing and covered pages are aims, not guaranteed outcomes. The radar is the prerequisite for deliberate control. Better methods wait on it, because a method that has been left cannot be steered.

What metacognition is, and why it governs learning, lives on [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition: The Control Layer]] — this page is the monitoring practice that model requires.

## Signs the catch is moving earlier

The radar is working when reading turning into scanning is noticed in the act. Copying is caught before the whole session is lost. The shortcut being taken can be named. One small adjustment happens without restarting everything.

The named thing being caught when a shortcut can be said out loud is [[wiki/Concepts/The Shortcut Problem|The Shortcut Problem]].

The radar is too weak when the session is only recognised as passive after it has ended. Catching in the act and being caught by a review afterwards are different, and retrospective-only is where everyone starts. Some drift will only ever be visible in the after-review, which is why the review step exists.

The first review often finds the active state was almost never occupied — the expected first result, not evidence of failure. Progress shows up as the catch arriving earlier inside the session, and as more marks at first rather than fewer. It should feel like catching the moment the session changes quality.

## What it costs and what it does not do

The quality-change that gets caught is a change in effort, and high effort is not the target. Effort raised by distraction or by struggling with the language of the material is high effort that builds nothing. The signal is a change against one's own baseline while the material stays the same. This trains noticing a decoupling from the material. It does not train reading load as a measure of the quality of thinking.

What effort is trying to say, and why high load is not automatically good, is [[wiki/Concepts/Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue|Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue]].

The material being marked is still not the learning. A session spent annotating marks is a monitoring session. Catching afterwards is weaker than starting active, so the sheet is scaffolding toward starting active rather than a way to study passively and repair later.

The active state costs more effort in the session and can make a session feel slower; the return is fewer repetitions later. The sheet itself costs a piece of paper and a few seconds per catch.

The radar tells that a drift has happened, and nothing about what to do next. Which active move is switched to matters far less than that a switch happens. The sheet prescribes no method.

If the sheet is where the session's attention is going, the monitoring has replaced the studying, and the sheet belongs on part of a session rather than all of it. If after several weeks the catches still only arrive in the after-review, the review stays and no schedule is waited on for a focused in-session catch. No trial tests the A/P sheet, and there is no measured number of sessions to competence.

You are not trying to be perfectly focused. You are trying to notice the shift from thinking into autopilot quickly enough to correct it. The radar makes a deliberate adjustment possible and is never the adjustment itself. Detection without a repertoire leaves the work at the catch.

The full cue → monitor → respond loop is [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition as a Skill|Metacognition as a Skill]]; the radar is the monitoring step in isolation, which is why detection alone leaves the work at the catch.

## Open Questions

- How many sessions are needed before the catch of passive drift becomes easy is unknown. There is no measured session-count. Progress is recognisable as self-catches arriving earlier in the session.
