# How to shift your brain to be motivated (when you don't feel like it)

Motivation is the feeling of wanting to do something, and it rises and falls with sleep, mood, health, money and the people around you. Someone who waits for the feeling before starting has many days with little done. The method separates the feeling from the action, so work can start on a tired or flat day, and spends the motivation that does show up on building habits that later run without it.

## Core takeaways

- Motivation varies by day, so work that depends on it varies too.
- Tiredness is a sensation, and acting on it is optional.
- Acting like a person who is not tired changes the next thought.
- Start with a short working time and lengthen it slowly.
- Remove the triggers for distraction from the workspace.
- Spend motivation on building habits, which then do the daily work.

## Why waiting fails

Motivation comes from outside, such as pay, status or fear of punishment, and from inside, such as values, purpose or enjoying the task. Outside sources swing with things you do not control. Inside sources are steadier, and they still move with sleep, mood and health. If what you do depends on how you feel, the only way to be consistent is to feel good every day, which nobody does.

- People with high self-control succeed mainly through habits.
- They do not resist temptation more often than others.
- The idea that willpower runs out with use failed large replications.
- Habits are triggered by surroundings, so changing surroundings changes behaviour.

## The four steps

The method has four steps, done in order: separate, act as if, extend, and clear the workspace. Separating and acting as if let a person work while the tired or flat feeling is still there. Extending makes that working state last longer, and clearing the workspace removes what sets off distraction. Each step takes practice.

- Separate: notice the feeling, the thought and the action as three events.
  - Hunger is a sensation, and "I must eat" is a later thought.
  - A racing heart before a talk is the body state of excitement.
- Act as if: behave like someone who does not feel tired.
  - An actor who arrives tired still plays an alert character.
  - Watching yourself work makes you think you are less lazy.
- Extend: 10 minutes today, 12 tomorrow, then 14, 20, 30.
- Clear the workspace: remove what sets off distraction.
  - Keep a sheet while working and write down each distraction.
  - Later remove those triggers: apps, devices, objects in view.

```
usual:      tired --> "I must rest" --> stop

separated:  tired --> act as if --> work 10 minutes
                                         |
            "maybe I am not lazy" <------+
```

Noticing a feeling works better than fighting it, since a thought pushed away tends to come back more often, while a thought noticed as an event passes. Telling yourself "I am excited" before a stressful task works better than trying to calm down.

## What motivation is for

Building a new skill or habit takes weeks to months before the habit runs by itself. The early part of that stretch is where people give up, because effort is high and progress is hard to see. Motivation is most useful spent there, and daily tasks that a habit could run are a poor use of it. Visible records of small progress keep motivation up until the habit takes over.

- Decide the outcome you want.
- Name the skills and routines it needs.
- Unlearn old habits and remove barriers.
- Learn the new skills.
- Carry on with the new habits, which now need little motivation.

Waiting for the feeling is one cause of putting work off, and there are others. When the tiredness is real, from poor sleep or overload, this method is the wrong tool and recovery comes first.
