## Sources

- Craik & Lockhart 1972: the process, not the procedure, determines the trace.
- Morris, Bransford & Franks 1977: transfer-appropriate processing: encoding should match what the test will ask.
- Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham 2013: highlighting and rereading have low utility and high surface appeal.
- Cronbach & Snow 1977: aptitude-treatment; no universal ranking of methods.
- Bower, Clark, Lesgold & Winzenz 1969: organisation aids recall.
- Bjork, R. A., Dunlosky, J. & Kornell, N. (2013): fluency is a poor cue for learning; recognition can feel like knowledge, and fluency is a poor signal of what will hold.
- Koriat 1997: ease and organisation are the cues that inflate judgments of learning.
- Taylor & Rohrer 2010: interleaving forces discrimination.
- Birnbaum, Kornell, Bjork & Bjork 2013: discrimination reveals organisation against mere recognition.
- Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer 2006: knowledge decays without review.
- Roediger, H. L. & Karpicke, J. D. (2006): retrieval as a learning event, not only a test of what is already held.
- Rawson, K. A. & Dunlosky, J. (2011): restudy after retrieval; gaps that stay closed on later attempts are the useful local metric.
- Karpicke, J. D., Butler, A. C. & Roediger, H. L. (2009): rereading produces an illusion of competence that active retrieval does not.

No trial of the six-question diagnostic as a named protocol exists, and the five-step sequence has not been trialled as a package. No measurement shows that the six-row table reduces premature abandonment. The five-step sequence does not separate high performers from anyone else.
