# Silly Mistake Syndrome

A silly mistake is an exam error that looks careless afterwards, such as a wrong unit or a dropped sign. Students who file these errors as silly tend to keep making them, because the label hides the cause. Most have a cause that can be found and fixed, and the fix is usually in how the material was learned more than in exam technique.

## Takeaways

- Most "silly" mistakes come from gaps in understanding.
- An answer that makes sense when read may not be producible.
- Trying to be more careful rarely works.
- Find the biggest cause, test one fix, then look again.
- Getting a question right while unsure still marks a gap.
- Pressure tools help only after preparation is sound.

## Where they come from

Silly mistakes have two sources: poor attention to detail, and missing logic. Poor attention to detail can be temporary, after a bad night's sleep or a stressful week, and more often it is a habit built by years of waving these errors away. Missing logic is the more common source. The student reads the model answer, it makes sense, and they miss that they could not have produced that chain of reasoning themselves.

- Recognising an explanation is easier than producing one.
- Unit errors often stop once the student understands what units mean.
- A wrong conversion then conflicts with what the units mean.

## How to fix them

Ignoring the mistake or resolving to try harder both leave the cause in place. The fix is a short loop of diagnosis and testing, repeated until the errors stop. The pattern of the mistakes points to the cause. Fix preparation first, then exam technique.

| Pattern of mistakes | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random, worse in high-stakes exams | Test anxiety | Timed practice under pressure |
| Specific facts and details | Weak fact recall | Add flashcard-style review |
| Links between concepts, strategy | Weak higher-order knowledge | Compare and relate while studying |
| Procedures, such as maths steps | Too little practice | Mixed practice problems |
| Mixed, repeated in practice tests | Undetected gaps | Double-answer practice test |

- Name the biggest cause.
- Try one targeted fix.
- Reflect on the result and look for other causes.
- Repeat.

## The double-answer practice test

A practice test marked too early lets errors slip through. In this version the student produces every answer twice, once under test conditions and once as a model answer written from notes. Writing the model answer forces study around each question, including material the question did not test. Answering in your head is not enough, because it hides the gaps a written answer exposes.

- Answer every question in writing, as in the exam.
- Mark each answer you were unsure of.
- Write your own model answers from notes and books.
- Compare your answers with your model answers.
- Compare your model answers with the official ones, if any.
- Restudy every unsure, wrong or weak item.

## Performing under pressure

When attention to detail drops only under pressure, five tools can help, remembered by the letters MR FIG: mirror calibration, which means self-affirmation, a pre-test ritual, focus training, image training, and graduated exposure to test conditions. The evidence for each is uneven. None of them replaces sleep or sound preparation.

- Mirror: the tested form is writing about an unrelated personal value.
  - Positive self-talk can backfire when self-esteem is low.
- Ritual: dampens the brain's response to failure, with no exam gains shown.
- Focus: short meditation training improves attention and working memory.
- Image: picture the steps of doing well, as well as the result.
- Graduated exposure: practise under timed, gradually harder conditions.
  - Treatment for test anxiety raises test scores.
