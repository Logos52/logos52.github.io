# Retrieval

Retrieval is producing knowledge from memory with the book closed. Doing this is the learning itself. It is not a check that comes after the learning. Rereading until the page feels familiar spends the same hour without producing anything from memory, and that is the trap. Reconstructing from memory sorts the material, makes it last longer, and lets it transfer to new problems.

Retrieval is one of the five parts in [[wiki/Dimensions/Dimensions of Learning|Dimensions of Learning]].

## Core takeaways

- Producing knowledge from memory with the book closed is the learning. Rereading until the page feels familiar does not produce anything from memory.

## The Encoding Trap

The Encoding Trap is spending the study hour making the page feel known, and almost none of it producing the knowledge with the book closed. The notes look ready. Then a timed test, a new setting, or a delay of a few days is enough for the same notes to fail to rebuild from memory. The smooth feeling of another pass through the text is the fluency illusion the trap depends on. The hour feels like work. The later demand is the first time the learner is asked to produce the knowledge.

The outcome of the trap reaches past the study hour. It shows in how well knowledge resists forgetting, and in whether the knowledge can be reached and used in varied or high-stakes settings. It shows in whether what is known is accurate and flexible rather than only recognisable, in whether testing can find and close gaps, and in whether performance holds when the setting is an exam, real work, or teaching someone else.

Five habits keep the trap in place.

- Sessions only confirm what already comes back, so no gap is exposed.
- Spacing and mixing settle into a routine instead of being matched to the current goal and to the kind of knowledge in play.
- Hard formats and weak areas get skipped because they feel uncomfortable, and the gap stays hidden.
- Performance holds in some subjects or conditions and fails in others.
- Recognition substitutes for production. The page is familiar, but the learner cannot reconstruct it under pressure or after time has passed.

## How retrieval is practised

The standing method is [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]]. Spacing puts gaps in time between sessions to work against forgetting. Interleaving mixes topics so similar items have to be told apart and then used flexibly. Without this method retrieval stays narrow or repetitive: recognition stays intact and flexible use fails.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/WPW|WPW]] reteaches a topic from memory in three passes: the whole shape, each part, then the whole again. It tests retrieval at more than one level and shows whether the structure still joins up. Without it, what comes back is surface recall that does not reintegrate.

The [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Interleaving Table|Interleaving Table]] is the format reference. Facts, procedures, and knowing when and why each need a different kind of retrieval. Sticking to a few comfortable formats neglects the others. [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Reconstruction - Retrieval Beyond Recall|Reconstruction]] sits above recall. It builds new relations among known nodes and judges them, rather than replaying a stored answer.

Harder self-testing is also how retrieval trains encoding. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]] builds rich and connected structures. Without retrieval those structures stay unreliable to reach. Weak retrieval also fails to expose shallow encoding. A complex self-test requires processing deeper than the learner would do by habit.

Whether a failed recall is used as a diagnostic or avoided because it feels uncomfortable is a matter of [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]], and so is whether the miss triggers a change. [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]] is the protected time and calendar consistency that spacing needs. Without it retrieval stays sporadic. Whether effortful or failed recall is read as data or as a threat is a matter of [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset|Mindset]]. With a fixed mindset the learner goes back to easy recognition.

In professional work the test can be the work itself. [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Opportunistic Retrieval|Opportunistic Retrieval]] is that reading, with deliberate practice filling the gaps that work does not cover. There is no generic retrieval challenge, because the right method depends on goals and schedule. That is why [[wiki/Dimensions/30-Day Challenges|Dimension Practice Tracks]] carries no fifth track for this capacity.

## What retrieval costs

The cost is paid during the session. A closed-book hour feels worse than another pass through the notes, and it is the hour that produces the later gain. Failed recalls are visible, the fluent feeling is gone, and sessions run slower. That discomfort is how the method works. A session in which every recall succeeded confirmed what was already there and exposed no gap. The repair is wider spacing, a harder format, or weaker material.

## When retrieval is not the thing to work on

Retrieval strengthens what encoding built. Run against unstructured material, it turns into [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Rote Learning and Memorisation|rote memorisation]]: fragments that come back one by one and connect to nothing. When recalls keep succeeding while application keeps failing, retrieval is no longer the thing to work on. The work belongs to [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]]. The problem is the structure of the knowledge, and repetitions do not fix structure.

## How to practice this

1. Close the book and produce the material from memory before rereading it. Notice which parts fail to come back. Each failure is a gap that another pass through the text would have hidden.
2. Put gaps in time between sessions and mix topics inside each session. Notice whether similar items can be told apart when they sit next to each other.
3. Reteach a topic from memory in three passes: the whole shape, each part, the whole again. Notice whether the parts still join into the whole on the last pass.
4. Name the kind of knowledge in play: facts, procedures, or knowing when and why. Notice whether the formats used match that kind or are only the comfortable ones.
5. After a session, count the recalls that succeeded. If every recall succeeded, the session exposed no gap. Widen the spacing, choose a harder format, or pick weaker material next time.
6. Watch for recalls that keep succeeding while application keeps failing. That pattern means the structure of the knowledge is the problem, and the next work belongs to Deep Processing.

- Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011). Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping. *Science*. Retrieval can train encoding; both remain required.
- Kornell, N., & Bjork, R. A. (2008). The promise and perils of self-regulated study. *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*. Judgments of learning often run the wrong way: the smoother method is rated better and retains worse.

- Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*. Practice testing rated high utility.
