# Prestudy

> Part of [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]]

A lecture's detail has somewhere to land when a rough map of the whole topic's relationships is already in hand. The map is built from headings, diagrams, and guesses about how the main ideas connect, and none of the content is learned yet. That pass is prestudy: readiness for a lecture, class, reading, video, meeting, or dense session. It is a finished version of none of them.

Without the map, each piece of the session arrives with no place to go. With the map there is a reference picture and a gap each piece can go into. The frame is a set of places where things can land, built before the picture itself is known. After a short pass the learner holds a rough outline: what the topic is about, the major ideas, how they may relate, which questions matter, and where later detail will eventually fit. The *main learning event* is whichever session would otherwise be first contact.

The same pass runs across a set of dense sources. When several research articles or primary sources sit on one topic, the frame is built from the easiest material first and the hardest last, so each hard page arrives with somewhere to put what it says. That version is called syntopical reading.

## Core takeaways

- A short pass over headings, diagrams, and guesses about how the main ideas connect, taken before a lecture or dense reading, gives the detail that follows somewhere to land. Load in the room drops because nothing arrives for the first time at full speed.
- The pass has four constraints: details are avoided, the main ideas and their groups are named, the relationships between them are named, and a big picture is reached. Everything else is dosage around that core.
- Prestudy is a step in time, placed before the event. Aim is how the mind is pointed, and it carries on after the event starts. They usually run together, and the pass without the questions is shallow previewing.
- Ten minutes is the floor: skim, list five to ten ideas, circle three, ask why each matters, walk in with two or three questions. A comprehensive pass over the whole topic turns the main event into a gap-finder and detail-filler.
- When several dense sources sit on one topic, the pass runs as an ease ladder: images and diagrams, then headings and vocabulary, then a textbook or review article, then research articles last. The paper that looks most important is not the first one to open.
- The pass fails when it becomes memorising, polished notes, a single-lecture preview, or a session that ends with no questions. A paper that feels unreadable is a readiness signal, and the repair is to drop to an easier layer.

## Why the frame comes first

**Detail before structure is ordinary study moved earlier.** Load drops in the room because the brain is no longer meeting everything for the first time at full speed. Across weeks the same split runs both ways. Untreated, the overload of a first event is hard to recover from; when such events land weekly the deficit accumulates, and the cheaper moves take over: copying, memorising, sitting through the hour. Treated, the confusion is spread over more sessions, big-picture relevance is settled first, and each later session starts from a stronger foundation.

The untreated chain is recognisable from inside. No frame, the event feels dense, load spikes, details arrive before any structure exists, and retention and application stay weak because nothing had a home. The treated chain is the same events reversed: a rough topic frame, questions about how the pieces relate, lower load during the event, cleaner grouping, and a usable handoff into encoding and retrieval.

Information that has a place to fit is easier to process than information that arrives as random pieces, and the payoff is both retention and speed of recall. A random string of coordinates does not stay. A phone number does, because its place and its relations are already known; its length is not the reason. Introductory material pitched at a higher level of abstraction, generality, and inclusiveness has been the supported move for six decades, and a complex lesson lands better when the names and behaviours of its key elements are already known.

Detail-level prestudy early activates isolated, lower-order processing. The information arrives without a home, and that is the usual reason the pass feels wasted. The reason the detail still gets learned first is insecurity about not touching the small things. [[wiki/Concepts/Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue|Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue]] is what the spike is signalling. [[wiki/Concepts/The Shortcut Problem|The Shortcut Problem]] is why the cheaper-feeling version of the hour is the one that fails.

The same spike shows up with a hard paper. The feeling that a paper is not written in English is usually a readiness signal and rarely a problem with the paper. The paper did not get harder; the scaffold is not there yet. That feeling is what the spike is trying to cue, and the move is to drop to an easier layer of material rather than to blame the page.

## What the pass leaves behind

The pass leaves a usable frame and readiness for the event. It does not leave a finished artifact, and it does not leave completion.

The pass should leave big-picture familiarity, rough groups, early questions, likely relationships, a sense of what matters, and enough context to follow the event. It should not leave perfect notes, memorised facts, detailed flashcards, polished maps, complete understanding, or false confidence.

At its core the pass is four constraints: the smaller details are avoided; the main ideas and the groups they fall into are named; the key relationships between those ideas and groups are named; a big-picture understanding of the topic is reached. Everything else is dosage around that core.

Good prestudy feels broad, shallow, and slightly uncertain. Shallow and broad rather than deep and narrow is an instruction to follow, and the uncertainty is expected.

The pass is complete when:

- the structure is a big picture, not a term list
- the whole topic or subtopic was covered, not only the next class
- no details or facts were memorised, because the focus was exclusively the big picture
- the major ideas have visible relationships
- some hypotheses exist about how the concepts connect
- the main event feels like the next step, not first contact

## Where it sits next to Aim and the rest of the pipeline

Prestudy is about *when* the work happens. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Aim|Aim]] is about *how* the mind is pointed while it happens. The two get confused because they share a target: both aim at finding basic relationships through active exploration, building simple networks, and reaching a big-picture understanding quickly. They differ in kind. Prestudy is a chronological step, an act of studying placed before an event that would otherwise overwhelm. Aim is a cognitive step. It begins in the setup pass and carries on through later sessions until the topic is encoded. It does not end when the main event starts.

They usually run together. The setup pass sits before the event; the why, how, and relationship questions are asked during it. Prestudy without those questions becomes shallow previewing, and a pass run with the wrong methods can be a waste of time. The questions without prestudy still work, but they are harder inside a fast lecture or a dense text, because there is less time and more load.

The sequence this step opens is Prestudy → Aim → Shoot → Skin → [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|SIR]]. Prestudy sits in front of the [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Bear Hunter System|Bear Hunter System]] as the first-contact layer. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Skin|Skin]] is the cleanup at the end of that loop. A change at the earliest point of a study process moves everything downstream, so where prestudy is missing, adjustments further along cannot be judged.

Where no prestudy happened, the questioning still has to happen somewhere worse. Inside the main event it is very hard, and that difficulty is the reason it belongs in a self-paced pass. After the event it is more workable and still not the same thing: the session is spent keeping up and forming rough structure, and the real encoding starts afterwards. The step matters most when the upcoming event is too fast, too dense, or too high-stakes to meet cold. [[wiki/Syntheses/Prestudy, BHS, and SIR - Turning Information into Usable Structure|Prestudy, BHS, and SIR: Turning Information into Usable Structure]] walks the pipeline end to end with this step named in it.

## The pass itself

How long the pass runs decides how much of the questioning step fits inside it. Ten minutes supports the largest chunks and the major relationships only. A comprehensive pass supports the full version, and the step after it can be pulled in as well.

**Skim for scope.** Headings, learning objectives, diagrams, summaries, keywords, problem sets, lecture slides, chapter introductions, and the table of contents. The goal is the boundary of the topic.

**Find the main ideas.** Major ideas, processes, cases, mechanisms, formulas, people, or arguments, listed rough. Not a glossary. Where a term is unfamiliar, just enough is read that the word means something, because a word with no meaning cannot be grouped with anything. No memorising. Grouping of this kind is *chunking*; the judgment of those groups lives on [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Importance-Based Chunking|Importance-Based Chunking]].

**Group by relationship.** Function, cause and effect, sequence, contrast, shared problem, shared mechanism, part-whole, application. The first grouping is a hypothesis and is allowed to be wrong, because the categories are expected to move as more is learned.

**Ask what matters.** More time goes to why something matters than to remembering or understanding it. The questions that implement that comparison: why does this matter, what problem does it solve, what does it connect to, what would break if it were misunderstood, what kind of task would require it, which ideas look central against supporting. This is also the only step that reaches outside the material being previewed: analogies are built, and what is being previewed is connected to what is already known.

Notes here, if any, are relational and non-linear, because linear notes cannot hold relationships at a usable accuracy. Where notes are not possible, the mental version still counts.

**Leave with a hunt.** Questions to answer, relationships to verify, chunks to revise, confusing areas to watch, details that would strengthen the frame. Then the session. [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Shoot|Shoot]] is what fills the frame once that session starts.

## How far to take it

```text
Ten minutes.
Skim headings, diagrams, summaries.
List five to ten key ideas.
Circle the three most important.
Ask why each matters.
Guess how they relate.
Walk in with two or three questions.
```

That floor is enough to stop first contact.

The comprehensive version previews the whole topic: a rough map of the major chunks, the recurring mechanisms, contrasts and sequences, questions for each major chunk, a light first pass at the biggest ideas, and later classes doing verification and detail-filling. What it buys is a change of job. The main event becomes a gap-finder and detail-filler rather than first contact.

Whole-topic scope is about where the boundary falls, and volume is a separate matter. A class boundary cuts relationships in half, and covering several sessions at once is what keeps them intact. A first pass covers more ground than expected, because the goal is familiarity rather than thoroughness. Work ahead of the events where the curriculum allows, because assessments are timed to the end of the classes and studying ahead leaves more room for encoding and retrieval afterwards.

When the week will not hold everything, prestudy outranks reviewing old material. Review can be caught up later, and prestudy leaves less to catch up on. The time is scheduled and protected. A common reason a study schedule collapses is retrieval sessions taking the slot the setup pass needed. The calendar shape is a pass over the coming week at the weekend, or over the day in the morning: broad and superficial first, more depth on each later pass over the same topic. [[wiki/Self Management/Study Scheduling|Study Scheduling]] is the weekly rhythm this pass is scheduled inside, and what it displaces.

## Several dense sources: the ease ladder

Syntopical reading is building a simple picture of a topic from easier sources first, so several hard pages have somewhere to land. A published method carries the same name and compares authors on questions set in advance; the ease ladder is a different procedure from that comparison. Until the simple picture exists, a dense page has nowhere to put what it says.

The ladder belongs where several research articles or primary sources sit on one topic, where the technical pages feel unreadable, where the job is to synthesize across a set, or where a course or project draws from many heavy sources. Ordinary prestudy builds a rough frame before a single lecture or chapter. The ladder builds a frame before a set of dense sources.

Sources get layered upward by how much support they need, not by how important they are and not by publication order. The useful counter-habit: the important paper is not the first one to open. Importance is a reason to reach the paper with a picture already built, and it is no reason to open it cold.

The lowest-load first move is images and diagrams: fast visual chunks and relationships, a way to make the topic feel real before the words arrive. It is a first move and not a law. Some domains have no useful visual layer, and there the ladder starts one step up.

The next layer is a survey pass: headings, vocabulary, major branches. An encyclopedia article, a review's heading list, a syllabus outline, any of those will do. The job is the shape of the field without high density, and no particular site is required.

A textbook or a review article sits in the middle: clearer conceptual treatment at moderate load, filling relationships while the scaffold is still forming. Research articles come last, for precision, methods, edge cases, and disagreements, because new information now has places to land.

Each layer creates hooks for the next. Images give something concrete to point at. Headings give structure. Textbooks give clearer concepts. Articles add complexity inside a frame that can hold it. Without those hooks the ladder is only a reading-order preference.

## Same mental work, more or less support

The mental work does not change as the material gets denser: connecting, simplifying, and finding what is missing. What changes is how much support that work is given. A diagram and a methods section ask for the same mental operations; the diagram supplies more of the structure, so the operations have something to hold. That is what makes the ladder a method rather than a preference for easy reading.

Strong work of this kind is [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Aim|Aim]] done at the level of the entire set rather than one article: the orienting questions are questions across the set. Shoot is then placing pieces from several sources into one working map, and Skin is cleaning the cross-source relationships.

A checkable expectation: a stretch of the hard paper now places itself on the scaffold without a word-by-word fight. The hard page is readable because readiness changed, and the paper itself did not get easier. The next time a paper feels unreadable, the move is to drop a layer.

## Where it goes wrong, and where it does not pay

The warning signs are memorising facts too early, trying to understand every detail, making polished notes, previewing only one lecture, and finishing with no questions.

| Failure | Repair |
|---|---|
| Detail trap | Return to ideas, relations, and questions |
| Preview theater | End with questions and rough chunks |
| Lecture-only scope | Skim the whole topic first |
| Perfect-map trap | Treat the map as a hypothesis |
| No handoff | Walk in with explicit questions |
| Late recovery | A short pass before the next exposure |

[[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/The Technique Is Only as Good as the Thinking It Produces|The Technique Is Only as Good as the Thinking It Produces]] is why a method performed correctly can still produce nothing: preview theater.

Across a set of dense sources there are five more ways the pass fails.

The paper that looks most important is the one the session wants to start with, and that is the main failure. With no structure yet, the page either drowns in detail or treats every sentence as equally urgent. The importance was real; the order was the error.

The second is treating every source as something to fully understand before the next one opens, sequential instead of relational. The set is the unit. A source that has handed over its shape can be left unfinished while another source fills a gap.

The third is going too deep on one branch while major gaps remain elsewhere. That is segmental mapping: one branch mapped in full while the other branches hold nothing. The repair is to fill major gaps first, then deepen.

The fourth is skipping the visual or intuition layer and starting with text. Images are not always the easiest layer; the reason to start there is that the first concrete chunk is what later sentences attach to.

The fifth is turning the pass into ordinary note-taking too early. Maps and definitions stay hypotheses until the hard pages have been placed. A polished note from the survey pass pretends the picture is finished.

The ladder is the wrong tool for a single easy chapter. Images-first is a heuristic, and some domains have no useful visual layer. The price is an extra easy-source pass before the paper that feels most important. The quit signals are still drowning after the easy layers, one deep branch and an empty field, and notes crystallized while the map should still be a hypothesis.

The single-session pass has its own price and limits. The pass costs a scheduled block before each topic, protected when the week is full, and it is paid for out of review time on purpose. Covering the content is not sufficient on its own, and most learners do not yet have the study efficiency to work comprehensively faster than class pace. The usual situation is the opposite: class pace is too fast and the learner gradually falls behind. The comprehensive version is not available merely by deciding on it.

Where attendance is compulsory and the prestudy has left the learner far ahead, the class becomes dead time. The deliberate answer is a shallower pass, so more of the load lands in the room and the session is worth attending, with the freed self-study time going to retrieval instead. Prestudy moves load out of a session, so a session that needs load moved in gets less of it.

If no relationships can be made at all while studying, the material is too detailed for the current level of knowledge, and the repair is a surface-level pass of this kind.

If the pass leaves no questions, memorised fragments, and a session that still feels like first contact, the technique is wrong rather than the step. The next move is the same pass run against the four framing constraints; abandoning the pass is the wrong move. After a correct pass the next event should feel less overwhelming, the main ideas should already be familiar when they arrive, what happens in the room should be easier to consolidate, and it should be possible to ask deeper or more connected questions there.

Strip everything and the framing step is what stays: details avoided, main ideas named, relationships named, big picture reached. Even where the pass has been thorough enough that the session is no longer necessary, the session still opens with it. Where there is no time for anything else, it alone still pays.

## How to practice this

1. Ten minutes before the next lecture, skim the headings, diagrams, and summaries, and list five to ten key ideas. Circle the three most important and ask why each matters. Walk in with two or three questions and notice whether the lecture feels like the next step.
2. Group the listed ideas by function, cause and effect, sequence, contrast, or shared problem. Treat the first grouping as a hypothesis. Notice the groups moving as more of the topic is learned.
3. After the pass, check it against the four constraints: details avoided, main ideas named, relationships named, big picture reached. If it left memorised fragments and no questions, run the same pass again against those constraints.
4. When several dense papers sit on one topic, open images and diagrams first, then headings and vocabulary. Read a textbook or review article next and the research articles last. Notice a stretch of the hard paper placing itself on the scaffold without a word-by-word fight.
5. When a paper feels as if it is not written in English, drop to an easier layer of material. Notice that the page becomes readable once the scaffold exists.
6. At the weekend, run a broad pass over the coming week's topics before any review of old material. Notice whether retrieval sessions have been taking the slot the setup pass needed.
