# Knowledge Mastery - From Recognition to Usable Knowledge

## Related pages

- [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]]: the dimension the levels are produced in. Encoding is where a level is set.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Dimensions of Learning|Dimensions of Learning]]: the parent model this ladder sits inside.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Bear Hunter System|Bear Hunter System]]: the four-pass encoding routine aimed at levels 3–4.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Spaced Interleaved Retrieval|Spaced Interleaved Retrieval]]: retrieval spread over time and mixed across topics, which tests every level rather than one.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval|Retrieval]]: the dimension where a level, once built, gets tested.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation|Self-Regulation]]: decides whether a failed level is repaired by encoding, retrieval, practice, or self-management.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Importance-Based Chunking|Importance-Based Chunking]]: the level-4 method in full. Grouping by why things belong together, then ranking the groups.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Metacognition - The Control Layer|Metacognition: The Control Layer]]: the monitoring layer the five failure questions belong to.
- [[wiki/Concepts/Learning Efficiency|Learning Efficiency]]: efficiency measured as retention at the level the subject requires, rather than as hours.
- [[wiki/Concepts/Memory Handling|Memory Handling]]: the working-memory window that makes isolated detail expensive to hold.
- [[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/WPW|WPW]]: the reteaching cycle used here as a level-4 instrument. Its return depends on the quality of the encoding underneath it.

## Sources

- Anderson & Krathwohl (2001): revised taxonomy of educational objectives, cognitive process crossed with knowledge type. One of the two accounts the ladder is built on, not this system's own model.
- Biggs & Collis (1982): SOLO, with the levels unistructural, multistructural, relational, extended abstract. The other account the ladder is built on, based on structural complexity.
- Chi, Feltovich & Glaser (1981), *Cognitive Science* 5:121–152. Experts organise by principle, novices by surface. The basis for "integrated" versus "isolated."
- Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1): practice testing supports isolated recall (level 1).
- Craik & Lockhart (1972); Craik & Tulving (1975): elaborative, meaningful encoding is more durable.
- Roediger & Karpicke (2006): retrieval practice is still required after better encoding. Meaning-first does not replace testing.
- Morris, Bransford & Franks (1977): transfer-appropriate processing. The test should match the encoding.
- Barnett & Ceci (2002): far transfer is mixed. "Across contexts" is the target, not the guarantee.
