## Sources

- Kirsh, D. (1995). The intelligent use of space. *Artificial Intelligence, 73*(1–2), 31–68. Complementary strategies: the world as external memory and scaffold.
- Cowan, N. (2001). The magical number 4 in short-term memory: A reconsideration of mental storage capacity. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 24*(1), 87–114. The focus of attention is about four chunks — the reason the offload is worth doing.
- Risko, E. F., & Gilbert, S. J. (2016). Cognitive offloading. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 20*(9), 676–688. Review of putting intermediate products into the world so a limited focus can handle more structure.
- Tversky, B. (2011). Visualizing thought. *Topics in Cognitive Science, 3*(3), 499–535. Sketches change what can be noticed next; they reveal structure the sketcher did not see in the head.
- Schön, D. A. (1983). *The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action.* Basic Books. Reflection-in-action: a conversation with the marks already on the page.
- How to Think So Clearly People Assume You're A Genius. Public video. The workbench contrast, and the unlock that the page is allowed to be ugly.
- If You Have A Bad Memory, I'll Help You Fix It In 28 Minutes. Public video. Offload as the move when the window is full.
- How To Learn So Fast That AI Can Never Replace You. Public video. Notes as processing rather than as a transcript of the hour.
