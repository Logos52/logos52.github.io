# Deep Processing Tanking

Deep processing is the brain's capacity to pull meaningful learning out of information. Deep processing tanking is when someone still does well at learning with only average methods, because unusually strong processing covers for weak technique. A classic "genius," in the sense used here, is someone with great deep processing skills who can do this.

This can be confusing for learners who do not have the same deep processing strength. They do not get matching results even when they copy the same strategies as those top learners.

Tanking is not enough when the learning goals become hard enough. As such, even people who have relied on it need to train their other dimensions if they want to learn as well as they can.

Stronger deep processing brings firmer memory, richer understanding, and a greater ability to put new information to complex use. That is the strength being used as compensation when technique is mediocre. For most people, that strength is shaped in a large way by genetics and by experiences in early childhood, and it can also be trained through steady, purposeful practice.

## Open questions

- How does relying on unusually strong processing to cover weak technique change what happens when the goals become hard enough that processing alone cannot cover?
- How does watching someone else succeed with the same shallow method change what you conclude about your own ability?
