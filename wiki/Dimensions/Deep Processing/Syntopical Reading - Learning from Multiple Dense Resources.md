# Syntopical Reading - Learning from Multiple Dense Resources

Syntopical reading is building a simple picture of a topic from easier sources first, so several hard pages have somewhere to land. The published method under the same name compares authors on questions set in advance; the name here is the ease ladder, not that comparison. Until that picture exists, a dense page has nowhere to put what it says.

## When the sources will not land

The method belongs where several research articles or primary sources sit on one topic, where the technical pages feel unreadable, where the job is to synthesize across a set, or where a course or project draws from many heavy sources. It is an advanced form of [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Prestudy|Prestudy]] when the material is both dense and multi-source. Ordinary prestudy builds a rough frame before a single lecture or chapter. This builds a frame before a set of dense sources.

The feeling that a paper is not written in English is usually a readiness signal, not a source problem. The paper did not get harder; the scaffold is not there yet. That signal is what [[wiki/Concepts/Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue|Cognitive Load & What Mental Effort Is Trying to Cue]] is trying to cue. The move is to drop a layer, not to blame the page.

## The ease ladder

Sources get layered upward by how much support they need, not by how important they are and not by publication order. The useful counter-habit is the important paper is not the first one to open. Importance is a reason to reach the paper with a picture already built, not a reason to open it cold.

The lowest-load first move is images and diagrams: fast visual chunks and relationships, a way to make the topic feel real before the words arrive. It is a first move, not a law. Some domains have no useful visual layer, and the ladder starts one step up.

The next class is a survey pass: headings, vocabulary, major branches. An encyclopedia article, a review's heading list, a syllabus outline — any of those will do. The job is the shape of the field without high density, not a particular site.

A textbook or a review article sits in the middle: clearer conceptual treatment at moderate load, filling relationships while the scaffold is still forming. Research articles come last — precision, methods, edge cases, disagreements — because new information now has places to land.

Each layer creates hooks for the next. Images give something concrete to point at. Headings give structure. Textbooks give clearer concepts. Articles add complexity inside a frame that can hold it. Without those hooks the ladder is only a reading-order preference.

## Same operations, changing support

The mental work does not change as the sources get denser: connecting, simplifying, and finding what is missing. What changes is how much support that work is given. A diagram and a methods section ask for the same mental operations; the diagram supplies more of the structure so the operations have something to hold. That is what makes the ladder a method rather than a preference for easy reading.

Strong work of this kind is [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Aim|Aim]] done at the level of the entire source set, not one article: the orienting questions are questions across the set.

## How it fails

The paper that looks most important is the one the session wants to start with, and that is the main failure. With no structure yet, the page either drowns in detail or treats every sentence as equally urgent. The importance was real; the order was the error.

A second failure is treating every source as something to fully understand before the next one opens — sequential instead of relational. The set is the unit. A source that has handed over its shape can be left unfinished while another source fills a gap.

A third is going too deep on one branch while major gaps remain elsewhere. That is segmental mapping: one corridor fully painted, the rest of the building empty. The repair is to fill major gaps first, then deepen.

Skipping the visual or intuition layer and starting with text is the fourth — not because images are always easiest, but because the first concrete chunk is what later sentences attach to.

The fifth is turning the pass into ordinary note-taking too early. Maps and definitions stay hypotheses until the hard pages have been placed. A polished note from the survey pass pretends the picture is finished.

## Where it sits

The ladder maps onto the [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/Bear Hunter System|Bear Hunter System]] without becoming a second manual: Aim is the easiest orientation — images, headings, and questions across the set; Shoot is placing pieces from several sources into one working map; Skin is cleaning the cross-source relationships. It is a child of [[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing|Deep Processing]], encoding that builds relations rather than copies.

The method is the wrong tool for a single easy chapter. Images-first is a heuristic, and some domains have no useful visual layer. The price is an extra easy-source pass before the paper that feels most important. Quit signals: still drowning after the easy layers; one deep branch and an empty field; notes crystallized while the map should still be a hypothesis. A checkable expectation: a stretch of the hard paper now places itself on the scaffold without a word-by-word fight.

The hard page is readable because readiness changed, not because the paper got easier. The next time a paper feels unreadable, the move is to drop a layer.
