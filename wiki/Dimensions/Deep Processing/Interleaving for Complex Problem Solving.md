# Interleaving for Complex Problem Solving

Interleaving for a complex problem means solving the same problem again from a different angle, context, constraint or outcome, in place of repeating one attempt. Most trouble with hard knowledge work comes from how the problem was set up, and rebuilding the problem from new angles is what improves the setup.

## Core takeaways

- A problem is complex when many factors change each other.
- Solving one has three parts: understanding, approach, execution.
- The approach is seeing which factors exist and how they relate.
- Most people who struggle skip the approach and start executing.
- The brain rebuilds a memory each time it is used.
- Let the outcome choose how many factors to practise with.
- A working professional needs no schedule, only varied real use.

## How it works

Each time a memory is used, the brain builds it again. Rebuilt from the same angle with nothing to check it against, a memory drifts, and years later a retelling can differ a lot from what happened. Rebuilt from a new angle, with the outcome as the check, it shows where an idea holds and where it stops. In a physical skill, changing the angle or speed of a kick shows what is too hard, too soft or off target, and in a thinking skill the same edge is the point where a way of thinking stops making sense.

- Varying the angle finds the edges of an idea.
- An edge shows which factor changes the answer.
- It also shows which relationship between factors is weak.

The approach is usually the step that holds the work up. A thinking task with no time pressure can be solved by a conscious decision at every step, and swimming cannot, because muscles move faster than thought. So in thinking work, automatic skill adds speed first and accuracy later, and a person who cannot apply what they know usually needs practice at mapping the factors and their links.

- Example: ten hours on an essay that comes out disorganised.
- Every hour of writing was also an attempt to organise.
- The repair: about three hours organising the essay's argument first.

## The steps

The steps form a loop: name the outcome, list the factors and map how they relate, try a first approach, then change one thing and compare the result with the outcome. The outcome has to be stated precisely enough to mark against, such as teaching an idea at a set level of detail or building software to set criteria. Marking the result also shows which part of the process the struggle sits in, so the next round goes there.

- Execute only enough to get feedback.
- Change one factor: context, framing, format, audience, a constraint.
- Rebuild the solution under the new condition.
- Mark: what happened, why, what rule, what changes next.
- Take the next smallest improvement, then repeat.

## Choosing the variation

The outcome sets how complex the practice should be. If two or three factors influence the outcome, practising with seven or eight is too many, and recalling single facts is too few. A factor is added because it plausibly matters and interacts with the others, never only to make the exercise harder. Depth follows the mastery needed: a subject to master fully gets whole explanations rebuilt over years, and a subject held only to manage a specialist gets small variations.

- Outside reading pays when a gap keeps coming back.
- Two hours of reading for a detail seen once rarely pays.
- While reading, keep the problems to solve in view.
- What feeds a plan gets learned, and the rest stays reference.

When an AI agent does the execution, the approach is still the weak step, since fast code says nothing about whether the problem was framed well. Useful variations are rewriting a feature as acceptance criteria, running a task small and then larger, and explaining the design before writing code.

## Where it fails

Variation fails when it has no anchor. Without a stated outcome nothing can be marked, so changes feel random and extra factors add noise in place of showing a weak link. Mixing can also start too early. While a category is still being learned, practising one type at a time can beat mixing, though mixing wins on a later mixed test.

- Variation feels random: return to the outcome, change one factor.
- Execution continues while the approach stays untouched.
- Working: the same idea looks different under a new constraint.
- Working: confusion gets more specific and execution cleaner.

## The language case

Language is a rare skill where automatic fluency is needed, because conversation moves too fast to build word by word. Most practice is therefore varied use: different speakers and topics, listening and reading, and small amounts of speaking and writing. Vocabulary is memorised only to the point that lets a slightly harder conversation happen.

- Aim for conversation slightly above current comfort.
- Waiting to speak until fluency arrives has no research behind it.
- Past the intermediate stage, finding one's own gaps gets hard.
