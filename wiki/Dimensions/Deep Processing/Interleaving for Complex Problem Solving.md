# Interleaving for Complex Problem Solving

Interleaving for a complex problem means solving a complex problem a second time from a different angle, context, constraint or outcome instead of repeating one attempt. Most trouble with hard knowledge work comes from how the problem was set up, and rebuilding the problem from new angles is what improves the setup.

## Core takeaways

- A problem is complex when many factors are in play and the factors change each other.
- Solving one has three parts: understanding it, the approach, and execution. The approach is seeing which factors exist and how they relate.
- Most people who struggle skip the approach and start executing. The result comes out slow and messy because nothing was organised first.
- Knowing how to do something is still knowledge that can be stated. Automatic skill is the kind that runs without thinking, and thinking work rarely needs it.
- Each time a memory is used the brain builds it again. Interleaving makes the rebuild deliberate by changing the angle every time.
- The outcome chooses the variation. Practise with as many interacting factors as the outcome has, and no more.
- A working professional needs no schedule for this. Each real use of the knowledge only has to differ from the last.

## How it works

- Memory is built fresh at each use.
  - Rebuilding from the same angle with no check against the facts lets the memory drift. Years later the retelling can differ a lot from what happened.
  - Rebuilding from a new angle, with the outcome as the check, shows where the idea holds and where it stops.
- Variation finds the edges.
  - In a physical skill, changing the angle, side or speed of a kick shows what is too hard, too soft, or the wrong direction.
  - In a thinking skill, the edge is the point where a way of thinking stops making sense: which factor changes the answer, which relationship is weak.
- The approach is usually the bottleneck.
  - With no time pressure, a thinking task can be solved by a conscious decision at every step. Swimming cannot, because muscles move faster than thought.
  - So automatic fluency in thinking work adds speed first and accuracy later. It does not replace a clear approach.
  - If a person cannot apply what they know, the gap is in how to approach the problem, and the fix is practice at mapping the factors and their links.

Ten hours spent writing an essay that comes out disorganised is the common shape of a skipped approach. The essay was never organised, so every hour of writing was also an attempt to organise, and both suffered. The repair is about three hours spent organising the essay's argument first, then the writing.

## The steps

```
name the outcome
      |
list the factors -> map how they relate -> first approach
                                                |
                              execute enough to get feedback
                                                |
      change one factor, rebuild  <-------------+
                |
      mark the result against the outcome
                |
      take the next smallest improvement, repeat
```

- Name the outcome precisely enough to mark against: teach the idea at a set level of detail with set factors covered, write a clean argument, build software to set criteria.
- List the factors in play, then map how they relate.
- Choose a first approach and execute enough to get feedback.
- Change one meaningful factor: the context, the framing, which factors are included, the format, the audience, a constraint, the domain.
- Rebuild the solution under the new condition.
- Mark the result against the outcome: what happened, why, what rule that suggests, what changes next time.
- Take the next smallest improvement.
- Marking against the outcome also shows which part of the process the struggle sits in, so the next round goes there.

## Choosing the variation

- The outcome sets the complexity. If two or three other factors influence the outcome, practising with seven or eight is too many; recalling single facts is too few.
- Add a factor because it plausibly matters and interacts with the others, never to make the exercise harder.
- Depth follows the mastery the outcome needs. A subject to master fully gets whole explanations rebuilt from new angles over years. A subject held only well enough to manage a specialist gets small variations a few levels down.
- Going outside the topic helps when it gives information a place to sit. Two hours of outside reading for a detail seen once rarely pays; a gap that keeps coming back does.
- While reading, hold the problems to solve and the decisions to make in view, and keep testing whether a plan is becoming possible. What feeds a plan gets learned; the rest stays as reference.
- When an agent does the execution, the bottleneck is the same. Fast code says nothing about whether the problem was framed. Useful variation: rewrite a feature as acceptance criteria, run the task at small scope then larger, explain the design before writing the code.

## Where it fails

- Variation feels random. Return to the outcome and change one factor at a time.
- The outcome cannot be stated, so nothing can be marked and nothing calibrates.
- Extra factors add noise instead of showing a weak link.
- Execution continues while the approach stays untouched.
- Mixing from the first minute of a new topic. While a category is still being learned, practising one type at a time can beat mixing; mixing wins on a later test that mixes the types.
- Working looks like this: the same idea looks different under a new constraint, confusion gets more specific, choosing the approach gets easier, execution gets cleaner.

## The language case

- Language is the rare skill where automatic fluency and stateable knowledge block each other in turn, because conversation moves too fast to assemble word by word.
- So most practice is varied use: different speakers, topics, listening and reading, small amounts of speaking and writing.
- Vocabulary is memorised to the minimum that lets a slightly harder conversation happen. The aim is dialogue slightly above current comfort, difficult and still followable.
- Waiting to speak until fluency arrives has no research behind it.
- Past the intermediate stage, finding one's own gaps gets hard, and that difficulty is what slows learning from there on.
