# Exit Strategy For Objects

An exit strategy for an object is a plan for how it will leave your home, made before you buy it. Objects that are large, awkward or wanted by few people cost the most to get rid of, and without a plan they stay for months or years. Deciding the exit at the point of purchase settles the question once, instead of leaving it to come back again and again.

## Core takeaways

- Plan how an object will leave before it comes in.
- Large, awkward, low-demand objects cost the most to remove.
- Waiting for a good resale price keeps objects for weeks.
- Give each resale attempt a short deadline, then use the next exit.
- Throwing something away is a valid exit.

## How it works

Two well-studied habits keep unwanted objects in a home. People value what they own more than a buyer would pay for it, so a listing at the owner's price sits unsold for weeks. People also want to recover some of what they paid, even when the sum is small, so the object stays while they wait for that recovery. Neither habit gets the money back, and the object keeps taking space and attention the whole time.

- Owners price above what buyers will pay.
- A small hoped-for recovery keeps the object in place.
- Each week of waiting adds another week of ownership costs.
- Volume matters more than count when moving house.

## The exits

Most objects have several possible exits, and the exits differ in speed and effort. Selling brings back some money and is the slowest. Giving away is faster and still finds the object a use. Discarding is the fastest and ends every cost at once, which makes it the exit to take when the others stall.

- Return it to the shop within the return period.
- Trade it in against a replacement.
- Sell it online or to a second-hand shop.
- Sell it back through a maker's buy-back scheme.
- Give it to someone who asked for it.
- Donate it to a charity shop.
- Lend or pass it on inside a family or group.
- Recycle it through a collection point.
- Discard it with normal rubbish.

## Questions before buying

Asking a few questions at the shop turns the exit into part of the purchase decision. Each one is answered about the object in hand, before any money changes hands. An object with no easy answer is the one most likely to stay unused.

- How it will leave once its use is over.
- Who would want it second-hand.
- How big and heavy it is to move.
- Whether it can be returned if it does not fit your life.
- Whether you would pay to have it removed.
- Whether a smaller or rentable version exists.
