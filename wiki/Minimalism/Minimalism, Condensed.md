# Minimalism, Condensed

The main limit is bandwidth, meaning the attention available for maintaining things. Money is often not the limit. Every object, subscription, and commitment uses some of that attention. The full cost of possessions is attention, space, maintenance, effort, and limits on how you can live. The question to ask is "what am I maintaining that no longer supports the life I am building?" Reduction comes before optimization. Deciding not to want an object costs less than getting rid of the object after you own it. The room is part of the system: its layout prompts actions and sets default behaviour before willpower is used. Reduction gives you slack, which is empty space held in reserve, and it makes your identity clear.

---

## 1. The costs

- **Owning an object costs more than its purchase price.** The price is paid once. After that, an object keeps costing space, maintenance, cleaning, attention, storage, effort to set it up, and future decisions ([[wiki/Minimalism/Ownership Cost|Ownership Cost]]).
- **Maintenance attention is the limit.** Money is often not the constraint. The constraint is the attention needed to maintain things. For that reason, owning less helps even when income stays the same ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).
- **Storing an object postpones a decision.** Putting an object away postpones the decision to keep it or get rid of it, and the cost of that postponed decision grows the longer it waits ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).
- **Dropping the want is the cheapest way to avoid an object.** Most buying is done to signal an identity. Naming that motive ends the want before any cost is incurred ([[wiki/Minimalism/Wanting Less|Wanting Less]]).

## 2. The operations

- **Reduce first, optimize later.** Organizing a system that holds too much improves the wrong thing. Remove items first, then arrange the ones you keep ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).
- **Decide how an object will leave before it comes in.** Buy an object only if you have decided, before buying, how it will leave: used up, returned, sold, or given away. Every object that comes in is something you will later have to remove ([[wiki/Minimalism/Exit Strategy For Objects|Exit Strategy For Objects]]).
- **Reduce by zone, high-cost first.** Gather everything from one zone in one place so you can see how much there is. Remove the objects that cost the most first. Use what you learn from each discard when you make the next decision ([[wiki/Minimalism/Product Reduction|Product Reduction]]).
- **Do the purge in one short, intense session.** One concentrated session works better than removing a few things now and then over a long period. Working quickly without stopping gets through decisions that slow, careful thought does not ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).

## 3. How the room affects behaviour

- **The environment affects behaviour before willpower does.** A space prompts actions, uses attention, and sets what you do by default during the day. Arrange the prompts and the defaults instead of making resolutions ([[wiki/Minimalism/Environment Design|Environment Design]]).
- **Empty space is room held in reserve.** When there is no free space, moving one thing means moving other things first. Empty space is the room you need to move things, so it is not waste ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).
- **What is in view sets the day's defaults.** Objects left out are the objects you use. The objects you use shape who you are. Arrange the things in view to fit the life you intend to live ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]], [[wiki/Minimalism/Environment Design|Environment Design]]).
- **Minimalism makes identity clear.** Reduction removes objects that belonged to identities you have given up. The objects that remain show what your life is for ([[wiki/Minimalism/Minimalism as Systems Design|Minimalism as Systems Design]]).
