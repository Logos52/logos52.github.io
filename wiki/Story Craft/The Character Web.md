# The Character Web

A character web is a cast designed together rather than one at a time: one question underneath all of them, and each person built as a different answer to it. Because the answers oppose, the meaning of the story lives in the collisions between them rather than in any one person's speech or any one person's plot. The answers are visible long before the question is ever stated — an umbrella packed for someone who did not ask for it and an envelope sent home every month are two positions on the same matter, and a reader watching both will feel the question without it being named.

## The question and the floor

The work starts from a single **controlling idea**: a value joined to a cause by *when*, *if*, or *because*, said in one sentence. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]] is that sentence — the theme the web argues. That page and this one disagree on how the authorship spine is cast; the disagreement is left standing. This page keeps the doctrine (one question, four corners as floor, a dual-spine test) and works the love-spine as the live web.

The house through-line on that same spine is the sentence the love-web argues: love given in a language the beloved cannot receive reads as no love at all, until each learns to receive the other's. Every recurring character is a **stance** on that sentence — one defensible answer, played in behavior. A stance that cannot be played, only announced, is a speech waiting to happen, which is the failure this method exists to prevent.

[沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html)'s stance is presence. He shows up. He packs the umbrella nobody asked for. [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html)'s stance is provision, as one expression of chosen care: the envelope home every month. Neither is wrong. The series never lets either win. That constraint keeps the question open long enough to argue for the length of a series. If one stance were allowed to defeat the other, the web would collapse into the two-part slogan the floor is there to prevent.

```
Question: love given in a language the other cannot receive.
Presence: show up, pack the umbrella.
Provision: send the envelope, refuse the hand.
Neither wins. The question stays open.
```

Moral complexity has a floor: a hero, a main opponent, and at least two more, each a whole value system attacking a different face of the central question. That floor is **four corners**. Two-part opposition — one hero, one villain — is the primary failure, and it waits in the tests below. The second spine, *who authors a life you did not choose?*, is cast to this floor as doctrine. The names that fill those corners are not settled on this page.

The test that this is one web, not two, is whether the central characters answer *both* spine questions at once. Love and authorship have to share a body, or the reader is watching two casts under one title. Presence-lead is presence (love) and authorship (builds the small one, gathers the armored one, lets the provision-lead adopt him). Provision-lead is provision (love) and authorship (a life she did not choose, a found family she builds anyway). When a character sits on only one axis and never touches the other, the cast has split into two subcasts wearing one uniform.

**One question. Distinct stances. Meaning in the collisions.**

## Theme from collision

Every relationship drawn on the web doubles as a position in the argument. Collisions argue. Parallel tracks do not.

Over the story, each major character makes, in dialogue, a moral argument justifying what they do to reach the goal. The author's vision lands in the collision of those arguments, not in a speech someone is handed. A speech can be copied. A collision has to be watched, and the watching is what earns the theme. A dispute over money stages this as form: provision against presence, and neither holds the microphone. The standard is no one is right, no one is wrong, each arguing from a value they hold. That is a steelman rule for the writing, not a claim that every scene already passes.

The theme's full statement waits for a wordless reconciliation that says the controlling idea without a thesis line. The rule is the wait. The unpublished climax is not written here.

Which members can carry that wait is a round-versus-flat decision. [[wiki/Story Craft/Round Characters and the Telling Detail|Round Characters and the Telling Detail]] owns that distinction — the capacity to surprise convincingly, not a different book's flat *arc*. Two leads are built to take the pressure of the question. [將軍](/tsumugu/cast/general.html) the café cat is fixed contrast, a running gag with no arc, and that is correct casting. A flat member is a fixed reading of the question the round members are still working out. Two adults — [蘇老師](/tsumugu/cast/teacher-su.html) and [吳老闆](/tsumugu/cast/boss-wu.html) — are held deliberately light: full stances, low spotlight, adult-generation echoes.

Scene and dialogue have to come *from* the character. A surname pasted onto generic prose is not a stance.

[[wiki/Story Craft/Foils and Pairings|Foils and Pairings]] is the two-character case of the same web: one pair, shared ground, opposite method.

## Stance you can watch

At the first floor the reader has words for actions only, so the stance that was designed as an answer to the question is *played* through action and object. There is no thesis line available yet. The umbrella and the envelope have to hold the argument until the words arrive. This project's first book sits on that floor — about A1, not the later B1 of the language exams. Assign each recurring member a fixed position and let vocabulary-limited behavior carry it.

[[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] is how that playing is done.

A silent plant can do the work: a photo from home surfaces, and the subject changes before anyone explains it. The image is an unexplained picture he will not discuss. It is not a live grandmother-Want. The stance itself — presence-hunger, a family he never had — is fully present in action and object, and it is legible at A1 whether or not that photo is the vehicle.

Front-load the handful of Han for the theme's key value words, so climactic dialogue is readable when the argument comes to a head. The vocabulary curve is the reveal curve. The web lets the debt bank behaviorally at A1 and settle in dialogue once the ceiling lifts.

## Failures and tests

Two-part opposition — one hero, one villain, no third or fourth corner — collapses the argument into a slogan. That is the primary failure.

```
Weak: one hero, one villain, a slogan.
Strong: four value-systems, no clean winner, a question a reader can name.
```

A **name-swap ensemble** is the next failure: characters who sound identical and hold the same value, so the web argues nothing.

A character conceived only to fill a corner reads as schematic, an argument with a face. One corner on the second spine stays thin on this page — *what making is for*, mend versus make — and it stays thin rather than being dressed with a fresh invented detail.

Who should not use the method: a tight two-hander or a single-protagonist story pays the four-corner tax and gets a debate panel.

The price is that tax. Four whole value-systems have to be kept live. Spotlight has to be rationed so the book, not any one room, holds the web. [[wiki/Story Craft/Spotlight Rotation|Spotlight Rotation]] is when each member anchors a scene; this page is who they are.

Quit when the evidence says so.

- A justifying line moves into another mouth with no loss. Same answer. Merge or re-cast.
- A scene needs all four corners in the room to make its point. Redistribute. The book holds the web, not the room.
- A test reader picks a clean winner in a value-dispute. A corner collapsed into villainy.
- A reader partway through cannot name the one question. The tracks were parallel.

What should be checkable:

- State the one question and place two or three characters as opposed answers.
- A dispute reads as two people both right.
- A wordless beat lands the idea without a thesis line.
- An early object registers as a live absence to an A1 reader.

The companion pairing-grid — fifteen duets, three finer engines — is a different object. It is not imported here.

## The question a reader can name

The ensemble is still one argument. If a reader cannot name the question, the tracks were parallel. An umbrella and an envelope are already two answers. The test is whether anyone watching them can say what they are answering.
