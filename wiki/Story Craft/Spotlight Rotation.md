# Spotlight Rotation

Spotlight rotation is a way to schedule a large cast across a serial. Pick a window of episodes. Inside that window, every returning character has to be seen doing something well and seen paying a cost. That is two columns to tick beside each name, and keeping that ledger is the whole method.

Without the ledger a large cast turns into a solo show with extras. Equal time is not the goal, and chasing equal time produces filler. What matters is that each character's window closes with both marks. A character with only one of the two is the next assignment, and that is usually the character the author has started to overlook.

## Core takeaways

- The unit of the guarantee is a pair of beats per returning character: shine and shadow.
- The unit of the schedule is a thread hierarchy written in three letters, A, B and C. The letters name weight, not order.
- A character who only gets shine reads as a mascot. A character who only gets shadow reads as a punching bag. Both together read as a person.
- Equal screen time is not the target, and forcing it inflates the cast and starves the central relationship.
- The method fits a bounded serial ensemble of round characters. Flat support does not owe the pair.
- The check is what a reader can say afterward: over one book-level, they can name what each recurring character does well and where they fail.

## Shine and shadow

Shine is a competence beat, a win, or a warmth. Shadow is a flaw, a cost, or a failure. A character who only banks shine reads as a mascot. A character who only banks shadow reads as a punching bag. The two beats together read as a person.

Shine is not one expression. [星野](/tsumugu/cast/hoshino-haruka.html)'s shine is high-energy sociability. [阿迪](/tsumugu/cast/adi.html)'s shine is the quiet craftsman. Both still need a shadow, and the story has to earn it.

## A, B and C threads

A serial cannot service a full cast at once, so the threads get ranked. What is ranked is weight, not order, and what is scheduled is not screen-share.

The A-story is the lead's primary line. Hour-drama rooms treat it as the majority of the hour, and a common default is half to three-fifths of runtime. It is present in every act, and it opens and closes the unit. The B-story is a secondary character or pair, running as emotional or thematic counterpoint. The C-story, also called a runner, is lighter or long-arc, and it surfaces about three times across the unit. Three is a cap, not a minimum.

The same letters run at two scales. Inside a lesson, one reading is the A-story: the plot-and-arc line bound to the series theme. The remaining readings are B and C texture, which means subplot, ensemble, and a breather. Across a book-level, the A-line is [沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html), and the falsifiable target is double availability: competence and cost both land somewhere in the run. More scenes of the same beat do not count. The B-lines are foil two-handers. [星野遙香](/tsumugu/cast/hoshino-haruka.html) and [林薇](/tsumugu/cast/lin-wei.html) trade belonging: one fled a cage, one cannot leave. [白龍](/tsumugu/cast/bai-long.html) and [金多恩](/tsumugu/cast/kim-da-eun.html) sit as two people who see, not as a romance. A C-runner surfaces three times.

## Two kinds of runner

A recurring gag asks only to be recognized. [將軍](/tsumugu/cast/general.html)'s rank game (敬禮, 命令, 陸軍 / 海軍 / 空軍) is that kind.

A serial-seed runner plants a small early warning so that a later event reads as inevitable in hindsight: weather, a crack, a rumor. The plant does not lock in an unpublished disaster. The seed's job is recognition of a planted promise, not a new event. The practitioner shorthand for that spine is "campfire then battle", and [[wiki/Story Craft/Seeding and Payoff|Seeding and Payoff]] covers it.

## What the pair costs

Any writers' room already ranks plots. The ranking here exists so that the pair can land, not so that the calendar looks fair.

The pair is expensive. Every B or C is runtime the A-line does not advance, and the ledger is bookkeeping. A plant has to be paid off later, and that debt is heaviest across a vocabulary ramp: [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] gives the reason a late payoff cannot invent words the earlier plant never taught. A runner past three beats starts to crowd working memory.

Flat support does not owe the pair at all. 將軍 asks nothing but recognition. Round characters can carry shine and shadow, and flat ones are built not to. Forster's test is the filter for who belongs on the ledger.

The method belongs to a bounded serial ensemble of round people. A novella, a two-person story, or one lead with flat support pays the bookkeeping cost and gets nothing back.

## Bottle units

A bottle unit keeps existing sets and the core cast, lets the external plot idle, and gets its pressure from who the people already are to each other. Confinement helps: a locked room, a storm, sealed days. The form is the idle plot, not the lock. One famous lab-and-fly hour is the cited instance, and the title is in Sources. On a vocabulary ramp the same form doubles as a rest: few new words, people the reader already knows, and lines the reader can already decode. Important story pieces stay exempt from a floor. The rest-hour is compatible with that exemption, and it is a general form rather than a private specification.

## The spotlight ledger

The spotlight ledger is the table that stops the author servicing only the character they personally track. It has three columns: each character's recurring shine, the planted confession and who draws it out, and the beat it pays off at. The confession column comes from [[wiki/Story Craft/Companion Arcs and Party Banter|Companion arcs and party banter]], which holds the per-character payoff and the dedicated loyalty-mission beat that rotation routes toward. The game title is in Sources, and nothing here requires playing it.

## Five standing rules

1. Give each character a shine and a vulnerability every few lessons.
2. Mix tone across a five-reading set so that three sad readings never stack. The ban on stacking belongs to [[wiki/Story Craft/Tonal Modulation|Tonal Modulation]], as one clause rather than a second essay.
3. Spend two-handers on the key relationships.
4. Seed every big beat.
5. Put a shine near every heavy beat.

## The case against forced rotation

Even distribution is something a story may not want. Forcing shine and shadow onto a character who was designed as steady contrast inflates the cast. A beat that is only "due" starves the central relationship. A spotlight thread the main plot does not need reads as a detour. Mechanical fairness produces a name-swap ensemble wearing a schedule: characters who argue the same value, swapped on a calendar. [[wiki/Story Craft/Foils and Pairings|Foils and pairings]] already names that failure. The letters and the pair are not a fairness doctrine.

## Signals to stop

Texture readings that get skimmed demote to fixed contrast or a gag, which is the cat model. A scene that runs past what a listener can track gets narrowed. A beat with no plant and no payoff gets cut. Only shine, or only shadow, across a book-level means the ledger is not being kept honestly.

## How to check it

Over one book-level, a reader can name what each recurring character does well and where they fail. After a bottle unit, the same reader can name what changed between two people, with no external event. If the reader cannot, the hour was dead air. A C-runner payoff lands as recognition. If it feels like a new event, the plant failed.

## What the rotation is for

The turns are not taken for fairness. They are taken so that each returning character is still a person, rather than a mascot, a punching bag, or a name on a calendar. A large ensemble that keeps the pair inside the window does not collapse into a solo show with extras. A schedule that only equalizes time does not deliver the guarantee.

## Where the terms come from

"Spotlight episode" and "shine and shadow" are practitioner formalizations, not terms with one inventor. Rotating focus is the settled part. The letters sit on a [[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|character web]] that decides who the cast is, and rotation is only when. Four-corner opposition and B-line two-handers belong to that web and to foils. Loyalty missions route to companion arcs. Cold opens and tags are room furniture listed in Sources, and rotation does not cover them.

## How to practice this

1. Pick a window of episodes. List every returning character inside it.
2. Give each name two columns, shine and shadow. Tick shine when the character does something well. Tick shadow when the character pays a cost.
3. Rank the window's threads as A, B and C by weight. Check that the A-story opens and closes the unit. Check that it is present in every act.
4. Hold each runner to about three appearances. Watch for a runner past three beats crowding working memory.
5. Read the ledger for any name with one tick and not the other. Make that character the next assignment.
6. At the end of a book-level, ask a reader what each recurring character does well and where they fail. A name they cannot answer for is a window that did not close.
