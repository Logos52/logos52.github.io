# Story Craft, Condensed

A graded serial under a vocabulary ceiling reveals character only as fast as the reader learns the words to understand it. The cast answers one controlling question in different defensible ways. Nobody has to die: tension comes from bonds, standing, and self-image the reader was made to value first. Inner life travels on objects and gestures until words for feelings exist. Theme is proved at the climax, never preached. These rules are the operating spine for [[wiki/Tsumugu/Tsumugu|Tsumugu]] and any long ensemble where the word list is also a reveal schedule.

## The governing constraint

[[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] treats the word list as a timetable. Structure does not depend on language, because want, obstacle, reversal, and change need no particular words. A complete arc can run in the first few hundred characters, while depth accrues as the list grows. Hold peak scenes near ninety-eight percent known words so the reader feels instead of decodes. Introduce a scene’s nouns in the quiet opening so that at the peak the reader knows nearly every word.

## Theme is proved, never stated

[[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]] asks for one provable sentence: trait, mechanism, end. The controlling idea is a value plus its cause in one line, because two sentences mean two stories or a muddy spine. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|The Character Web]] designs the cast around one question, with each recurring character holding a different value position on it. A concrete object can carry the proposition: introduce it without weight, return it under pressure, and never explain its meaning.

## The character interior

[[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]] chains wound, lie, want, and need. Keep want and need different or the arc has no distance to cross, because getting the need must cost the want. One interior contradiction, spent on a costly choice before the reader can name it, makes a person round enough to surprise believably (see [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]]). Five arc types share one skeleton, and the choice at the **crux** (where lie and truth cannot both stand) names the type. Details sit in [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Craft|Story Craft#Arc types at the crux]].

## Interiority within the vocabulary limit

[[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] is the default channel when the list has no words for feelings: one belonging per lead, re-shown in changed condition, plus gesture at the peak. Voice (register, refusals, sentence length) needs no extra vocabulary and can carry the wound in how someone talks. Work out the full world and leave most of it off the page, since omission only works from knowledge. Pressure on want, do, and say, not tours of scenery, is [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Craft|Story Craft#World as pressure and setting]].

## Series structure

[[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] and seeding discipline require every payoff to be planted as a concrete object or habit the reader can recognize books later. Chekhov’s rifle is a plant in a neutral scene, not a speech about theme. Quiet downtime draws out confidences a curious friend can reuse under stress, so the reader already knows the secret when crisis hits. Each round character earns one loyalty-mission close, and relationship values do not reset to zero for convenience. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|The Character Web]] holds spotlight rotation, foils, and companion banter: rotate who carries strength and flaw across a fixed span and track it on a ledger so no one vanishes for a whole season. Party-RPG **approval meters** are borrowed craft, where warmth and cool move in visible objects (seat kept, name withheld) and never snap back to full reset without on-page cost.

## The emotional line

[[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]] establishes the losable thing before endangering it. McKee’s gap (expectation minus returned result) does not need a body. Escalate by making the reader value it more rather than making the threat louder, refusing serial inflation (bigger danger with a smaller felt hit each time). The low point’s real loss is often the truth the character has just begun to see, and death may signal it without supplying it. Pair peripeteia and anagnorisis when you can, so catharsis discharges pity and fear through recognition the reader can point to. [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Craft|Story Craft#Tone across a run]] sequences warm, comic, tender, and heavy so three sad installments in a row do not numb the reader. After a heavy tentpole, place comedy **after the crest**, not on the beat still climbing. Macbeth’s porter after Duncan’s murder is the timing receipt.

## Tensions resolved

**Consistent yet surprising.** Surprises come from traits already on the page, not from imports at the climax. **Set up early vs. hide the plant.** A setup must be memorable across a four-book gap yet live inside joke or routine object until it fires. **Round vs. flat.** Only a few characters stay round, while flat supports get one memorable trait rather than a full flaw-and-strength pass. **Show vs. tell.** At the lowest floor one plain sentence is allowed when showing alone loses the reader. **Flat character vs. flat arc.** Forster’s flat character (one idea) is not Weiland’s flat arc (truth-holder who changes the world). **One premise vs. many threads.** Keep the founding pair’s spine fixed while still naming secondary lines. **Omit vs. skip the work.** Withholding without a built iceberg reads hollow. **Deepen vs. expand.** Reuse locations and nouns before adding cities. **Front-load vs. finisher.** Deepest payoffs land late by choice, not by accident. **Small wins vs. tidy victory.** End on a named change with some burden still in place.
