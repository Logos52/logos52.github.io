# Story Craft, Condensed

**A story written under a rising vocabulary limit is built from its characters, and it reveals those characters only as fast as the reader learns the words needed to understand them. The cast is designed around a single controlling question. Each recurring character gives a different answer to that question, and each answer can be defended. No character states the theme. The theme is shown by which value wins at the climax. The earliest books have words for actions and no words for feelings, so a character's inner life is shown through a concrete object and a gesture the reader can see. The object and the gesture are introduced plainly at the 250-character level, and their meaning is named books later, once the reader has the words for it. No character dies. Tension comes from threatening a bond, a character's standing, or a self-image that the reader has first been made to value, and catharsis comes through a recognition the reader can name. A wound leads a character to believe a lie. The lie produces a want that conflicts with a hidden need. The distance between the want and the need drives every arc type, from the positive change arc to the three negative arcs. All five arc types use the same underlying structure, so characters in the cast can follow different arcs while every character stays alive. Three practices keep the whole cast easy to follow. The focus moves from character to character in turn. Only a few characters are round, and the rest are flat, with one memorable trait each. The tone varies, so a heavy scene never arrives when the reader is already emotionally worn out. Every payoff is also set up early enough that a fixed cast the reader loves can still surprise the reader in a believable way. Done this way, character depth is revealed at the rate the reader can understand it. If the method is not followed, the reader meets more unknown words than the reader can handle, or meets a cast whose characters take no positions on any question.**

## 1. The governing constraint

**The story reveals character at the rate the reader learns vocabulary.** A character's depth is revealed only as fast as the reader learns the words needed to understand it. So the minimum character count set for each book also limits how much each book can reveal, and a word held back until the reader reaches its level can itself be the reversal. [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]]

**Structure does not depend on language.** A want, an obstacle, a reversal, and a change do not require any particular words. So a complete arc can be written with 250 characters, and each later book can add depth to it. [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]]

**Make sure the reader understands the scene before the emotional peak.** Introduce the scene's few nouns in the quiet opening moments. Then at the peak the reader knows nearly every word and can attend to the emotion without working out what words mean. [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]]

## 2. Theme is proved, never stated

**Write the premise as one provable sentence.** The sentence names a trait, a mechanism, and an end that are true for this one story, and the story's structure is built from it. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]

**The controlling idea is a value plus its cause.** State in one sentence the value charge the climax leaves and the reason it occurred. If the statement needs two sentences, you have either two stories or an unclear central storyline. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]

**Design the whole cast around one controlling question.** Each recurring character takes a different value position on that question. The theme comes from the conflict between those positions, each of which has good reasons behind it. No single character carries the theme. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|The Character Web]]

**A concrete object stands for the story's proposition.** Attach a tag to the object. Give it no emotional weight when it first appears, bring it back with emotional weight, and never state what it stands for. If the prose explains the object, the object becomes exposition and stops working as a symbol. [[wiki/Story Craft/Motif and Symbol|Motif and Symbol]]

## 3. The character interior

**A character has a wound, a lie that comes from the wound, and a want that comes from the lie and conflicts with a need.** A character seems real to the degree that this sequence causes a choice the reader can see the character make under pressure. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]]

**Keep the Want and the Need different, or the arc has no change in it.** If reaching the external goal would also give the character the internal truth, there is no distance between them for the arc to cover. So getting the Need must cost the character the Want. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Change Arc|The Change Arc]]

**One interior contradiction, shown before it is named.** A round character is one who can surprise the reader in a believable way. So give the character one defining contradiction, and show it affecting a costly choice on the page long before the reader has the words to state it. [[wiki/Story Craft/Round Characters and the Telling Detail|Round Characters and the Telling Detail]]

**Five arc types share one structure.** The arc type is decided only by what the character does at the crux, the point where the character can no longer keep both the Lie and the Truth. Because of this, the characters in one cast can follow different arcs while all of them stay alive. [[wiki/Story Craft/Arc Types|Arc Types]]

## 4. Interiority within the vocabulary limit

**Show inner life through objects and gestures.** Changes in an object's condition, and actions the reader can see, show a feeling that the word list has no word for. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]]

**Work out the whole world and leave seven-eighths of it off the page.** Leaving things out works only when the author knows what is left out. So write the full bible of material that will not appear. If the author leaves out material that was never worked out, the reader can tell that something is missing. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Iceberg and World as Pressure|The Iceberg and World as Pressure]]

**Make the world limit the characters.** A place, a capability, or a culture is worth including when it limits what characters can want, do, and say. It does this through limits and costs. It does not do it through explanation or through power. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Iceberg and World as Pressure|The Iceberg and World as Pressure]]

**A setting works like a character when it means different things to different people.** The same place can mean freedom to one person and confinement to another. Culture is shown through groups of concrete habits, and a narrator does not lecture about it. [[wiki/Story Craft/Setting as Character|Setting as Character]]

**Voice is the one way to show inner life that the vocabulary limit does not block.** Sentence length, verbal tics, register, and the topics a character refuses to name need no extra vocabulary. They let the reader hear the character's wound in the way the character talks. [[wiki/Story Craft/Character Voice|Character Voice]]

## 5. Series structure

**Set up every payoff, and pay off every prominent setup.** The reader remembers a setup across the books in between only when a concrete object that appears repeatedly is attached to it. The payoff has an effect only when it touches a value the reader already holds. [[wiki/Story Craft/Seeding and Payoff|Seeding and Payoff]]

**First a quiet scene, then a stress scene.** Have a character reveal a confidence during quiet downtime, drawn out by a curious friend, one or two readings before it matters. Then use it in a later scene under stress, so the reader already knows the confidence when the story uses it. [[wiki/Story Craft/Companion Arcs and Party Banter|Companion Arcs and Party Banter]]

**Give each companion one scene that closes their arc, and never reset the relationship.** Give each round character one loyalty-mission payoff that changes the ending. Track each relationship as a running value, and do not let any scene set it back to zero because that is convenient. [[wiki/Story Craft/Companion Arcs and Party Banter|Companion Arcs and Party Banter]]

**Rotate focus among the recurring characters, and track it in a ledger.** Within a fixed span of readings, give every recurring character a moment that shows a strength and a moment that shows a flaw. Track this on a table so that no character goes a whole span without being shown. [[wiki/Story Craft/Spotlight Rotation|Spotlight Rotation]]

**A pairing works through contrast.** Build a foil who has something in common with the other character but acts by a different method. Then a single scene with only the two of them develops their relationship and reveals character at the same time. [[wiki/Story Craft/Foils and Pairings|Foils and Pairings]]

## 6. The emotional line

**Establish the value, then endanger it.** A threat affects the reader in proportion to how much the reader already cares. So build up the bond, the standing, or the self-image before the story puts it at risk. [[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]]

**Escalate by making the reader value the threatened thing more. Do not escalate by making the threat bigger.** Emotional and moral stakes keep their effect longer than physical stakes. When every threat is made life-or-death, no threat matters to the reader any more. [[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]]

**At the low point, the internal loss must seem final.** The loss that seems final is losing the Truth the character has just begun to see. So place the moment the character first sees the Truth before the last stand. A death can be one optional signal of the low point, and a death is never what makes the low point work. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Low Point and Catharsis|The Low Point and Catharsis]]

**Make the reversal and the recognition happen at the same moment.** Peripeteia and anagnorisis have the strongest effect when they happen together. Write the recognition as a character saying aloud a Truth that was set up books earlier, at a lower level. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Low Point and Catharsis|The Low Point and Catharsis]]

**Vary the tone across the series.** Never put three heavy readings in a row. Place a light moment near each heavy scene, so the reader takes the sadness as a deeper layer under a charming story and does not take it as the story's main tone. [[wiki/Story Craft/Tonal Modulation|Tonal Modulation]]

## 7. Tensions resolved

**Consistent yet surprising.** The specific details that let the reader know a character are also the details that make a late change in that character believable. So a surprise comes from something already present in the character that has not yet shown. It is not introduced from nowhere. [[wiki/Story Craft/Round Characters and the Telling Detail|Round Characters and the Telling Detail]]

**Set up early vs. do not signal it in advance.** A setup must stand out enough for the reader to remember it across a gap of four books. It must also be placed inside a joke or an object in regular use, so the reader treats it as background detail until it pays off. [[wiki/Story Craft/Seeding and Payoff|Seeding and Payoff]]

**Every character an arc vs. rotate the focus.** Only a limited number of characters can be round. Lead characters get contradictions, and flat supporting characters get one memorable trait each. If every character whose job is to provide a fixed contrast is also given a strength moment and a flaw moment, the cast becomes more than a reader can keep track of. [[wiki/Story Craft/Spotlight Rotation|Spotlight Rotation]]

**Show-don't-tell vs. a minimal tell.** Show-don't-tell is a technique to use when it helps, and it can be set aside when it does not. In the earliest books, one well-placed plain statement is the cheapest way to make sure the reader understands. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]]

**Flat character vs. flat arc.** Forster's flat character (one idea, cannot change) and Weiland's flat arc (a full interior that holds the Truth and changes the world around it) measure different things. Treating them as the same thing is the most common error in writing secondary arcs. [[wiki/Story Craft/Arc Types|Arc Types]]

**One premise vs. a multi-threaded series.** Keep the premise of the founding pair fixed. Choose the single sentence for the whole series later, on purpose. Run one main idea with named secondary storylines, and do not reduce every arc to one line. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]

**Omit vs. do not skip the work.** The iceberg method gives a world depth only when the author has fully worked out the material that is not shown. If the author leaves out material that was never built, the world seems vague. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Iceberg and World as Pressure|The Iceberg and World as Pressure]]

**Deepen before you add vs. err toward the awesome.** By default, develop the same few locations further and link them to each other. Set that rule aside only when a strong scene needs one new element. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Iceberg and World as Pressure|The Iceberg and World as Pressure]]

**Front-load for the median reader vs. serve the finisher.** Place the deepest culminating scenes late in the series, after most readers have stopped reading, and accept that those scenes reach the fewest readers. [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]]

**Small wins vs. the tidy victory.** End on a changed value the reader can name. Make a burden lighter and leave some of it in place, because a resolution that fixes everything cancels the emotional weight that the cost created. [[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]]

**Vary the tone vs. do not become predictable.** Change how many light moments come before each heavy moment, so the reader feels the relief and does not notice a fixed schedule. [[wiki/Story Craft/Tonal Modulation|Tonal Modulation]]
