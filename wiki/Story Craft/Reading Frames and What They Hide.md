# Reading Frames and What They Hide

A reading frame is a fixed list you score a character against, present or absent, and its strength is that it cannot be charmed. Every claim has to land on something visible on the page, which catches what affection would otherwise launder. Its blind spot is structural rather than a matter of tuning. A choice does not appear as a behaviour; it appears as an absence — the thing this person could have done, had every reason to do, and did not. A present-or-absent list scores that as nothing having happened, and comes back looking complete while the beat that mattered most went unrecorded.

The limit sits in the unit. Each behaviour becomes a symptom present or absent, so the frame has one axis, and a chosen restraint has nowhere to land.

## What the list catches, and what it files as further symptom

That missing landing-place is what the worked case shows. A filmed diagnostic reading of the 2003 pirate film and its sequels is the case. The inventory is real. He owes a debt; the coin he offers is another man's soul; asked if he can send a friend into lifelong service, he says yes. He owes money to every lord of that world, and to one more person he cheated out of a ship. A later film confirms the plans were made up as he went. Four shipmates have tried to kill him and one managed it; the locker rescue is not a reunion. Against the cluster — reckless, unreliable, careless of who gets hurt — each item is on-screen and correctly assigned. This page is not defending the pirate.

The read names its own boundary and steps over it. One could say the behaviour is just the genre. In the real world, the same behaviour is treated as a sign of the clinical label the source applied — antisocial personality disorder, named here as what the source used, never as a diagnosis of a fictional person or of a reader. A criterion list cannot hold "this is just how the genre dresses" as a rival account. Once a behaviour is admitted, it is evidence. The on-screen therapist would apply the villain diagnosis.

The same clip set already holds the four moments the list cannot file as restraint.

Told he cheated, the reply is one word: the label of the job. No denial, no justification, no charm. An accurate charge accepted. Hanging an opponent from a boom, with nothing left to explain, he states the rule anyway: the only rules that really matter are what a man can do and what a man cannot. The frame files that as not caring about law or ethics. The same clip is also a man speaking his held rule at the one instant every card is his.

Told he will discover he is a good man, the reply is that all evidence is to the contrary. Charm declined; the compliment refused on his own testimony. Asked his purpose, and no lies: he states the criminal intention in full and is disbelieved. The frame takes the speech as the mission statement of egocentrism. Nothing false is said. A slap later draws a concession of fault and no apology — the exact combination the remorse criterion, as the read scores it, cannot score. What *this* reading scores as missing remorse is that nobody heard sorry. A spoken sorry is not a conscience. Those two facts come apart here.

Four moments, already in the source's own selection, used for something else. The frame is not fabricating clips. It is filing them under the only axis it has.

## Change scored as remission

What the frame registers as zero growth — said of the first film — is a character who holds still. That stillness is the shape a **flat arc** takes when the story turns the man on the world rather than turning the world on him. "Zero growth" and a fixed point the world moves around are the same observation. Only the second names an arc type. [[wiki/Story Craft/Arc Types|Arc Types]] already carries the type: Truth held, turned on the world. A frame with one axis can register change only as remission.

The later films are the source's own evidence, not this page's plot summary. The read credits an arc accurately: a heart given up; immortality given up; later, a neck risked at a lighthouse and a family reunited. The explanation offered is desire for glory, and a speech treated as therapy with desire as the lever — those labels are the read's, not findings about that therapy. Underneath sits a spectrum model: every box ticked, and still enough want of contact left in him for it to show. That is a coherent account. It is also the only account a single axis can give.

The more heroic figure is judged a little bit less interesting. Moving a fixed point costs that, in the source's own aesthetic, not a later-retracted claim. Score a person as a pile of symptoms and there is no vocabulary left for stillness as design.

The diagnostic manual's remorse criterion is broader than the read's operationalization. The manual looks for indifference *or* rationalizing after hurting another, not only for a missing sorry. The read did not invent a sloppy test; it applied the manual's own surface. The fault is using that surface as a model of intention and of arc. Every behavioural observation survives. Only what they add up to changes. A criterion list is a weak model of intention.

## What a writer does with the second reading

The repair still uses the same evidence. Put the refusals next to the acts — what he turns down at a cost to himself — and the missing category returns. The best evidence for how a person works often sits inside a read that reached the opposite verdict, in the footage that read already picked. Turning something down while holding every advantage says more than an action taken under pressure. Same clips, second reading: declines the compliment, states the rule, takes the name.

[[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]] grades a *build* — eleven structural checks, not symptom load — so it does not inherit this fault. The word "diagnose" on that page is a build audit. A clinical frame on a person is a different instrument.

The transfer a writer can actually use is the anti-hero case. A flat arc whose Truth is disreputable still reads as no arc to a frame scoring improvement. Arc Types owns the type; this page owns that misread.

[[wiki/Story Craft/The Moral Core|The Moral Core]] holds that the audience believes the price, never the speech. The source contains one stated law and no scene charging it. What would charge that rule into a held code stays open. This page does not invent a beat to close it.

Roundness as the capacity to surprise convincingly is what a criterion list cannot score, because a convincing surprise reads as inconsistency to a checklist. Forster's test and the list collide; titles in Sources. [[wiki/Story Craft/Round Characters and the Telling Detail|Round Characters and the Telling Detail]] owns the surprise test.

[[wiki/Red Team/Applied Critical Thinking - Testing Frames|Applied Critical Thinking: Testing Frames]] gets a worked example for step two — identify the frame. [[wiki/Concepts/Bias and Framing|Bias and Framing]] is why a criterion list arrives with valence already attached. [[wiki/Concepts/Charisma|Charisma]] is the live disagreement this page does not settle: the source treats charm as buying exemption; that page's signalling account disagrees about what charm is for.

No more pirate plot. No lecture on the manual.

## The clips already had the choice

The list was always good at catching bad behaviour. The choice it missed was already inside the first reading's own clips: something the person could have done, at advantage, and did not. The repair does not need new evidence. It needs a second category for the evidence already on the table.
