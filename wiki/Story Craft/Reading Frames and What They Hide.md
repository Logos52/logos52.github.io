# When the Frame Cannot See the Choice: Diagnostic Reading of Character

## Core Thesis

A clinical frame reads behaviour against a fixed criterion list, and that discipline is what makes it useful on a character: it is immune to charm, it forces every claim back to something visible on screen, and it catches what an affectionate reading launders. Applied to Jack Sparrow across five films it produces an accurate inventory. The limit sits in the unit of analysis. A diagnostic frame converts each behaviour into the presence or absence of a symptom, so it runs on a single axis, more symptomatic or less, and a chosen restraint has nowhere to land: a rule stated at the moment of total advantage, a compliment declined, a charge accepted without defence all arrive as further evidence of disregard. What the frame registers as *a lack of remorse* is the absence of an apology. What it registers as *zero growth* is a character who holds still, which is the shape a flat arc takes when the story turns him on the world rather than turning the world on him. Reading the refusals alongside the acts — what a character declines when declining costs him something — restores the category the criterion list cannot carry, and it uses the same evidence the diagnosis was built from.

## Compressed Takeaways

- A criterion list is a strong evidence discipline and a weak model of intention: it grades behaviour, so anything only visible as a choice reads as a symptom.
- "A lack of remorse" is a behavioural finding about apology, not a finding about conscience. The two come apart on the page.
- A frame with one axis can register change only as remission.
- "Zero growth" and a fixed point the world moves around are the same observation. Only the second names an arc type.
- The strongest evidence for how a character actually operates is often already inside a read that reaches the opposite verdict, sitting in the clips it chose.
- A refusal made at the moment of advantage carries more information than an action taken under pressure, because nothing external forced it.

## What the criterion list catches

The read is precise where it is behavioural, and the clips carry it. A debt is owed to Davy Jones and the payment offered is someone else's soul; asked outright whether he can condemn a friend to a lifetime of servitude in his name, the answer is "Yep, I'm good with it." Money is owed to every Pirate Lord, and to one more person bait-and-switched out of a ship she was owed. Plans are confirmed in the fourth film to be improvisation. Four of a crew have tried to kill him and one succeeded, and nobody who comes to retrieve him from the locker missed him. Set against irresponsibility, impulsivity, and disregard for how outcomes land on other people, each of these is real, on-screen, and correctly assigned.

The read also names its own boundary and then steps over it. "You could say, Well, that's just a pirate. But in the real world, that is a sign of antisocial personality disorder" — the fictional register is raised and discharged in one breath, and the criterion list runs on. That move is where a diagnostic reading of character usually turns, because a criterion list has no way to hold "this behaviour is the genre's furniture" as a competing explanation. Everything admitted becomes evidence.

## The evidence the read passes over

Four moments appear in the source's own clip selection and are used for something else.

Told he cheated, the one-word reply is "Pirate." No denial, no justification, no charm deployed. The accurate charge is accepted and the label taken.

Hanging an opponent from a boom, in a position where nothing has to be explained to anyone, the rule gets stated anyway: "The only rules that really matter are these... What a man can do, and what a man can't do." The frame files this under indifference to legal and ethical behaviour. It is also a man saying out loud what he holds, at the one moment he holds every card.

Told "you'll discover something. That you're a good man," the reply is "All evidence to the contrary." A read watching for charm as a laundering device arrives at the moment charm is declined, and the compliment is refused on the character's own testimony.

Asked his purpose in Port Royal, with "and no lies" attached, he states his criminal intention in full and is disbelieved: "Unless, of course, he knew you wouldn't believe the truth, even if he told it to you." The frame takes this as the mission statement of egocentrism. Nothing false is said in it. A slap later draws "I may have deserved that," which concedes the fault and offers no apology — the exact combination the remorse criterion cannot score.

## Change read as remission

The read does credit an arc, and credits it accurately: the heart goes into Will's hand, the immortality is given up, and the later films show a man risking his neck in a lighthouse and reuniting a family. The explanation offered runs through the criterion list — the desire is for glory, for adulation, for what admiration tastes like, and the therapeutic reading of Elizabeth's speech is cognitive behavioural therapy with libido as the lever. Underneath sits a spectrum model: someone meets every criterion and still has enough capacity for connection buried in there for it to surface. That is a coherent account, and it is the only account the frame can give, because a single axis leaves change with one direction to move in.

The tell arrives in the aesthetic verdict that follows. The more heroic Jack is judged "a little bit less interesting" — which is what a fixed point becoming a moving one costs. A frame that scores character as symptom load has no way to say that the stillness was the design. The observations survive intact either way; the reading method decides what they add up to, and the same clips support a second reading in which the man declines the compliment, states the rule, and takes the name.

## Open Questions

- A rule stated once and never charged is not yet a code. What would have to appear on screen — a refusal that costs the character something he wants — to move it from stated to held?
- Does the flat-arc reading survive five films, or does it hold only for the first, where the read's own "zero growth" observation was scoped?
- Where is the honest boundary of a clinical frame on fiction? It catches laundering that a fond reading misses, and it converts genre convention into pathology. Which characters is it the right instrument for?
- Is the "less interesting" reaction a reliable signal that a fixed point has been moved, and can it be used deliberately as a diagnostic on one's own drafts?
