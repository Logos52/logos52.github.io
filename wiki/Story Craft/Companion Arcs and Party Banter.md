# Companion Arcs and Party Banter

Companion arcs and party banter are the two tools role-playing games use to make the people travelling with the player feel alive. A companion arc is one character's personal story inside the main plot. Party banter is the short talk between companions that depends on who is in the group at the time. Both show a writer how to give a large cast depth without giving each character a full plot.

## Core takeaways

- Give each companion one well-placed personal crisis.
- Tie that crisis to the main ending so it matters.
- Companions remember how they were treated and react later.
- Banter depends on which two companions are together.
- Short reactions to each other make companions feel alive.
- Plant the crisis early, in quiet scenes, so it lands later.

## One beat per companion

Mass Effect 2, a game from 2010, gives each companion a loyalty mission, a side mission about a private problem such as a family member or an old debt. Finishing it earns that companion's loyalty, and loyalty decides whether the companion is likely to survive the final mission. Each companion gets one such mission and no more, so each one has the player's full attention while it runs. One well-placed crisis costs far less writing than a companion story told across three acts.

- One private crisis per companion.
- The crisis connects to what is at stake in the ending.
- Which companion gets the spotlight when is a scheduling choice.
- Plant a hint early, then a spark, then the crisis.

## Approval that sticks

Baldur's Gate 3 and the Dragon Age games keep a running score, called approval, of how each companion feels about the player's choices. A companion remembers what the player did and brings it up later. High approval makes a later hard conversation easier to win. The conversation still happens, so the history shapes the scene without skipping it.

- Companions carry memory of past choices.
- Memory changes the odds in a later scene.
- Memory never cancels the scene outright.

## Banter keyed to the party

Party banter only plays when the right characters are travelling together. In Mass Effect 2 one companion will comment on another's private mission, which rewards the player for the group they chose. Companions need brief reactions to the people around them, or they do not read as alive. Pairs that contrast with each other give the best banter.

- Write banter for pairs of companions.
- Let companions react to each other's private stories.
- Keep each exchange short.
- Use quiet stretches to plant things a later crisis will use.

## Sources

- *Mass Effect 2* (2010). Loyalty missions and endgame survival; composition-keyed party banter (one companion reacting to another's private mission). The one-beat scarcity comes from this game, not a multi-act companion run.
- *Baldur's Gate 3*; the *Dragon Age* series. Approval as sticky memory: companions remember and react; high approval eases a later check and does not erase it.
- Chris Avellone, companion-reactivity interviews (GDC cluster). Brief reactions to the person they travel with, or they do not read as alive.
