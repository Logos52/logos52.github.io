# Story Craft

Story Craft gathers the writing methods for long serial character fiction under a vocabulary ceiling, chiefly [[wiki/Tsumugu/Tsumugu|Tsumugu]], where the Han word list is both a constraint and a reveal schedule. It covers plot and arc typing, ensemble design, non-lethal stakes, motif and payoff, world pressure, tonal sequencing, and two specialty shapes: the burnout arc and the reading-frame audit. The leaf pages linked in each section carry the full audits.

## Jack Sparrow’s entrance and reading frames

A first appearance can be a **template**: later scenes repeat and vary the same gap between presentation and fact. *Pirates of the Caribbean* builds Jack as grand captain, then shows the sinking dinghy. The distance drives the film. One therapist-led reading of Sparrow scores his antisocial traits fairly: debts, the soul offer, charm as cover. Yet the chosen clips also contain refusals that the one-axis list files as more harm. He accepts “Pirate” as an accurate label. He names what a man can and cannot do with a rival helpless. He rejects “good man” on his own evidence. He states his criminal plan at the dock and is not believed.

A **reading frame** is any fixed trait checklist. Strength: charm cannot sway marks that require visible acts. Weakness: restraint has no row, so refusals score as zero or as symptoms. Writers add a second column: **could have done, chose not**. Anti-hero code becomes visible there. Late sacrifices (the blade in a dying friend’s hand, giving up immortality) read as choices foreshadowed by early blunt honesty, not as symptom remission. The same audit applies to the grey cast in Tsumugu: track what 白龍 does not do with binoculars and what 沈文 does not ask for, before scoring harms.

Jack’s entrance is the blueprint pattern that [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]] checks for: impressive presentation, puncture, then the gap repeated in variations. His code is what he will pay for, and it shows in refusals and spends. That code is the moral core a story’s premise has to prove.

## Arc types at the crux

Arc **type** is decided at one moment: the **crux**, when the character cannot keep both the lie and the emerging truth. Five shapes share Lie, ghost (wound), want, need, truth, and normal world. They diverge only in the last act.

| Type | At the crux | End-state |
|---|---|---|
| Positive change | Rejects lie, takes truth, usually loses want | Changed, whole |
| Flat | Already holds truth, turns it on the world | World shifts, self steady |
| Disillusionment | Reaches truth, and the truth is bleak | Awake, still standing |
| Fall | Refuses truth, and the lie destroys them | Undone, worse |
| Corruption | Had truth, trades it for lie | Morally worse |

Type the **draft**, not the outline. A flat arc’s doubt is about the **cost** of holding truth, never about whether the truth is true. The Captain America trilogy shows the flat arc: the world changes, converts watch, and doubt is the price of the truth, not a question about it. Tragedy needs the truth **offered in view and refused**: “could have changed and refused.” Comedy and tragedy pair when you write the same crux twice with opposite last moves, because both run on one five-act shape. A **flat character** (one idea, no surprise) is not a **flat arc** (a truth-holder who converts the cast). A household cat as grace note may carry motif without arc, so do not “fix” it with fake interiority.

Ration types across the ensemble so early books anchor on a steadfast truth-bearer at beginner vocabulary. Negative arcs show externally until abstract emotion words arrive. **Disillusionment** holds bleak truth while standing, which makes it the lightest negative. **Corruption** is positive change run backward. A near-fall can show refusal without a shut door. Project canon may lack a completed fall or corruption, and the table is no reason to invent one.

Beat percentages (quarter, half, three-quarters) are scaffolding, not physics. Snyder’s All Is Lost sits slightly before 75%, the dark night is the reaction valley, and the Vogler and Campbell ordeal and belly beats are related, not identical. Crisis need not sit on the low point in every model. **Hamartia** is an error in action discovered late, not a fatal flaw labeled in advance.

## Stakes, low point, and catharsis

[[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]] sets the stakes rule for this section: establish bond, standing, or self-image, then risk it. McKee’s **gap** (expectation minus returned result) needs no corpse. Escalate by deepening value, not inflating danger. The low point’s felt finality is often losing the truth just grasped. The “whiff of death” is a relationship, place, or ghost object, not a body count. Catharsis pairs reversal and recognition when the page can hold both. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] owns beat cards, seeding, and breaking the story before prose.

## Motif, symbol, and payoff

A **motif** is concrete (a bowl, a route, a phrase) and returns in a harder scene than its first sighting. Meaning accrues on repetition with a changed emotional key, and explanation spends it. Eliot’s **objective correlative** (emotion carried by objects, a situation, a chain of events) and Chekhov’s rifle discipline both apply: plant as ordinary detail, fire in a charged return. The rifle line survives in two wordings, one of them secondhand, so do not merge them into one quotation. **Leitmotif** binds a tag to one character (voice texture, rank gag, four-syllable burst). Wagner popularized the term, and von Wolzogen’s guide coined the label.

Cast-sized examples (value level, not bible spoilers): 金多恩’s mended bowl with a visible seam, 阿迪’s batik streetwear carrying workshop legacy, 星野’s camera hoarding proof, 林薇’s red thread at 霞海城隍廟, 將軍’s service-branch rank joke, and 小圖’s sprout eyes as a growth tag. One signature object per major character. Two characters sharing one object collapse the distinction. Truby’s **symbol web** across hero, opponents and world is checked by that rule.

Ledger plants across books, and first-pass readers must still read each return as a plain event. Admired objects must act or be cut, because decoration breaks trust. When the feeling on the page exceeds the object carrying it, cut the naming sentence, add a charged return, or merge characters. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] also covers payoff for causes left dangling across gaps.

## World as pressure and setting

A setting **works like a character** when it presses differently on different people. 星野 hoards proof of belonging in Taiwan while 林薇 wants out of the same streets. Give the hub a **direction** (lease, season, owner exhaustion) legible through behavior, not lecture. Culture arrives as two or three seized particulars: the red thread at 霞海, the envelope as provision, keigo as assigned life. In an 1886 letter Chekhov has broken glass on the dam imply moonlight without saying “moonlit.” The famous “glint on glass” line is a later paraphrase, not his words.

**World as pressure** combines three ideas. Hemingway’s iceberg says to omit only what the author knows, and the page goes hollow when the author never built the mass underneath. McKee’s gap supplies the turn. Sanderson’s laws say limitation, weakness, and cost make a capability dramatic. They also say to expand existing locations before adding new ones, and to bend a rule toward an awesome beat rather than kill the scene. Cost hits the page as an object: cracked hands, not a speech about exhaustion. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] and [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] tie the object spine to the 字 ramp.

## The burnout arc

The **burnout arc** has no villain: love becomes work, joy drains, skill fails inwardly. *Kiki’s Delivery Service* is the reference. Flight and the cat’s voice are **gauges** for confidence the film never labels. When Kiki compares herself to fashionable town girls, the doubt stays small and everyday, which is how burnout reads without a lecture on pathology. Recovery needs rest offered by another and a reason for the work that belongs to the witch. Ursula’s painter mirror (blocked when copying others, returned when the style is hers) parallels Kiki’s comparison trap. Power returns clumsily under pressure to save someone cared for, with three bounces off walls before the push-off lands. A kind world keeps the obstacle inside the character, and the ending keeps some sadness after the rescue (the letter home: still sad, still loves the town). Map burnout onto cast members by choosing one outward skill that tracks inner state (binoculars, delivery speed, a photo hoard), and let kindness in the world prevent externalizing the loss into a beatable enemy.

## Tone across a run

**Tonal modulation** means ordering heavy and light readings across a **run** of installments, not inside single scenes. Three heavy installments in a row land the third on a spent reader. Comedy after the crest reads as relief, while comedy on a still-climbing beat steals weight. Under a vocabulary ceiling, humor travels on situation, gesture, and repetition (將軍’s rank gag, the binocular cover, a recurring phrase), not puns the first book cannot hold. Each heavy beat wants a **shine** nearby: warmth or competence, so sadness stays depth under charm. Tragicomedy keeps stakes real without pushing learners into dread that blocks input. Krashen’s compelling, low-anxiety input is a hypothesis, used here as a teaching constraint and not as a proven mechanism.

**Runs vs. scenes:** a single scene may be fully heavy. The failure is three heavies adjacent in the reading schedule. **Bottle vs. tentpole:** small lesson installments are the relief format, and higher-字 tentpoles detonate seeded arcs. Four tentpoles consume neighbor slots on both sides, so plan eight breathers per season. **Five-reading lessons:** one anchor (arc or theme), while texture readings carry the ensemble and the B-story against A gravity. **Unpredictability:** fixed light-heavy alternation becomes a schedule, so vary the count of warm beats before a drop.

Registers out of scope: unbroken horror dread and unbroken farce, because those genres commit to one pole on purpose. The lowest floor allows warm, warmer, and a hint of tender. Dread without feeling-words must use objects or wait for vocabulary.

## Ensemble, theme, and serialization

- [[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|The Character Web]]: one controlling question, competing value positions, foils, a spotlight ledger, and companion banter with an approval meter (borrowed from party-RPG craft, never a silent reset). Foils share a problem and take opposite routes, so one scene does two jobs.
- [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]: one provable sentence. The climax proves which value wins, and no character preaches the theme line.
- [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]]: breaking the story on cards locks the sequence before prose can protect a weak order.

**Structure for the ear:** in audio-first installments a character exists only while heard or named. Every exit is a disappearance from the record, so plan re-entries and tag voices to match. **Companion economics:** loyalty missions buy one closing beat using habits planted in calm. **Spotlight rotation** demands that each returning character show strength and price inside a counted span, so the ledger does not lie.

## Diagnostics and practice

- To audit a draft character, run [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]]. Its eleven checks turn this hub into tests: wound and lie spend, tell vs. show balance, rogue code visibility, and the entrance template.
- To use the merged leaf material in one pass, start with [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Craft, Condensed|Story Craft, Condensed]].
- To schedule words and coverage, open [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]].

**Practice loop for a new installment:**

1. State the premise sentence and the controlling value.
2. Assign an arc type only after a crux draft exists.
3. Plant one object per lead at floor vocabulary.
4. Mark gap beats, and establish the stake before the threat.
5. Schedule a tonal shine beside any tentpole.
6. Verify there are no merged-theme speeches and no false stakes reset.
