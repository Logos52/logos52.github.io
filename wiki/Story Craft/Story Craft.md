# Story Craft

## Jack Sparrow’s entrance and reading frames

A first appearance can be a **template**: later scenes repeat and vary the same gap between presentation and fact. *Pirates of the Caribbean* builds Jack as grand captain, then shows the sinking dinghy; the distance drives the film. Cinema Therapy’s Sparrow read scores antisocial traits fairly—debts, soul offer, charm as cover—yet the chosen clips also contain refusals the one-axis list files as more harm: “Pirate” as acceptance of an accurate label; naming what a man can and cannot do with a rival helpless; rejecting “good man” on his own evidence; stating his criminal plan at the dock and not being believed.

A **reading frame** is any fixed trait checklist. Strength: charm cannot sway marks that require visible acts. Weakness: restraint has no row—refusals score as zero or as symptoms. Writers add a second column: **could have done, chose not**. Anti-hero code becomes visible there; late sacrifices (blade in dying friend’s hand, giving up immortality) read as choices foreshadowed by early blunt honesty, not as symptom remission. Same audit applies to grey cast in Tsumugu: track what 白龍 does not do with binoculars, what 沈文 does not ask for, before scoring harms.

Jack’s blueprint matches [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Character Blueprint]]: impressive presentation, puncture, repeat the gap in variations. Pair with [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Moral Core]]: code is what they will pay for, shown in refusals and spends.

## Arc types at the crux

Arc **type** is decided at one moment: the **crux**, when the character cannot keep both the lie and the emerging truth. Five shapes share Lie, ghost (wound), want, need, truth, and normal world; they diverge only in the last act.

| Type | At the crux | End-state |
|---|---|---|
| Positive change | Rejects lie, takes truth, usually loses want | Changed, whole |
| Flat | Already holds truth; turns it on the world | World shifts, self steady |
| Disillusionment | Reaches truth; truth is bleak | Awake, still standing |
| Fall | Refuses truth; lie destroys them | Undone, worse |
| Corruption | Had truth; trades it for lie | Morally worse |

Type the **draft**, not the outline. A flat arc’s doubt is about **cost** of holding truth, never about whether truth is true—Captain America trilogy essays (Cinema On Trial, Implicitly Pretentious) are the flat-arc receipt: world changes, converts watch, doubt is price not truth. Tragedy needs the truth **offered in view and refused**—“could have changed and refused.” Comedy and tragedy pair when you write the same crux twice with opposite last moves (Yorke’s one five-act shape). **Flat character** (Forster: one idea, no surprise) is not **flat arc** (truth-holder who converts the cast). Household cat as grace note may carry motif without arc—do not “fix” with fake interiority.

Ration types across the ensemble so early books anchor on a steadfast truth-bearer at beginner vocabulary; negative arcs show externally until abstract emotion words arrive. **Disillusionment** holds bleak truth while standing—lightest negative; **corruption** is positive change run backward; near-fall can show refusal without shut door. Project canon may lack completed fall/corruption; do not invent to fill the table.

Beat percentages (quarter, half, three-quarters) are scaffolding, not physics—Snyder’s All Is Lost slightly before 75%; dark night is reaction valley; Vogler/Campbell ordeal/belly are related, not identical. Crisis need not sit on the low point in every model. **Hamartia** is late-discovered error in action, not pre-labeled fatal flaw.

## Stakes, low point, and catharsis

[[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]] is the house rule: establish bond, standing, or self-image, then risk it. McKee’s **gap**—expectation minus returned result—needs no corpse. Escalate by deepening value, not inflating danger. The low point’s felt finality is often losing the truth just grasped; the “whiff of death” is a relationship, place, or ghost object, not a body count. Catharsis pairs reversal and recognition when the page can hold both. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] owns beat cards, seeding, and breaking the story before prose.

## Motif, symbol, and payoff

A **motif** is concrete—a bowl, route, phrase—returning in a harder scene than its first sighting. Meaning accrues on repetition with changed emotional key; explanation spends it. Eliot’s **objective correlative** (emotion via objects, situation, chain) and Chekhov’s rifle discipline apply: plant as ordinary detail, fire in a charged return—1889 Lazarev letter for the rifle; 1904 Gurlyand “first act… fired” is secondhand, do not merge quotations. **Leitmotif** binds a tag to one character (voice texture, rank gag, four-syllable burst). Wagner popularized the term; von Wolzogen’s guide coined the label.

Cast-sized examples (value level, not bible spoilers): 金多恩’s mended bowl with visible seam; 阿迪’s batik streetwear carrying workshop legacy; 星野’s camera hoarding proof; 林薇’s red thread at 霞海城隍廟; 將軍’s service-branch rank joke; 小圖’s sprout eyes as growth tag. One signature object per major; two characters sharing one object collapse distinction. Truby’s **symbol web** across hero, opponents, world is checked by that rule.

Ledger plants across books; first-pass readers must still read each return as a plain event. Admired objects must act or cut—decoration breaks trust. Fail Eliot’s test when feeling exceeds the correlative: cut the naming sentence, add charged return, or merge characters. Payoff discipline overlaps [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|Seeding and Payoff]] on dangling causes across gaps.

## World as pressure and setting

A setting **works like a character** when it presses differently on different people—星野 hoards proof of belonging in Taiwan while 林薇 wants out of the same streets. Give the hub a **direction** (lease, season, owner exhaustion) legible through behavior, not lecture. Culture arrives as two or three seized particulars: red thread at 霞海, envelope as provision, keigo as assigned life. Chekhov’s 1886 letter: broken glass on the dam implies moonlight without saying “moonlit”; the famous “glint on glass” line is later paraphrase, not his words.

**World as pressure** fuses Hemingway’s iceberg (omit only from knowledge; hollow when the author never built the mass), McKee’s gap, and Sanderson’s laws: limitation, weakness, and cost make capability dramatic; expand existing locations before adding new ones; bend a rule toward an awesome beat rather than kill the scene. Cost hits the page as object—cracked hands, not a speech about exhaustion. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] and [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] tie object spine to the 字 ramp.

## The burnout arc

The **burnout arc** has no villain: love becomes work, joy drains, skill fails inwardly. *Kiki’s Delivery Service* is the reference—flight and the cat’s voice are **gauges** for confidence the film never labels. When Kiki compares herself to fashionable town girls, the doubt stays small and everyday; that is how burnout reads without pathology lecture. Recovery needs rest offered by another and a reason for the work that belongs to the witch; Ursula’s painter mirror (blocked when copying others, returned when style is hers) parallels Kiki’s comparison trap. Power returns clumsily under pressure to save someone cared for—three bounces off walls before the push-off lands. A kind world keeps the obstacle inside the character; the ending keeps some sadness after the rescue (letter home: still sad, still loves the town). Map burnout onto cast members by choosing one outward skill that tracks inner state—binoculars, delivery speed, photo hoard—and let kindness in the world prevent externalizing the loss into a beatable enemy.

## Tone across a run

**Tonal modulation** sequences emotional register across a **run** of readings, not single scenes. Three heavy installments in a row land the third on a spent reader. Comedy after the crest reads as relief; comedy on a still-climbing beat steals weight. Under a vocabulary ceiling, humor travels on situation, gesture, and repetition—將軍’s rank gag, binocular cover, recurring phrase—not puns the first book cannot hold. Each heavy beat wants a **shine** nearby: warmth or competence so sadness stays depth under charm. Tragicomedy keeps stakes real without pushing learners into dread that blocks input (Krashen’s compelling/low-anxiety constraint—hypothesis, cited as pedagogy here, not proven mechanism).

**Runs vs. scenes:** a single scene may be fully heavy; the failure is three heavies adjacent in the reading schedule. **Bottle vs. tentpole:** small lesson installments are relief format; higher-字 tentpoles detonate seeded arcs—four tentpoles consume neighbor slots on both sides, so plan eight breathers per season. **Five-reading lessons:** one anchor (arc/theme), texture readings carry ensemble and B-story against A gravity. **Unpredictability:** fixed light-heavy alternation becomes schedule; vary count of warm beats before drop.

Registers out of scope: unbroken horror dread, unbroken farce—those genres commit to one pole on purpose. Lowest floor allows warm, warmer, hint of tender; dread without feeling-words must use objects or wait for vocabulary.

## Ensemble, theme, and serialization

[[wiki/Story Craft/The Character Web|The Character Web]] — one controlling question, competing value positions, foils, spotlight ledger, companion banter and approval meter (borrowed from party-RPG craft, never silent reset). Foils share a problem and take opposite routes so one scene does two jobs. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]] — one provable sentence; climax proves which value wins; no character preaches the theme line. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|Breaking the Story]] and beat boards lock sequence on cards before prose protects weak order.

**Structure for the ear:** in audio-first installments a speaker exists only while heard or named; every exit is disappearance from the record—plan re-entries and tag voices accordingly. **Companion economics:** loyalty missions buy one closing beat using habits planted in calm; **spotlight rotation** demands each returnee show strength and price inside a counted span so the ledger does not lie.

## Diagnostics and practice

[[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]] turns doctrine into eleven checks—wound/lie spend, tell vs. show balance, rogue code visibility, entrance template. When merged leaf material is needed in one pass, start [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Craft, Condensed|Story Craft, Condensed]]; when auditing a draft character, run Diagnosing; when scheduling words and coverage, open Vocabulary Ceiling.

**Practice loop for a new installment:** (1) state premise sentence and controlling value; (2) assign arc type only after crux draft exists; (3) plant one object per lead at floor vocabulary; (4) mark gap beats and stake establishment before threat; (5) schedule tonal shine beside any tentpole; (6) verify no merged-theme speeches and no false stakes reset.
