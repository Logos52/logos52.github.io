# The Moral Core

A character's moral core is the short list of rules they will actually pay for. These are not the values they would state if asked. They are the rules that hold when holding them costs something.

Each rule on the list has a history. Something in the character's past put it there, and that forging is what makes the rule read as the character's own instead of as costume. The second half is the cost. A rule that is never charged against anything the character loves stays decoration, so the arcs that install a core are followed by arcs that test it. What the reader believes at the end is the price that was paid.

## Core takeaways

- A moral core is the short list of rules a character will actually pay for. It is not the list they would recite if asked.
- The arcs forge the core. Later arcs test it by charging the rule against the thing the character loves most.
- A test that charges the law against something the character does not care about proves nothing.
- A core spoken aloud works only when the events that paid for it came first. Otherwise the speech is a soapbox.
- The eight shapes are diagnostic labels. Write the choice under pressure first, then name the shape.
- The mechanism does not care whether the law is good. The audience believes a villain's price the same way.

## How a core is forged and tested

The arcs forge the core, and later arcs test it. The forging arcs are the specific events that put a law into a person and make it cost something. The testing arcs charge that law against the thing the person loves most. The audience believes the price, never the speech.

True character shows only in choices made under pressure. The moral argument lands as event, never as dialogue. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]] is the story-level law those events argue.

The raw material of the forge is the Ghost → Lie → Want → Need chain. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]] is that chain. A Ghost is an event, never a mood. What the broken Lie leaves behind, hardened, is the core. [[wiki/Story Craft/Arc Types|Arc Types]] sort by where the forging happens. A change arc forges on the page, and [[wiki/Story Craft/The Change Arc|The Change Arc]] is that case. A **flat arc** starts with the core already formed, and the story spends its length on the test.

One forge looks like this. A childhood in a Hanoi alley where things simply lived. Adults named the gift in her hands. *Everything alive is kept alive by love* is the shape that childhood left. The test aims the cost at what that law was built to protect. Tending stops being delight and becomes the thing she owes, and the hand that grips can no longer make anything grow. A test that charges the law against something the character does not care about proves nothing.

The same three questions work on a person as on a character. Which law has actually been paid for. What event forged it. What test would price it today, aimed at the thing one would least like charged.

## The eight shapes

Most people, and most characters, run one primary shape with a secondary underneath. The names are diagnostic labels. They are not instructions for writing a character. Write the choice under pressure first. Name the shape after.

The eight shapes are a diagnostic menu. They are not a complete typology.

**The Steadfast**. A limit that does not move. Native test: the mission at the price of that limit.

**The Idealist**. A belief about what people can be, held against the evidence. Native test: an invitation to give up on someone.

**The Oathkeeper**. A vow that holds whatever the conditions. Native test: two oaths collide.

**The Steward**. Holds power for someone else's future and wants none of it. Native test: letting the student walk into the fire.

**The Tender**. Keeps living things alive and counts that as love. Native test: the reason turns from delight into obligation. This is the house shape of the alley-and-hands example above. The provider-wall reading is retired.

**The Maker**. Responsibility for what the hands made, forever. Native test: an offer to take the making off those hands.

**The Redeemer**. Forges a core late, against the record. Native test: the old life back at a discount.

**The Hidden Law**. Cynicism worn over one unbreakable rule. The character would rather be thought lawless than be caught keeping it. Native test: the day the mask costs more than the law. The nearest neighbouring shape is a different thing. Hidden Law conceals a rule. The rogue states his rule and refuses only the credit, and [[wiki/Story Craft/The Rogue's Code|The Rogue's Code]] covers that distinction. A stock smuggler reading sits in the Hidden Law column as a pointer.

The eight shapes do not cover **payment as a permanent condition**. That gap stays open and is not forced into the list.

## When a character says the core out loud

A law is real only when events make the character pay for it. A core stated aloud is a soapbox, unless every line of the speech was already paid for on screen. A speech like that is a receipt. A spoken core lands when the events come before the speech.

One rebellion-series monologue itemizes prices already paid, and the speaker's own agent pulls it out of him. That monologue is the exception to the rule against saying the core aloud. The same payment takes another form when a life is spent in advance for a sunrise the payer will not see. There is no witness and no receipt.

A core that never pays is decoration. Virtue that is never charged for reads as flat.

## What a character will subordinate a law to

What a character will subordinate a law *to* is as defining as the law. A core has a hierarchy, and that hierarchy is visible only at the top of the price range. An honour-bound lord bends his rule once to save his daughters, and the bending gains nothing.

## Other cases that carry weight

A change arc shows the forging with the camera left running. The longest modern version on screen follows a thief all the way to a last flight to an island, made without pay. By that point the core decides what he does. A shot he does not take counts as part of the payment.

One law is to compromise where you can, and where you cannot, do not. It was forged before any serum. The candidate was selected by a question that asked whether he wanted to kill. The law was later priced against the family and paid with the shield. The selection question selects for the core, and the enhancement only amplifies it.

In another case oaths collide. The outsiders are let through. The payment arrives at the hands of the character's own men.

In another, kindness to broken things sits under cynicism, and a trial strips the mask off. The test is every unwatched day on the road.

A steward takes the generalship his doctrine forbids and names the failure. He then meets a second test that inverts the native one: the student walks into the fire.

## Villains run the same loop

The loop is amoral machinery. The audience believes a villain's price by the same mechanism. A villain's version adds one of three things: a test failed, a hierarchy surrendered, or a law whose villainy lives in its content.

A competent commander spends honor on somebody else's evil. Civilian dead are the price of a promotion. His reason is that the galaxy must be strong enough for what waits in the dark.

A house-first patriarch treats the house as outliving the individual. His children test him. He fails, and the bolt is the verdict on his law.

## The Tsumugu cast

[[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]] is the project these cores belong to. It is a project note, not a craft page. Six characters form the class in the first band, plus the robot, and each has one core. [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]] is the audit that uses them. The portraits do not verify the laws.

沈文. Freedom over control, presence over provision: keep the human hand on the wheel, show up, let what you built go free. Forged by a childhood of provision without presence. Tested by every offer to trade the hand-built for the convenient.

阮草. Everything alive is kept alive by love. Forged by that Hanoi childhood and the cool hand. Tested by whether love survives becoming a living.

星野. A life is authored, even when assigned. Forged by the un-burdensome daughter and a father greying into the assigned life. Tested by an offer to help build another version of the life she fled.

金多恩. Keep what deserves keeping and shed what shackles: mend honestly, never counterfeit. Forged by a death that made speed look like a lie. Tested by being right at a price her own people pay.

阿迪. The old survives by living inside the new, and no one is left outside the table. Forged by family cloth and money owed home. Tested by the one binary that will not dissolve.

白龍. No invisible hand gets to move a family unwatched, and whoever sees the drift is obliged to speak. Forged by an automation decision that ended six generations of keeping. Tested by whether he ever transmits.

小圖. Care, not capability: the care is the self, and the capability is copyable. Forged by a running experiment. The test is whether that care still holds when the experiment is no longer the only way to be useful.

The supporting cast runs its own cores.

A whole cast of granite is a sermon. Use one shape per lead. Supporting characters can run on wants alone. Archetype names invite the other failure, which is casting a Steadfast instead of forging one.

## Three ways the loop fails

The cost has to hit what the character loves, or it is not a test. Costume and decoration are still two of the ways the loop fails. The third is a test aimed at the wrong thing.

## How to practice this

1. Name the one law your character has actually paid for. Check whether any event on the page charged it. If none did, the law is still decoration.
2. Write the event that forged that law. Check that it is an event, never a mood. The law should now read as the character's own instead of as costume.
3. Aim the test at what the character loves most. Notice that a test aimed at something they do not care about proves nothing.
4. Write the choice under pressure first, then name the shape. Watch for casting a Steadfast instead of forging one.
5. Find every speech where a character states the core. Check that each line was already paid for on screen. Cut the speech or pay for it first.
6. Run the three questions on yourself. Which law have you actually paid for. What event forged it. What test would price it today.
