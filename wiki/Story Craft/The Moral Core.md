# The Moral Core

A character's moral core is the short list of rules they will actually pay for — not the values they would state if asked, but the ones that hold when holding them costs something. Each rule on the list has a history: something in the character's past put it there, and that forging is what makes it read as theirs rather than as costume. The other half is the bill. A rule never charged against anything the character loves stays decoration, so the arcs that install a core are followed by arcs that test it. What the reader believes at the end is the price that was paid.

## The loop

The loop runs both ways. The arcs **forge** the core — they are the specific events that price a law into a person. Later arcs **test** it — they send the bill to the thing that person loves most. The audience believes the price, never the speech.

True character shows only in choices made under pressure. The moral argument lands as event, never as dialogue. [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]] is the story-level law those events argue.

The raw material of the forge is the Ghost → Lie → Want → Need chain. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]] is that chain: a Ghost is an event, never a mood, and what the broken Lie leaves behind, hardened, is the core. [[wiki/Story Craft/Arc Types|Arc Types]] sort by where the forging happens. A change arc forges on the page — [[wiki/Story Craft/The Change Arc|The Change Arc]] is that case. A **flat arc** walks in already forged; the story spends its length on the test.

One forge, self-carried. A childhood in a Hanoi alley where things simply lived. Adults named the gift on the hands. *Everything alive is kept alive by love* is the shape that childhood left. The test aims the bill at what that law was built to protect: tending stops being delight and becomes the thing she owes, and the hand that grips can no longer make anything grow. A test that charges the law against something the character does not care about proves nothing.

The same three questions work on a person as on a character. Which law has actually been paid for. What event forged it. What test would price it today, aimed at the thing one would least like billed.

## Finding one

Most people, and most characters, run one primary shape with a secondary underneath. The names are diagnostic labels, never instructions for writing one. Write the choice under pressure first. Name the shape after.

Eight shapes is a diagnostic menu, not a complete typology.

**The Steadfast** — a line that does not move. Native test: the mission at the price of the line.

**The Idealist** — a belief about what people can be, held against the evidence. Native test: an invitation to give up on someone.

**The Oathkeeper** — a vow that outranks weather. Native test: two oaths collide.

**The Steward** — holds power for someone else's future and wants none of it. Native test: letting the student walk into the fire.

**The Tender** — keeps living things alive and counts that as love. Native test: the why curdles from delight to obligation. This is the house shape of the alley-and-hands illustration above. The provider-wall reading is retired.

**The Maker** — responsibility for what the hands made, forever. Native test: an offer to take the making off those hands.

**The Redeemer** — forges a core late, against the record. Native test: the old life back at a discount.

**The Hidden Law** — cynicism worn over one unbreakable rule; would rather be thought lawless than caught keeping it. Native test: the day the mask costs more than the law. The nearest neighbour is not the same object. Hidden Law *conceals* a rule. The rogue *states* his and refuses only the credit. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Rogue's Code|The Rogue's Code]] owns that seam. A stock smuggler reading sits in the Hidden Law column as a pointer, not as an argument made here.

## The audience believes the price

A law is real only when events price it. A core stated aloud is a soapbox — unless it is a **receipt**: a speech whose every line was already paid for on screen. A spoken core lands when the events precede the speech. One rebellion-series monologue itemizes prices already paid, pulled out of the speaker by his own agent. That is the exception that proves the gag order. Another shape of the same payment: a life burned in advance to make a sunrise the payer will not see — no witness, no receipt.

A core that never pays is decoration. Virtue without a bill is the flattest thing on a page.

## What the list misses, and the other side

What a character will subordinate a law *to* is as defining as the law. A core has a hierarchy, visible only at the top of the price range. An honour-bound lord bends the line once to save his daughters, and the bending buys nothing.

A change arc is a forge with the camera left running. The longest modern version on screen walks a thief all the way to a last island flown unpaid. By then the core is load-bearing. A shot not taken is part of the receipt.

The eight-shape menu misses **payment as a permanent condition**. That miss stays a miss. It is not forced into the list.

Other load-bearing cases, described by kind, titles in Sources. Compromise where you can; where you cannot, do not — forged before any serum, selected by a question that asked whether the candidate wanted to kill, later priced against the family, paid with the shield. The selection question selects for the core; the enhancement only amplifies it. Oaths collide; the outsiders are let through; payment arrives at the hands of one's own men. Kindness to broken things under cynicism; a trial strips the mask. The test is every unwatched day on the road. A steward takes the generalship his doctrine forbids, names the failure, and meets a second test that inverts the native one: the student walks into the fire.

The loop is amoral machinery. The audience believes a villain's price by the same mechanism. What the other side adds is a test failed, a hierarchy surrendered, or a law whose villainy lives in its content. A competent commander spends honor on somebody else's evil, with civilian dead riding on a promotion, because the galaxy must be strong enough for what waits in the dark. A house-first patriarch treats the house as outliving the individual, is tested by his children, fails, and the bolt is the law's verdict.

## The cast, and the case against

[[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]] is the project these cores belong to — a project note, not a craft page. Six who form the class in the first band, plus the robot: one core each. [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]] is the audit that uses them. The portraits do not verify the laws. Each row below is self-carried.

沈文. Freedom over control, presence over provision — keep the human hand on the wheel, show up, let what you built go free. Forged by a childhood of provision without presence. Tested by every offer to trade the hand-built for the convenient.

阮草. Everything alive is kept alive by love. Forged by that Hanoi childhood and the cool hand. Tested by whether love survives becoming a living.

星野. A life is authored, even when assigned. Forged by the un-burdensome daughter and a father greying into the assigned life. Tested by an offer to help build another version of the life she fled.

金多恩. Keep what deserves keeping and shed what shackles — mend honestly, never counterfeit. Forged by a death that made speed look like a lie. Tested by being right at a price her own people pay.

阿迪. The old survives by living inside the new — and no one is left outside the table. Forged by family cloth and money owed home. Tested by the one binary that will not dissolve.

白龍. No invisible hand gets to move a family unwatched, and whoever sees the drift is obliged to speak. Forged by an automation decision that ended six generations of keeping. Tested by whether he ever transmits.

小圖. Care, not capability — the care is the self; the capability is copyable. Forged by a running experiment. The test is whether that care still holds when the experiment is no longer the only way to be useful.

The supporting cast runs its own cores and gets its own pass. This page does not carry that pass.

A whole cast of granite is a sermon. One shape per lead. Supporting characters can run on wants alone. Archetype names invite the other failure: casting a Steadfast instead of forging one.

## Close

The bill has to hit what they love, or it is not a test. Costume and decoration are still the two ways the loop fails. The third is a test aimed at the wrong thing.
