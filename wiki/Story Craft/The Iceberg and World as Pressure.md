## Sources

- Ernest Hemingway, *Death in the Afternoon* (1932). The iceberg paragraph: omit from knowledge; hollow places from ignorance; one-eighth above water.
- Brandon Sanderson, First, Second, Third, and Zeroth Laws. Free essays at [brandonsanderson.com](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-second-law) (Second Law: limitations > powers). First Law: satisfaction tracks how well the reader already understands the resolving rule. Third: expand before add. Zeroth: err toward what is awesome.
- Robert McKee, *Story* (1997): the gap.
- Anton Chekhov, letter to Alexander Chekhov, 10 May 1886: seize a few concrete details. The "glint of light on broken glass" line is a later paraphrase (Gurlyand / later craft books), not the 1886 wording.
- Ursula K. Le Guin: one to three economic or occupational details per place; Gont's sheep and Lorbanery's silk in the Earthsea novels; method discussed in her craft essays and interviews adjacent to *Steering the Craft*.
