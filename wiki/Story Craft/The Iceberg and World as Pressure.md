# The Iceberg and World as Pressure

A story world does its work by pressing on the people inside it. The pressure narrows what they can want, what they can do, and what they can say. Two disciplines produce it.

The first is omission. Most of what a writer builds stays off the page, and the reader feels it anyway. Omission works only from knowledge, because leaving out something you never worked out makes a hollow place rather than a submerged one. The second is limitation. A world becomes interesting through what it cannot do and what it costs, rather than through what it can do. Explanation spends both disciplines.

## Core takeaways

- A story world limits what the people inside it can want, do, and say. That limiting is the work the world does.
- Omission pays only when the writer knows the omitted material exactly and every object on the page obeys it. Otherwise the gap reads as a hollow place.
- A capability is dramatic because of its limitation, its weakness, and its cost. A capability with none of the three stops generating conflict.
- Cost reaches the page as an object, not as a speech.
- A crisis feels satisfying in proportion to how well the reader already knows the rule that resolves it.
- Deepen the places that already exist before adding new ones. Bend a rule when it would otherwise kill a beat that matters.

## Leaving things out only works from knowledge

Hemingway wrote in 1932 that a writer who knows enough may leave out what they know, and that a reader who is being written to truly will feel the omitted mass as if it had been stated. He pictured the mass by how it moves: seven parts hidden, one part showing. Treat that fraction as a feel-test. Almost everything built never reaches the page.

Omission works only from knowledge. Leaving out what was never worked out makes hollow places, his name for the hole the reader can feel. The iceberg here is that discipline: most of what the writer knows stays off the page, and the reader feels the mass only if the writer actually knows it. Withholding a fact you hold is not the same as stacking unexplained puzzles. A mystery box is a hole the writer hopes to fill later. With an iceberg the writer already has the mass.

The test before a cut is three questions the scene does not state. What did this person last eat. Who do they owe. What are they not saying, and why. Those three questions come from here, not from Hemingway.

[沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html) is the form built correctly: an unstated history present only in what he does not say and the objects he does not offer. The contents of that history are not given here. His silence should already be doing work before any scene stops to explain it. A later paragraph of backstory is the proof the mass was never there. The price is that the author holds the full hidden history and keeps it consistent for as long as the story runs.

## The setting forces the choice

The setting is not a backdrop. Place acts. It corners people until they choose, and the choosing is the reveal. McKee's gap is the space between what a person expected and what the world returned. The forces of antagonism open that space, and the space reveals them. His book is listed under Sources.

World-as-pressure is the name for the fusion of that submerged mass with the gap and with the ordinary craft move of a setting that counts down. The name is a house fusion, not a citation.

## Cost reaches the page as an object

Cost reaches the page as an object, not a speech. [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html)'s provision is kitchen hours and a forcing hand. The hands that used to be careful are cracked and burned from the grind. The pressure generates the character, and the object carries it. A reader should register the cost from the hands alone, before any line states she is exhausted. That object-and-action spine, under a vocabulary floor, is [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]].

## Using both disciplines together

Build seven-eighths underwater. Make a world, a capability, or a culture interesting by what it cannot do and what it costs. That applies past magic and is not reserved for a system of spells. A narrator who stops to describe the world has already lost the force the world was for. Description is the rival approach: richness as proof of a world. The iceberg is knowledge withheld, not a stack of boxes the writer has not opened yet. The plant that teaches a rule so a later crisis can spend it is previewed only; [[wiki/Story Craft/Seeding and Payoff|Seeding and Payoff]] owns that spine. [[wiki/Story Craft/Setting as Character|Setting as Character]] owns the hub as an entity that wants and resists.

## What a capability cannot do, and what it costs

What a power refuses, and what it charges per use, is what makes it dramatic. What it can do is the least interesting part. That is the Second Law. Three knobs sit under it. Limitation is what it cannot do. Weakness is what using it exposes. Cost is the price per use.

The knobs travel past magic. Money, status, a lie, fluency, and a language barrier all take the same three. House instances run at value level. The graded cast is bounded by the few words the current book allows. The vocabulary ceiling is an in-world constraint, which is the limitation, and [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] is the page that makes the ceiling itself the iceberg. [星野遙香](/tsumugu/cast/hoshino-haruka.html)'s keigo is a language built to keep her in her place. Every polite form re-inscribes the office-lady future she dreads. That is weakness. [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html)'s provision costs rest and presence. Every hour earning is an hour not spent with the people who need her. That is cost: tender, grind, no scam.

A capability with no limitation, weakness, or cost stops generating conflict. Resolutions then read as unearned.

## The other three laws

First Law. How well the reader already knows the resolving rule is how satisfying the crisis feels. A rule that appears only at the save reads as the world cheating. The plant is how the reader is taught the rule, and that join is Seeding again, not a recap of it.

Third Law. Deepen and cross-link what already exists before introducing anything new. One café table, one cat ([將軍](/tsumugu/cast/general.html)), one street, each return thicker than the last, rather than a new set for each book. One street and one café are enough. Private rooftops are not load-bearing here.

Zeroth Law. If a rule is about to kill a beat that matters, bend the rule toward the beat. The setting is never the reason the page exists. [小圖](/tsumugu/cast/xiao-tu.html) was made to care rather than to be able, and that invariant is what keeps the bend from becoming a cheat. The spoiler worked examples that once sat under Zeroth and First stay cut.

## Culture through small concrete details

Treat the recurring hub as an entity that shifts, wants, and presses. Setting as Character owns directional force. 珍貓 already carries three pressures a reader can watch: [吳老闆](/tsumugu/cast/boss-wu.html) is tired, the café barely breaks even, and it was his late wife's dream. Those three lean toward closure. They do not assert an ending. Dialogue capped at three or four speakers still lets the countdown read.

Chekhov wrote to his brother in 1886: seize a few small concrete details and let the reader assemble the picture. The famous "glint of light on broken glass" is a later paraphrase, not his words. Grouped in the wordlist, two or three are enough: who fights to pay the bill, shoes at the door, what is left on the plate. [林薇](/tsumugu/cast/lin-wei.html)'s 月老 and the red thread at 霞海城隍廟 teach folk culture as a thing she does. The temple is a real public place, and her practice is private. Le Guin used one, two, or three particulars per place, usually how people earn a living, and let conduct follow that living: sheep on Gont, silk at Lorbanery. 將軍's rank gag smuggles old-school military vocabulary through behavior.

## Where this fails

Skip the submerged work and the withhold just looks like dodging. A big withheld fact only pays if it is already known, exactly, and every object on the page obeys it. Building limits first slows the hour and can leave the story without enough force. Three-knob discipline is a diagnostic, not a mandate to weaken everything.

This is wrong for a travelogue, for some science fiction where the idea is the payload, and for comedy that runs on set-piece invention. Call a place a character and then hang a city name and a drink on it, and the place is costume: no behavior, no money logic. The full costume kit lives on Setting as Character.

## Signs to stop, and what to do instead

A payoff that needs a paragraph of fresh backstory means the iceberg was hollow. When beta readers ask what the character's problem is, put the cost back on an object. When a reader asks where that came from, plant the rule earlier. When deepening the same locations reads as claustrophobic, add the one new element and err toward the beat that matters.

## What the squeeze costs the author

The world still earns its place only by pressing. The press now has a price: a hidden history kept consistent, novelty traded for the same street, and a standing permission to bend a rule toward the beat that matters. The author builds and maintains far more than ships. The Third Law trades novelty for continuity. The First Law's teaching reads as slow. The world is still not the point.

## How to practice this

1. Before a cut, answer three questions the scene never states. They are: what this person last ate, who they owe, what they are not saying and why. If you cannot answer them, the omission will read as a hollow place.
2. Take one capability in the draft and name its limitation, its weakness, and its cost. Notice whether any of the three is missing. A capability with none of them stops generating conflict.
3. Find a cost the draft states in a line of speech and move it onto an object. Check that the reader registers the cost from the object alone. The stated feeling should come after it, or not at all.
4. Find the rule that resolves your crisis and the place the reader was taught it. If it appears only at the save, plant it earlier. Notice whether the resolution then reads as earned.
5. Before adding a new location, return to one you already have and make it thicker. Notice whether the return carries more than the last one. If the deepening reads as claustrophobic, add one new element.
6. Give one place one to three particulars about how people earn a living. Let conduct follow that living. Notice whether the reader assembles the picture without a description of the world.
