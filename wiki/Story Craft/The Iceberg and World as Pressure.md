# The Iceberg and World as Pressure

A story world does its work by pressing on the people inside it, narrowing what they can want, what they can do, and what they can say. Two disciplines produce that pressure. The first is omission: most of what you have built stays off the page and the reader feels it anyway. That only works from knowledge, because leaving out something you never worked out makes a hollow place rather than a submerged one. The second is limitation — a world becomes interesting through what it cannot do and what it costs, rather than through what it can. Explanation is the thing that spends both.

## Omit from knowledge

Hemingway, 1932: a writer who knows enough may leave out what they know, and a reader who is being written to truly will feel the omitted mass as if it had been stated. He pictured the mass by how it moves: seven parts hidden, one part showing. Treat that fraction as a feel-test. Almost everything built never reaches the page.

Omission works only from knowledge. Leaving out what was never worked out makes **hollow places** — his name for the hole the reader can feel. The **iceberg** on this page is that discipline: most of what the writer knows stays off the page, and the reader feels the mass only if the writer actually knows it. Withholding a fact you hold is not the same as stacking unexplained puzzles. A mystery box is a hole you hope to fill later. An iceberg is a mass you already have.

The test before a cut is three questions the scene does not state. What did this person last eat. Who do they owe. What are they not saying, and why. Those are this page's three, not his.

[沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html) is the form built correctly: an unstated history present only in what he does not say and the objects he does not offer. The contents of that history stay off this page. Silence should already be doing work before any scene stops to explain it. A later paragraph of backstory is the proof the mass was never there. The price is the author holding the full hidden history and keeping it consistent for as long as the story runs.

## The world returns a different result

The setting is not a backdrop. Place acts. It corners people until they choose, and the choosing is the reveal. McKee's **gap** is the space between what a person expected and what the world returned; the forces of antagonism open that space, and the space reveals them. Title in Sources.

**World-as-pressure** names the fusion of that submerged mass with the gap and with the ordinary craft move of a setting that counts down. The name is a house fusion, not a citation.

Cost reaches the page as an object, not a speech. [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html)'s provision is kitchen hours and a forcing hand; the hands that used to be careful are cracked and burned from the grind. The pressure generates the character. The object carries it. A reader should register the cost from the hands alone, before any line states she is exhausted. That object-and-action spine, under a vocabulary floor, is [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]].

## What the two disciplines are for together

Two disciplines, held together. Build seven-eighths underwater. Make a world, a capability, or a culture interesting by what it cannot do and what it costs — generalized past magic, not reserved for a system of spells. The narrator who stops to describe the world has already lost the force the world was for. Description is the rival reading from the opening: richness as proof of a world. The iceberg is knowledge withheld, not a stack of boxes the writer has not opened yet. The plant that teaches a rule so a later crisis can spend it is previewed only; [[wiki/Story Craft/Seeding and Payoff|Seeding and Payoff]] owns that spine. [[wiki/Story Craft/Setting as Character|Setting as Character]] owns the hub as an entity that wants and resists.

## What it cannot do, and what it costs

What a power refuses, and what it charges per use, is what makes it dramatic. What it can do is the least interesting part. That is the Second Law. Three knobs sit under it. **Limitation**: what it can't do. **Weakness**: what using it exposes. **Cost**: the price per use.

The knobs travel past magic. Money, status, a lie, fluency, a language barrier all take the same three. House instances, at value level. The graded cast is bounded by the few words the current book allows; the **vocabulary ceiling** is an in-world constraint, which is the limitation, and [[wiki/Story Craft/Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling|Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling]] is the page that makes the ceiling itself the iceberg. [星野遙香](/tsumugu/cast/hoshino-haruka.html)'s keigo is a language built to keep her in her place; every polite form re-inscribes the office-lady future she dreads — that is weakness. [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html)'s provision costs rest and presence; every hour earning is an hour not spent with the people who need her — that is cost, tender, grind, no scam.

A capability with no limitation, weakness, or cost stops generating conflict. Resolutions then read as unearned.

**First Law.** How well the reader already knows the resolving rule is how satisfying the crisis feels. A rule that appears only at the save reads as the world cheating. The plant is how the reader is taught the rule; that join is Seeding again, not a recap of it.

**Third Law.** Deepen and cross-link what already exists before introducing anything new. One café table, one cat ([將軍](/tsumugu/cast/general.html)), one street, each return thicker than the last — not a new set for each book. One street and one café are enough. Private rooftops are not load-bearing here.

**Zeroth Law.** If a rule is about to kill a beat that matters, bend the rule toward the beat. The setting is never the reason the page exists. [小圖](/tsumugu/cast/xiao-tu.html) was made to care rather than to be able — that invariant is what keeps the bend from becoming a cheat. The spoiler worked examples that once sat under Zeroth and First stay cut.

## Culture, costume, quit

Treat the recurring **hub** as an entity that shifts, wants, and presses. That sentence is a pointer. Setting as Character owns directional force. 珍貓 already carries three pressures a reader can watch: [吳老闆](/tsumugu/cast/boss-wu.html) is tired, the café barely breaks even, it was his late wife's dream. Those three lean toward closure. They do not assert an ending. Dialogue capped at three or four speakers still lets the countdown read.

Chekhov, 1886, to his brother: seize a few small concrete details and let the reader assemble the picture. The famous "glint of light on broken glass" is a later paraphrase, not his words. Grouped in the wordlist, two or three are enough: who fights to pay the bill, shoes at the door, what is left on the plate. [林薇](/tsumugu/cast/lin-wei.html)'s 月老 and the red thread at 霞海城隍廟 teach folk culture as a thing she does; the temple is a real public place, her practice is private. Le Guin: one, two, or three particulars per place, usually how people earn a living, and let conduct follow that living — sheep on Gont, silk at Lorbanery. 將軍's rank gag smuggles old-school military vocabulary through behavior.

Skip the submerged work and the withhold just looks like dodging. A big withheld fact only pays if it is already known, exactly, and every object on the page obeys it. Building limits first slows the hour and can leave the story without enough force. Three-knob discipline is a diagnostic, not a mandate to weaken everything.

Wrong for a travelogue, for some science fiction where the idea is the payload, for comedy that runs on set-piece invention. Call a place a character and then hang a city name and a drink on it, and the place is costume: no behavior, no money logic. The full costume kit lives on Setting as Character.

Price, in the same breath as the benefit. The author builds and maintains far more than ships. Third Law trades novelty for continuity. First Law's teaching reads as slow.

Quit. A payoff that needs a paragraph of fresh backstory: the iceberg was hollow. Beta readers ask what the character's problem is: put the cost back on an object. "Where did that come from": plant the rule earlier. Deepening the same locations reads as claustrophobic: add the one new element, and err toward the beat that matters.

## What the squeeze costs the author

The world still earns its place only by pressing. The press now has a price: a hidden history kept consistent, novelty traded for the same street, and a standing permission to bend a rule toward the beat that matters. The world is still not the point.
