# Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling

A vocabulary ceiling is a fixed word list the story may not exceed. Treat it as a **reveal schedule**: what the reader is about to learn and what a character is about to admit can land in the same beat.

Want, obstacle, reversal, and change are not words. A complete arc fits inside the first few hundred characters of running text, and interiority arrives at the rate the list grows. [[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]] maps five book floors (250, 450, 750, 950, 1000 字) onto a public language ladder. Marquees, deep two-handers, and specials are exempt from the floor while still obeying peak coverage.

## Coverage and the affective load

University fiction studies put adequate unassisted comprehension near 98% of running words known, and gist-with-effort near 95% (roughly one unknown word in twenty). Those percentages tell a writer whether a reader can follow the scene, not whether the reader will memorize every new word. In practice, on a forty-word page, three new items already dip under 95%.

A reader decoding hard words during a hard beat learns neither language nor story, so **peak beats need the highest coverage** (near 98%, spending new words in calm beats instead). Comprehensible input (i+1), compelling input, and low affective filter are contested hypotheses, yet the quitting behavior still holds: hard decoding during a hard beat leaves nothing retained.

## What is not made of words

**Gap** is expectation minus result, staged in actions. **Premise** is one arguable sentence *X leads to Y*, held fixed across books so plants at 250 字 can pay off in words that did not exist yet (see [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]). **Bone structure** (physiology, sociology, psychology) is a separate tool from premise.

The **story circle** (comfort, want, threshold, search, find, pay, return, change) nests from scene to season. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]] splits Lie, Want, and Need across the ramp, putting Want early and Need late.

## Floors and scheduling

| Book band | Floor (字) | What language affords |
|---|---|---|
| First | 250 | Actions, objects, and stance in behavior |
| Second | 450 | Reasons and sequence |
| Third | 750 | Named wants and thicker motive |
| Fourth–Last | 950–1000 | Feeling, abstraction, and sayable Need |

Putting the core emotional turn before the end of the third book reaches the median reader, while holding the deepest interior reflection for later books means fewer readers will reach it.

Working structure first means the last-book payoff is known before the first-book page. [沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html) and [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html) at 250 字: flinch versus envelope counted, hand refused, with provision-love legible before theme-words exist.

## Where new words go

First book: one character's load shown as counting, and another's as refusal. Second book: motives stated when the grammar arrives. **Circling** in prose means key objects appear plainly in quiet beats so they are known before the turn. Withholding a word until its book floor arrives can turn vocabulary timing into the reversal itself.

Hold peaks near 98% known words. Concrete objects stand in for feelings the floor forbids, and wounds can stay off-page if an object carries them. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] is the full object-and-gesture discipline.

Wants must be folktale-strength (hunger, promise, hidden gift). This project's first-book load-bearers are chosen family and secret provision, both visible at the floor.

Plants must be **behaviors** the later book can re-show, never stated themes. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] owns PLANT, SPARK, and PAYOFF across the ramp.

## Structure for the ear (audio-first)

In recording, a character exists only while speaking or being named, because silent bodies on page are absent on the ear. Audio production for [[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]] follows four rules:

- **Sequence** is one informative sound (often one line or one effect), so trim arrival chains to the knock at the door.
- **Speaker cap** sets six voices as the hard ceiling for a stranger listening to a half-hour drama, with **three to four per scene** as the working norm for graded readings.
- **Vary four dials** (sequence length, speaker count, pace, and location) before recording so the writer does not fix flat pacing afterward.
- **Runtime** is measured by reading the script aloud against a clock rather than by page count.
- **Cold listen** tests whether a listener can assign every line without the script, the same check as tag-stripping in [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]].

Podcast chapter-lift percentages without methodology are not used as targets.

## Costs and failures

Every repeated noun is priming tax. Ceilings suit stories whose depth lives in behavior and want, rather than dense monologue or register irony.

Common failures include worksheet prose (rebuild from want), placing a new word on the turn (move it to a calm beat), stating a first-book hint as a theme (replace it with a re-showable behavior), hollow omission (write the wound even if it never ships), and exceeding four speakers (split crowded rooms across short readings).

A well-placed tell ("she was tired") can beat hollow inference. Omission works only when the author knows the unsaid eighth.

## How to practice this

1. Count unknown words on one page, and notice any peaks below 95%.
2. Introduce new words in calm openings rather than on emotional turns.
3. Front-load key nouns in quiet scenes so readers recognize them later.
4. Verify that first-book plants are behaviors rather than stated themes.
5. Write submerged wound notes, then re-read to cut empty omission.
6. Count speaking roles, and split crowded scenes across readings.

## Open questions

- Whether 98% as a peak-scene target survives larger samples.
- Whether marquees with no floor still honor one unknown in twenty on run-ups.
