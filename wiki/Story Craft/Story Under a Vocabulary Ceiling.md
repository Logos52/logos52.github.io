# Story Under a Vocabulary Ceiling

A vocabulary ceiling is a fixed word list the story may not exceed. Treat it as a **reveal schedule**: what the reader is about to learn and what a character is about to admit can land in the same beat.

Want, obstacle, reversal, and change are not words. A complete arc fits inside the first few hundred characters of running text. Interiority arrives at the rate the list grows. [[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]] maps five book floors (250, 450, 750, 950, 1000 字) onto a public language ladder; marquees, deep two-handers, and specials are exempt from the floor but still obey peak coverage.

## Core takeaways

- Hold scene peaks near **98%** known words. Spend new words in calm beats, not on the turn.
- Below **~95%** (one unknown in twenty), decoding competes with feeling and readers quit.
- Want is sayable early in concrete nouns (envelope, umbrella, empty chair). Need waits for later vocabulary.
- Withholding a word until its level arrives *is* the reversal.
- Audio-first work: three to four speaking voices per reading; crowded rooms become many short readings.

## Coverage and the affective load

University fiction studies put adequate unassisted comprehension near 98% of running words known; gist-with-effort sits near 95%. Numbers are design guidance for *comprehension*, not a promise of acquisition. Practical rule: on a forty-word page, three new items already dip under 95%.

A reader decoding hard words during a hard beat learns neither language nor story. **Peak beats need the highest coverage.** Comprehensible input (i+1), compelling input, and low affective filter are contested hypotheses; the quitting behavior still holds: hard decoding plus hard beat → nothing retained.

## What is not made of words

**Gap** is expectation minus result, staged in actions. **Premise** is one arguable sentence *X leads to Y*, held fixed across books so plants at 250 字 can pay off in words that did not exist yet — see [[wiki/Story Craft/Premise and Controlling Idea|Premise and Controlling Idea]]. **Bone structure** (physiology, sociology, psychology) is a separate tool from premise.

The **story circle** (comfort, want, threshold, search, find, pay, return, change) nests from scene to season. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Wound and the Lie|The Wound and the Lie]] splits Lie / Want / Need across the ramp: Want early, Need late.

## Floors and scheduling

| Book band | Floor (字) | What language affords |
|---|---|---|
| First | 250 | actions, objects; stance in behavior |
| Second | 450 | reasons, sequence |
| Third | 750 | named wants, thicker motive |
| Fourth–Last | 950–1000 | feeling, abstraction, Need sayable |

Core emotional material before end of third book reaches the median reader; deepest interiority is read by the fewest. That is a priced choice.

Working structure first means the last-book payoff is known before the first-book page. [沈文](/tsumugu/cast/shen-wen.html) and [阮草](/tsumugu/cast/ruan-cao.html) at 250 字: flinch versus envelope counted, hand refused — provision-love legible before theme-words exist.

## Where new words go

First book: one character's load shown as counting; another's as refusal. Second book: motives stated when the grammar arrives. **Circling** in prose: key objects appear plainly in quiet beats so they are known at the turn.

Peak near 98%. Objects stand in for feelings the floor forbids. Wounds can stay off-page if an object carries them. [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] is the full object-and-gesture discipline.

Wants must be folktale-strength (hunger, promise, hidden gift). This project's first-book load-bearers: chosen family and secret provision — both visible at the floor.

Plants must be **behaviors** the later book can re-show, never stated themes. [[wiki/Story Craft/The Beat Board|The Beat Board]] owns PLANT → SPARK → PAYOFF across the ramp.

## Structure for the ear (audio-first)

In recording, a character exists only while speaking or being named. Silent bodies on page are absent on the ear. Consequences for [[projects/tsumugu|Tsumugu]]:

- **Sequence** is one informative sound (often one line or one effect); trim arrival chains to the knock at the door.
- **Speaker cap**: six voices is a stranger-heard half-hour drama ceiling; **three to four per scene** is the working norm for graded readings.
- **Variety** on four dials: sequence length, speaker count, pace, location — revision check before record, not post-hoc diagnosis.
- **Runtime** from read-aloud against a clock, not page count.
- **Cold listen**: assign every line without the script; same test as tag-stripping in [[wiki/Story Craft/Diagnosing a Character|Diagnosing a Character]].

BBC radio-play guidance supplies the load-bearing rules (silent presence, six-cap, sequence unit, read-aloud clock). Podcast chapter-lift percentages without methodology are not used as targets.

## Costs and failures

Every repeated noun is priming tax. Ceilings suit stories whose depth lives in behavior and want, not dense monologue or register irony.

Failures: worksheet prose (rebuild from want); new word on the turn (move to calm); first-book hint as stated theme (replace with re-showable behavior); hollow omission (write the wound even if it never ships); more than four speakers (rotate cast across readings).

A well-placed tell ("she was tired") can beat hollow inference. Omission works only when the author knows the unsaid eighth.

## How to practice this

1. Count unknowns on one page; notice sub-95% peaks.
2. Move new words out of the turn into calm beats.
3. Front-load key nouns in quiet openings.
4. Verify first-book plants are behaviors, not themes.
5. Write submerged wound notes; re-read for empty omission.
6. Count speakers; split crowded scenes across readings.
