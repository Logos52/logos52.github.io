# The Rogue's Code

## Sources

- *A Gest of Robyn Hode*, printed around 1500: the plough-ban and the ban list, quoted from the original text at Harvard's Geoffrey Chaucer site (https://chaucer.fas.harvard.edu/gest-robyn-hode-original); also edited in the METS volume *Robin Hood and Other Outlaw Tales*, and catalogued as Child ballad 117.
- *Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl* (2003) and *Dead Man's Chest* (2006): the worked example, meaning the five dialogue moments, the salute, the debt scene, and the field of trading company, navy, and letters of marque.
- "Psychology of an Anti-Hero: JACK SPARROW," Cinema Therapy, 2023: the dialogue compilation, the in-world reading of how the other characters treat him, and the clinical frame this page declines. The decline is argued on Reading Frames and What They Hide. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iUN6d7ur5Dg
- "You don't realize how genius Jack Sparrow's introduction is," From The Screen, 2026: the salute's recognition reading and the presentation/reality collision. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lMTdmEW08v8
- Encyclopaedia entries for gentleman thief, outlaw, picaresque novel, and trickster, with the quoted definitions attributed there: the social-bandit definition is Eric Hobsbawm's (*Bandits*, 1969), with the note that the broader thesis has been contested in the historical literature; the trickster's "boundary-crosser" is Lewis Hyde's, and the disruption-and-re-establishment line Paul Mattick's.
- Honest dishonesty and comparative forgiveness are this vault's own thesis (2026-07-27), not a finding from any source.
