## Sources

- Anton Chekhov, letter to his brother Alexander, 10 May 1886. Seize small details; broken bottle glass on a mill dam gives a moonlit night without the phrase.
- The later "don't tell me the moon is shining; show me the glint of light on broken glass" is a widely circulated paraphrase, not a letter. Mechanism his; wording not his.
- Brandon Sanderson, "Sanderson's Third Law of Magic" (2013), brandonsanderson.com. Expand what you already have before you add something new; depth over breadth, applied to worldbuilding in the same essay.
- Ursula K. Le Guin, Earthsea, especially the labor-geography of Gont, Lorbanery, and Havnor, and the craft essays collected in *The Language of the Night* and later afterwords. Culture from how people make a living.
- Ernest Hemingway, *Death in the Afternoon*, and the 1958 *Paris Review* interview. Omission only from knowledge; a reader senses the vacuum.
- Robert McKee, *Story*. Antagonism opening a gap between expectation and result. Used here only as one half of the house fusion world-as-pressure.
