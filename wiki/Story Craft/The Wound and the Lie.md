# The Wound and the Lie

Interiority runs on one causal chain: a past event, the false belief it installed, the external goal produced by this belief, and the inner truth this goal blocks. The chain pays nothing for existing in a notebook. It starts earning at the point where it bends a decision a reader is watching happen, under pressure, at a cost to the character making it. Where a story is written to a fixed word list a learner can read without a dictionary, that reader's vocabulary sets the clock for the whole run, so a scene near the bottom of the ladder and a scene near the top of it name different amounts of the same chain.

## The four links

The first link is the Ghost, one specific past event, nameable in a single sentence: the shop that burned, the parent who left on a Tuesday morning, four years of being fed and clothed and never once asked a question. A sad childhood in general is not a Ghost, because a mood has no shape that a later scene can press against.

The Lie is what the event installed — a belief about the world or about the self the character has never examined and acts on anyway. A Lie its owner can recite on request has already been examined, which is why the Lie is legible from choices long before it is legible from anything said.

The Want is where the Lie sends them, concrete enough to be photographed and counted: the debt cleared, the shop reopened. The Need is the truth they have to accept before anything changes, and going after the Want is what keeps the Need out of reach. A Want that turns out to be the Need in outdoor clothes leaves nothing to fight over — the goal arrives, the truth arrives with it, and no arc has happened.

**A backstory that never distorts a decision in the scene at hand is a fact about a character, not a Ghost.** Per scene, the same test asks what in the scene presses on the false belief. A scene pressing on nothing may be doing good work of some other kind; it is not doing arc work.

## The fear behind the mask

Two links sit between the Lie and anything a reader can see, and they are the ones usually skipped. The Lie produces a fear, nameable in one clause: the thing that must never happen, or the state that must never be true of them. Against the fear the character builds a persona, a settled way of being in a room that keeps the feared thing at a distance. The persona is the outer trait a reader meets on the first page. Underneath it is the essence, which is the Need in everyday clothes, and the arc is the persona giving way to the essence.

That middle pair is what makes a defining contradiction derivable rather than invented. A defining contradiction is one outer trait visibly at odds with one inner truth, and it makes a lead legible within a few lines. Chosen for effect, it stays a taste judgment. Derived from a named fear, it can be checked back against the fear that produced it, and the derivation also runs backwards from a trait already written:

```text
trait on the page     over-fills every broke student's cup, swears the extra scoop was an
                      accident, has no idea what to do when someone looks after him
fear it holds off     being the one who needs looking after
Lie behind the fear   care taken is a debt owed
Need underneath       to be looked after anyway
```

The café owner in that derivation is [吳老闆](/tsumugu/cast/boss-wu.html), a widower in his early sixties who feeds first and asks questions after, and both halves are on his card: the red-bean bun nobody ordered, and the blank spot where being cared for should go.

Those same specifics do a second job later. A character counts as round when a surprise from them also convinces, and the details that made someone knowable early are the only material a late turn can be built from. A turn a reader cannot find in hindsight had no latency behind it, and a surprise that fails to convince marks a flat character dressed as a round one.

## When the flaw turns moral

The weakness doubles along the same chain. A psychological weakness costs only its owner: no day off in a year, cracked hands, the drink taken alone. A moral weakness costs other people, and it is usually the same trait pointed outward, since the wall that keeps one person self-sufficient is the wall somebody else is standing outside.

The derivation runs one way. The psychological weakness comes first, the immoral action falling naturally out of it is where the moral weakness lives, and the moral need is whatever that action makes necessary. A story carrying a moral need has an argument to make, and the argument is settled by a decision rather than announced in a speech: pressure forces the revelation, and the revelation shows up as a changed act. Character shows in choices made under pressure, and the greater the pressure the deeper the revelation, which is why a confession in a calm room is weaker evidence than a small decision made in a bad one.

**The argument reaches a reader through what someone does with an object, never through a line about it.**

## One question, several engines

An ensemble is not a set of chains running in parallel. It is one moral question with a different answer per member, and the frictions between the answers are value-clashes: friction produced by two answers to the same question rather than by a disagreement about facts. Two answers colliding in a single scene expose two interiors at once, which is how an ensemble pays for itself under a word ceiling.

Depth is a budget. A flat character is recognizable the moment they enter and memorable afterwards, and that is the whole of what the form asks of them. [將軍](/tsumugu/cast/general.html), the café's ginger tom, carries exactly one note: the group treats his name as a military rank and keeps a standing argument going about which branch he served in. He has no lines anywhere, and the note still makes him recognizable on entry and quotable after. Depth not spent on him stays available for the two or three leads whose chains are load-bearing.

## The reveal clock

The governing constraint is the word list, and it decides when each link can be said out loud. One widely used ladder runs 250, 400, 700, 1,000, 1,400, 1,800 and 2,500 headwords across seven levels; comprehension research treats 95% coverage of the running words as gist-with-effort and 98% as reading that flows. The ceiling forces the order everything else follows.

| Headword band | What the words afford | What the chain can show | What waits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 250–400 | actions, objects, concrete nouns | behaviour bent by the Lie: money counted twice, a flinch away from warmth | any statement of cause |
| 700–1,400 | reasons, causes, sequence in time | backstory explaining itself; the Want named out loud | the Need |
| 1,800–2,500 | feeling, abstraction, inner states | the Need sayable; self-revelation landing in words | nothing |

So the Want and the Need split across the ramp on their own: the Want is sayable from the first band, in nouns and verbs a learner already holds, while the Need waits for the top of the ladder, which puts self-revelation next to a reader's peak vocabulary. Where exactly an abstract Need turns sayable has never been measured. Abstract and affective vocabulary is lower-frequency and therefore later, which makes the direction safe and the specific band a guess until a reading — one lesson-length text a learner finishes in a sitting — has been tried on learners at that level.

Silence is the exception to the whole schedule, since what a character never says costs no vocabulary at all. It comes in two kinds, and treating them as one instrument wastes the better half. Deliberate withholding is topic-avoidance, the short non-answer, the turn back to the weather; a reader files it as somebody hiding something. The unsayable is what the character cannot name even privately, because it sits below their own awareness, and that is where the Lie lives. Both cost zero words, and only the second one keeps the wound invisible while it is being shown.

Voice runs on the same free budget. An idiolect is one speaker's total speech variety — habitual sentence length, tics, register — and it lets a reader name the speaker with the tags stripped off. [白龍](/tsumugu/cast/bai-long.html)'s card gives one-word replies (嗯) in a slow, flat bass; [星野遙香](/tsumugu/cast/hoshino-haruka.html)'s gives quick up-tilting exclamations tumbling over each other with 超 in front of everything. Neither fingerprint costs a headword, and both of them survive with the dialogue tags removed.

Payoffs need seeding on the same clock: PLANT → SPARK → PAYOFF, where the fact is planted quietly early, sparked once in passing so a reader keeps hold of it, and paid off at the beat that needs it. The confiding scene is a two-hander, a scene with exactly two speakers and nobody else in the room, and it sits well ahead of the crisis it feeds. Audio drama sizes those scenes at three or four voices, with four to five the point where listeners start losing the thread, and gives group scenes a dominant speaker plus frequent name-checks.

## What the chain costs

Every engine bills its setup before it pays anything. One or two two-handers ahead of the arc beat, an object carrying the Need for the levels where no words exist for it, and a persona that has to visibly tighten under pressure at least once so the later crack has something to have grown from. Those slots compete with shine — the beat where a character is good at the thing they are good at, in front of the others — and with the ordinary banter that makes a group worth reading.

The arithmetic decides how many engines a cast can hold: scenes per unit, speakers per scene, engines per lead. Running every member as a full chain puts the plant slots over the ceiling first, and the visible symptom is a cast where everyone is confiding and nobody is doing anything.

The deferred payoff is the heavier cost. A single prop carries the Need across several levels, which is a long time for an object to hold something the text is not yet allowed to say, and each level that passes without a fresh spark loses some of the reader's grip on it.

## The case against

Taken all the way down, the chain turns into an equation with a solution, and a solved character stops reading as a person. A lead whose every trait traces cleanly back to one wound is an explanation, and explanations do not surprise anybody: what convinces on the page needs slack the derivation cannot supply.

Two failure modes come out of trusting the machine to do the work a choice is supposed to do. The first spends the reveal on a sentence:

```text
Weak:   "Ever since my father left, I can't trust anyone."
Strong: he counts the change twice, every time, and folds the receipt into his wallet.
```

The second collapses the Want into the Need and removes the conflict the distinction exists to protect.

The chain also assumes a shape of arc that is one of three. A flat arc holds a truth the character already has and changes the world around it instead; a negative arc runs as disillusionment into a bleaker truth, as a fall, or as corruption. Some designs deliberately stop short: a real gain lands while the underlying crisis stays open, and forcing a clean self-revelation onto one of those over-resolves the arc and cheapens the cost a reader watched accumulate.

## Quit signals

- A wound that never changes what its owner chooses in the reading at hand is not load-bearing there, and one flat memorable note buys the same presence for a fraction of the slots.
- A wound surfacing only when somebody narrates it is a wound the level cannot carry yet, so object and action hold it until the naming moves up the ladder.
- A Want and a Need landing in the same sentence mean the Need is mis-specified, since the Want is the concrete reachable thing and the Need is what chasing it blocks.
- A payoff landing flat is usually a missing plant rather than a weak beat.

## Checkable expectations

- A learner at the earliest band, given only a character counting an envelope and turning down a free meal, can say she is carrying something alone before any sentence says so, and a reader who cannot has been shown behaviour too quiet to read.
- A reveal carried by an object the reader watches lands its full weight in the beat afterwards, and a line needed to make it land means the plant failed upstream.
- Dialogue tags stripped from an early group scene still leave most speakers nameable from voice alone, and tags doing real work in that test mean the voices have drifted toward one shape.
- Early chapters re-read after a late turn show it already latent in the small specifics, and a turn reading as a cheat on the second pass had no latency behind it.

Between them those four tests settle whether the engine is running where a reader can see it. The level settles the rest: which link is sayable at all today, which one an object has to carry until the ladder reaches it, and how many engines a cast can afford to run at once.
