## Sources

- Eysenck, M. W., Derakshan, N., Santos, R., & Calvo, M. G. (2007). Anxiety and cognitive performance: Attentional control theory. *Emotion*, 7(2), 336–353. Anxiety impairs the goal-directed attentional system, and relational and shifting work is impaired first. Tunnel vision is the folk name for that narrowing.
- Hembree, R. (1988). Correlates, causes, effects, and treatment of test anxiety. *Review of Educational Research*, 58(1), 47–77. Test anxiety reliably lowers exam scores.
- Newbury, C. R., Crowley, R., Rastle, K., & Tamminen, J. (2021). Sleep deprivation and memory: Meta-analytic reviews of studies on sleep deprivation before and after learning. *Psychological Bulletin*. Memory consolidation takes place during sleep on the night before. Later recovery sleep reduces the cost but does not remove it, and it cannot restore exam-day performance in time.
- Walker, M. P., & Stickgold, R. (2004). Sleep-dependent learning and memory consolidation. *Neuron*, 44(1), 121–133.
- Orr, W. C., Shadid, G., Harnish, M. J., & Seneviratne, S. (1997). Sleepiness and performance after a solid meal versus water. *Physiology & Behavior*. A solid meal decreases sleep-onset latency compared with water.
- Cleveland Clinic. Postprandial somnolence: sleepiness after meals usually peaks one to two hours later and is stronger after larger, higher-energy meals. Clinical summary, not a trial.
- Ariga, A., & Lleras, A. (2011). Brief and rare mental "breaks" keep you focused: Deactivation and reactivation of task goals preempt vigilance decrements. *Cognition*, 118(3), 439–443. Brief, rare, actively controlled disengagements maintain vigilance.
- Dewar, M., Alber, J., Butler, C., Cowan, N., & Della Sala, S. (2012). Brief wakeful rest after learning enhances memory. *Psychological Science*. Ten minutes of quiet wakeful rest after learning improved retention.
- Zaccaro, A., et al. (2018). How breath-control can change your life: A systematic review on psycho-physiological correlates of slow breathing. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 12, 353. Slow or cyclic breathing can prime a calm, alert state. No branded method is required.
