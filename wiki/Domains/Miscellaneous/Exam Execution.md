# Exam Execution

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

Exam execution is the late part of the learning system, when the material is already learned and the remaining work is to perform on the day. Sleep the night before comes first, because what you studied stays unusable until deep sleep moves it into longer-term memory. Calm comes next, since anxiety narrows attention until an obvious answer vanishes. Inside the paper, priority checking and a time budget per question protect the marks you can already earn. Four methods then cover the late-stage work, which are writing questions you cannot yet answer, structuring long answers, fixing mistakes that get called silly and studying in a group with set roles.

## Sleep, calm and pacing in the paper

The night before an exam belongs to sleep, since studied material stays shut away until deeper sleep carries it into higher-level memory. Cramming and all-nighters have no place on that night. A full night also protects problem-solving, and sleep loss can cost as much as 20 IQ points.

Calm is the next thing to protect, because anxiety produces the tunnel vision that makes an obvious answer disappear. A long, slow exhale of about 10 seconds triggers the rest-and-digest response and turns down fight-or-flight. Nerves that appear only in high-pressure sittings respond to practice under more and more exam-like pressure beforehand, and a written pre-exam checklist keeps stress from erasing these moves. Last-minute studying at the exam-room door does little to help.

Once the paper starts, the same calm carries into how you spend time on it. Checking every answer the same way wastes time and raises the chance of flipping a right answer to a wrong one, so the working method is priority checking. You keep a steady pace, mark the shaky items and return only to those. A hard question gets a best attempt and a star once it runs past its time budget, because coming back later often opens it up.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Breaching Questions|Breaching Questions]] finds the gaps that remain late in learning, including the unknown gaps you cannot feel. You write the hardest question you can on each weak spot, one you cannot answer yet, and the effort of answering it closes the gap.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Deep Processing/ReCOVer System|ReCOVer System]] plans an exam essay in four steps called Recall, Chunk, Order and Verbalise. The flow of ideas is planned before any sentences get written.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Regulation/Silly Mistake Syndrome|Silly Mistake Syndrome]] treats a mistake called silly as a sign of a gap in understanding or application. It traces the root cause, and for attention that slips under pressure it offers a sequence of five steps named MR FIG.

[[wiki/Dimensions/Retrieval/Group Study|Group Study]] gives a study group three roles, which are leader, timekeeper and fact-checker. The leader presses the group into harder discussion, while the timekeeper stops it running long and the fact-checker keeps it accurate.

Two neighbouring problems sit outside exam execution. [[wiki/Domains/Miscellaneous/How to diagnose and fix exam mistakes|How to diagnose and fix exam mistakes]] reads the pattern of your errors to name the biggest likely cause, then tries one fix aimed at it and repeats that loop. [[wiki/Domains/Miscellaneous/How to prepare for ultra high-volume exams|How to prepare for ultra high-volume exams]] deals with a huge syllabus where only about 1 percent to 2 percent will be tested, so efficiency shows up as coverage and retention.
