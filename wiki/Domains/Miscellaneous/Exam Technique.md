# Exam Technique

Exam technique here is the set of protections you put in place before the day, in the weeks leading up, each aimed at a way performance leaks that has nothing to do with how much you know. There are four. Enough time in bed to actually sleep seven to nine hours, since one recovery night does not repay an all-nighter. A priority check on the answers you marked unsure. The least-confident heading on your syllabus turned into the question you are hoping will not appear, and then answered while there is still time. Slow breathing, which is the one of the four you can still run once you are in the room.

## The four nets

Sleep is the first, especially the night before, when sleep-dependent consolidation does the work and losing it forfeits the day's encoding. Time in bed is a buffer, not a threshold: enough time in bed to actually sleep seven to nine hours, which for most people means more than seven to nine hours in bed. A later recovery night shrinks the cost but does not erase it. Sleeping in after the exam does not repay the lost night.

**Priority-check** is looking twice only at the answers already flagged as unsure, while keeping a steady pace on everything else.

**Think like an examiner** is evaluating knowledge by turning it into problems, especially on the least-confident angles, the highest-value ones to invent. The move without any table: take a syllabus heading felt least confident about and write the question hoped will not appear.

**Slow deep breathing** triggers the parasympathetic response and cuts an anxiety spiral. Repeating it across the season is habituation from use, not a finding about pathways. Any slow breathing already known is enough.

These four are a starter set.

## When nerves are the limiter

If only high-pressure exams rattle, the limiter is nerves, not knowledge. Graduated exposure is practising the same task under conditions that get steadily closer to the real exam, so the nerves arrive in rehearsal instead of on the day. The house ladder is strict time limits, then real settings, then friends added for competitive pressure. The first timed paper is not saved for the week of the exam. Avoidance is what keeps the anxiety.

Items get forgotten under pressure. A pre-exam checklist is written and given a start date, possibly weeks out. A list without a start date is a poster. The date is what makes the habit survive the week.

If the limiter is missing knowledge, these nets will not find it. The price is a full night, one dated checklist, and one timed paper sat early. Two high-stakes sittings that still produce a random, stakes-linked error pattern after the ladder has been climbed is the quit signal: diagnosis, not another breathing cycle.

The week of the exam, what is still standing is those nets: sleep protected, the checklist already dated and in use, one timed paper already sat.
