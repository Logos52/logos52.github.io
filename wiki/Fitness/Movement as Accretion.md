# Movement as Accretion

Movement as Accretion is a plan, still being tried on this desk, to make movement a daily habit by having each session add to something that lasts. A missed day costs nothing, because nothing resets. The plan is the first use in this wiki of a wider idea, the accretion frame, which keeps a practice going through a record that grows and survives breaks, in place of streaks, daily targets and willpower.

## Core takeaways

- Each session adds to a record that keeps its value.
- A missed day removes nothing from the record.
- Streaks and closed rings reset, so every break reads as a loss.
- An honest measure still means something after a month off.
- Low-effort movement, such as daily walking, keeps starts easy.
- A skill sport with a group keeps growing and pulls people back.

## How it works

A streak counts days in a row, so its number is also the size of the loss at the next break. After a month off, a streak and a closed activity ring say nothing useful about the person. A dated personal best still does, because a best lift, a best distance or a best climbing grade stays true. The plan swaps the first kind of measure for the second, and it adds a visible next step whose result is not yet known, which gives a reason to come back.

- Keep a training log of sets, reps and weight.
- Each session tries to beat the last entry.
- Keep dated bests, since they survive a layoff.
- Drop counters of days in a row.

```
streak:  5  6  7  8 | miss | 0  1  2      (reset)
record:  best 40 kg | miss | best 40 kg   (kept)
```

## Keeping the effort low

Hard, sweaty training is a common reason a movement habit stops, and it is not needed to get lean. Walking every day costs no mental energy, is good for the joints and the mood, and does not raise hunger the way running does. Calm cardio keeps the heart rate low and lowers stress.

- Walk every day.
- Pick calm cardio: walking, walking in a pool, paddle boarding.
- Drop hard, sweaty cardio if it is what stops the habit.

## Sports that grow

Climbing, skating, dance and martial arts run on a skill that keeps growing, so each session adds to something. The people in the group keep the sessions happening, and without them each start takes longer. A sport done beside the gym helps both. A combat sport teaches movements that gym work also trains, and the gym work makes the sport easier.

- Choose one skill sport with a regular group.
- Use gym work to build what that sport needs.
