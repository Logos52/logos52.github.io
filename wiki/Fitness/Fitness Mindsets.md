# Fitness Mindsets

Fitness mindsets are the beliefs that decide whether a person keeps training and eating well for years. Most people who want a leaner or stronger body already know the sets, the meals and the daily steps, and the people who know the most about nutrition are often not the leanest. Results depend on whether the routine is still running after a year, and a person who trains much less but never misses is ahead of a hard trainer who stops within six months.

- Change who you are, and the routine follows.
- Saying no to food and plans matters more than a big calorie deficit.
- Do fewer things and keep them for years.
- Decide once, so daily choices take no thought.
- Start smaller than your motivation wants.
- After a slip, go back the next day without guilt.

## Who you are comes first

A behaviour does not last while the beliefs under it stay the same, and those beliefs come from the story a person tells about who they are. The first job is to bring that sense of self in line with the body and the way of eating the person wants. The old identity will feel safer for a while, because the person already knows how other people react to it. A habit also cannot change until the person can see it, so the habit has to be noticed in the moment before any attempt to stop it.

- Name the beliefs that make it hard to keep going.
- Look at upbringing, the food at home and friends' habits.
- Stop insulting your body, since stress hormones work against fat loss.
- Set an exact goal, then monthly milestones you can hit.
- Compare yourself with yesterday rather than with photos on your phone.

## Saying no

For getting lean, turning down the food, drinks and plans that break the routine matters more than the size of the calorie deficit. When friends, family and circumstances decide what and when a person eats, that person does not control their own body. A fixed answer works better than a fresh decision each time, and it gets easier when it is practised in small situations first. Family push food out of love, and people who mock training are usually comforting themselves.

- Use one fixed line: "I don't eat that."
- Give no apology and no reasons.
- See less of people whose habits keep your body as it is.
- Spend more time around people doing better than you.

## Fewer things, over years

Attention spread over many things does each of them worse, and each extra thing gives only a short lift. The same cut applies to exercises, meals, clothes and commitments. Change also needs a year or two. The body resists fast change with hormones and hunger, and people who lose weight fast usually gain it back within months.

- Train three times a week on the key exercises.
- Five or six exercises, three sets each, done within an hour.
- Keep the same exercises so technique keeps improving.
- Rotate three to five meals whose calories you worked out once.
- Form good enough to hit the muscle safely is enough.
- Aim for six to twelve months at about one kilogram a month.
- Thirty, sixty and ninety day programmes do not produce lasting bodies.

## Deciding once, starting small

Decision fatigue is what wears people down during a diet, and one decision made properly makes the later ones easy. Motivation runs out, so a plan built on a burst of it collapses when the burst is spent, often within weeks. Starting smaller than feels right keeps training something a person mostly looks forward to, and more can be added later. On a day with no wish to train, pack the bag and leave the house without debating it, since once out of the door it is too late to talk yourself out of the session.

- Give training and the daily walk a fixed time in the calendar.
- Show up the way you show up for work.
- Arrange the home so the snack takes effort to reach.
- Begin with one to three sessions a week.
- On a bad day, do one to three exercises.

## After a slip

Guilt and shame after a missed day push people into eating more. Skipping builds its own momentum, so two or three skipped days make the next skip easier. Consistency is judged over a year, and a year of training with three missed weeks beats two perfect months, two months off and two more perfect months. Weight also goes up and down on the way down, and plateaus come to everyone.

- Go back to the plan the very next day.
- Take today's weight as the new starting point.
- Watch the trend over weeks instead of today's number.
- Expect plateaus before they arrive.

## Hunger, stress and tracking

Stress is what makes people binge, because it raises insulin and weakens the signal of being full. The hunger that breaks a diet is often a need for something other than food, such as affection or confidence. Real hunger means the day's calories were too low, and any nourishing food will answer it. Tracking works best as a short learning period followed by estimating.

- Wait five or ten minutes before a second helping.
- Ask what the craving is trying to fill.
- Track four things: weight, calories, steps, workouts.
- Count calories accurately for two weeks, then estimate.
- Log sets, reps and weight, and try to beat the last session.

## The same rules in clothes

The rules for training carry over to dressing. A few basics with the right fit do most of the work, in the same way that a few exercises do. A wardrobe in matching colours takes no thought in the morning, like a rotation of known meals. Effort that shows makes an outfit look worse.

- Own a few basics and get the fit right first.
- Buy mostly white, black, navy, grey, beige, khaki, brown, olive.
- Wear a garment until it takes the shape of your body.
- Take one item off before leaving the house.
- No logos, and no clothes chosen to show off muscle.
