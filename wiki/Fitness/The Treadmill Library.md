# The Treadmill Library

Filling workout time with something worth hearing looks like a solved problem from the outside — podcasts by the million, audiobooks by the hundred thousand, a machine with a bottle holder and forty free minutes. On the machine, the abundance thins out fast. For me the failure splits the same way every time: half of what I try is too light to hold any interest, and the other half is dense enough to need a stillness I don't have mid-stride. The fix I've landed on is to stop hunting and start making — a library generated for the machine, with sessions planned around what plays. It's the [[wiki/Concepts/The Accretion Frame|accretion frame]] getting its first purpose-built tool: movement runs best when it grows something, so the listening should be a thing that grows too.

The two failure modes turn out to be two axes. Interest is the plain one — most shows circle topics I would never chase in text, and taste doesn't negotiate. Density is the quieter one. A dense argument wants pausing and rereading, and a moving body grants neither; the frame page carries the research on why, and the short version is that exercise taxes exactly the kind of attention dense audio demands. So the mismatch was never a concentration failure to push through. Pushing through belongs to the old engine anyway — the one that runs under the shadow of guilt — and retiring that engine is the point of this whole rebuild of movement.

Generation reverses the direction of fit. Found content has to happen to match the window; made content is built for it. The wiki read back to me as short audio editions, so the second brain talks while the legs work. The past day's news compressed into one morning brief, voiced before I'm downstairs. And the piece I care most about: Chinese audio from [[wiki/Tsumugu/Tsumugu|Tsumugu]], played with the text following along — tap a character for its meaning, tap again for the whole sentence — so listening practice and the dictionary work already built finally meet on one screen. Each item is made for the effort it will share the body with: lighter where the legs push hard, denser where the stride is easy.

The library also behaves the way the [[wiki/Fitness/Movement as Accretion|movement experiment]] says an artifact should: it only fills. Every session moves something to the played shelf, and the shelf doesn't un-fill. A skipped day leaves episodes waiting, and waiting costs nothing — no number anywhere can die. The regret a missed session produces, if the design works, points at the queue and never at me.

A memory from years back confirms the shape. I played Zombies, Run! for a while and liked it — the run came wrapped in a story, an episode that continued the next time out, supplies collecting along the way. Pokémon GO worked the sibling mechanic: the walk filled a collection, and the Pokédex was fuller by the time I got home. The part of both that pulled was the pile — a story further along, a collection grown — the accretion engine wearing game clothes, sitting in my own history years before the frame had a name. A treadmill game in that lineage is tempting, and it stays a later problem — parked on purpose until the library itself is running.

For now the build stays small. The app is called tan — 反, the old unit for a bolt of woven cloth, one kimono's worth — a sibling to Tsumugu's name: 紡ぐ is the spinning, and a tan is what the finished weaving measures out to. It runs on one phone, for one user. Content gets generated at the desk and loaded on, so the machine only ever meets finished audio. Played or unplayed is the only state that matters, nothing resets, and the Chinese reader — audio with the text following, tap-to-see — is the first real feature. Everything after it waits until sessions start wanting to happen.

That last clause is the whole test, and it's the same one the movement experiment set: three weeks in, a skipped day should point at something concrete — episodes waiting, a shelf that didn't grow. If the pull doesn't come, the content changes, never the discipline. The machine downstairs stays exactly what it is. What changes is what's playing on it: content built for one listener, at the density the stride allows, on a shelf that only fills.
