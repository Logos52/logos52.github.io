# The Uncertainty-Opportunity Tradeoff

The uncertainty-opportunity tradeoff is the pattern where, as time passes and more is known about a choice, the chance to benefit from it also shrinks. Waiting until you feel sure usually means waiting until others have acted and the gain is smaller or gone. The useful response is to gather information fast so you can decide while the opportunity is still large.

## Core takeaways

- Uncertainty about a choice falls as information comes in.
- Opportunity tends to fall at the same time.
- Zero uncertainty usually arrives after the chance has passed.
- Putting off a decision is a decision to trade opportunity for comfort.
- Information comes from research and from trying things.
- Investigate the opportunity and the risk at once.

## How it works

At the start of a choice little is known and uncertainty is high. As information spreads, uncertainty drops, and more people become willing to make the same choice. Once many people make it, the advantage of making it shrinks. Early investors in Bitcoin faced high uncertainty and high opportunity, and later buyers faced less of both.

```
high |\  uncertainty
     | \ \  opportunity
     |  \  \
     |   \   \
low  |____\____\_______ time
        decide here   too late
```

- The trend is general and has exceptions.
- Fixing your study method after failing an exam removes all uncertainty.
- It also removes the chance to pass that exam.

## Waiting and the status quo

People prefer to keep things as they are, even when the odds favour change. Someone might turn down a method with a 70% chance of reaching a goal to keep one with a 10% chance, because the familiar option feels safer. Waiting until you feel better about a choice is a choice to let opportunity fall in exchange for certainty. That trade can be sensible, provided you make it knowingly.

- Compare the odds of changing with the odds of staying.
- Count staying as a choice with its own risk.
- Waiting costs whatever opportunity is lost meanwhile.

## How to decide sooner

Treat uncertainty as risk you can reduce, and treat information as something you go and get. A day or two of active research can reach the level of knowledge that passive waiting would take months to reach. That lets you decide while opportunity is still high. The research runs on two fronts at once.

- Front 1, the opportunity
  - Is it real and legitimate?
  - Is it as large as it looks?
  - Is someone overselling it?
- Front 2, the risk
  - What exactly could go wrong?
  - How large would each loss be?
  - Can the biggest loss be protected against?

Waiting does pay when new information is likely to arrive soon and the chance will stay open meanwhile.
