# The Uncertainty-Opportunity Tradeoff

The uncertainty-opportunity tradeoff is the pattern where the longer you wait on a choice, the more you know about it and the less there is to gain from it. Waiting until you feel sure usually means waiting until other people have acted and the gain is small or gone. The useful response is to find things out fast and decide while the opportunity is still large.

## Takeaways

- Uncertainty about a choice falls as information comes in.
- The opportunity in the choice tends to fall at the same time.
- Zero uncertainty usually arrives after the chance has passed.
- Delaying a decision is itself a choice, and it costs opportunity.
- Information comes from research and from trying things.
- Look into the opportunity and the risk at the same time.

## How it works

At the start of a choice little is known, so uncertainty is high. Information spreads over time, uncertainty drops, and more people become willing to make the same choice. Once many people have made it, the advantage of making it shrinks. Early buyers of Bitcoin faced high uncertainty and a large opportunity, and later buyers faced less of both.

```
high |\   uncertainty
     | \  \   opportunity
     |  \   \
     |   \    \
low  |____\_____\________ time
       decide here    too late
```

- The trend is general and has exceptions.
- Fixing a study method after a failed exam leaves no uncertainty.
- That same moment leaves no chance to pass the exam.

## Waiting and the status quo

People prefer to keep things as they are, even when the odds favour a change. Someone may turn down a method with a 70% chance of reaching a goal and keep one with a 10% chance, because the familiar one feels safer. Waiting until a choice feels better is itself a choice, and it lets the opportunity fall in exchange for certainty. That trade can be sensible when it is made on purpose.

- Compare the odds of changing with the odds of staying.
- Count staying as a choice with its own risk.
- Waiting costs whatever opportunity is lost in the meantime.

## How to decide sooner

Uncertainty is a risk you can reduce, and information is something you go and get. A day or two of active research can reach the level of knowledge that passive waiting would take months to reach. That lets you decide while the opportunity is still large. The research runs on two fronts at the same time.

- Front 1, the opportunity
  - Check that it is real and legitimate.
  - Check that it is as large as it looks.
  - Check whether someone is overselling it.
- Front 2, the risk
  - Name exactly what could go wrong.
  - Put a size on each possible loss.
  - Find out whether the biggest loss can be covered.

Waiting does pay when new information is likely to arrive soon and the chance will stay open until it does.
