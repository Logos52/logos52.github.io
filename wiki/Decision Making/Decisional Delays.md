# Decisional Delays

A decisional delay is the time spent working out what to do next, in the gap after one task ends and before the next begins. People notice time passing during a task and rarely while sitting between tasks, so these gaps go unmeasured. Reported totals run from about thirty minutes to two hours a day, which is an extra task's worth of time.

- The gap between tasks is where the time goes.
- Five to ten minutes per gap adds up over a day.
- A long gap often turns into procrastination.
- A plan that names the next task removes most gaps.
- Noticing the gap comes before fixing it.
- Once noticed, start the next task without deliberating.

## How it works

A task ends and a question opens: what now, is there time for something, is a break needed. Each question invites another, and the answer is often an hour of something unplanned. Finishing early and finishing late both open the gap. The deliberating also breaks any focus that the task before it had built up.

- Finish early, deliberate, watch videos, arrive late to the next task.
- Finish late, ask whether there is time, lose more time asking.
- Each gap resets the focus the last task built.

```
task A --> [ what now? ] --> task B
            5 to 60 min
            not on any clock
```

## How to fix it

The first fix is a plan. A schedule that already says what comes next leaves nothing to decide in the gap, and writing the next action down before the current task ends makes starting it easier. The second fix is awareness, built by a short review at the end of each day. The review looks for the points where the day stopped for a decision.

- Keep a schedule that names the next task at each point.
- Before finishing a task, write the first step of the next one.
- At day's end, ask where you stopped to decide what came next.
- Log each gap you find.
- The first few days may show nothing.
- The habit of looking trains you to catch gaps as they happen.
- When you catch a gap, move straight into the next task.

## How it differs from a hard choice

In a decisional delay the next task is usually obvious, and the time goes to hesitating. A tangled choice with many options is a different problem and needs a different method, such as the chain of yes or no questions called choice throttling. A gap that keeps growing into avoidance is procrastination, which has its own fixes.
