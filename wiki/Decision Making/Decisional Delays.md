# Decisional Delays

A decisional delay is the pause spent deciding what comes next. It sits in the gaps between activities, and it is easy to miss because you notice time passing while you are doing something and rarely notice it while you are only thinking. Across students and working people, these gaps often add up to 30 minutes to 2 hours in a day, and sometimes as much as 3 hours. Even 5 minutes to 10 minutes of deciding after each task can stack into a whole extra activity you never got.

They also break flow. You finish 15 minutes early, wonder what should fill the leftover time, open a video, and an hour later you are late for the next block. Or you finish 10 minutes late, wonder whether you still have enough time or need a break, and the wondering itself becomes the delay.

## See them first

Good plans, schedules, and task lists remove most of the question, because the next block is already chosen. Even then, finishing early or late can reopen it.

The first skill is noticing. At the end of each day, look back over the schedule and ask where you stopped and wondered what to do next, whether to do it, or how to do it. Keep a short log. The first day or two you may find nothing. Looking for the gap trains you to catch yourself in the middle of one on later days.

Once you see that you have entered a delay, go straight into the next task. Awareness has to come first, which is why the daily look-back comes first. Reclaiming even 1 hour or 2 hours a day is like adding a spare hour of rest, or dropping a hidden commute you never meant to take.

## Open questions

How many times today did I finish one thing and then stand still, asking what should come next?

How much time would I get back if each of those pauses shrank to the next task already written on the plan?
