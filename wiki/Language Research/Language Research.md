# Language Research

Language Research holds facts about how a language works: spelling, sound, borrowing, and orthography. Each fact is recorded with its confidence level. Claims that were checked and found false are recorded too, so the same search does not have to be run again. What a learner should do is covered by the language-learning wing, which is a separate section of the vault. This wing covers how the language itself works.

## What is in it

The wing has one note. [[wiki/Research/Transliteration Into Chinese Bank|Transliteration Into Chinese (音譯)]] covers how a foreign name is rewritten in Chinese one syllable at a time, using a small set of characters chosen for their sound. The tones of the result are whatever tones those characters already have. The note uses 可口可樂 as a worked example against the claim that transliterations have flat tones. [[wiki/Language Research/How Foreign Words Become Chinese|How Foreign Words Become Chinese]] explains the same process in continuous prose for a reader who is not a linguist.

The [[wiki/Language/Refold Language Learning System|language-learning wing]] has no hub page of its own, so its pages are listed below. The pages stay in their current folders, and this wing only links to them. The language-learning wing is written for learners: it covers what to do, in what order, and with which tool.

## The learning wing, by job

### Method and immersion

- [[wiki/Language/Refold Language Learning System|Refold Language Learning System]]: an immersion-first system for learning a language, in which fluency comes from experience that is made progressively more comprehensible. The general system of the same kind, built on diagnosis and a loop, is [[wiki/Syntheses/ICS System|ICS System]]
- [[wiki/Language/Three Pillars of Language Learning|Three Pillars of Language Learning]]: preparation, interactive immersion, and freeflow immersion; each one does something the other two cannot do
- [[wiki/Language/Preparation|Preparation]]: orientation (sounds, words, characters, grammar, patterns) that lets a learner notice things during immersion instead of hearing it as noise
- [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]: using tools and attention to make a learning session out of real input
- [[wiki/Language/Freeflow Immersion|Freeflow Immersion]]: an attentive mode of immersion with little pausing and few lookups
- [[wiki/Language/Attention is Important|Attention is Important]]: attention is what separates immersion from background noise
- [[wiki/Language/Noticing Game|Noticing Game]]: with hard input, the goal is reduced to finding pieces the learner recognizes
- [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu|Hacking Comprehension Menu]]: methods that bring real content into a useful range of difficulty without dropping that content
- [[wiki/Language/Immersion Metalayers|Immersion Metalayers]]: subtitle control, popup lookup, replay, auto-pause, translation support
- [[wiki/Language/YouTube Immersion Account|YouTube Immersion Account]]: training the recommendation algorithm until the feed shows target-language input
- [[wiki/Language/Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning|Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning]]: write down what the material must answer, work through the source into a rough outline, then cut the outline down to what can be reconstructed without the source

### Grammar, script, characters

- [[wiki/Language/Language Isn't Math|Language Isn't Math]]: grammar is a system of patterns absorbed from meaningful exposure, and is not learned as a set of rules applied to output
- [[wiki/Language/Refold Grammar Primers|Refold Grammar Primers]]: grammar explanations used to direct attention during immersion, instead of a reference for speaking or writing
- [[wiki/Language/Vietnamese Grammar Primer|Vietnamese Grammar Primer]]: Vietnamese grammar written to help a learner notice it; still a draft, not yet checked against native references
- [[wiki/Language/Character Primer|Character Primer]]: a first introduction to character writing systems, covering components, recognition, and repeated exposure
- [[wiki/Language/The Han-Viet Bridge|The Hán-Việt Bridge]]: the part of Vietnamese vocabulary that comes from Chinese, and the early advantage it gives a learner; the figures are on that page
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/How Chinese Characters Work|How Chinese Characters Work]]: the hub page for the character pages. Writing records speech. When the components of each character are classified by the function they have in that character, the script becomes predictable
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Chinese Characters, Condensed|Chinese Characters, Condensed]]: the character pages shortened to a list of rules, one rule per line
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Three Attributes of a Character|Three Attributes of a Character]]: memory of a character's form, sound, and meaning decays independently, so each has to be trained separately
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Surface vs Deep Structure|Surface vs Deep Structure]]: break a character down only as far as its functional components; breaking a radical down further leads to folk etymology
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Form Components|Form Components]]: semantic components that depict something; where stylization has removed the resemblance, the ancient forms show what was depicted
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Components|Meaning Components]]: semantic components with abstract, extended meanings in 形聲字 (a character with a meaning part and a sound part); a component's role is assigned in each character separately, not once for its shape
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]]: phonetic components record Old Chinese sound; use them to predict the range of possible readings, then look up the reading; tone is a separate fact
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Series|Sound Series]]: reading every character in a series shows the sound relation that sound change has made invisible in any single character
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]]: shapes that have no function in the character; each is learned with a one-line explanation of its cause, not an invented story
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]]: how a character's meanings derive from one another; the percentages are on that page
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The IME Method|The IME Method]]: imagine the pinyin input method's list of candidate characters, and match plausible sound components against plausible meaning components
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The Pipelining Strategy|The Pipelining Strategy]]: sessions are staggered so that a written form is learned and linked to a spoken word the learner has already overlearned

## What accumulates here

This wing exists so that a research pass that took a lot of search time is recorded once and cited after that. Each record keeps its confidence level, and claims found false stay marked as false. The wing has one note. The language-learning pages are not moved into this wing. What collects here is descriptive material, one level below the learning wing's advice. A hub page that restates the pages it lists becomes a second copy of them that goes out of date. A new note is added here only when it records a fact about how the language works, with its confidence marked.

## Open Questions

No Chinese-language study of 音譯詞聲調 that uses Taiwan-specific data has been found. The reliable findings on the tones of loanwords come from English-language studies of Mainland or general Mandarin.

For one worked name, the mapping by way of Japanese has no source other than aggregator sites. A match between syllables can suggest that route but does not count as evidence.

[[wiki/Language/Vietnamese Grammar Primer|Vietnamese Grammar Primer]] is still a draft and has not been checked.

Transliteration is one way a language borrows words. Calques, semantic loans, and graphic loans are not covered yet. [[wiki/Language/The Han-Viet Bridge|The Hán-Việt Bridge]] is the closest related page.
