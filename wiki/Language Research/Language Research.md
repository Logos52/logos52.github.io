# Language Research

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

Language Research describes how a language works, while the learning pages describe what a learner should do about it. Its research so far covers 音譯, the way a foreign word enters Chinese by being rewritten one syllable at a time with characters chosen for their sound. Each of those characters keeps its own meaning and tone in the new word, so the translator's only real choice is which character writes each syllable. The learning pages are gathered here by job, from immersion method through grammar and Chinese characters to related memory pages. They share one model, in which a language is acquired through large amounts of attentive time in it, with preparation and tools making that time easier to understand.

## What is in it

The research covers one process from two directions. The Transliteration Into Chinese Bank is a research bank on 音譯 that stays off the public site. It covers who sets naming standards in the PRC and in Taiwan, the pool of characters a translator draws from and what decides the tones. It works through 可口可樂, 德魯伊 and 波拉勒斯 character by character. [[wiki/Language Research/How Foreign Words Become Chinese|How Foreign Words Become Chinese]] explains the same process in plain prose, including the real Coca-Cola naming history and the two legends that replaced it.

## The learning wing, by job

### Method and immersion

Immersion is the core activity, and it rests on three pillars, with tools that make the time usable.

- [[wiki/Language/Refold Language Learning System|Refold Language Learning System]]: a course in two phases, first fundamentals and then reading plus listening, which balances three pillars and treats time in the language as the core of progress. [[wiki/Syntheses/ICS System|ICS System]] is the general learning system this wiki is organised around.
- [[wiki/Language/Three Pillars of Language Learning|Three Pillars of Language Learning]]: preparation, interactive immersion and freeflow, which have to be balanced for progress.
- [[wiki/Language/Preparation|Preparation]]: a short orientation to the sounds, words, characters, grammar and tools of a language, done so the next stretch of immersion becomes noticeable.
- [[wiki/Language/Interactive Immersion|Interactive Immersion]]: a family of activities that use tools to go deeper into input, including the noticing game, sentence mining, intensive listening and transcription.
- [[wiki/Language/Freeflow Immersion|Freeflow Immersion]]: watching content without lookups or rewinding while attention stays high, which builds self-sufficiency.
- [[wiki/Language/Attention is Important|Attention is Important]]: time in a new language counts only when attention is high, even before the language is understood.
- [[wiki/Language/Noticing Game|Noticing Game]]: high-attention looking for handholds such as cognates, known words or list words inside a barely understood stream.
- [[wiki/Language/Hacking Comprehension Menu|Hacking Comprehension Menu]]: a menu of tools, techniques and content choices that make immersion easier to understand.
- [[wiki/Language/Immersion Metalayers|Immersion Metalayers]]: a tool layer on top of a show or video that lets you look up words, pause after each line and jump between subtitles.
- [[wiki/Language/YouTube Immersion Account|YouTube Immersion Account]]: a separate YouTube account used to find target-language videos compelling enough to hold attention.
- [[wiki/Language/Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning|Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning]]: a loop that starts from a passage you can almost follow, turns each missing word or pattern into a flashcard and uses it in new sentences the same day.

### Grammar, script, characters

Grammar and script both rest on noticing patterns during immersion over learning rules.

- [[wiki/Language/Language Isn't Math|Language Isn't Math]]: a language cannot be reduced to vocabulary and grammar rules that translate any thought, because native speech is set and specific in unpredictable ways.
- [[wiki/Language/Refold Grammar Primers|Refold Grammar Primers]]: a grammar primer exposes patterns so they can be noticed in immersion, and it is not something to memorize or drill.
- [[wiki/Language/Vietnamese Grammar Primer|Vietnamese Grammar Primer]]: a short catalog of the separate words and word order that carry meaning in Vietnamese, where the verb does not change form.
- [[wiki/Language/Character Primer|Character Primer]]: Chinese and Japanese characters are picture-like symbols that are easier to remember once broken into meaningful components.
- [[wiki/Language/The Han-Viet Bridge|The Hán-Việt Bridge]]: a method for Vietnamese speakers learning Mandarin, built on the Sino-Vietnamese readings already inside everyday Vietnamese words.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/How Chinese Characters Work|How Chinese Characters Work]]: the hub for the character pages, where naming the job of each component makes the script predictable.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Chinese Characters, Condensed|Chinese Characters, Condensed]]: the character pages stated as rules, one rule per line.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Three Attributes of a Character|Three Attributes of a Character]]: a character binds form, meaning and sound, and each link has to be encoded and tested on its own.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Surface vs Deep Structure|Surface vs Deep Structure]]: deep structure is the sound-and-meaning logic of a character, and surface structure is how it is supposed to look when written.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Form Components|Form Components]]: a form component uses a picture to express meaning, so the ancient form comes first when the modern shape no longer resembles it.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Components|Meaning Components]]: a meaning component carries an abstract extension of a concrete original sense, and it is learned through tables of sibling characters.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]]: a sound component is a prediction tool that points at a range of pronunciations, not at one guaranteed reading.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Series|Sound Series]]: the set of characters that share a sound component, grouped by seven related-initial groups.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]]: a component that does no sound or meaning work in a given character, such as a distinguishing mark, leftover corruption or decoration.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]]: a character's meanings grow from the original sense tied to its form into extensions, loans and branches for each reading.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The IME Method|The IME Method]]: recalling a forgotten character by listing possible sound and meaning components and cross-matching them, the way an input method offers candidates.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The Pipelining Strategy|The Pipelining Strategy]]: a cohort of three to five characters moves through a three-day cycle, so form, meaning and sound are never learned on the same day.
