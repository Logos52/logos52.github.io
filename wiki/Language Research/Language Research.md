# Language Research

This wing holds research into how languages behave — the standards bodies, the phonology, the borrowing mechanics, the orthographic conventions — banked as fact with its confidence marked, so a claim can be picked up months later and used without re-running the search. It is the descriptive counterpart to [[wiki/Language/Refold Language Learning System|the language-learning wing]]: that wing answers what a learner should do, this one answers what the language is doing. Notes here carry every fact their research turned up, including the facts that turned out to be wrong, because a killed claim is the part most likely to resurface.

**Start here:** [[wiki/Language Research/Transliteration Into Chinese|Transliteration Into Chinese (音譯)]] — a foreign name is rewritten one syllable at a time out of a small pool of characters picked for sound, and the tones that arrive are whatever those characters already had; 可口可樂 (3-3-3-4), 德魯伊 (2-3-1), and 波拉勒斯 (1-1-4-1) are worked character by character against the claim that transliterations come out flat.

**The explainer:** [[wiki/Language Research/How Foreign Words Become Chinese|How Foreign Words Become Chinese]] — the same mechanism as one continuous read for a non-linguist, from the sound-versus-meaning choice and the bleached character pool through to the real Coca-Cola naming history and the two legends that replaced it.

## The learning wing, linked not restated

The Language wing at `wiki/Language/` was built from the Refold method (2026-05-06) and the Outlier character course (2026-06-10/11), and it is a learner's wing: what to do, in what order, with what tool. It has no front door of its own, so its members are listed below in place. Nothing moves; this wing links.

### The Chinese character cluster

- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/How Chinese Characters Work|How Chinese Characters Work]] — the cluster hub: writing records speech, so components classified by function per character make the script predictive instead of arbitrary.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Chinese Characters, Condensed|Chinese Characters, Condensed]] — the whole cluster compressed to doctrine, one rule per line with a link to the page that owns it.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Three Attributes of a Character|Three Attributes of a Character]] — one item, three links (form, sound, meaning) that decay independently and have to be trained separately.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Surface vs Deep Structure|Surface vs Deep Structure]] — decomposition stops at functional components; pushing a radical into a deep-structure question is where folk etymology enters.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Form Components|Form Components]] — depiction-based semantic components, with ancient forms as the bridge once stylization killed the resemblance.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Components|Meaning Components]] — abstract-extension semantic components in 形聲字, with roles assigned per character rather than per shape.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Components|Sound Components]] — phonetic components record Old Chinese sound; predict the range, then look it up, and treat tone as a separate fact.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Sound Series|Sound Series]] — reading a whole series restores the signal that sound change erased from any single character.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Empty Components|Empty Components]] — the fourth category: shapes doing no work at all, repaired with a causal one-liner instead of an invented story.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/Meaning Trees and Original Meanings|Meaning Trees and Original Meanings]] — etymology proper: derivation genealogies and original meanings, with ~85% of entries carrying a derived sense and ~18% a sound loan.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The IME Method|The IME Method]] — run the pinyin candidate window in your head, cross-matching plausible sound and meaning components to recover a blocked form.
- [[wiki/Language/Chinese/The Pipelining Strategy|The Pipelining Strategy]] — three staggered sessions in cohorts of 3–5 so a written form binds to an already-overlearned spoken word.

## Open questions

- No Chinese-language study of 音譯詞聲調 with Taiwan-specific data has been located. Every solid finding on loanword tone assignment is English-language and Mainland or general-Mandarin based, which leaves the Taiwan tone claims resting on sources that never looked at Taiwan.
- The claim that 德魯伊 entered Chinese through Japanese ドルイド rather than straight from English is unsourced past aggregator sites. The syllable mapping fits better than the English does, which is suggestive and not evidence.
- [[wiki/Language/Vietnamese Grammar Primer|Vietnamese Grammar Primer]] is still marked draft and unchecked against native references.
- Transliteration is one borrowing mechanism among several. Calques, semantic loans, and the graphic loans Vietnamese and Japanese share with Chinese are uncovered here, and [[wiki/Language/The Han-Viet Bridge|The Hán-Việt Bridge]] is the nearest existing anchor for the next deposit.

The wing exists so a research pass that cost real search time gets banked once and cited afterwards, with its confidence intact and its dead claims kept dead in writing. One note is in it. The learning wing next door is complete on its own terms and stays where it is; what accumulates here is the descriptive layer underneath it.
