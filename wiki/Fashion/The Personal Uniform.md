# The Personal Uniform

A personal uniform is a small set of clothes that a person wears nearly every day, chosen once and then repeated. People expect that kind of minimalism to make everyone look the same, and from a distance the uniforms do look interchangeable. Up close each one identifies its wearer, because it was filtered through the needs of one particular life.

## Takeaways

- Keep few things, each one used in daily life.
- The look is a side effect of what the clothes are for.
- Repetition filters out whatever does not fit one person's days.
- Two minimalist uniforms rarely match up close.
- A bad purchase costs again each time things are moved or stored.
- A piece that breaks the look shows the choice was about use.

## How it works

Wearing the same few pieces every day tests each one constantly. Anything uncomfortable, fussy or unused shows up within weeks and leaves the wardrobe. What stays fits the person's work, climate, travel and habits, so two people who both keep a small repeated wardrobe end up with different wardrobes. For someone who moves home often, each item is also one more thing to pack and carry, so fewer purchases are worth making.

- Wear each piece often enough to learn its faults.
- Remove what fails, and do not replace it by default.
- Keep a practical item even when it spoils the look.

## Dressing rules that reach the same wardrobe

Fitness Mindsets, another page in this wiki, gives a set of dressing rules that arrive at the same kind of wardrobe from a different direction. The rules favour a few basics, colours that all go together, and no visible effort. When every piece matches every other piece, getting dressed takes no thought.

- Own a few basics and get their fit right first.
- Buy mostly neutral colours: white, black, navy, grey, beige, olive.
- Wear a garment until it softens and takes your shape.
- Buy good fabric, since it ages better with washing.
- No logos.
