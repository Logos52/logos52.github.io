# Are You Learning, or Just Using Techniques

A technique can look correctly done while results stay flat. The test is not whether you executed the steps. The test is whether the method is producing real results. Judge techniques by outcomes.

A technique is working when it creates the effect you chose it for. Pick methods for the mental effect you want inside your learning system, not on a generic rating such as calling something a top-rated method. Ratings mislead, since which method fits depends on the goal in front of you.

Techniques matter for the way they shift your thinking. Technique drives thinking, and thinking drives performance, so any method has to be tied back to the quality of thought it forces. The level of the goal then decides which strategy fits. Building a schema by connecting knowledge supports long-term retention. Managing cognitive load keeps you out of overwhelm while leaving enough challenge that you do not go passive.

How progress feels can drift away from how you actually perform. Measure with objective data, real-world results, and outside feedback, then refine the strategy from that. The brain also finds shortcuts that skip hard thinking, and unnoticed habits silently stall progress. Finding those gaps, including messy organisation in how you think, is where the real jumps appear.
