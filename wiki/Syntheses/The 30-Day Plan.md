# The 30-Day Plan

Students meet a month-long planning process early in the program, and it walks them through building a personal success plan. The process raises the chance of success by building plans that notice overlooked pieces, especially habits and tendencies, and by clearing barriers in advance so the goal is easier to hit consistently.

Marginal Gains is a different tool. It is a viewpoint and method for watching and speeding up skill growth through compounding incremental changes, the kind often described as 1 percent gains. It tells you how to grow a skill. The 30-Day Plan is a dated plan covering the coming 30 days so those desired gains actually get taken.

The two sit on different axes. Marginal Gains focuses on skill development, getting better at a skill. The 30-Day Plan focuses on self-management, how to take action productively. Marginal Gains relies on the idea that skill grows faster, more easily, and with more motivation when tiny gains compound, rather than when you aim only at big jumps. The 30-Day Plan relies on the idea that goals land more often when the plan names barriers in advance. Marginal Gains works through experimentation and reflection as you track and stack those 1 percent gains. The 30-Day Plan works through clear goal-setting and a look at habits and behaviours so the plan has a high chance of success.

Usage differs as well. Marginal Gains should run continuously, whenever you want a skill to grow. The 30-Day Plan is used when you need a solid, low-risk plan that can move a goal quickly. Scope differs too. Marginal Gains breaks bigger skills into smaller, focused gains. The 30-Day Plan breaks larger goals into a month-long target plus a clear plan. The end states differ. One aims at strong competence in the skill you chose. The other aims at a medium-term goal.

They combine cleanly when Marginal Gains tracking sits inside the 30-day structure. Start with the 30-day plan by naming a meaningful medium-term goal and breaking it into the main near-term goals that measure performance, then look at habits and environment that can block those behaviours. Supplement that by picking the 2 or 3 skills, processes, or attributes that matter most for those goals, and aim to track and stack 1 percent improvements on them. Make pace adjustments and keep the small gains going in the areas that still need them.

A worked example is a student aiming for an A star in A-level examinations 4 months out. The 30-day layer names the important processes for the performance goals, such as scheduling, efficient encoding, and mixed retrieval practice, and it treats procrastination as a limiting habit that needs a scripted action. The gains layer finds the weakest links, here ineffective interleaved retrieval and procrastination. It then targets a 1 percent improvement by practising relational teaching every other day as interleaved retrieval. Against procrastination, it works through BEDSM with attention for a week. Daily reflection then makes tiny technique adjustments as new mistakes and openings appear.

## Open questions

How would you pick the 2 to 3 skills that most affect the medium-term goal you are actually chasing?

What barrier would you write a scripted action for before the next 30 days begin?
