# The Energize Test

The energize test is a check at the end of a workday on whether the work left you with more energy for the evening or used it up. Most people come home drained and spend the first hours decompressing, scrolling or snapping at family. Work that passes the test is a sign you have found something that suits you, and work that fails it every night is worth changing.

## Core takeaways

- Ask each night whether the day's work gave you energy or used it up.
- Energizing work is work where time passes without notice.
- Possessions keep taking time and attention after you buy them.
- Status symbols follow other people's idea of success.
- Money is a late signal of success, and it shifts with the market.

## The nightly check

The test looks at the hours after work. If the day left you calm and present for the people at home, the work suits you. If you need hours to unwind before you can be pleasant, the work is draining you, however well paid. The check is quick, and repeated over weeks it shows which kinds of task do which.

- Passes: you arrive home with your full attention for others.
- Fails: you need to decompress before you can talk to anyone.
- Track which tasks you did on the good days.

## Finding energizing work

People usually know some activities where hours pass unnoticed, and those point toward work that will pass the test. Parents, teachers and school often tell people what they are good at, and those opinions can hide what the person actually enjoys. Games, risk and hard problems are one person's set; another person's might be teaching, building or caring for people. Leaving a degree or career already chosen is hard, and no simple method exists for it.

- List what you would do for hours with no pay.
- Look for an interest that has stayed the same for years.
- Test a small version of it before changing jobs.
- Not everyone wants to build things, and that is fine.

## What drains energy

Things bought for status keep needing time and attention after they are bought. A collection of mechanical watches is a clear case: each needs servicing when unworn, then a winding machine, then a specialist jeweller, then insurance so high the watches are stored elsewhere. Each step is small, and together they take hours that could have gone to the work. Status symbols that used to mark the most successful people are now common on social media, and chasing them means living by someone else's measure.

```
buy watch --> needs servicing --> buy winder
    --> find jeweller --> insurance --> off-site storage
          (hours spent, none on the work)
```

- More possessions means more small upkeep jobs.
- Selling things frees time and attention for the main work.
- Paying to save time, such as a quicker trip, is a different case.
- Money is a late sign that a field happened to pay well.
- Which fields pay well changes, so chasing pay is guesswork.
