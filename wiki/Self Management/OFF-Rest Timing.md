# OFF-Rest Timing

OFF-rest is stopping work at the first slip in sharpness and taking a short rest then, while the work is still good. Push past that into genuine tiredness and recovery costs the rest of the day. The quantity being optimized is total focused output across every cycle, not the opening sprint.

## The sensor and the cycle

OFF stands for Optimised-For-Focus. The cycle is the rule, not the name: stop at dull, rest about a third of the time just worked, pick the next break by how fresh the body still feels.

**Dull** is still functional. Understanding still happens but takes slightly longer; focusing costs a touch more effort; enthusiasm slows. **Tired** is fried: sore eyes, headache, dehydration, the urge to stretch. Recovery now costs more than the extra minutes bought.

Energy restores fastest while reserves are still high. A boulder still rolling needs a nudge; a boulder that has stopped needs a shove. Breaks will arrive while the work still feels good enough to continue. That feeling is the technique working, not a failure of will.

A clock starts with the work. At the first dullness, elapsed time is the cycle's data. Rest is about one-third of that work — 45 minutes of work to 15 of rest, 30 to 10, 20 to 7 — as a starting knob, not an optimum. A block that cannot last 45 minutes has not failed the technique. The sensor is dullness, not the clock. The rest is timed too.

Cycles shrink across the day: an hour in the morning, fifteen to twenty minutes by evening. The schedule flexes. Meticulous first, then intuitive, once the ratio has been felt. Frequent small recoveries hold the whole day near the top of the focus curve.

## What the day is optimizing

A heroic three-hour opening sprint that leaves the afternoon cooked loses to shorter cycles that stay sharp until evening. The unit is the day's sum.

Fixed-interval timers fail both ways. They cut [[Flow State]] short on an arbitrary clock, and they force grinding when the block cannot last the interval. Grinding has a hidden cost: inefficient work, practised, starts to feel like the normal way to work. That is a reason to stop, not a finding that tired work trains tiredness.

Letting the dull signal set the boundary means no alarm interrupts a block that is still holding, and low-quality minutes never accumulate as the day's training data.

## The break menu

The break is gated on how fresh the body still feels.

Fresh enough: a topical break that stays near the work — long sightlines, greenery, a stroll with a notebook of gaps for the next cycle — or a miscellaneous chore done slowly. Nature and a long view restore attention as a class. Tired: active relaxation, five to fifteen minutes of focus training or breath. [[Attention Span and Focus Training]] owns those sitting reps as daily training; this page only picks them as the tired-menu item.

Eye strain can masquerade as sleepiness. Distance vision sometimes releases it. That is a maybe, not a diagnosis.

Five to fifteen minutes of focus training recharges faster than an hour of scrolling. Scrolling is a high-stimulus break that raises distractibility. With under ten minutes, a slow breathing round — cyclic sighing is the public form — is enough. No named method is required.

Genuine tiredness should only appear late. Hitting it after the first cycle means the dull signal was ignored.

The protocol is only for long blocks, about an hour and a half or more, where several cycles fit. With forty-five minutes before dinner, the leftover is spent working.

A break counts as productive only if it moves the day's goals overall, mental and emotional state included. A break that silences work but leaves stress running compounds that stress. [[Recovery]] owns matching break quality to the system that is depleted. [[Focus Management - How to Enter & Recover Inside a Work Block]] owns entry and drift-recovery *inside* the block; this page times the cycles around it.

This is the wrong protocol for a single leftover short block. The one-third ratio is a knob, not a law. Early rest will feel like quitting. The price is a clock on every cycle at first, and rest that arrives while the work still feels good. First cycle ending fried, or rest that itself raises stress, is the quit signal. The checkable marks: the next cycle starts sharper than the last minutes of the one before; tiredness, if it comes, comes late.

The afternoon is still there because the stop happened while the work was still good. Fresh enough to continue is the technique working.
