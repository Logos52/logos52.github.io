# Habits, Productive Routines & PEER

A habit is a behaviour that starts from a cue in your surroundings and runs without a decision. Work that depends on feeling motivated gets done on good days only, while work built into habits gets done on most days. PEER is a five-step routine for building a productive habit: Prep, Easy, Exit, Reward and Peer.

## Core takeaways

- Run routine work on habit and keep motivation for hard days.
- Every habit runs cue, then behaviour, then reward.
- Change the cues around you before relying on willpower.
- Make the first step so small that skipping it feels silly.
- Allow yourself to quit at every step.
- Reward only after the work, and keep the reward locked otherwise.
- Habits take months to become automatic, not three weeks.

## How a habit works

A cue triggers a craving, the craving triggers a behaviour, and a good feeling afterwards makes the behaviour more likely next time. The step from cue to craving can take a fraction of a second, so people rarely notice what set them off. That is why changing your surroundings works better than trying to catch yourself in the moment. Procrastination follows the same loop: a phone in view, an urge, a scroll, a small relief.

```
cue --> craving --> behaviour --> reward
 ^                                   |
 +------ repeated, it runs alone ----+
```

- A cue can be an icon, a notification, a chair, a colour.
- Reward conditions the behaviour, and it works even if you know it.
- Order matters: work first, enjoyable thing second.
- In one study, habits took a median of about 66 days to become automatic.

## The five steps

Each step targets one part of the loop. Prep changes the cues. Easy and Exit lower the effort of starting and remove the fear of a long task. Reward conditions the behaviour, and Peer adds a cost for not doing it.

- Prep: remove at least three cues for the unwanted behaviour.
  - Phone in another room, blockers on, social apps off the home screen.
- Prep: plant at least three cues for the wanted one.
  - Books open on the desk the night before, running shoes on the door.
- Easy: set a minimum goal that needs almost no motivation.
  - "Sit at the desk" or "stand up" counts.
- Exit: build a ladder down from the full goal.
  - Two hours, one hour, fifteen minutes, sit at desk, look away.
  - At each rung you may stop, and usually you keep going.
- Reward: pick something you enjoy and allow it only after the work.
  - Start with two minutes of work, since starting is the hard part.
  - Raise the bar to 10, 30, then 60 minutes as starting gets easy.
  - Keep going if you can; do not stop early to claim it.
- Peer: tell someone the goal and agree a written penalty.
  - A small fine you would dread paying, and not one so large you would lie.

## How to use it

Most days, Prep with Easy and Exit is enough, and the minimum goal is the one step never to drop. The full five steps are for tasks that feel very hard or for someone who has procrastinated for years. As a behaviour becomes automatic, the reward and the peer matter less and can fade out. When the system seems not to work, the usual cause is a step done half-heartedly or skipped.

- Put real effort into the cues; a half-done setup weakens every later step.
- If a cue setup fails, change it and try again.
- Make the unwanted behaviour take many steps.
  - Two separate website blockers, a phone handed to someone else.
- Browse in a private window so recommendations do not load.
- Pick a reward that will not eat into the next task, including sleep.
