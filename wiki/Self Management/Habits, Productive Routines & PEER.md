# Habits, Productive Routines & PEER

A habit is a behaviour that starts from a cue in your surroundings and runs without a decision. Work that depends on feeling motivated gets done on good days only, and work built into a habit gets done on most days. PEER is a routine of five steps for building a productive habit: Prep, Easy, Exit, Reward and Peer.

- Run routine work on habit and keep motivation for hard days.
- Every habit runs cue, then behaviour, then reward.
- Change the cues around you before relying on willpower.
- Make the first step so small that skipping it feels silly.
- Allow yourself to quit at every step.
- Reward only after the work, and keep the reward locked otherwise.
- A habit takes months to become automatic, far longer than three weeks.

## How a habit works

A cue sets off a craving, the craving sets off a behaviour, and a good feeling afterwards makes the behaviour more likely next time. The step from cue to craving can take a fraction of a second, so people rarely notice what set them off. Changing your surroundings therefore works better than trying to catch yourself in the moment. Procrastination runs on the same loop: a phone in view, an urge, a scroll, a small relief.

```
cue --> craving --> behaviour --> reward
 ^                                  |
 +------- repeated, it runs alone --+
```

- A cue can be an icon, a notification, a chair, a colour.
- A reward conditions the behaviour even when you know it does.
- Order matters: work first, enjoyable thing second.
- One study found a median of about 66 days to become automatic.

## The five steps

Each of the five steps works on one part of the loop. Prep changes the cues. Easy and Exit lower the effort of starting and remove the fear of a long task. Reward conditions the behaviour, and Peer adds a cost for skipping it.

- Prep: remove at least three cues for the unwanted behaviour.
  - Phone in another room, blockers on, social apps off the home screen.
- Prep: plant at least three cues for the wanted one.
  - Books open on the desk overnight, running shoes by the door.
- Easy: set a minimum goal that needs almost no motivation.
  - "Sit at the desk" or "stand up" counts.
- Exit: build a ladder down from the full goal.
  - Two hours, one hour, fifteen minutes, sit at the desk, look away.
  - At each rung you may stop, and usually you keep going.
- Reward: pick something you enjoy and allow it only after the work.
  - Start at two minutes of work, since starting is the hard part.
  - Raise it to 10, 30, then 60 minutes as starting gets easy.
  - Keep going if you can, and never stop early to claim it.
- Peer: tell someone the goal and agree a written penalty.
  - A small fine you would dread paying.
  - Too large a fine makes people lie about the work.

## How to use it

On most days Prep with Easy and Exit is enough, and the minimum goal is the one step never to drop. The full five steps are for a task that feels very hard, or for someone who has procrastinated for years. As a behaviour becomes automatic, the reward and the peer matter less and can fade out. When the routine seems not to work, the usual cause is a step done half-heartedly or skipped.

- Put real effort into the cues.
- A half-done setup weakens every later step.
- If a cue setup fails, change it and try again.
- Make the unwanted behaviour take many steps.
  - Two separate website blockers, a phone handed to someone else.
- Browse in a private window so recommendations do not load.
- A reward must not cut into the next task or sleep.
