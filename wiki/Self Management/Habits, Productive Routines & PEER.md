# Habits, Productive Routines & PEER

Waiting on motivation to start work burns the reserve you need on hard days. Studying, movement, and focus training can run like brushing teeth: autopilot for the default case, motivation saved for rare pushes. Chronic procrastination is also a trained habit, so motivation often fights downhill. PEER (Prep, Easy, Exit, Reward, Peer) is a five-step setup that tilts cues toward wanted action, shrinks the first move, and adds accountability until the routine holds on its own.

The method has a short learning curve when every step is done fully. Skipping a step or running them halfway is the usual reason people report failure.

## Prep

A habit runs from cue to response. Prep shapes cues before you spend willpower. Find at least three cues that start unwanted behavior and make them hard to reach (blockers, distance, lockouts). Find at least three cues for wanted behavior and place them where you cannot miss them. If the first layout fails, change the room and try again. Environment moves behavior more reliably than repeated pep talks.

## Easy

Write the real goal, then list steps backward until the first few are absurdly small (minimum viable goals). For procrastination, standing up or sitting at the desk can be a valid first goal. Students often set the first step too high.

For behavior you want to stop, map how few steps the unwanted path already has (one tap to open a feed). Prep and Easy together lengthen the bad path and shorten the good one.

## Exit

At each tiny goal, give yourself explicit permission to stop. The live decision is only the next small step or leave. That keeps the full target from scaring you off at the threshold.

## Reward

Pick two or three rewards you enjoy that will not spill into the next commitment. Lock them until work is shown, or ask someone else to release them. Require a real stretch of work before payout; you decide what counts as enough. Do not leave the reward in view and try to resist by willpower.

## Peer

Add one person who will check the agreement. Write a consequence that is unpleasant but not so extreme you would lie to escape it. Drop this step once the action runs without it.

PEER pairs with [[wiki/Self Management/Time Management, Attention & Scheduling|procrastination setup]] (room, log, schedule, tiny first step) and with [[wiki/Self Management/Priority 0+1 System|Priority 0+1]] so the habit you install is the one worth protecting first each day.

## When PEER fits and when it does not

Use PEER when you start strong and fade, or when consistency breaks every time life gets noisy. It is weaker when the goal itself is wrong—run [[wiki/Planning and Automation|Reverse Goal Setting]] first so you are not automating a task you no longer want.

Common failures: rewards left in view, first steps still too large, peer consequences so harsh you lie, or Prep done once instead of iterated until the cue reliably fires. Another failure is running PEER on five goals at once; install one route, then add the next.

As the habit stabilizes, peel steps off deliberately. Keep Prep lean, shrink Peer, and let the routine run on cues you still maintain. The method is scaffolding, not a permanent crutch.
