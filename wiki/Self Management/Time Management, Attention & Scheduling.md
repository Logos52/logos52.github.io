# Time Management, Attention & Scheduling

> Not yet reviewed by Wedge.

Managing time breaks into three layers that fail in different ways. Task management decides which work is worth doing at all. Scheduling puts that work onto the week and the day. Prioritization picks the order, so the work with the most impact gets done even on a day that falls apart. Underneath all three sits attention, because a perfect schedule still wastes the hours if your mind is elsewhere during them.

## The three layers

A good calendar cannot rescue poor choices about what goes on it. Gut feeling is a weak guide here, because it ranks urgent things as the most important ones. The result is urgency trapping. Close deadlines and homework fill every slot, while slow, high-impact work such as building a skill or fixing a weakness never gets a turn. The fix is to place important but not urgent work early in the day, before the urgent load arrives. Then even an unfinished day has done its most valuable work.

## The child pages

**Prioritization.** [[wiki/Self Management/Priority 0+1 System|Priority 0+1 System]] sets one or two top priorities each day, places them in the schedule first, and protects them when the day goes wrong. Everything else fits around them. Busy weeks fall slightly behind and free weeks catch up, so progress stays steady.

**Scheduling.** [[wiki/Self Management/Study Scheduling|Study Scheduling]] applies the same idea to study, with spaced review fixed into recurring weekly and monthly blocks.

**Attention.** [[wiki/Self Management/Flow State|Attention, Focus & Flow]] covers getting into focus, staying there, and running the day as a chain of priorities rather than clock slots. [[wiki/Self Management/Attention Span and Focus Training|Attention Span and Focus Training]] covers building the baseline capacity over months. [[wiki/Self Management/Hyper-Focus and Hyper-Distractibility|Hyper-Focus and Hyper-Distractibility]] adjusts the methods for people at either extreme.

**Starting.** [[wiki/Self Management/Procrastination - a System Problem|Procrastination: a System Problem]] covers the block that never starts, and fixes it through setup rather than motivation.

**Gaps between tasks.** [[wiki/Decision Making/Decisional Delays|Decisional Delays]] covers the time lost deciding what to do next. That gap is hard to spot, since time feels like it is passing while you work on a task and not in the pauses between tasks. Good plans remove most of it, and a nightly look back trains you to catch the rest.

**Energy.** [[wiki/Self Management/The Energize Test|The Energize Test]] asks whether the day's work leaves you with energy for the evening.

## What these practices are for

Scheduling and attention create the conditions that study and deep work need, so they belong to [[wiki/Dimensions/Self-Management|Self-Management]]. They only set aside the time. The learning methods that fill it live under [[wiki/Techniques/Techniques - Learning Craft|Techniques - Learning Craft]]. The test of the whole cluster is an ordinary day, not a good one.

## Open questions

- How do I check, at the end of a week, whether my important but not urgent work actually got the early slots?
- How do I tell when a problem belongs to task choice, to the schedule, or to attention, so I fix the right layer?
