# Study Scheduling

A repeating calendar removes the daily question of what to study. The template below is enough to start. Days can move. What should not move is the order of the work. A first pass that maps how ideas relate comes before detailed review, and later sessions ask you to bring the material back from memory.

If the pile of material is large, add a review in the middle of the month, or a 2-day review in the middle of the week.

You can move the days. Saturday or Monday morning can stand in for Sunday. You can prestudy 2 weeks at a time rather than 1 week. Later practice can stretch that first pass across a whole topic of about 4 weeks to 6 weeks.

## Protect the first pass

A good first pass through new material saves revision later, because you forget less. When you have to choose between a strong prestudy session and reviewing older material, choose prestudy. You can catch up on review. You cannot as easily undo a week of class that you walked into with no map.

## When the calendar does not hold

Struggling to follow the template is often a sign of a more basic gap. Either time and task management is slipping, or retrieval sessions are being put ahead of a good first pass. Those are cues to go back to earlier self-management work, even if you have already moved on to later techniques. Strong learners move back and forward by watching where their gaps are.

Give the new calendar a couple of weeks. Scheduling itself does not take long to learn. Making it fit your week does take some experiments and a weekly look at what failed.

## Open questions

How often did I drop this week's first pass so I could review something I had already seen?

How would the week change if the end-of-week retrieval session were a fixed block I refused to move?
