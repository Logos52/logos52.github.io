# Techniques in School

A time box is a fixed amount of time assigned to a piece of work, with a hard stop at the end. Forty minutes is one example. The number is whatever is honest for the task. When the box closes, the work is handed in at the standard the grading asked for, not the standard you could have reached with three more hours.

A time box is not a time block. A time block reserves a slot that may run long if the work is still live. A time box stops when the time is up.

The reason to cap graded work is the time it leaves over. That leftover time is where a real study method can run. On a notebook nobody will ever inspect, that means using whatever encodes the material rather than whatever resembles the assignment. None of this requires arguing with anyone about grades.

## Core takeaways

- A time box has a hard stop and a time block does not. That stop is the difference between the two.
- Weighted work gets a box sized to the standard it is graded against. Hand it in when the box closes.
- On unweighted work, do the minimum that meets the requirement.
- The leftover time is the point. Use it for methods that encode the material.
- If notes are required in a linear or passive form and nobody inspects them, use the better method. Once the notebook is marked against the teacher's format, meet the format.
- Schedule the important work first. What is left over, and it will be less than you are used to, is the box for compulsory unimportant work.

## Working inside the classroom

Mainstream teacher training can leave classroom methods outdated or based on anecdote, and occasionally harmful. That is a possibility, not a measured rate. It is also not a reason to pick a fight that costs you the grade.

If notes are required in a linear or passive form and nobody inspects them, run the better method anyway. The moment the notebook is marked against the teacher's format, that workaround is spent. Satisfy the requirement.

On unweighted work, do the minimum that meets the requirement. Perfectionism on low-value mandatory tasks uses up the evening. The price of a workaround is whatever marks that assignment carried. A workaround that costs you the grade is not one to run.

Use class time as a quiz. Hunt for what is still unclear and ask the deeper question. Retrieval during the lesson works better than sitting through a pass of material you already have. If the material is already known, doing homework during class is an option. It is not a rule, and it stops being available the moment the room forbids it.

## What the cap does

Time blocking reserves a slot that may run long if the work is still live. Time boxing does the opposite. When the hour ends, the work stops. The cap is the only part of this worth using.

A tiny box will not finish a real exam. The limit is a reasonable number of hours, not a squeeze that makes the work impossible.

## Two kinds of work that need a box

Boxing fits two opposite cases at once.

The first is work that is very important, not urgent, and too large to finish in one sitting. It gets a recurring box so it is not crowded out. Self-study is the case here, with the encoding methods already chosen.

The second is work that is very unimportant and still compulsory. It gets a box so it reaches a sufficient standard without running past what it is worth. Unweighted homework is the named case. The goal is the best outcome in the time available, not a perfect one.

## Putting the important work first

Schedule the important work first. The leftover time, smaller than habit, is the box for the compulsory unimportant items, finished as fast as that box allows. [[wiki/Self Management/Study Scheduling|Study Scheduling]] is where boxed study time goes once the cap has protected it. The same hard stop is a counter to [[Procrastination - a System Problem]]: open-ended delay ends when the hour ends.

If the first week runs over the box and the important work is what slipped, the order is backwards.

## What a week looks like

The hour ends. Work that earned more hours still has them. Unweighted work was finished inside the box. The graded format was satisfied, and the requirement was not picked as a fight. [[Time Management, Attention & Scheduling]] is the cluster this cap sits under: deciding, protecting attention, turning that into a day.

## How to practice this

1. Give your next weighted assignment a box of forty minutes. Hand it in when the box closes. Notice whether the mark matches the standard the assignment asked for.
2. Check whether anyone inspects your notebook. If nobody does, use the method that encodes the material instead of the required form.
3. Do the next unweighted homework at the minimum that meets the requirement. Notice how much of the evening is left afterwards.
4. In your next class, write down what is still unclear and ask the deeper question. Notice whether you recall more than from sitting through the pass.
5. Put a recurring box on your self-study before you schedule anything else. Notice whether the compulsory work still fits in the time left.
6. At the end of the first week, check what overran. If the box overran and the important work slipped, the order is backwards.
