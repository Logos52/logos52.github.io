# Positions

A people is a set of values rather than a bloodline, and on that ground I am 100% American: born here, served. No other ground was ever available to me — not white enough to count as white, not Asian enough to count as Asian — and the Army was the first place that took me for what I could do instead of for who I was. America has givers and takers, and the line between them is wanting to give more than you take. I have been on both sides of it, which is why I treat it as a direction rather than a category.

Responsibility does not transmit by descent, for the same reason a skill doesn't: what a person can do is acquired, and nothing acquired is inherited. Descent-guilt is incoherent rather than merely unfair, and admission turns on capacity to give — either a culture known for it, or a person who has it.

Assimilation is a spectrum and where an arrival sits on it is the question. The 2020 mass intake needs remigration, and what I do not want is to be ruled by arrivals holding voting power over local policy. The wage argument is the one that can be made in public and it is the one I would make; it does not make me anti-corporate on labour.

I am Asian-American, my family assimilated completely — I speak only English, none of theirs — and I was ashamed of that for a long time. I am not ashamed of it now, because what the shame was about turns out to be a path I count as a success. Banana was the insult and it was accurate, and the man it describes served honorably. As an Asian I have been on the receiving end of the discrimination white people describe.

I am for merit over DEI, and not because it cost me anything, though it did and it followed me into the job market. A remedy that sorts by category keeps the category doing the work, which advances racism rather than hindering it. Nobody should be compelled to do business with anyone either, which is what freedom of association means when it is real.

A measured gap does not establish discrimination, and the causes are mostly cultural — that is where I put them, and it is why they are located here rather than on the disparity page, which stays an inference page. The accusation is a mechanism for ending discussion, and I have met that argument in people. The grooming cover-up is the same mechanism at full strength: naming a protected pattern ends a career, so the record stays empty. Mainstream media, especially in England, is captured that way.

Schooling is the one I saw from inside, having taught in it for years. A seated format filters by sex, and the boys it fails get explained afterward as a problem with boys rather than as a problem with the format. Women's suffrage I understand and do not feel strongly about; the page on it examines an argument and is not a position of mine.

I am generally for LGB, and would rather the conduct be quietly accepted than advertised or made into a duty to participate. T is gender dysphoria and should be treated as an illness. Adults can do what they want, and youth transitioning is a hard no. Abortion I would stop after the first trimester rather than ban outright, and I still don't like them.

I am not Christian — secular and agnostic, living in America — and I can agree with some of Christianity's values without following it. Arguments from the Bible do not interest me, because what a faith asks of the state is the religion question I care about. That is also why Islamic reform interests me, as somebody outside the faith looking at what it asks.

Socialism names real faults and then expands the state that produced them. A debt the holder profits from when you miss, and that bankruptcy cannot kill, is extraction. A moral vocabulary only has leverage on people who still hold the commitments it appeals to. Remove the top 2% and society stops; remove the bottom 2% and it improves on income, crime and net giving. Whether a small organized group always ends up governing, I am not sure.

That puts me near the centre of today, which is not where it would have put me twenty years ago — I was far left then, and by that measure I now read as left-of-center, because the window moved further than I did. Part of what moved me is that the left I was in dislikes America and thinks it needs redoing, and the Army is where I stopped agreeing. Pro-America as it has traditionally been: not perfect, better than the alternatives. Generally pro-MAGA and not all the way there. Gun control does not interest me much either way.

This note is the exception, because hiding the positions is how the pages start speaking for me.
