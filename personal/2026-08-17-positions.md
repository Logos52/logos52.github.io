# Positions

## Where the whole sits

- Measured by today's standards these positions sit near the middle, and measured by the standards of twenty years ago they would count as left of centre, because the range of opinion that counts as normal has moved further than the positions have.
- In favour of America as America has traditionally been, which is not perfect and is still better than the alternatives.
- Generally in favour of Trump's MAGA movement, though not a full supporter of it.
- On gun control there is no strong view either way.

## Belonging

- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Mass Immigration - Cohesion|Mass Immigration - Cohesion]]** — The people who came into the United States in the wave of immigration that began in 2020 under the Biden administration should be sent back, and people who arrive should not get a vote over local policy. Whether someone who arrives belongs depends on how far they have taken on the American way of life, so belonging is a matter of degree rather than a yes or a no. The argument against mass immigration that works in public is that it pushes down wages for the people already in the country, and a person who makes that argument is not anti-business for making it. The page asks who gets let in and whether the people let in can live under the same rules once they are in.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association|The Civil Rights Act - Freedom of Association]]** — Nobody should be forced by law to do business with anyone, because being free to associate means being free not to associate, and the act ended that freedom in order to end refusal by race. The page weighs what was ended against what was gained.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Woke Retreat - The New Electorate Claim|The Woke Retreat - The New Electorate Claim]]** — Since 2024 the American left has been moving away from the positions it held in the 2010s, such as pronoun rules and men in women's sport, and one claim says that the immigrants of those years made this happen by rejecting those positions and now deciding elections. That claim gets the order the wrong way round, because the groups that do not take on the American way of life vote for the Democrats for the reason that the Democrats tolerate that, which means the party's permissiveness comes first and the vote follows it. The page tests the claim and finds no link between the immigrants and the retreat.

## Gaps, and what gets read into them

- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference|Racial Egalitarianism - The Disparity Inference]]** — A gap in outcomes between two groups does not on its own show that anyone discriminated against the group that came out behind, because most of the gap comes from differences in culture, in what families expect and in what people value. The page examines the step from a gap in outcomes to an accusation of discrimination.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/DEI as Anti-Discrimination|DEI as Anti-Discrimination]]** — Hire and promote on merit, and drop diversity, equity, and inclusion programs, which are the programs that hire and promote by race, because a fix that sorts people by race in order to undo sorting by race keeps race as the thing that decides, so it makes more of the very thing it was meant to get rid of. The page weighs the business case for those programs and the moral case for them separately.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/White Guilt|White Guilt]]** — Nobody is guilty for what their ancestors did, because guilt comes from what a person does, and what a person can do is something they build in their own lifetime, so it cannot be handed down any more than a skill can. Guilt by descent is not only unfair, it also makes no sense. The page asks whether an inherited debt of that kind can make sense at all.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Who Gets Called Racist|Who Gets Called Racist]]** — Calling someone racist is mostly a way of ending an argument rather than a description of the person. The page looks at what the accusation does and what it takes to answer it.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Grooming Gang Cover-Up|The Grooming Gang Cover-Up]]** — Gangs of men abused girls across British towns for twenty years, and the police, the social services, and the prosecutors hid it, because anyone who named the men's ethnic and religious background lost their job, so nobody named it and nothing went on record. The British press stayed quiet because naming the background would have cost them their jobs too. The page traces why staying silent was worth it for the people who stayed silent and what it cost.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Moral Language as Leverage|Moral Language as Leverage]]** — Moral words only move people who still hold the values behind them, and on anyone else they have no effect, no matter who is saying them. The page is about how a society keeps order with moral words.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Schooling - The Format Filter|Schooling - The Format Filter]]** — Sitting still at a desk all day suits girls better than it suits boys, and when boys fail at it, schools blame the boys instead of the way the day is set up. The page follows what a school day set up that way taught each sex about the other.

## Faith

- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/What Faiths Demand of the State|What Faiths Demand of the State]]** — The question that matters about a religion is what it would have the state do, and that matters more than whether the religion is true. These positions come from someone who is not Christian, who is secular and agnostic, and who holds that some Christian values are good ones even without the belief behind them, so a quote from the Bible does not count as an argument here. Islam's reformers are worth watching for the same question, which is what they would have the state do, and a non-Muslim can watch that from outside the faith. The page sets out what Christianity and Islam each ask of the state.

## Money and the state

- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Socialism as Moral Correction|Socialism as Moral Correction]]** — Socialism starts from real problems, such as low wages and people left behind, and then makes the state bigger, even though the state is what caused those problems. The page takes up the moral case for socialism that the economic case rests on.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Financial Slavery|Financial Slavery]]** — A debt that pays the lender more when the borrower misses a payment, and that bankruptcy cannot cancel, is a way of taking people's work without owning them. The page describes how that works on a whole population.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/The Organized Minority|The Organized Minority]]** — There is no settled position here, because whether an organized few always end up running things is still an open question, though the page argues that they do.
- **The top and bottom two percent.** If the two percent of a society who are most able are removed, the society stops working, and if the two percent who take the most and give the least are removed instead, the society does better on income, on crime, and on how much people give. There is no page for this yet.

## Positions with no page behind them

- **Being American.** Being American comes from what a person holds and does, so it can be taken on by someone whose parents came from somewhere else.
- **Givers and takers.** America has people who give more than they take and people who take more than they give, and the difference between them is what a person wants to do, so a person can move from one to the other and neither is a fixed kind of person.
- **Who a person is.** Who a person is comes from what that person learns to do, not from what they are born as, and nothing a person gains that way can be inherited by anyone else.
- **Abortion.** Stop abortion after the first trimester rather than ban it outright, and allowing it that far does not mean liking it.
