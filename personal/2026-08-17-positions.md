# Positions

The political pages examine other people's arguments. The positions below are mine, and they are narrower.

America has givers and takers. I have taken and also given. The line is wanting to give more than you take, versus the reverse. A people is a set of values. I consider myself 100% American on that ground: born here, served, willing to put my life on the line. The Army is what replaced loathing the country for not doing enough with love of it.

I do not believe in descent-guilt. Admission is capability of giving — a culture known for it, or individual capacity. The 2020 mass intake needs remigration. Assimilation is a spectrum. I would rather not be ruled by mass immigrants who have voting power over local policy. The wage argument is the one that can be made in public. I am not 100% anti-corporate on labour.

I am Asian-American. My family assimilated completely — I speak only English, no longer theirs — and I used to be ashamed of that. I am not. Banana was the insult; it is also an accurate description of a path I now take as a success after serving honorably. As an Asian I have been on the same kind of discrimination white people describe. Their plight has become mine.

I am for merit over DEI. I landed on the wrong side of it as an Asian. It followed me into the job market. I think it advances racism more than it hinders it. Compulsion to deal is bad. I would want some shops able to refuse some people.

Schooling is what I lived. A seated format filters by sex. Gender is the story told afterward. Women's suffrage I understand and I am not hard-headed about. That page is an examination, not a position of mine.

I am generally for LGB and would rather the conduct be quietly accepted than advertised or made into a duty to participate. T is gender dysphoria, treated as an illness. Adults can do what they want. Youth transitioning is a hard no.

I am not Christian. I can agree with some of Christianity's values and still not follow it — secular, agnostic, living in America. Bible arguments do not interest me. Islamic reform does, from outside the faith.

I am generally centrist relative to today. Relative to twenty years ago I read as left-of-center. I was far left then. Times changed. Pro-America as it has traditionally been. Not perfect. Better than the alternatives. Generally pro-MAGA, and not 100% that either. Not a total criminal ban on abortion; a stop after the first trimester. I still don't like them. My position is not extreme. Gun control does not interest me much.

Remove the top 2% and society stops. Remove the bottom 2% and it improves. Income, crime, net giving — all of it. I am not 100% sure on the story that a small organized group always governs.

A measured gap does not establish discrimination. Collective guilt fails. Responsibility does not transmit by descent. The accusation functions as a mechanism that ends discussion. I have met that argument in people. Causes are mostly cultural. That location lives here. The disparity page is an inference page and stays one.

The grooming cover-up is a machine: naming a protected pattern ends a career, so the record stays empty. Remigration is a different tool. Socialism names real faults and then expands the state that produced them. A debt the holder profits from when you miss, that bankruptcy cannot kill, is extraction. What a faith asks of the state is the religion question I care about. A moral vocabulary works on whoever still holds the commitments.

Premises in that wing default to true. I do not like showing values directly on those pages. This note is the exception, because hiding the positions is how the pages start speaking for me.
