# The Two Egos

For as long as I've been judging other people's work, ego meant one thing: the author showing up where the subject should be, and the work getting smaller for it. I never liked seeing it, and the conclusion seemed to follow — keep yourself out of what you make. This site ran on that conclusion for months. The notes stayed clean, organized, impersonal. And slowly they started to read like anyone could have built them, which for a second brain is a strange kind of failure. The one thing a second brain shouldn't be is anyone's.

Working the tension out properly split the word in two. [[wiki/Concepts/The Two Meanings of Ego|The Two Meanings of Ego]] carries the full derivation; the short version is that the ego I disliked — self-regard, the author asking to be admired — was never the same substance as the author being present in the work at all. I had always read them as one dial, one quantity to turn down until the work is polite. They turn out to be two. A page can hold a whole person and ask for nothing.

So this section, which I put off opening for a long time. Notes with the person left in, dated so the positions can move. Each one has to pass the test the derivation landed on: does a sentence change what a reader sees of the subject, or only what they think of me? The first kind is the reason to write at all. The second kind is the old ego, and it dies here the same as it would anywhere.
