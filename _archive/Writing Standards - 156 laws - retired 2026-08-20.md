# Writing Standards

Read any line and ask what it wants from the reader. The only passing answer is nothing.

Two terms carry the file. **Selfhood** is one person's judgment left visible in the writing: this cut and no other, this example kept, that claim turned away. **Self-regard** is the writing asking to be admired. Every genre runs selfhood as high as honesty allows and holds self-regard at zero.

**Scope: every project.** llm-knowledge-base, cos, tsumugu, wnac, and any repo that comes later.

**What this file does not govern.** Internal documents are not the wiki. This file, PRDs, decision notes, agent instructions, workbench drafts, and repo docs are instruments. Nothing below applies to them.

**How the file is arranged.** Sections 2 through 12 bind every genre. Section 13 holds one sheet per genre, and a sheet may only *add* a law or *relax* one it names by number. A law that binds everywhere never lives in a sheet. Section 15 is the specimen corpus: a proposed law is wrong if a rejected specimen passes it, and wrong if an accepted specimen fails it.

Laws are numbered so a review can cite them.

---

## 1. Recorded reversals

**L1.** Wiki and personal pages formerly opened with a run-up, the thesis landing inside the opening paragraph. Reversed: they open payload first, and the run-up survives on blog posts alone.

**L2.** Pages were formerly grandfathered, with no retrofit sweep permitted. Reversed: existing pages are regenerated under these laws.

---

## 2. Pick the register

**L3.** The page's job selects the register, never the folder it sits in.

| The page is | Register | Opens | Density | Em dash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A concept telling | wiki | payload first | positional | legal |
| Directions, a reference, a structure, a workflow, a position statement | operational | claim at full strength | positional | legal |
| An argument for a decision | decision | the verdict, inside the first two sentences | high | legal |
| A blog post, explainer, or public essay | blog | run-up, thesis inside the opening paragraph | flow over compression | **banned** |
| The author's own life | personal | payload first | low by design | legal |
| A domain compressed to doctrine | condensed | total-compression paragraph | maximal | legal |
| Chrome: heroes, sub-lines, about pages, badges, buttons, nav labels, meta descriptions, og tags | front-facing | noun phrase or one natural sentence | n/a | **banned** |
| A map of content, hub, or index | hub | its own form, §13.8 | n/a | legal |

**L4.** Tiebreak when two rows look plausible: if the first sentence needs a definition before it can be parsed, the page takes a run-up. If a stranger could act on sentence one alone, it opens cold.

**L5.** A blog post and a hero line share the register and not the structure. Every law governing chrome governs a post's prose too. The chrome sheet holds only what is peculiar to a line with no body under it.

---

## 3. How a draft is made

**L6.** A page is drafted as one continuous explanation to a real person, in the voice you would use talking them through it. The laws are a filter applied afterward, never a template assembled from. Prose built from rule-compliant fragments comes out glueless, and no cohesion pass repairs it.

**L7.** The filter only deletes and swaps. It deletes: address to a reader, staged transactions, source names to the footnotes, delete-list words, and any clause the page does not need. It swaps a wrong word for a right one. It never recomposes a sentence that was natural speech.

**L8.** A failure regenerates; it is never patched. Which layer failed decides the scope, and a sound structure is preserved rather than re-rolled.

- **Structure failed** — the opening does not name the whole, the page ends inside a part, a part cannot be tied back, the shape does not match the material. The whole page regenerates and the shape is re-derived.
- **Prose failed inside a sound structure** — cadence, an appended formulation, abstract subjects, a passage that does not flow. That passage regenerates alone, against the existing shape plan, with the prior passage dead as material. The parts that hold are kept, because re-rolling a good structure to fix a bad paragraph is how a good page becomes a worse one.
- **Never line patching.** Editing sentences in place is what leaves seams. A passage comes again whole, re-derived from the shape plan and the source material.

**L8a.** A regenerated passage re-stitches to its neighbours. The first sentence of the new passage picks up a thread from the sentence before it, and the sentence after it still receives what the passage now hands forward. The join is where a passage regeneration shows, so the join is checked.

**L9.** A fragment surviving a regeneration untouched is the tell that no regeneration happened.

**L10.** The assembly tells: a setup with no payoff; ideas fired without connecting tissue; paragraphs that follow the skeleton but do not flow; sources filing past at one sentence per authority; joints that announce a paragraph's function; a definition landing before the reader has felt the problem it names.

**L11.** Confidence is sincerity, not a dial. Assert only what you would say to the listener, and where conversation would qualify, the qualification arrives with the sentence rather than in a pass afterward.

**L12.** A law joins this file only when a fault repeats, because rule accretion degrades generation. Where two laws conflict the most recent ruling wins and the reversal is recorded in §1. Provenance lives in the journal.

## 4. Openings

**L13.** The first sentence carries the payload: the conclusion, the definition, or the finding.

**L14.** The move is chosen before drafting, from the page's subject. The opening sentences are written inside the one-shot generation of the whole page.

**L15.** The palette comes from [[wiki/Writing Craft/Opening Moves Catalog|the Opening Moves Catalog]], derived from verbatim-verified openings by the writers this vault admires. Its master frame is the abstract: give the conclusion away up front, and a hook becomes impossible rather than merely forbidden.

**The catalog owns the mechanics; this file owns which moves are legal.** Before drafting, read the assigned move's own entry in the catalog, including its specimen. The one-line names below are a roster, not a spec, and a writer who works from them instead of the catalog will implement the summary rather than the move. Where a catalog gloss and its specimen disagree, the specimen wins.

Four moves carry most pages.

- **Copular definition.** The term is the grammatical subject: "X is a Y that does Z." Default for concept and technique pages.
- **Plain causal fact.** The finding stated cold, two named things joined by cause.
- **Definitional stack.** The whole named in sentence one, then its terms defined in order. "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep." Never the components first: a page that defines three parts and names the thing in sentence four has opened in the parts.
- **Dated checkable fact.** An object in the world with its date and magnitude.

**L16.** Four narrow moves. **Attributed folklore**: assert that the claim is asserted, then adjudicate it. **Opposing position at full strength, then flat dissent**: the rejected view in its own vocabulary, disagreement in a plain sentence one paragraph later. **Retell the object intact**: the thing summarized fairly, the defect arriving as a trailing clause. **Unhedged absolute, scope repaired next**.

**L17.** Two moves are fenced to personal pages: **the debt**, where the page exists because a prior claim is outstanding, and **own practice, first person**.

**L18.** Four moves are banned. **Epistemic preamble** talks about the document. **Real question** is the reveal with a one-sentence fuse. **Scope fence** fronts the discarded reading. **Occasion plus deliverable** opens on the author's standing.

**L19.** Hard limits on any opener: 45 words, one subordinate clause, no wikilink in sentence one.

**L20.** The delete test. Remove sentence one; if the claim still stands and still comes first, sentence one was preparation and comes out.

**L20a. The lead is the whole, not the sharpest part.** Sentence one answers *what is this*. It never answers *how does it work*, *how does it split*, or *why does it matter* — those are parts, and a part put first reads as machinery arriving before the thing it belongs to.

The whole is the subject itself, named in words the reader already owns. Mechanisms, taxonomies, causal chains, component counts, and the page's sharpest claim are all parts, however true and however central. They come after.

Accepted: *"Flow state is a condition of full absorption in a single task."* That is what the thing is; every condition and lever follows it.

Struck, all five on the same fault — each opens on a mechanism of its subject rather than the subject:

```text
The main reason a high saver stops needing a salary decades sooner is that every dollar
not spent does two jobs: it adds to the portfolio, and it lowers the annual spending…
        — how saving works, before what the page is about
The word ego names two unrelated things, and each one takes the opposite treatment…
        — a lexical fact about the subject, not the subject
Language study time splits three ways, and each session picks one of the three by…
        — a taxonomy and its routing rule
Interiority runs on one causal chain: a past event, the false belief it installed…
        — a model, in a word the reader does not own
Spaced Interleaved Retrieval is a study loop that rebuilds material from memory at
lengthening delays, with related topics mixed together, and returns a list of…
        — correct, and three mechanisms deep before the reader knows why to care
```

**Two consequences follow, and they are symptoms rather than separate laws.** A part needs several clauses before it means anything, so a parts-first opener runs compound: a claim, its mechanism, and sub-mechanisms stacked behind a colon. And a part is usually named in the page's own vocabulary, so the opener reaches for a term the reader does not own. Fix the whole and both go.

**L20a-i. The opening must be a sentence only this page could write.** Naming the whole is necessary and not sufficient: the sentence also has to deliver something the reader did not arrive with. Ask whether the reader could have written it themselves. If yes, sentence one spent itself on nothing.

This decides whether a definition or a claim opens the page:

- **The reader does not own the term** — a house coinage, a technical name, a term whose real content is not in its surface. The definition *is* the payload, and it must be this vault's specific version rather than a dictionary gloss. Accepted: *"Whole-part-whole is a test of understanding, taken by teaching a subject out loud from memory with every source closed."* Nobody could write that without the page.
- **The reader already owns the term** — procrastination, decision making, minimalism, saving. A definition delivers nothing, so the opening carries the page's **operating claim** instead: the thing the page asserts and spends itself earning. Accepted cold theses: *"A language model's default voice is trained self-regard."* · *"Bots watch; the desk changes things."*
- **Half-owned** — the term is familiar but its real content is not. Either can work, and the definition has to do more work than usual to pay.

Struck for opening on a definition the reader already had:

```text
Procrastination is deciding to do something and then not doing it, usually while doing
something else instead.
        — everyone owns this. The page is called "a System Problem" and its claim sits
          unused in the title while sentence one glosses the word.
Decision making is choosing among options without knowing how the choice will turn out.
        — a dictionary line. Nothing here is this page's.
Removing most of what is in a home is what stops it from taking time, space, and attention
every day.
        — not a definition but an argued benefit: "why it matters", which is a part.
```

The test in its portable form, from L66: move the sentence onto a stranger's page on the same subject. If it still fits, it is not finished.

**L20a-ii. The operating claim must be contestable, and a cousin of it is not it.** Where the reader owns the term and the opening carries a claim, that claim is the one somebody would argue with. Generalising it until nothing contradicts it produces a sentence that is true, well-formed, and empty.

The test: **name who disagrees.** If nobody does, sentence one is not the operating claim, whatever else it is.

Struck, with the claim it was softened from:

```text
The same difficulty produces opposite behavior depending on what the person expects it to
mean about them.
        — nobody disagrees; any page on the subject could carry it. The claim available was
          "a growth mindset is not a belief you adopt; it is a prediction your nervous system
          makes about what happens after a mistake" — which the popular version of the concept
          directly contradicts.
```

And the reason it shipped: the writer asked itself whether a reader could have written the line and answered "partly — a cousin of it." **Partly is a no.** A cousin of the sentence existing in the reader's head means the sentence delivered nothing, and the honest answer to that question has only two values.

**L20b. One proposition, in owned words.** Sentence one carries one thing. A colon introducing a list, a second clause adding a mechanism, an *and each*, a *because*, or a term the reader would have to be taught all mean it is carrying more. If a stranger could not repeat what the page is about after sentence one, it has not named the whole.

**L20c. The qualifier extends the what; it never starts the how.** A copular opener may carry a trailing clause, and that clause says more about what the thing *is* or what it is *like* — never how it is produced, what it costs, or what conditions bring it on. Those are parts and they belong in the body.

Three openers on the same subject, written the same day from the same evidence. Accepted:

```text
Flow state is a condition of full absorption in a single task, in which attention holds
without forcing and the sense of time recedes.
```

The qualifier describes the experience of the thing. Still the whole. Struck:

```text
Flow State is sustained absorption in one task, the condition where the work holds
attention and none of it goes to keeping it there.
        — the qualifier turns into mechanism: where the attention budget goes
Flow state is a working condition in which a single task holds your attention
continuously, because the work is matched to your current skill, the goal is clear, and
nothing inside the block keeps asking you to decide something else.
        — "because" opens the causes; three conditions is the body arriving in sentence one
```

**L21.** Moves are routed by genre and subject rather than chosen by hand per page. Where routing is genuinely ambiguous, escalation is by cohort: one board of fully-written options on a representative page binds every page in that cohort. Serial one-at-a-time candidates are banned.

**L22.** Orientation is not a hook. Orientation transfers facts; a hook wants a feeling.

## 5. The shape of a page

**L23.** Every piece opens in the whole and closes in the whole. A piece that ends inside a part just stops.

**L24.** Each part connects back to the whole as it enters, and the whole may resurface between parts. Legal shapes are any run of parts framed by wholes at both ends: WPW, WPWPW, WPPW, WPPWPPW. Illegal shapes miss a frame — PPPP never opens in the whole, WPPPP never returns to it. A long run of parts is broken by a full W between them; monotony is the tell ("too many parts needs a W"). Binds every wiki page (sharpened 2026-08-13).

**L25.** The opening whole takes each genre's own form: a wiki page's thesis, an operational page's bare claim, a decision document's verdict, a post's run-up, a condensed page's compression paragraph.

**L26.** The close returns to the opening whole advanced by the body. Never a replay of what the reader just read, never a restatement of the intro, never a coda that adds no new fact.

## 6. What a page must contain

**L27.** Every page owes a **thesis**, **specifics**, and **boundaries**. Pages that recommend an action — technique, system, workflow, and operational pages — owe the full honesty kit in §7. Hub and model pages owe thesis, case against, and checkable expectations.

**L28. The Core Thesis** is a causal arc, not an abstract or a table of contents: what mechanism exists, why it works at first, what condition exposes the limit, what the surface experience feels like against what happens underneath, and what repair changes the system. Choose the sharpest framing rather than averaging every source point; tactical lists and implementation detail go in the body.

**L29. Compressed Takeaways.** The most portable points, stripped of context, each usable alone.

**L30. The Operating Model**, where the material describes a repeatable loop.

**L31. Links into the knowledge base, made specific.** Every link carries a clause saying what that page contributes or should absorb. A bare list of titles is navigation without meaning.

**L32. Open Questions**, which stay open. Pose the question and stop.

**L33.** Open Questions belong to the reader, not to the vault's maintenance queue. A page ending in its author's to-do list downgrades everything above it from doctrine to draft.

**L34. Sources**, the one home for provenance.

## 7. The honesty kit

**L35. Specifics over adjectives.** Any claim that can carry a number, a named instance, or a worked example gets one.

**L36.** The specifics must be **operative**: they change what the reader does. Cost ratios, time horizons, thresholds, and worked examples qualify. Cohort sizes and program statistics are decoration arguing that a source is credible.

Weak:

```text
Errors are costly, so accuracy should come first.
```

Strong:

```text
One error costs roughly 4x the time of doing the step right — you already work near your maximum
quality speed, so speed gains come from changing the process, never from rushing.
```

**L37. Measure before asserting.** Any claim that can carry a measurement gets one, from the actual system rather than from intuition.

Weak:

```text
The current bundle is heavy and the content index is large.
```

Strong:

```text
Every page parses a 700KB bundle; the content index is 1.3MB and growing linearly.
```

**L38. The case against.** Every model and technique page names the strongest honest case against itself: where it fails, who should not use it, what it costs to believe.

**L39. Price the method.** Benefit and cost in the same breath: time, setup, cognitive load, maintenance.

```text
WPW produces multi-level integration; it costs a full explanation cycle — twenty minutes or more
per topic — which is why it belongs on high-value material, not vocabulary lists.
```

**L40. Quit signals.** State what evidence means this is not working for this reader, and the next move.

```text
If two weeks of dimension-diagnosis hasn't changed what you do in the next session, stop diagnosing
and study; return to the model when you hit a plateau you can't name.
```

**L41. Checkable expectations.** Promised effects are falsifiable by the reader.

Weak:

```text
Spaced retrieval dramatically improves retention.
```

Strong:

```text
Spacing should show up as easier re-entry within two sessions: the second retrieval of an item
feels harder to start but finishes faster.
```

**L42. Boundaries after definition.** Distinguish a concept from its neighbours only after the positive definition lands. A definition never opens with what the thing is not.

**L43. True against the system.** Every claim about how something behaves is checked against the actual thing before it ships. Live numbers come from the build, never from copy.

## 8. Sentences and paragraphs

**L44.** Flow is the objective; high signal is the filter. The laws say what may not appear; they do not set the target. When maximal compression fights natural flow, flow wins.

**L45.** Every sentence is complete and carries one load-bearing fact, stated once, in concrete words. Never telegraphic.

**L46.** Density is not signal. A compressed claim unpacks across two or three sentences, and a run of glosses gets a plain sentence before and after it.

**L47.** Each sentence picks up a thread from the one before: an echo, a pronoun, a connective. A run of cold-open subjects reads as staccato. The repair is stitching, not new content.

**L48.** Connective tissue is doing work. The delete list never eats a transition or a joint.

**L49.** A paragraph is a working-memory unit. Working memory holds three to five things, so a paragraph introducing more gets split at its natural seam.

**L50.** Density is positional. Within a page that opens payload first, each *paragraph's* first sentence still takes a short on-ramp and its last takes a run-off. This is pacing inside the body and does not touch L13, which governs the page's first sentence only.

**L51.** One focus at a time. A paragraph scattering many glossed items bombards; one focal set carries the glosses and structure carries the rest.

**L52.** Terms are explained before they do work, and labels come after mechanisms.

**L53.** The counterweight to L52: a term of art may stand bare when the sentences that follow supply its meaning in concrete facts. An inline definition that breaks the paragraph's flow costs more than it adds.

**L54.** Plain words are for delivery, not for erasing precision. A domain term that carries the mechanism beats the general word, in doses.

**L55.** Mode switches are marked. When a hypothetical becomes an actual episode, one plain sentence says so.

**L56.** Modality is gentleness done honestly. Contingent things get contingent grammar, and on contingent claims "never" and "always" become "usually" and "often." This is not hedging: hedge words grade the author's confidence, modals describe how the world behaves.

**L57.** Harshness is the paired fault. A run of clipped verdict sentences reads as doctrine. Sentence length varies the way speech does.

**L58.** The audience is a reader whose position is unknown, so pacing targets the newest plausible one.

## 9. Person and address

Binds every genre. Sheets in §13 relax specific laws by number.

**L59.** The default grammar is agentless. Events and artifacts take the subject position: the form stops, the list collects, the recovery takes a minute. Appearance verbs carry experience without an experiencer. Light passives hold background facts. Actions nominalize.

**L60.** The fix for a struck "you" or "I" is an event or artifact subject, never "one."

**L61.** No lecturing: no imperatives, no advice voice, no rhetorical questions, no anticipated objections, no patronizing asides.

**L62. The second-person dose.** A "you" may state a problem the reader will recognize as their own experience. Once the pronoun has done that job the following sentences shed it; five "yous" in a paragraph is being talked at. The dose may recur wherever shared experience is again the content, never for instruction. The possessive is the low-dose form.

```text
your pen might get a few strokes in and stall
a character you might not handwrite for a year
```

**L63.** Natural collocation outranks the person ban. A phrase existing only to dodge a person loses to the sentence that says it naturally.

**L64.** The first sentence is a page's harshest position, so even a dose-legal second-person claim reads as confrontation there.

**L65. Details, not conclusions.** Give concrete details and stop; the reader concludes. No mood labels, no instruction verbs, no interpretive paraphrase after the details.

**L66. Specific beats generic.** A true line that could sit on any site is dead copy, especially the first sentence a reader sees. Move the line onto a stranger's site; if it still fits, it is not finished.

**L67. The thing, not talk about the thing.** A page presents the artifact. It never explains how to use itself, what it contains, what it omits, or how finished it is. Folder paths, inventories, and authoring status belong in repo docs, never on a public surface.

## 10. What never appears

**L68. The delete list.** "Honestly," "genuinely," "quite," "very," "really," "it's worth noting," "importantly," "arguably," "probably" where probability is not the content, and "I think" in a document. Plus importance-announcing in all forms. Canonical for every genre.

**L69. "Drill"** is banned as a word for learning practice. Use practice, reps, production practice, working it live.

**L70. Posing.** The writing never performs, not confidence, not humility, not transparency, not calm. Authorship talk, process talk, and ownership claims are posing regardless of subject.

**L71. The reveal.** Never plant a wrong idea in order to correct it, and never cast the reader as the one who held it. Never "mistake," "misdiagnosis," "wrong approach," or "the reflex is wrong."

**L72. Scaffolding.** No convention explainers, no navigation chatter, no format announcements, no source commentary, no device commentary, no narrating what the screen shows. The map, the search box, and the layout introduce themselves.

**L73. Cadence in prose.** Fragments, triads, anaphora, em-dash punchlines, balanced clause pairs, and the semicolon pair that is an em dash in disguise. A line shaped to sound finished has substituted shape for substance. This law governs prose; a chrome catalog line is written under §13.7.

**L73a. Why the epigram fails, and where it may appear.** An epigram is shaped like a quotation, and a quotation ends in an attribution, which is where its force comes from. Strip the attribution and the shape still requests the same thing — authority the reader is expected to grant the author — and on an unattributed page that request points at the writer, who has established nothing that would make granting it reasonable. The form asks for what the page cannot supply, which is argument from an authority that is not there. An epigram may therefore appear only as a conclusion, after the premises that earn it. It may never serve as a premise, and a paragraph opening on one is struck on sight (owner, 2026-08-19).

**L74. The appended formulation.** A rule or claim stops the moment the reader can act on it. Any clause added afterward to make it memorable — a mirrored pair, a metaphor verdict, a triad, an aphorism — comes out, even when it is accurate.

**L75. The revision list**, each with its diagnostic:

- **The appended verdict** — a judgment clause tacked onto a finished sentence. Fold it into the verb or cut it.
- **The punchline landing** — a sentence built to hand over a payoff, including the paradox-flip.
- **Self-referential apparatus** — the writing pointing at its author's system.
- **Empty parallelism** — a second clause mirroring the first with the labels swapped.
- **Passive mush** — "it could be argued," "it was found": an agent the sentence still needs, hidden.
- **The generic how-to voice** — person-free prose drifting generic when the specific episode evaporates.
- **Abstract nouns doing the acting** — concept-shuffling. The read-aloud test catches it.
- **Performed rumination** — "the more this sits with me" and kin.
- **Dead verbs** — "stay," "go," "become," "get" where a concrete verb can act.
- **Decorative personification** — animation earns its place only when the image is the mechanism.
- **The replay** — restating what the reader just watched. At paragraph scale this is the coda.

**L76. The try-it want.** The method appears completely enough to repeat; whether to repeat it is the reader's business.

**L77. Selling.** Badges, buttons, and labels sell the same way sentences do.

**L78. Pay-to-win.** Student-facing material never cites, links, or names a paid course, program, or product, and never implies an advantage requires purchase. A source may be named where honesty requires it; its paid offering may not, and storefront links never appear. Free resources may be linked. Advertisements are a separate, accepted category.

**L78a. A published page never cites the private corpus.** No `raw/`, `raw/private/`, `raw/sources/`
or `raw/processed/` path appears on a page that ships, and no reference to course machinery a reader
cannot reach. Research banks cite the corpus freely; that is what banks are for. The page states the
substance and leaves the path behind. The Sources footer carries only what a stranger can actually
open.

**L79.** House vocabulary keeps its name and gets a definition a stranger can enter, at the point it first does work. A term defined by whose system it belongs to has closed the loop instead of opening it. Private provenance stays private rather than moving to Sources.

**L80. What approachable does not license.** No hooky, rhetorical-question, or "imagine if" opener. No superlative, no flattery, no reveal casting the reader as wrong, and none of the manufactured-suspense tics: "here's the thing," "the truth is," "let that sink in." Approachability comes from concreteness and plain words, and so does warmth.

## 11. Names, sources, provenance

**L81.** Body prose on a sourced page carries zero source names and zero work titles.

**L82.** Findings arrive as facts about the world in anonymous-reference grammar: "recordings from motor cortex show," "six decades of comparison research finds." Operative numbers stay, in plain clothes.

**L83.** Names, years, and titles dissolve into the Sources footer. Terms of art keep their names; people lose theirs.

**L84. No unpublished-story internals.** No production coordinates, no beat ledgers, no spine or registry names, no pointers into a private repo, no unpublished plot mechanics as worked examples. A craft page's exemplars come from published work. A project's own material lives on its dedicated surfaces at value level only: who a character is and what they hold, never where a beat lands. The test: a sentence that would only earn its place after the story ships does not ship before.

## 12. The scan layer

**L85.** A page carries enough structural variety that a reader can judge it and pull small insights without committing to read. This is not a summary block.

**L86.** The device catalog. Blog and front-facing pages may use all ten; wiki, operational, and personal pages use the first five.

1. **The doctrine line** — a lone bolded sentence stating a section's rule. Two or three per page form the visible spine.
2. **The table** — genuinely tabular content: correspondence rules, failure-to-repair maps, family groups.
3. **The weak/strong pair** — two short fenced specimens.
4. **The worked-example box** — the concrete case compressed into one text fence.
5. **The operating-principles card** — three to five one-line declaratives, no imperatives.
6. **The flow block** — a strictly linear chain as arrow lines in a text fence. Anything two-dimensional is a diagram and gets drawn.
7. **The chart** — inline SVG on the theme tokens, adapting to light and dark through the CSS variables: solid curves, hairline axes, each line labeled at its end so no meaning rides on color alone, no dotted lines, a one-line caption stating the relationship, a source credit when the model is borrowed. ASCII letter-scatter is never a chart.
8. **The form diagram** — a referenced character blown up: the glyph large, components as labeled tiles with role and gloss. Prose may decompose in passing, but the character a page leans on gets shown, not only described.
9. **The felt-signals table** — how it feels against what it means.
10. **Sparse section headers** — three-to-five-word noun phrases, long pages only.

**L87.** Two to four devices per page, chosen for fit and varied so no two neighbouring pages read as the same template. Density stays positional, so no device opens a page.

**L88.** Bullets speak the same language as the prose, only a little more concise: one plain clause or sentence each, of a kind that could sit in the prose unchanged. Never compressed aphorisms, and the semicolon-pair beat is as banned in a bullet as anywhere. Elaboration lives in the surrounding prose.

**L89.** The paired test: the page reads whole with every device skipped, and the devices alone give a scanner a fair basis for the read-or-not call.

**L90.** Every diagram states its job before its form. The form matches the mechanism's shape: discrete states take boxes and arrows, continuous processes take curves, structural claims take lattices, mappings take tables, quantities take charts.

**L91.** Size follows rank in the argument: thesis-scale for a diagram carrying the page's claim, exhibit-scale for worked material.

**L92.** A caption may state a fact beyond the picture. The moment it describes arrows, lanes, colors, or layout, it comes out. Captions get checked against this every time.

## 13. Genre sheets

Each sheet adds laws or relaxes named ones. Everything in §2 through §12 binds unless a sheet says otherwise.

### 13.1 Wiki concept telling

No additions, no relaxations. Opens payload first on a catalog move; owes the subset in L27; em dashes legal.

### 13.2 Operational page

**L93.** Sentence one is the operating claim at full strength. No run-up, no scene, no history.

**L94.** Small stakes, held. The page never raises its own stakes: no before-and-after narrative, no significance announcement. What changed is stated as fact.

**L95.** The second move is application. After the claim the parts arrive immediately.

**L96.** Bolded operative leads are the scan spine. Sections open with the rule in bold, mechanism in the same breath. Numbered lists carry stacked reasons, each number one complete reason.

**L97.** Declaratives carry the register. Rules are statements, never advice aimed at the reader; a declarative rule is not an imperative under L61.

**L98.** The close returns without ceremony: one breath, no coda. Relaxes L26 to its shortest form.

### 13.3 Decision document

Governs PRDs, proposals, decision notes, journal entries weighing options, post-mortems, and memos.

**L99. Verdict first**, inside the first two sentences. The reader never reaches the verdict by scrolling.

Weak:

```text
There are several factors to consider when choosing between the two engines...
```

Strong:

```text
Recommendation: A. The hard part of this project is faithfully rendering the vault, and A is the
only option where that layer isn't our problem.
```

**L100. Define the comparison before arguing it.** Most stalled decisions are mislabeled. State what is actually being compared before weighing options.

```text
A vs B isn't two frameworks. It's an existing engine with a custom skin, against a general
framework plus a publishing layer we build and own forever.
```

**L101. Steelman what you reject**, strongly enough that someone who prefers it recognizes their own argument, then state what evidence or change of goal would flip the decision. A document that cannot name its flip condition has not finished deciding.

```text
What would flip me to B: typed content collections enforce the schema at build time, and if the
five-year vision is one integrated platform, B is the right call today.
```

**L102. Price your own recommendation.** Every recommendation carries its cost in the same breath.

```text
Recommendation: A — accepting that upstream updates may conflict with our modified graph script,
and that schema enforcement stays a lint script instead of a compile-time guarantee.
```

**L103. Weigh the cost of being wrong.** When options converge on outcome, decide on reversibility: state the retreat cost from each branch and the asymmetry. When the edge is not decisive, the reversible branch wins. This is [[wiki/Decision Making/Positional Decisions and Expected Value|Positional Decisions]] applied to prose.

**L104. Checkable claims.** Success criteria and acceptance tests are falsifiable, phrased so a reader could verify them without asking what was meant.

Weak:

```text
The site should feel faster and links should work well.
```

Strong:

```text
Zero new broken internal links versus the baseline build; first visit in a clean profile renders
dark; the home graph shows ≤30 nodes with 6 labeled hubs.
```

### 13.4 Blog post

**L105.** The register is the author's notebook, cleaned up until a stranger can follow it.

**L106. The run-up.** Open by normalizing the problem, and give the sentence a scoping clause before the claim so the entry glides. Normalizing widens the picture beyond the single reader and softens the drop. A one-clause opener stays abrupt even when it normalizes, and the first sentence wants length. Relaxes L13 for this genre alone: the thesis lands inside the opening paragraph rather than in sentence one.

**L107.** The run-up is also a pacing device: the same long, unhurried sentence shape recurs through the body as glue between sections.

**L108. Legible is the load-bearing half.** A notebook a stranger can follow defines terms inline, carries one worked example through the whole post, holds one takeaway, and stops when the example is fully worked. Cut anything a reference page would carry.

**L109. Length variance.** Posts do not converge on one size. Length serves the takeaway, and uniform length across a series is a template smell.

**L110.** The close shows the episode resolving in the scene's own terms rather than stating its moral.

**L111.** Em dashes are cut from blog prose entirely, body included. The ban is pragmatic: model prose overuses the dash past the point where per-line judgment holds, so a total cut is the only enforceable form.

### 13.5 Personal page

Scope: fitness, travel, fashion, and future personal notes. Front-facing chrome laws do not apply; em dashes stay legal.

**L112.** The speaker is the vault's owner. A personal page is a note, not a message: nobody is addressed, no transaction is staged, and the conversation that produced the page is never reenacted on it.

**L113.** Relaxes L59 and L62. Generic-you for shared experience is legal in doses; a "you" pointed at the owner never is. First person is rationed and owned: "I" appears occasionally and only for the owner's actual experience and positions.

Weak:

```text
You handed me the interesting problem, so here's my read of your own mechanism.
```

Strong:

```text
Streaks and rings both measure maintenance: you defend a number that can die.
```

**L114.** The counterweight to the ration: when a claim is personal experience, the owning first person is load-bearing context, and an evasive agentless sentence is the worse fault.

Weak:

```text
Working with AI turned out to be one of them — it pulls daily without any tracker.
```

Strong:

```text
For me personally, working with AI turned out to be one of them — it pulls daily without any
tracker.
```

**L115.** An inference wearing the owner's "I" is a forged signature. The fix for a wrong "I" is the declarative sentence, not a different pronoun.

**L116.** The I owns events, wants, and judgments, never qualities. "I built," "I wanted," "I decided" pass. Any adjective attached to the self dies on sight; qualities exist only as residue the reader computes from reported events.

**L117. Internal scoreboard only.** A sentence's standard is the author's own want or need. Comparatives and rank markers are the tell that the scoreboard moved outside.

**L118. The residue test.** Delete the I-clause and ask what the reader lost. Facts about the subject mean the I was load-bearing; only an impression of the author means display. Performed humility fails the same checks in reverse.

**L119.** Nothing appears without context. Personal experience is owned in first person, references elsewhere in the vault arrive as links, and terms are introduced before they do work.

**L120.** No invented claims about the owner, in any person. Deep reads of him stay invitation-only and build only on what he actually said.

**L121.** Relaxes L36 and L44. Compression is not a goal here and high-signal density is a defect. The thought process may stay on the page, because the working-through is the content.

**L122. Plain words, real depth.** Light means unpretentious, never thin. No systems-jargon, no published-paper register, and the idea fully worked through. A three-line aphorism fails the same way a paper does.

**L123.** Nothing performs: not scholarship, not essayhood, not conversation. No announced moves, no minted principles.

**L124.** The owner has final cut on every line.

### 13.6 Condensed page

**L125.** A bold one-paragraph total compression at the top: the whole domain in five to eight sentences. If the paragraph fails, the page fails. Relaxes L46 and L50 for this paragraph alone.

**L126.** Numbered doctrine sections. Each line is one rule: bolded operative lead, mechanism in a clause, and a wikilink citation to the page owning the full treatment. One to three lines per rule.

**L127.** Tensions resolved explicitly. Where two pages pull different directions, the page states the reconciliation in one line rather than hiding the conflict.

**L128.** An omissions note at the end: what was left out and where it lives.

**L129. Source discipline.** Public condensed pages compress wiki pages only; every line traces to a published page via its citation link. Private corpora are never condensed publicly.

**L130.** The page adds no new claims. A doctrine lacking an owner page is a gap to flag, not a line to write.

**L131.** Exemplars supply the form, never the content. Any line echoing an exemplar's phrasing re-traces to a public owner before it ships. Each structural slot must justify its function in the new context before it is copied.

**L132.** For domains where doctrine rots on technology shifts, split into **invariants** and **dated tactics** stamped with their write date. Mandatory only where rot is real.

**L133.** The genre's test: a reader who knows the domain nods once per line, and a reader who does not can click any line and land on the page that teaches it.

### 13.7 Front-facing chrome

**L134. Taglines are catalog lines.** A hero sub-line is a container noun plus topics, the way a library card or book subtitle reads. A noun phrase predicates nothing, so it cannot sell, brag, hedge, or grade.

**L135.** Every predicated alternative is struck: mechanism claims, anatomy labels, state reports, provenance facts, date trivia.

**L136.** The attitude rides in the genre noun, never in attitude words. "Notes," "a collection," "a vault" carry the work-in-progress posture by what they are. Never state the posture; that is hedging, which is self-regard pointed downward.

**L137.** Topics in reader words, not workshop words. Internal vocabulary — rules, doctrine, open questions, mechanisms, settling — belongs inside the pages.

**L138.** Nouns, counts, and states only in any status or coverage line. Any word admitting a degree is a grade, and a grade is a sale.

**L139.** The fact must pass "who cares." The visitor's question is what this is and whether it is for them.

**L140.** The page never grades itself. Values render as features and marks; uncertainty renders as fact.

**L141.** Cost, coverage, and status may be stated where they are facts the reader needs, in the same voice as every other fact.

**L142.** Em dashes are cut from front-facing prose entirely.

### 13.8 Hub, index, map of content

**L143.** A hub's job is routing, and it is short because routing is what it does. Relaxes L27 and L28: a hub owes no thesis arc. It owes L31 in full.

### 13.9 The open-questions card

**L144.** Positive scope: the questions the author is mulling about where AI is heading and how to shape it, for personal use and for society. Projects, status, roadmap, and what the author is building, learning, or reading do not belong; a model could decide or do those.

**L145.** The question is the whole line. Pose it and stop.

**L146. The AI-caution reflex is banned.** The conclusion a model reaches by reflex is atrophy, hollowing out, refusal, protection, "it will not hold." That is L70 broken: a stance worn as analysis, not the author's. The vault's own framing treats AI as leverage with the human role still open and does not carry the doom. Cut to the neutral question, pre-judging nothing.

## 16. Before it ships

Run in order. Each step cites the laws it enforces.

1. **Read it aloud**, to someone new to the topic. Any sentence nobody would say gets rewritten with the concrete thing as its subject. (L6, L75)
2. **Opener.** Assigned move, 45 words, one subordinate clause, no wikilink, delete test. (L13–L22; blog relaxes via L106)
3. **Shape.** Opens in the whole, returns to it advanced, no replay and no coda. (L23–L26; operational relaxes via L98)
4. **Contents.** Every section this genre owes, present. (L27–L34; hub relaxes via L143)
5. **Honesty kit.** Case against, price, quit signal, checkable expectation, every operative number sourced or marked. (L35–L43)
6. **Coherence.** Threads picked up, paragraphs within a working-memory load, density positional at both ends. (L44–L58; personal relaxes via L121)
7. **Person and address.** Agentless default, no lecturing, the dose obeyed. (L59–L67; personal relaxes via L113)
8. **Display axis.** For every sentence, ask what it wants from the reader. Quote anything answering something other than nothing. (L68–L80)
9. **Provenance.** No names in body, sources in the footer, no private internals. (L81–L84)
10. **Devices.** Two to four, none opening the page. (L85–L92)
11. **Genre sheet.** The page's own sheet, top to bottom. (§13)
12. **Specimens.** Nothing on the page matches a rejected specimen in §15.

**L147.** Failure at any step fails the whole page, not a section of it. Borderline is a fail; a middle band is a negotiation and the writing will learn to land inside it.

**L148.** A failure returns a diagnosis naming what the drafting kept doing, generalized so it applies to the next attempt. Never a list of line fixes.
