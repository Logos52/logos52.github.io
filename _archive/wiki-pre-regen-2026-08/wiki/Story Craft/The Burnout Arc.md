# The Burnout Arc

Burnout is the arc with no villain: the gift fails from the inside, so there is no enemy whose defeat can restore it, and the repair has to come from inside too. That structure sets the story's real work — build a **gauge** that makes the invisible collapse readable on the page, and build a **world kind enough** that an internal repair is believable without a rescue. *Kiki's Delivery Service* runs the complete mechanism in a G-rated slice of life and remains the reference: a girl leaves home with one inherited gift, turns it into a livelihood, loses it to no one's crime, and gets it back from inside — minus one thing that never returns.

## The mechanism

Eleven stages, each with the film's beat as its receipt.

1. **The gift becomes a job.** 「Flying used to be fun until I started doing it for a living.」 Commodified, the gift changes hue — the activity is identical, the joy is not.
2. **The ledger runs a joy deficit.** Her joy was always helping people, and the slide begins exactly where deliveries stop landing: the order that goes wrong, the granddaughter who shrugs at the pie carried through a storm — a delivery that cost a soaking, a fever, and a missed party, received with a door in the face. Work crowds out friendship and rest, and every unthanked delivery compounds.
3. **Identity fuses to output.** 「If I lose my magic, that means I've lost absolutely everything.」 Worth has narrowed to what she can still produce; the person and the function have merged.
4. **The collapse arrives without an event.** No witch curses her. The block is made of self-doubt, overthinking, and comparison — Ursula's diagnosis of her own block was imitation, copying other painters instead of finding her own pictures — and the film carries Kiki's version entirely in subtext: the complaints about the black dress, the red shoes in the window, the reflection she studies after the pretty girls pass. None of it is ever stated. Films that state it lose it.
5. **The symptom shows before the cause.** The cat stops talking; the broom snaps. The audience learns something is wrong the same way the character does — from the instruments, never from narration.
6. **The withdrawal.** 「I make friends, then suddenly I can't bear to be with any of them. The other me — the cheerful, honest me — went off somewhere.」 She pushes away exactly the connection she wants; the phone call to Tombo ends with "please don't call me anymore."
7. **Rest is given, never prescribed.** Ursula offers space and one instruction — stop trying: long walks, scenery, naps, no flying. The caveat travels with the advice: this works because the worry *is* the block. Where the problem is material, prescribing walks is a category error.
8. **The return runs on love, not discipline.** Fear puts her back on the borrowed broom — someone she cares about is hanging from a dirigible — and joy keeps her there. The gift restarts the moment it is exercised *for someone*, off the books, unpaid.
9. **The recovery is clumsy.** She flies badly, bounces off walls, adapts by the third bounce. No instant mastery; the gauge shows the repair as reps, not as a switch flipped.
10. **Something stays lost.** Jiji never speaks again. The film declines to refund the arc's cost, and is trusted because of it.
11. **The end-state is internal.** She no longer needs the whole town's applause — the insecurity that wanted everyone's acceptance has quietly retired. The film's last line, in a letter home: 「I still get sad. But I love this town.」

## The gauge

The craft move that makes an internal arc legible: give the inner state an **external instrument that visibly works or fails**. Kiki's interiority never needs narrating because it has two instruments — flight, and the cat's voice. The audience reads her collapse off the broom the way a pilot reads altitude. The gauge also disciplines the writer: the low point, the repair, and the residue all have to register on the instrument (flight lost → flight recovered badly → the cat still meows), which forbids the vague low point and the free recovery. This is [[wiki/Story Craft/Interiority Through Action and Object|Interiority Through Action and Object]] promoted from technique to load-bearing structure — under a vocabulary ceiling it is not optional, because the words for the feelings don't exist on the page yet and the jar, the broom, or the dish has to carry them.

## The kind world

The arc above ends generatively only inside a world built a particular way, and the film's world runs four rules.

**Support without agreement.** Kiki's parents doubt the timing, overrule the toy broom she made herself, and support the journey anyway — the mother sews the dress while disagreeing, the father calls the neighborhood for a send-off with not one naysayer in it. Doubt and support are independent axes; the kind world keeps them separate.

**Kindness is an economy, and it is the plot.** The pacifier delivery — offered at her lowest hour, after the near-crash, the scolding officer, the hotel refusal — becomes coffee, becomes a room, becomes the business. The Madam pays in full for a failed job; Kiki repays in chores and fires the old oven; the Madam bakes her a cake. The ripple structure replaces the conflict structure: every act of kindness finances the next scene.

**No villains, and no one unjustified.** Every negative beat is chance or a defensible person: the officer is right to be angry, the city is just busy, and the granddaughter who hates her grandmother's fish pies has a point — the grandmother doesn't listen. Blame is never assigned; weather is the only recurring antagonist. The closest thing to a rival appears in the end credits as a friend, unconquered and unhumiliated.

**Sadness survives the ending.** "I still get sad" *after* the town has cheered her rescue. The kind world is not the world without sadness; it is the world where sadness is met. A happily-ever-after would void the arc's honesty; the letter home keeps it.

## The case against

Two-thirds of the film passes without conflict, and a plot-first reader will price the stakes at zero — [[wiki/Story Craft/Stakes Without Mortality|Stakes Without Mortality]] is the counter-doctrine, and it must be actively run, not assumed. The kind world turns saccharine the moment kindness stops costing anything: the ripple economy works because every act is priced (the pacifier delivery is unpaid work at her hungriest hour), and unpriced kindness is decoration — the same law the [[wiki/Story Craft/The Moral Core|moral core]] enforces on virtue. Rest-as-repair generalizes badly: it holds where worry is the block and insults the character where the block is material. And an internal collapse without a gauge is invisible — the failure mode is mood narration, the paragraph explaining that a character feels empty.
