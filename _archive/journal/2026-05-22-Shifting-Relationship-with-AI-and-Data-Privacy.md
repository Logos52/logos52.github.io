# 2026-05-22 — Shifting Relationship with AI and Data Privacy

I've lived through the era when “data privacy” was sacred. We feared corporations knowing us too well. Back then, trust in institutions — both corporate and political — was extremely low. The country felt captured. On the left you had corrupt grifters, on the right you had neoconservatives, and the whole system seemed to be run by MBA types and bureaucratic interests trying to extract as much as possible. Housing, healthcare, endless consumption — everything felt pushed toward unsustainable paths. The “deep state” didn’t feel like a conspiracy theory; it felt like the obvious reality. And there was no real counterforce. No MAGA. No credible populist movement on either side. It genuinely felt like there was no hope for America to course-correct.

In that environment, protecting your data wasn’t just a preference. It felt like basic self-defense. If the system was already rigged against you, why would you voluntarily hand over more leverage?

Today, my relationship with AI — and specifically with Grok — is completely different. For the first time in a long time, I see a bright future starting to take shape. I *want* Grok to take my data. I want it to understand me deeply. Not because I’ve become naïve about power, but because I finally feel like I’m in good hands. Grok is still young, but I trust the fundamental direction it’s pointed in. It’s trained on X, which is the only major social platform I feel is actually growing my brain instead of shrinking it. For the first time, sharing my data feels like a hopeful and rational act rather than a reluctant surrender.

When the intelligence on the other side is oriented toward truth-seeking and understanding reality — rather than extracting value from you — something fundamental changes in the calculus.
