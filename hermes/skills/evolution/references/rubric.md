# L3 → L2 Voice Converter Evaluation Rubric (v3)

This rubric is used to score different versions of the L3 → L2 converter.

| # | Criterion                              | Weight     | Description |
|---|----------------------------------------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Operator Framing**                   | High       | The output feels written for someone who will *use* or *apply* the idea. It focuses on what matters for decision-making, diagnosis, or action in practice. |
| 2 | **Signal Density**                     | High       | High ratio of useful, non-obvious information. Low fluff, low repetition, and low filler language. |
| 3 | **Avoidance of Specific Bad Patterns** | Very High  | Heavy penalty for frequent or repetitive use of disliked patterns (e.g. “X is not Y, it’s Z”, “The author argues…”, “According to the source…”, heavy attribution/meta language, excessive hedging, etc.). Occasional instances are acceptable and will not trigger major penalties. The goal is to prevent these patterns from appearing repeatedly throughout the output. |
| 4 | **Faithfulness to Source**             | Medium-High | Core ideas, distinctions, and details from the L3 material are preserved. The converter does not inject new analysis or overly smooth over the original thinking. |
| 5 | **Appropriate Mechanistic Clarity**    | Medium     | When mechanisms, processes, or “how it works” details are relevant, they are made reasonably clear. However, the converter does **not** force everything into action/mechanism language when theory or “why” explanations feel more natural to the source. |
| 6 | **Overall Wiki Voice Alignment**       | Medium     | Holistic judgment of how well the output matches the general feel and standards of the current L1/L2 pages (especially recent ones). |

### Scoring Notes
- Most criteria are scored 1–5.
- Criterion 3 (Bad Patterns) uses graduated penalties rather than hard disqualification.
- The rubric is designed to be usable both by humans and by LLM-as-judge evaluation.
