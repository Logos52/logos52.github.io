# Cluster 1 — Procedural ↔ Declarative

**1. Procedural Fluency and Declarative Knowledge Gate Each Other**

In language, automatic output and the stored inventory of word forms and their senses each limit the other. Fluency runs on a declarative base, and that base grows only through procedural retrieval running in real time. Problems that present as output failures often trace to gaps in which senses a character carries and when to use the word.

Principle: procedural fluency and declarative knowledge gate each other. · Chinese application: before adding shadowing reps, check whether you know which senses a character carries and when to deploy the word. · Grounded in: Live Clinic 70 (lines 76, 98).

**2. The IME Method for Reconstructing a Forgotten Character**

When a written form goes missing mid-stroke, list the sound components that could carry the syllable, list the meaning components that fit the sense, and cross-match until one candidate pair matches a character you recognize. About 50 functionally-learned characters bootstrap the method. For 頓, the candidates {屯,盾,享} × {首,頁,百} resolve to 屯+頁; for 綁 heard as *báng, {封,邦,奉,丰,夆,方} × {巾,衣,糸,帛} resolve to 糸+邦, with sandhi explaining the surface tone.

Principle: a declarative-to-procedural lookup can run in the head. · Chinese application: cross-match candidate sound and meaning components until the character resolves, as 頓 resolves to 屯+頁. · Grounded in: The IME Method; Chinese Characters, Condensed §4; Live Clinic 70.

**3. Tone Pairs and Sentence Frames Train Through Spoken Production**

Real-time conversation does not leave room to consciously execute every character and its tone, since the attempt runs too slow to be understood. Procedural fluency grows through production across varied contexts, so tone pairs and sentence frames need production-dominant 30-second loops: produce the frame aloud, then correct it against a native recording. Recognizing mā/má/mǎ/mà on a card builds declarative knowledge and leaves the motor task untrained.

Principle: procedural fluency grows through varied-context production. · Chinese application: drill tone pairs and sentence frames in 30-second loops of speaking, hearing a native model, and adjusting. · Grounded in: Live Clinic 70 (line 76); ICS Fundamentals 195/198, Summit 224; Camp I 135/204.

**4. Locating a Stall in Comprehension, Approach, or Execution**

A stall sits at one of three layers. The comprehension layer holds gaps where the senses of the words are unknown. At approach, the functional system relating sound, meaning, and components is absent. Execution is where retrieval will not automate even with the knowledge present. Knowing what to say while the sentences will not flow is a production/procedural gap that speaking under time pressure addresses. When the word itself is forgotten, that is a knowledge/declarative gap, and a card addresses it. One worked case is a learner who knows 會議, 明天, and 改 but cannot build sentences in time.

Principle: stalls fall into comprehension, approach, or execution layers, each with its own repair. · Chinese application: knowing 會議/明天/改 yet unable to build sentences in time is an execution gap fixed by speaking under time pressure rather than a card. · Grounded in: Live Clinic 70 (lines 31-36, 89-100); Declarative, Procedural, and Conditional Knowledge.

# Cluster 2 — How Characters Encode Meaning

**5. About 85% of a Character's Senses Derive From One Original Meaning**

About 85% of the senses in a comprehensive dictionary derive from one original meaning, and about 18% are sound loans. Mark the sound loans and set aside the derived senses. Learn the root sense with its chain of extensions. 漢 carries 氵 because the form first named a river, then the river's people, then a dynasty, then an ethnic group, then an adult male of that group.

Principle: most dictionary senses chain from one original meaning. · Chinese application: memorize 漢's root sense of a river, then follow the chain through people, dynasty, ethnic group, and adult male. · Grounded in: Chinese Characters, Condensed §3; Meaning Trees and Original Meanings; OUTLIER-CONDENSED L9.

**6. Test a Character Mnemonic Against Its Semantic-Series Siblings**

A meaning component is an abstract extension of a concrete original, so a mnemonic holds only if it survives across the character's siblings, which makes the semantic series the unit of study. The 偷 folk reading of a person on a roof at night with a knife fails against its 俞 siblings 輸, 愈, 愉, and 喻, none of which involve a roof or a knife. The 射 pulling-string story collapses once 身 is read as collapsed bow-and-arrow residue.

Principle: a meaning-component story survives only if it holds across the character's siblings. · Chinese application: the 偷 roof-and-knife reading fails against its 俞 siblings 輸/愈/愉/喻. · Grounded in: OUTLIER-CONDENSED L5; Chinese Characters, Condensed.

**7. A Borrowing Chain Connects Two Characters at Retrieval Time**

When a character's form and meaning disconnect, the historical borrowing chain connects it to a second character at retrieval time. A drawn sense gets borrowed for a homophone, then a clarifying component accretes to write the original, and marking loans with 〇 blocks false bridges. 北 drew a person's back, was borrowed for "north," then took 月 to write 背; 合 ("to answer") migrated, so 答 was written for the original. The same chain runs through 必→柲, 其→箕, 常→裳, and 文→紋.

Principle: a historical borrowing chain links two characters that share a sound. · Chinese application: 北 (a back) was borrowed for "north," then 月 added to write 背. · Grounded in: OUTLIER-CONDENSED L6.

**8. Consulting the Ancient Form Restores a Character's Picture**

Stylization erased the pictures that modern glyphs once carried, so 日 no longer resembles a sun, and consulting the ancient form once at first encounter restores a picturable scene. 取 is a hand (又) seizing an ear (耳), from armies counting casualties in ears. 美 is a person in a feather headdress, with the 𦍌 residue later read as 羊 (sheep). 及 is a hand seizing a person. 秉 is a hand holding one grain stalk. 兼 is a hand holding two grain stalks.

Principle: the ancient form recovers a picture that stylization erased. · Chinese application: 取 reads as a hand (又) seizing an ear (耳). · Grounded in: Form Components; Rules of Effective Memorization; OUTLIER-CONDENSED L4.

**9. A Component's Role Is Assigned Per Character**

A component's role is assigned character by character. 立 supplies meaning in 端, sound in 拉, and nothing in 音. Decompose to the functional level and stop there. 古 feeds the {g,k,h} initials in 故, 估, 苦, and 胡 but the {j,q,x} initials in 居 and 鋸, one shape spanning two modern sound groups. A single misclassification cascades through later sound-series work.

Principle: a component's role of meaning, sound, or empty is set per character. · Chinese application: 立 gives meaning in 端, sound in 拉, and nothing in 音. · Grounded in: OUTLIER-CONDENSED L2-3; Chinese Characters, Condensed §1.

# Cluster 3 — Sound, Anchoring, and Pipelining

**10. Committing to a Guess Before the Dictionary Does the Encoding**

Committing to a meaning domain and a syllable range before the dictionary settles the answer does the deep processing, and the revealed reading then confirms or corrects it, with transfer moving from about 1:1 toward 10:1. Meeting 棠 cold, the 木 below points to a tree or wood domain and the 尚 above narrows the syllable to {shang, chang, zhang, tang, dang}. Commit to that, then look up the exact reading and its tone as a separate fact.

Principle: a committed guess before lookup does the encoding, lifting transfer toward 10:1. · Chinese application: place 棠 as a tree-domain character reading in {shang, chang, zhang, tang, dang}, then verify. · Grounded in: Sound Components (棠 walkthrough); Learning, Condensed (10:1 transfer).

**11. Seven Groups of Related Initials in Chinese Sound Series**

Seven related-initial groups, {t,d}, {zh,ch,sh}, {j,q,x}, {b,p,f}, {g,k,h}, {z,c,s}, and {m,w}, collect the initials that substitute for one another across sound change. An authoritative grouping stops no physical sound change, and once it stands, new instances of the pattern absorb with little effort. Verify a membership by mouth-place: say both initials and feel the same articulation. 監 *kram, 籃 *k.ram, and 藍 *g.ram surfaced as jiān, lán, and lán.

Principle: an authoritative grouping of related initials lets later instances of the pattern absorb cheaply. · Chinese application: verify membership by mouth-place, as 監/籃/藍 trace to *kram, *k.ram, and *g.ram. · Grounded in: Sound Series; Sound Components; OUTLIER-CONDENSED L7.

**12. A Character's Three Links Strengthen and Decay Independently**

A character's form-sound, form-meaning, and sound-meaning links strengthen and decay on their own, so a single "do I know this character?" hides which one is broken. One error in the first decomposition costs about 4× the repair time downstream, and pipelining splits the three links across three days. A day-1 failure points to a weak sound-meaning link. A day-3 failure shows the form binding never held. Either reading lets the repair target the specific link.

Principle: a character's three links decay independently, so the repair targets the link rather than the whole card. · Chinese application: a day-3 failure points to form binding, a day-1 failure to the sound-meaning link. · Grounded in: Chinese Characters, Condensed; The Pipelining Strategy; Learning, Condensed (4× repair cost).

**13. A Form Attaches Faster to a Word Already Known by Sound**

Attaching a written form to a word already known by sound and meaning takes a fraction of the effort of learning all three attributes cold, which is the order native literacy follows. Pipeline cohorts of 3 to 5 items across three days: the spoken word forward, then reversed, then the form attached. One cohort graduates each day, with intake near 20 items per day.

Principle: a known spoken word makes its written form cheap to attach. · Chinese application: pipeline 3-5 items over three days as spoken-forward, reversed, then form-attached, one cohort graduating daily near 20/day. · Grounded in: The Pipelining Strategy; Three Attributes of a Character; OUTLIER-CONDENSED L12.

# Cluster 4 — The Practice Loop

**14. The Aim-Shoot-Skin Loop for a Chinese Study Session**

Aim at material in the just-not-understandable band, where about half the words land and the gist still assembles. Shoot the gaps by looking up exactly what is missing, carding it, and applying it across speaking, listening, reading, and writing. Skin every few weeks by scanning the deck for shared roots and components. The aim-shoot alternation carries about 90% of the learning.

Principle: aim at the just-not-understandable band, shoot the gaps, skin the deck every few weeks. · Chinese application: read where about half the words land, look up and card what is missing, apply it across all four skills. · Grounded in: Aim-Shoot-Skin for Language Learning; ICS Ascent II 164/128.

**15. Rotating Retrieval Direction Keeps a Character From Binding Narrowly**

Each retrieval rebuilds the memory from its parts, so a character met always in one direction binds narrowly and drifts. About 80% of language practice should be interleaved, rotating sound→form, form→meaning, and meaning→component. The same rotation scales from single characters up to family level, as in the {b,p,f} series where 封, 邦, and 奉 substitute.

Principle: retrieval regenerates, so rotating the direction keeps a character from binding narrowly. · Chinese application: rotate sound→form, form→meaning, meaning→component, up to family level like the {b,p,f} series 封/邦/奉. · Grounded in: Live Clinic 70 (line 79); Learning, Condensed.

**16. Pre-Studying Vocabulary Before It Appears in Input**

Vocabulary primed before it appears in a drama, article, or conversation recovers 50 to 70% of the value that encounter-dictated order otherwise loses. Without priming, the brain logs "I've heard this" and encodes nothing, and short-term memory holds only a 15 to 30 second workbench. Before a drama or news article, pre-study 15 to 20 characters or phrases known to appear, drawn from subtitle lists or topic vocabulary.

Principle: priming vocabulary before an encounter recovers 50-70% of the value lost to encounter-dictated order. · Chinese application: pre-study 15-20 characters or phrases from a drama's subtitle list before watching. · Grounded in: ICS Niche 183/199; Live Clinic 71.

# Cluster 5 — Immersion and the Han-Viet Bridge

**17. The Han-Viet Bridge for Vietnamese Learners of Mandarin**

A Vietnamese learner of Mandarin already owns roughly 3,000 morphemes through the Sino-Vietnamese layer, parsing 學生 as học + sinh, "student," before learning the Mandarin reading, since cognate vocabulary is acquired faster without instruction when prior knowledge is the cheapest encoding. Sound rules make the bridge predictive: final -p, -t, and -c map to an open Mandarin syllable, -m maps to -n, and the huyền tone maps to Tone 2. 力 reads as sức (strength) and lực (force). False friends carry a real cost, as 緊張 ("nervous") differs from khẩn trương ("urgent"), and the reading aid drops once Mandarin starts coming out in Hán-Việt sound.

Principle (Vietnamese L1 learners): cognate vocabulary acquires fast because prior knowledge is the cheapest encoding. · Chinese application: parse 學生 as học + sinh before learning the Mandarin reading, using sound rules like -m→-n and huyền→Tone 2. · Grounded in: The Han-Viet Bridge; Learning, Condensed.

**18. Grammar Primes a Pattern That Input Then Confirms**

Language is too specific and idiomatic to generate reliably from rules, so the learner imitates attested input before producing their own. Grammar earns its place by priming a pattern the learner then notices in real input. The test for any prep activity is whether it makes the next immersion session more comprehensible or more likely to happen. Prime the 把 construction, then hunt it in real dialogue.

Principle: idiomatic language comes from imitating input, and grammar earns its place by priming a pattern the learner later notices. · Chinese application: prime the 把 construction, then hunt it in real dialogue. · Grounded in: Refold Language Learning System; Language Isn't Math.
