# Hidden ideology and labels

Recorded verbatim from the owner, 2026-08-22, while ruling on the What a Label Does draft. He said "maybe that deserves a separate page." Whether it becomes one is his call. Nothing here has been drafted into a page.

> i want a commentary on certain pages that hide their ideology under a false flag. leftist content always hides their ideology and just assumes that everyone agrees with them and that their content is just the normal content. it's sort of like a gaslighting for the world to assume that their ideology is the common sense and everyone else is some version of crazy. maybe that deserves a separate page. i don't like hidden ideologies and hidden labels. but i acknowledge they come with certain prejudices that i don't always want people to know about. people whom i respect, like Asmongold or Elon Musk, tend not to associate themselves with any labels and let their argument carry the weight. other people can categorize them after the fact. labels lead to prejudice from those who are uninformed. it's a mental shortcut used to avoid the cognitive effort to whether or not a person's arguments have validity

And, on the draft's sentence "I am Asian":

> i don't want to say things like "I am Asian" for that same reason as i just said

## Correction, later the same day

> most writers don't necessarily take the first-line label.  most writers actively try to avoid it

So the common case is avoiding the label, and the page's question is whether avoiding it is hiding it. The first-line label is the rarer case.

## More, the same evening

> labels are what other people assign to the speakers and it's a mixed bag, because holding onto it assumes other people will assign all the ideologies and ideas onto that person.  which might be true for leftism which actively preserves and ostracizes anyone who even has one idea that's outside of the norm.  vs the right which has more diversity of ideas.  i think 20 years ago this was not true (or maybe because i was on the left 20 years ago, so i interpreted that to be true)

Read back: a label is assigned by others, not chosen; it drags a bundle of positions with it, and the reader assumes the whole bundle; how tightly the bundle is policed differs by side, the left ostracizing over one idea outside the norm, the right carrying more range; he is unsure whether that was true twenty years ago or whether he saw it that way because he was on the left then. He marks the last point as uncertain.

## What this settles for the label page

- The page may not put a label on him, including the group his face puts him in. The page is about not doing that.
- The reason a first-line label is bad, in his words: it is a mental shortcut that lets a reader skip the effort of judging whether the argument holds, and it produces prejudice in readers who are uninformed. The draft said "placed in a group before he is heard." His reason is sharper and is his.
- The model he names: people who carry no label and let the argument carry the weight, and are categorized by others afterward.
