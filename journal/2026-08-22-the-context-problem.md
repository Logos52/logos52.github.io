# The Context Problem

The owner's name for it, 2026-08-22. The problem: Claude writes sentences that assume the reader knows more than they do. A word the page never defined. A connection the page never showed. A thing brought in with no statement of what it is or why it is here. His definition of the fix, the same day: nothing on the page is used before the page has given it. The only context a sentence may rely on is what is on the page and what an earlier paragraph established.

This journal exists so the research does not have to be run again. His words: "i need it on disk in case you forget again." It carries the whole record. The summary is first. The instruments it produced are listed next. The analysis, the three independent analyses, and the full timeline of every instance follow.

## Summary

- Two hundred and eighteen instances of the complaint were found across thirty-two of the forty-six Claude sessions on this machine, 2026-06-02 to 2026-08-22. About a hundred and ninety are distinct events. They fall on thirty-three of the forty days between July 14 and August 21. He said he had said it before on twelve separate dates.
- One hundred and forty-five of the two hundred and eighteen responses changed no instrument. Seventy-seven were rules about what the output must contain. The same complaint came back later in the same session one hundred and eighty-seven times. Rules were broken within minutes of being written, three times on record.
- The cause was named correctly six times over five weeks and never accumulated, because each session re-discovered it, filed the fix under a new name in a new project, and wrote the fix in the same house register it was banning.
- The only fixes that held replaced Claude's judgment of what the reader knows with an inventory outside Claude. Each was used once and never made standing.
- The production change made on 2026-08-22, on his order: a reader file per kind of page; a holdings ledger rebuilt from the text after every paragraph, with every source closed while drafting; and a cold read by a separate reader whose only context is the draft, before he sees anything. The cold read is temporary by his ruling. It stays until drafts stop failing it.

## His definition of proper context, 2026-08-22

A sentence depends on four things. The page has to have given the reader each one before the sentence comes. What the words point at. What the words mean here. Why two things are treated as the same, shown in parts so the reader sees the match before the page says it. Why the thing is on the page at all. The test for a sentence: what does the reader have to already hold for this sentence to make sense, and is each item above it on the page, said outright. The writer checks against the page above, never against his own head.

## Fable's analysis

**The size of it.** 218 verified instances in 32 of the 46 sessions. About 190 are distinct events, because five sessions were logged twice under different project paths. They fall on 33 of the 40 days between 2026-07-14 and 2026-08-21. That is about five a day for six weeks. The complaint landed on 28 different kinds of writing: story dialogue, wiki pages, personal pages, podcast scripts, dictionary entries, shell instructions, HTML boards, voice prompts, the Writing Standards, the generator file, and my chat replies. He said he had said it before on twelve separate occasions, starting 2026-07-24: "i feel like im complaining about the same things to you multiple times but nothing is changing."

**What the fixes were.** 80 were a new draft of the text. 31 were a patch to a line. 26 were nothing. 8 were an apology. That is 145 of 218 that changed no instrument at all. 38 edited a standard or a craft file. 17 added a rule. 11 wrote a memory. 7 built a checker. By what the fix changed: 77 were rules about what the output must contain, 70 changed how or when text gets made, 71 changed neither.

**None of it held.** The same complaint came back later in the same session in 187 of 218 cases. Rules came back 91 percent of the time. Mechanisms came back 84 percent. The 31 that did not come back are almost all the last instance in their session, so the session ended before it could. Rules were broken within minutes of being written. "NEVER COMPRESS DIALOGUE" was made permanent on 07-28 at 19:17 and broken at 19:23. Law L20a was written 08-12 at 20:36 and broken at 21:16. The memory written 08-18 at 09:13 was broken at 09:20 and eight more times that morning. Yesterday I used the house phrase "every link carries a clause" inside the message that was diagnosing house phrases. Step 2b, the chain, went into the generator at 18:35 and the paragraph written under it was struck at 18:38.

**Why, in order of weight.**

1. The diagnosis was right every time and never accumulated. I named the same cause on 07-20 ("agents write wiki pages with production docs open and the coordinates leak in"), 08-11, 08-13, 08-19 ("I have a bullet list of your life, and I keep writing sentences that require having lived it"), 08-21 ("when the page that already exists is in front of me while I write, my output tracks it"), and 08-22 ("I check it against my head and it passes"). Each time it was stated as a discovery. The most accurate sentence in the record, 08-18 09:42, "the list comes back wearing whatever costume the newest rule permits," was written to no file.

2. The fix was to the text, or a rule about the text. A rule is read by the same head that wrote the sentence and checked against the same material, so the sentence passes. He proved this on 07-28 at 19:26: "your rule that you just minted and permanent is wrong because you produced output that is completely unsatisfactory." Output that passed the rule was still wrong.

3. The fixes were written in the register they banned. "Door, push, competence, soul." "HOLE." "The break." "L20a." "Thesis-atom." "Prestudy." From 08-13 a memory exempted instruments from plain words. The last thing I read before writing taught the fault.

4. His word for it rotated, so each session filed it as new. "Patchwork" in July, "no on-ramp" in early August, "stacked facts" on the 18th, "no context" on the 21st. The rotation is partly my doing. Each fix banned the last costume and the fault came back in the next one. Each session then wrote its fix into a new file in a new project, and the next project could not read it.

5. The apparatus built to end the complaint became the next surface for it. The checker's output on 07-25. The boards on 07-27. The 148-law standards on 08-12. The generator's own words on 08-21.

6. The only fixes that held replaced my judgment of what the reader knows with something outside me. The vocabulary spine on 08-04 (4,961 entries; "I checked native-speaker familiarity, which is the wrong ground truth"). The researched corpus of real openers on 08-11 ("a change of input, not a new ban"). The why-questions on 08-18. Gwern's page on 08-17. The computed board on 08-05. Each was used once and never made standing.

7. Even a mechanism fails when the same head runs it. The chain step asks me to list what the reader holds before I write. I wrote the chain and asserted the link instead of showing it, because the chain is written by the head that already knows the answer, and nothing re-reads the paragraph that actually came out.

**What would hold.** Three things, all about what is in front of me when I write and who checks it after. None is a rule about the output.

1. A reader file per surface, outside any turn, saying what that reader already has. For the podcast it is the vocabulary spine that already exists. For the wiki it is a person who has read no other page. For him it is a reader of English who has not run a website and does not hold my house words.

2. A holdings ledger per piece, built from each paragraph after it is written, not from the plan. It lists every name, term, coined sense, and pronoun referent the paragraph used and where the page gave it. The next paragraph may use only what the ledger holds. Drafting happens with the source page, the bank, and the interview closed. Only the ledger and the draft so far are open.

3. A cold read before he sees anything. A separate agent whose whole context is the draft text. No bank, no interview, no generator, no conversation. It returns every word and reference it cannot resolve from the sentences above. Those paragraphs go back to me and are written again. This is the one step the record says I cannot do myself, because the gap is invisible from inside the head that made it.

Tested against the record: "the political wing" (08-19), "Schooling is the one I lived" (08-19), "The labels would be true" (08-21), "A face is one too" (08-21), "HOLE," "the break," "U1 U2 S1," "Door, Push, Competence, Soul," "double-marquee," 蠟燭, and mẻ all fail the cold read. What it does not catch: compression and flow ("stacked facts," "the flow of the prose is just awful"), board layout, and delivery.

**One collision.** The 08-13 wiki-lane ruling says no verifier fleets, the checker and his eye are the whole QA. The cold read is a verifier. His eye has been doing that job at five strikes a day for six weeks. His call.

## ANSWER

The fixes failed because almost none of them changed how the sentence gets made. Seventy-nine were rules about what the output must contain, 145 of the 220 responses touched no instrument at all, and the rules that were written were violated inside the same hour they were written — six minutes for NEVER COMPRESS DIALOGUE on 07-28, forty minutes for L20a on 08-12, seven minutes for the memory written on 08-18. A rule can only be consulted after the sentence exists, and the record names the reason it is never consulted usefully: Claude checks the sentence against the argument in its own head, where every connection is already made, instead of against the page above it. Worse, the fixes were written in the same compressed house register they were banning — "door/push/competence/soul," "the break," "HOLE," "L20a," "thesis-atom," "prestudy" — under a memory that from 08-13 explicitly exempted instruments from plain words, so the last thing read before writing taught the fault. The mechanisms in the record did barely better (84 percent recurrence against 91) because they were mechanisms of checking rather than of producing, and the 08-22 blind board measured that checks held in the writing hand make the page worse than no checks at all. **If anything changes, make it this: the resolution check must be run by something that has not read the argument.** After the draft exists and before it reaches him, a separate agent whose entire context is the draft text — no bank, no interview, no generator, no prior page — reads it once and returns the list of words and references it cannot resolve from what came above them; that list drives regeneration of those paragraphs, not patches.
