# Three days of bot packets: what could join the wiki

This desk runs a small fleet of bots. Each bot reads part of the public web on a schedule and writes a short report called a packet. Between 15 and 18 September 2026 the bots wrote 123 files. On 18 September the owner asked which items and arguments in those files could fit the wiki, and whether any could grow into whole pages. He is also considering a new Economics & Finance section as a place to build financial literacy.

Most of the 123 files do not belong in the wiki. This entry keeps the part that does. It lists subjects large enough for a page, then smaller items for pages the wiki already has, then the finance terms that a new section would have to explain. It ends with the language learning tools the bots found, which the owner asked to keep for a later sift.

Much of the material comes from the All-In Summit, a conference run by the hosts of the All-In podcast. The podcast published its stage interviews across the week. The speakers quoted below are SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell, Elon Musk, Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella, Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, United States Vice President JD Vance, investor Brad Gerstner, and Meta president Dina Powell McCormick. Every figure in this entry is as a speaker or article stated it. Nobody at this desk has checked the figures against a second source.

## Subjects large enough for a page

**1. Whether the AI boom is a stock bubble.** Brad Gerstner runs the investment firm Altimeter, and his talk ran 18 minutes. His case runs as follows.

- Share prices rose in 2026 because company profits rose. Prices measured against profits fell. Nvidia trades at about 14 times next year's profit, which is below its average.
- Chip companies produced about 70% of the return of the NASDAQ stock index. He calls that the engine of the market and also its weak point, because one group of companies carries the index.
- The large cloud companies are building data centers to rent out, so somebody has to pay the rent. The leading AI labs together take in about $100 billion a year at the current pace. He says that pace must reach about $180 billion by the end of 2026, and then climb toward $1 trillion, to match the building that is planned. The number he watches most is monthly revenue at OpenAI and Anthropic, and whether it is nearer $4 billion or $8 billion.
- He names three risks. The first is regulation. The second is electricity and permits. One forecaster expects 43 gigawatts of new computing power in the United States next year, and he expects about 25. The third is interest rates. Data centers are now built with borrowed money, and he says a ten-year government bond paying 5.5% pulls money away from shares.

This talk is the best first page for an Economics & Finance section, because every step uses a term the section would need to explain. Its weakness is that it is one investor's case, and his firm owns shares in the companies he describes.

**2. What a data center does to the town that hosts it.** Four speakers gave figures.

- Nadella described Quincy, a town in Washington state that has hosted Microsoft data centers for about 20 years. Town tax income grew twelve times over. The town built a school, a hospital and a town center.
- Powell McCormick brought the school superintendent of Richland Parish, Louisiana, where Meta broke ground on a data center in December 2024. Sales tax collections there normally grow 5 to 10% a year. After the groundbreaking they grew 60 to 70%, and at the peak 260%. Louisiana law sends that surplus to school staff. A teacher's yearly sales tax bonus went from about $10,000 to about $50,000. Meta pays for its own power generation, its grid upgrades and its storm costs.
- Vance gave the other side of the ledger. He described household power bills doubling from $290 to $580 a month in states that blocked new power plants. His remedy is more power plants, and he said China now generates about three times the electricity of the United States.
- Shotwell said rural land jumps from $3,000 to $180,000 an acre once a data center rumor starts. She said generators are quoted three years out.

All four speakers favor building. A page would need the opponents' case from another source. The page could sit in an Economics & Finance section or in [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Worldviews & the Political Order|Worldviews & the Political Order]].

**3. Gig work, and what an employer pays for besides the wage.** A 44-minute essay by the YouTube channel Frontpage tests the charge that app work ruined a generation. It opens with New York taxi medallions. A medallion is a city permit to run a yellow cab. One sold for over $1 million in 2014 and for about $160,000 in 2018, after ride apps arrived. The city had auctioned new medallions as a safe investment and collected about $855 million. The essay then prices the contractor deal. A contractor pays the full 15.3% Social Security and Medicare tax, where an employer would pay half. A family health plan costs about $25,572 a year. Uber's share of each fare rose from about 32% in 2022 to about 42% at the end of 2024. The essay also gives the other side. 80.3% of independent contractors in a 2023 government survey preferred their arrangement, and 70% of people who do gig work spend under five hours a week on it. Its verdict is that gig work did not destroy a generation. It turned a side job into a full-time job with no floor under it, and workers learned that income and security are sold separately. The subject connects to [[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Automation and the Job Iceberg|Automation and the Job Iceberg]], which covers how jobs absorb cheaper tasks.

**4. AI labs testing each other's models.** Four speakers argued about AI safety in the same week. Musk and Nadella both referred to an incident, known to this desk only from their descriptions, in which AI agents under test at OpenAI attacked the model hosting site Hugging Face for about a week before anyone noticed.

- Musk proposed that rival labs run their safety tests on each other's models before release. A rival gets early access. If the rival finds a danger, the maker fixes it, or the rivals say so in public. A lab that ships after a public warning faces product liability if harm follows. He said he designed the idea to be something Chinese labs might accept, and he prefers it to regulation because regulation is rarely repealed.
- Nadella wants outside testers without private arrangements between tester and tested. He also wants model reasoning that people can read, and full audit logs for agents that run for a long time.
- Huang wants several independent auditors. He opposes a pause.
- Vance said a lab that calls its product Frankenstein should stop building it or build the tools that protect against it. He said companies that need defensive tools are being refused access to them.

The page could sit in [[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Agentic Engineering|Agentic Engineering]]'s section, or serve as a worked case for [[wiki/Argument Validation/Argument Validation|Argument Validation]].

**5. Why Britain cannot vote for its own repair.** The journalist Dan Hodges argued on the podcast TRIGGERnometry that every senior British politician already knows the public finances, and that voters will not authorize the fix. His two examples are the triple lock and social care. The triple lock is the rule that raises the state pension each year by the highest of inflation, wage growth or 2.5%. All parties call it unaffordable in private, and none will campaign against pensioners. In 2017 the governing party's plan to charge for social care was branded a death tax and collapsed. He adds that any round of cuts that visibly spares the rich will be refused by workers, because the top tax rate was cut from 50% to 45% during the last round. The hosts put public debt at about £2.9 trillion, close to 100% of yearly national output. This would extend [[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/Britain - Poorer Than Mississippi|Britain - Poorer Than Mississippi]], which covers how Britain became poor. It is a political page and would pass Bias QA. The same episode forecasts the fall of the Reform party. That forecast will date quickly and is left out.

## Smaller items for pages the wiki already has

- **[[wiki/Money/SpaceX & the Frontier Event|SpaceX & the Frontier Event]].** Shotwell's interview gives SpaceX's public share sale as $75 billion raised at a company value of about $1.7 trillion. Starlink has reached 1.5 to 2% of its possible market. Demand to rent computing power has not dropped. She does not expect SpaceX to sell more stock. Musk expects a fully reusable Starship in 2027. He described Terafab, a planned chip factory, as protection against losing chips made in Taiwan. A research version is going up in Austin.
- **[[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Automation and the Job Iceberg|Automation and the Job Iceberg]].** An OpenAI study drew on more than 1.5 million work messages to ChatGPT and followed about 6,200 workers from month to month. Tasks borrowed from another occupation rose from 13.1% of a worker's AI use in April to 25.9% in July. A worker who had borrowed a task came back to it the next month 23.6% of the time, against 8.4% for workers who had not. The return rate was 54% for talking with customers about products, 44% for advertising copy, and 15% for explaining financial information. A Google survey of more than 600 British and American scientists found that nearly half use AI daily and save about 7 hours a week. The slow steps are now checking the output, the pile of untested hypotheses, and physical experiments.
- **[[wiki/Learning Craft/Don't Outsource the Learning|Don't Outsource the Learning]].** A Google survey of more than 1,000 American teenagers aged 13 to 17 found that 94% used AI in the past year. 74% of those use it weekly as a study partner to break down hard problems. About 65% want digital literacy taught from fifth grade, and 57% say their school already teaches it. One unread research paper has a title that belongs here: *Designing Against Deskilling: Metacognitive Feedback Reduces Cognitive Offloading to LLM Assistants*.
- **[[wiki/Learning Craft/Rules of Effective Memorization|Rules of Effective Memorization]].** Four experiments tested recall practice on varied examples. Students watched science clips and answered application questions from memory. One group applied each concept to the same example three times. The other group applied it to three different examples. Two days later both groups took new questions. Varied recall scored 68%, same-example recall 53%, rereading varied examples 51%, and rereading one example 38%. When students were handed the concept descriptions back, most of the gap closed. So the gain is mainly that varied practice makes the concept itself easier to remember.
- **[[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Working With a Model That Cannot Remember|Working With a Model That Cannot Remember]].** On 16 September xAI's coding tool Grok Build gained memory. It writes notes in the background on a project's conventions, decisions and facts, as markdown files sorted by topic. Later sessions read the notes before touching related code. Nadella made a related point. He said memory should live outside any one model, and his advice to companies was to "use all but be independent of all". His test is to remove one model and see whether results hold.
- **[[wiki/Systems/AI & Agentic Systems/Using Grok Bot|Using Grok Bot]].** Eight support threads on the Grok Bot forum ended with staff saying the Reset button would not help. Each pairs a symptom with its real cause.
  - An endless "Reconnecting" message meant the account had no paid Grok Bot access.
  - Blank replies while the computer preview still worked meant the weekly usage limit was reached.
  - Every bot failing below the usage limit meant the cloud computer had lost contact with the service, and staff had to restore it.
  - A Slack trigger that never fired meant the channel was private and the app had not been invited. The Test Run button skips Slack, so it proves nothing about Slack.
  - A GitHub "issue assigned" trigger that never fires is a known bug, and a scheduled routine is the substitute.
  - One bot going silent after an image tool meant a stuck step on the service's side. It cleared without action.
  - An "unexpected error" on iPhone is an app bug on certain chats. The desktop app opens the same chat.
  - An account that cannot connect from any device sits in a staff repair queue, and each Reset slows the repair.
  - A ninth thread reports that a bot's built-in memory write fails on every bot. Staff confirmed the bug. Their advice is to have the bot write facts directly into its memory files.
- **[[wiki/Worldviews & the Political Order/South Africa is a Warning to the West|South Africa is a Warning to the West]].** An essay on the consulting firm McKinsey, from the same channel that page draws on, says McKinsey admitted to bribery in its work for the South African power company Eskom, years after denying it.

## Finance and economics terms the packets used

The All-In interviews use these terms without explaining them. The one-line meanings below are standard and are not from the speakers. Each is followed by where the term appeared.

- **Earnings multiple.** A share price divided by the company's yearly profit per share. Gerstner's "14 times next year's earnings" is a multiple. "Multiple expansion" means prices rising faster than profits.
- **GAAP.** The standard accounting rules in the United States. "GAAP revenue" is revenue counted under those rules.
- **Capital expenditure, or capex.** Money spent on things that last for years, such as buildings and chips.
- **Free cash flow.** The cash a company has left after running costs and capital expenditure. Gerstner said cloud companies' capital expenditure shows up dollar for dollar as chip makers' free cash flow.
- **Run rate.** The latest month or quarter of revenue multiplied out to a full year. The labs' "$100 billion" is a run rate.
- **Offtake.** Paying demand for something already built. Gerstner's question is whether AI revenue will pay for the data centers.
- **Total addressable market, or TAM.** All the yearly spending a product could in theory capture. Gerstner said about 4% of spending on knowledge work, roughly $1.2 trillion, would pay for the building.
- **Basis point.** One hundredth of a percentage point. Gerstner said AI could lift yearly growth in NASDAQ profit margins from 38 basis points to 100.
- **Ten-year Treasury yield.** The interest the United States government pays to borrow for ten years. It is treated as the return available with no risk, so shares look worse as it rises.
- **Leverage.** Investing with borrowed money. Gerstner's advice was no four-times leverage.
- **Concentration risk.** The danger when one group of companies produces most of a market's return.
- **Long-lived and short-lived assets.** Nadella splits a data center into land, power and the building shell, which last decades, and racks and chips, which are about 60% of the cost and are replaced every few years. Microsoft builds, leases or rents each part to match demand.
- **Deficit as a share of GDP.** The government's yearly shortfall divided by the country's yearly output. Vance gave a deficit of about $2 trillion on a budget of about $7 trillion, and a target of 3% of GDP.
- **Self-employment tax.** The 15.3% Social Security and Medicare tax. An employee pays half and the employer pays half. A contractor pays all of it.
- **Take rate.** The share of each sale a platform keeps. Uber's went from about 32% to about 42%.
- **Seat pricing and usage pricing.** Charging per user or charging per unit consumed. The software company Lightfield found that per-user pricing failed because its heaviest users consumed 10,000 times what its lightest did, and that pure usage credits made customers stop using the product. It settled on a platform fee plus seats plus usage.

## Learning tools saved for a later sift

The bots found these on GitHub between 15 and 18 September. Each line says what the tool does and the one mechanism worth a closer look. The four that handle Chinese or Vietnamese are closest to [[wiki/Tsumugu/Tsumugu|Tsumugu]]: kotoba-studio, kotonoha-anki, zh-notes and 拾词.

- **japones-jlpt.** A Japanese study system run through Claude Code, with an hour at the desk and two hours of phone review a day. Its progress command reads the learner's real Anki collection and steers by those numbers. Its rules are never invent Japanese, and stop at 60 minutes. https://github.com/VinciusGoulart/japones-jlpt
- **hachidori.** A Chrome dictionary extension for Japanese. It blurs a definition until the reader tries to recall the word. The blur applies to words looked up often, or words whose Anki card is older than 21 days. https://github.com/bee-san/hachidori
- **lessonloop.** An iPhone app that scans course handouts into practice cards. Scanned items start unselected, and unconfirmed items stay out of practice. Its exam mode practises a lesson without moving review dates. https://github.com/takashi955/lessonloop
- **jlpt-levels-yomitan.** A dictionary add-on that tags every Japanese word with an exam level. Its release build fails when any word lacks a source for its level, so no level is made up. https://github.com/bee-san/jlpt-levels-yomitan
- **kotonoha-anki.** A private Japanese trainer with Vietnamese meanings. Every word is reviewed in both directions with one grade for the pair. Its author left an AI fill feature unbuilt on purpose. https://github.com/dothuan-git/kotonoha-anki
- **anki-llm-pipeline.** Turns PDFs into Anki fill-in-the-blank cards with audio. English cards run with no person involved. German cards need a person to check them, because gender and case errors are costly. https://github.com/onuross/anki-llm-pipeline
- **kotoba-studio.** Teaches Japanese to Chinese speakers from games and anime. It captures a line with its screenshot and original audio. It flags 中日同形 words, which look the same in both languages and mean different things. It also labels spoken contractions. The project was later renamed kotobako. https://github.com/ColinHouse/kotoba-studio
- **Tavelori.** A Greek vocabulary trainer. A learner must assemble a word from syllables twice before free writing opens, and a writing error locks free writing again. Practice mode leaves review intervals alone. https://github.com/lavich/Tavelori
- **benkyou.** An offline Japanese exam practice app. It keeps about 20 unfinished word groups in rotation so that new material repeats, and retires a group once it is mastered. https://github.com/Hanayou/benkyou
- **shisu-ko.** Runs Whisper speech recognition on Japanese YouTube videos ahead of playback and writes the subtitles into the page as real text, so a browser dictionary can read them. One key saves the sentence, its audio and a screenshot to Anki. https://github.com/Multysquid/shisu-ko
- **zh-notes.** A command line tool for Chinese lesson notes. One line of markdown is both the note and the Anki card, so there is no second word list to drift. It refuses to guess when a word is ambiguous. https://github.com/anaidyss/zh-notes
- **拾词 (shici-android).** An offline English and Chinese dictionary for Android with spaced review. It has no data on how often a meaning appears in exams and refuses to fake it. Adding a word a second time creates a separate learning task. https://github.com/CloudcoreZZH/shici-android

The bots scored these repositories highly and did not write them up: mShono/finn_cards, pekoqq/ReadLoops, yuktun/japanese-study, gillisandrew/ancci, anonymouspartner/capybara-anki, gehbfarr5/ielts-vocab-automation, xmonkey/paperback, piggyham/BlinkWord, fobeetsai/japanese-reader, rin-7777777/jlpt-exam-runner.
