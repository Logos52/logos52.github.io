# Bear Hunter System - Aim Phase

The Aim phase of the Bear Hunter System directs attention with high-leverage questions before and during learning. It replaces the red light of the earlier Traffic Light System and turns raw input into the start of a personal map of relationships and priorities.

## Direct Attention With the Two Core Questions

Use these on every important concept:

- Why is this important?
- How is this related to ___?

The first forces purpose and priority. The second forces comparison, contrast, and dependency thinking. Together they generate candidate relationships fast, then you prune to the strongest ones and begin the backbone.

Apply during skims of headings, objectives, and summaries. Capture rough concepts first, then question each. The answers become the raw material for non-linear structure.

## Build and Refine the Backbone

Group answers into initial chunks based on shared function, sequence, contrast, or mechanism. Rearrange and relabel as clarity emerges. The backbone reflects your understanding, not source order.

Draw relationships as soon as they appear. Mark open questions. Early passes feel slow because you are generating and evaluating more connections than you will keep.

## Signals During Ascent I Application and Adjustments

When applying Aim in the first stage of the climb, watch for:

- Too many definitional questions (increase "why important" and "how related" pressure).
- Early structures that feel forced (test alternatives before committing).
- Linear drift while questioning (force rearrangement in the notes).

These signals decrease with practice. The map becomes faster and more automatic as the brain learns the pattern.

## Integration in the Climb

BHS Aim is the directed version of prestudy and the foundation for Shoot (Ascent II) and Skin (Ascent III). It strengthens every later retrieval session by creating multiple useful orders from the start. Use it before lectures, reading, videos, and revision of fragmented topics. Carry the same questions forward into all subsequent stages.
