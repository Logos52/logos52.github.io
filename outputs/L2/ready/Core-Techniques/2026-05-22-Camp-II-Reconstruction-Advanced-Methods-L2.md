# Camp II - Reconstruction and Advanced Methods

Camp II introduces heavier reconstruction techniques once the core tools from Camp I are reliable. The emphasis moves from basic retrieval to actively rebuilding and testing complex structures under varied conditions.

## Primary Advanced Methods

Reconstruction requires you to rebuild the map from memory, often using WPW (whole-part-whole) to test big picture, relationships, details, and application in one pass. Revision strategy shifts from recognition to full reteaching and gap exposure.

These methods are powerful because they force the brain to do the work of retrieval at multiple levels simultaneously. They are demanding because they require the stable maps produced by prior Skin passes.

## Camp II Signals and Diagnostics

The Final checkpoint – Camp 2 confirms:

- You can perform reconstruction passes that surface precise gaps rather than general discomfort.
- WPW sessions produce usable teaching artifacts that improve the next attempt.
- Revision time is spent on weak relationships rather than uniform coverage.

When reconstruction collapses into vague summaries, the corrective is more deliberate Skin on the underlying structure or more basic retrieval (Camp I) until the foundation is automatic.

## Role in the Climb

Camp II proves the system can handle advanced reconstruction without external support. It prepares the ground for the high-pressure, high-volume work of Summit (multipass, exam execution under load). The reconstruction skill developed here is what allows later stages to maintain quality when time is short or stakes are high.

Use every Camp II session as both practice and data source for the next marginal gain in reflection.
