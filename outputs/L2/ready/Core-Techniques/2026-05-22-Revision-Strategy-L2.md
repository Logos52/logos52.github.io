# Revision Strategy

Revision is the work of turning initial understanding into durable, flexible knowledge that is available when needed. Good revision is not about repetition for its own sake — it is about strengthening the right connections and exposing the weak ones.

## Core Principle

Effective revision is retrieval practice with feedback. The goal is to make the material retrievable under realistic conditions (different orders, time pressure, without the original notes in front of you) while also identifying exactly where understanding is still thin.

Passive re-reading creates a false sense of fluency. Active retrieval + correction creates real capability.

## Main Approaches

**Spaced Interleaved Retrieval (SIR)**  
Revisiting material at increasing intervals and in different orders. This is the baseline for most high-performing systems. The interleaving forces the brain to discriminate between similar ideas and strengthens the ability to choose the right approach.

**Multipass Systems**  
Going through material multiple times with different purposes on each pass (overview → relationships → detail → application). This is especially useful for dense or high-stakes material.

**Reconstruction**  
Attempting to rebuild the material from memory (often in non-linear form) and then comparing against the source. The gaps and distortions revealed during reconstruction are often more valuable than the successful recall.

**Exam-Style Practice**  
Deliberately practicing under conditions that match the eventual use (timed questions, closed notes, mixed topics). This is the final layer for high-stakes performance.

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating revision as "going over the notes again."
- Only revising in the same order the material was originally learned.
- Avoiding the uncomfortable feeling of not remembering — which is exactly when the useful work happens.
- Not adjusting revision intensity based on how well the material is actually sticking.

## Integration with Other Techniques

Revision quality is heavily dependent on the quality of the original encoding. Strong Bear Hunter System work (good Aim, Shoot, and Skin) makes revision dramatically more efficient. Weak encoding makes even excellent revision strategies less effective.

Revision also feeds back into encoding — noticing what is hard to retrieve usually reveals where the original maps were too shallow or poorly organized.

## Recommended Stance

Build a small number of reliable revision methods that you actually use consistently rather than collecting many techniques you apply sporadically. The best revision system is the one you will actually run on the material that matters most.

Track which methods produce the biggest improvement in retrieval under pressure and gradually favor those.
