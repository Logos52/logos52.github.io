# Technique Training & Fundamentals

Technique Training and Fundamentals form the practical foundation for building reliable higher-order learning skills. This area focuses on the core methods and mental models that make consistent deep work possible.

## Core Orientation

Technique Training teaches the specific mechanics of high-quality encoding and thinking. Fundamentals provide the checkpoints and baseline standards that prevent technique errors and cognitive overload as complexity increases.

The goal is to reach a level of conscious competency where the methods become reliable tools rather than things you have to think about constantly.

## Key Techniques in This Area

**Inquiry-Based Learning & Traffic Light System (TLS)**  
The foundational questioning method that replaces passive reading with active exploration. The red light (Aim) forces prioritization and relationship mapping before loading new material. The green light maintains forward momentum while still allowing course correction.

**Note-Taking Variants**  
Different formats serve different purposes:
- Processed notes force active reorganization.
- Reflective notes support deeper evaluation.
- Visual / mind mapping notes help surface structure and relationships.

The choice of format should match the type of thinking you need to do.

**Chunking Methods**  
Moving from isolated facts to meaningful groups. Includes importance-based chunking, intuitive chunking, and methods like GRINDE maps that help evaluate the quality of your chunks.

**Revision & Retrieval Foundations**  
Early revision strategies and the shift from rote memorization toward understanding-based retention. Emphasis on making retrieval practice more effective as encoding quality improves.

## Stage Signals & Checkpoints

Progress through Technique Training and Fundamentals is marked by increasing ability to:
- Ask high-quality questions that actually change how you structure information.
- Notice when your current approach is creating shallow or brittle understanding.
- Self-diagnose technique problems and adjust without external help.
- Maintain focus and energy while doing demanding mental work.

Common signals that you are ready to move forward include reduced reliance on the original source order, faster creation of useful backbones, and the ability to explain why certain relationships matter more than others.

## Integration with the Larger System

These fundamentals underpin everything that comes later in the Growth Phases (Ascent, Camp, Summit). Weak fundamentals create brittle performance when pressure or complexity rises. Strong fundamentals make later techniques (Bear Hunter System phases, advanced self-management, etc.) significantly more effective.

They also directly support the Five Dimensions by providing the practical methods that turn abstract goals (better encoding, better revision, better focus) into repeatable actions.

## Common Failure Modes

- Treating techniques as checkboxes rather than thinking tools.
- Skipping checkpoints and moving forward with shaky competency.
- Using the same note-taking style for everything regardless of the thinking required.
- Underestimating how much deliberate practice is needed to make the methods automatic.

## Recommended Approach

Work through the core Technique Training and Fundamentals material until you can reliably use inquiry, chunking, and basic revision without the original source material in front of you. Use the checkpoints as honest diagnostic tools rather than things to rush past. Once these are solid, the more advanced stage-specific techniques become much more powerful.
