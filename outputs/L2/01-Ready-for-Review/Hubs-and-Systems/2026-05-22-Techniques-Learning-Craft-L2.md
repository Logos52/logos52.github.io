# Techniques - Learning Craft

This section collects the practical methods and mental models that make high-quality learning reliable over time. It sits alongside the Five Dimensions as the craft layer — the actual tools and ways of working that turn the dimensions into daily reality.

## Connection to the Five Dimensions

The Five Dimensions (Self-Management, Self-Regulation, Deep Processing, Mindset, and Retrieval) provide the overarching diagnostic and design framework. The techniques in this section are the concrete methods used inside those dimensions.

Strong technique work makes the dimensions far more effective. Weak or inconsistent technique work makes even good intentions in the dimensions fragile.

## Core Areas

**Encoding & Deep Work Techniques**  
Methods for turning incoming information into well-structured, retrievable knowledge. Includes inquiry systems, chunking approaches, note-taking formats, and the Bear Hunter System phases.

**Revision & Retrieval Systems**  
How to move from initial understanding to durable, flexible knowledge. Covers different revision strategies, retrieval practice design, and making review sessions genuinely effective rather than just repetitive.

**Focus, Attention & Energy Management**  
The systems that protect the conditions under which good technique can actually be used. Attention architecture, work block design, recovery, and maintaining sustainable energy.

**Habit & Routine Architecture**  
How to make the above techniques happen consistently without relying on constant willpower. Includes habit design, productive routines, and environmental shaping.

**Decision & Prioritization Systems**  
Methods for choosing what to work on, when to change direction, and how to allocate limited time and attention under real-world constraints.

**Getting Started & Orientation**  
Lightweight frameworks for beginning new learning projects or resetting when things have gone off track (Briefing-style overviews, Rapid Start methods, etc.).

## How to Use This Section

These pages are meant to be used as references and working notes rather than a linear curriculum. Most people will move between them depending on what skill they are currently strengthening.

The strongest results come from treating the techniques as living tools that get refined through use, not as things to learn once and then apply mechanically.

## Relationship to the Growth Phases

The stage material (Base Camp through Summit) shows how these techniques are typically introduced and deepened over time. However, the techniques themselves can be studied and improved independently of the stage progression.

Many people find it useful to strengthen specific technique areas (for example, revision or focus systems) even while working at an earlier stage in other areas.

## Current State

This section is being built from the detailed source material in the ICS corpus. Pages are added and refined as clusters of related raw material are synthesized. The goal is a set of clear, usable notes rather than an exhaustive reproduction of every original source.
