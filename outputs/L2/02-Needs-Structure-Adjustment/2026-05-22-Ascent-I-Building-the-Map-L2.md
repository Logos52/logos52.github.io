# Ascent I - Building the Map

Ascent I marks the shift into deliberate higher-order encoding. You stop collecting information in the order it arrives and start directing your brain with questions that surface importance, relationships, and natural chunk boundaries before the source fully loads.

## The Two Core Questions

Use these on every new concept during the Aim pass:

- Why is this important?
- How is this related to ___?

The first question forces priority and purpose. The second forces comparison, contrast, cause-effect, and dependency mapping. Together they turn raw input into the beginnings of a personal map instead of a transcript.

Apply them while skimming headings, objectives, diagrams, and summaries. Capture rough concept lists first, then question each item. The answers become the raw material for your backbone.

## Forming the Backbone

Group answers into initial chunks based on shared function, sequence, contrast, or recurring mechanism. Rearrange and relabel as relationships clarify. The backbone does not need to match the source order — it must match how the ideas actually connect for you.

Non-linear notes (maps, clusters, arrows) make the structure visible. Draw relationships as soon as they appear. Mark open questions instead of forcing premature closure.

Early passes generate many candidate relationships; prune ruthlessly to the strongest before committing to structure.

## Stage-Specific Pressures, Barriers, and Adjustments

Ascent I surfaces several distinct pressures from the grouped sources:

- From "Barriers to aiming": time objections, perceived hardness, fear of missing something, and assessment mismatch (what the exam actually requires vs. what feels safe to ask). Counter by keeping questions tied to performance needs and accepting that initial maps are incomplete.
- From "Distractions - Ascent I.md": cognitive switching penalty. Protect single-task focus during Aim; batch stray thoughts instead of following every association immediately.
- From "Inquiry clean-up - Ascent I.md" and "Maintaining focus": importance-based chunking and quality over quantity. Stop adding once the backbone supports the required relationships; extra detail can wait for Shoot.
- From "Ascent I check": use the competency table (realised higher-order thinking, practised non-linear notes, formed backbones, used Kolb’s, shifted to marginal gains lens). The checkpoint is passed when you can demonstrate these in the current topic.

When the map stays flat or questions remain definitional, increase "why important" and "how related" pressure. Revisit inquiry clean-up for loose ends. These adjustments come directly from the Ascent I cluster raws.

## Polish for Flow and Signal

The foundation built here — question quality, backbone discipline, and early prioritisation — determines how far the rest of the climb can go. When checkpoints pass consistently, the system is ready for the denser demands of Ascent II.

## Integration With Bear Hunter System

Ascent I is the first full application of BHS Aim. Prestudy creates the broad frame; Aim sharpens it into directed questions. The Shoot phase (Ascent II) then answers those questions while the material is live. Skin (Ascent III) later consolidates the map.

Carry the same two questions forward into later stages. They remain the fastest way to keep encoding at higher order even when time is short.

## Moving Forward From Ascent I

When checkpoints pass consistently, the system is ready for Ascent II demands: sustaining the map while handling denser input, adjusting on the fly, and using the emerging structure for retrieval.
