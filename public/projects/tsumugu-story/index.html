Tsumugu
Tsumugu Story

Tsumugu Story follows six students from different countries, coming to Taipei to study Chinese at a learning center. Not all of them speak English, so they can only communicate with each other with whatever broken Chinese they’ve learned at the time. It’s a slice-of-life story that spans two years, about as long as it takes to reach conversational fluency in Chinese. As your Chinese progresses, so does the depth of their relationships and their character development.

沈文 Shěn Wén

the builder

He builds: robotics, AI, whatever's broken on the table in front of him. He says the least of anyone there and is the reason there's a table; his handmade robot, 小圖, rides on his bag strap wherever he goes.

這個可以修。

Zhège kěyǐ xiū. · "This can be fixed."

阮草 Ruǎn Cǎo

the Tender

She grows mushrooms and runs ferments off a shelf in the corner of the café, and the group borrowed her word for it — mát tay, the cool hand. Headstrong, direct, and warmly certain she's right, she shows love by doing: the right mushroom pulled for the right person on the right day.

活的東西急不來。

Huó de dōngxi jí bù lái. · "Living things can't be hurried."

星野遙香 Xīngyě Yáoxiāng

the super-fan

The Osaka super-fan who's more into Taiwan than the locals are — loud, quick, and always behind her film camera. She's the group's energy, the half-step-ahead one who gets everyone out the door: first to try the stinky tofu, last to want the night to end.

超級好看的啦！

Chāojí hǎokàn de la! · "It's so good-looking!"

金多恩 Jīn Duō'ēn

the conservator

Quiet, sharp, unhurried — the group's best test-taker, who says the least and notices the most. She lands the plain hard verdict in a handful of cold words, then quietly picks up the broken thing everyone else stepped over and mends it.

看得出來，才是好的。

Kàn de chūlái, cái shì hǎo de. · "That you can see it is what makes it good."

阿迪 Ā Dí

the maker

The warm, laid-back maker from Jakarta who stitches batik into streetwear. What the shirts earn goes home to his family; he carries that quietly, and he is the last one in the room to decide anything.

不要緊張，又不會怎樣。

Bùyào jǐnzhāng, yòu bù huì zěnyàng. · "Don't stress — nothing's going to happen."

白龍 Bái Lóng

the deadpan far-seer

A towering, bone-dry Englishman with a cavernous bass and binoculars round his neck — he says the least in any room and sees the furthest, calling the rain or the crowd a beat before it lands, then saying nothing about having been right. The café's ginger tom 將軍, who likes nobody, sleeps across his shoulder.

等一下就會下雨了。

Děng yīxià jiù huì xiàyǔ le. · "It'll rain in a bit."

Tsumugu Story is meant as a Chinese language learning supplement. Its main story arc is a companion piece for the textbook A Course in Contemporary Chinese, and it ties into the vocabulary students are learning there, so as the six learn and interact, you study the same things they study.

The intention is like having classmates who undergo the same type of drama that students experience in their own lives: stories about fighting, loneliness, mental health and belonging. This is not meant to entirely be a wholesome story; it’s meant to cover the full breadth and depth of human experience, in order to fully encompass all the ways in which we communicate and share our lives.

It takes loose inspiration from several sources: the American sitcom Friends, which Chinese students use to study English; character-driven role-playing games such as Baldur’s Gate 3, where the characters share one story but each has their own struggles and missions; and the characters in that textbook, who mirror a student’s experiences for a lesson or two and then disappear.
