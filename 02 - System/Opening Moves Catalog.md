# Opening Moves Catalog

Openers are picked from this catalog at design time, by page genre — the way the corpus's own writers do it (Woźniak types his pages, and the type dictates the opening shape). Every specimen below is verbatim, verified at collection; the full evidence base is [[wiki/Research/Blog Craft Research Bank|the blog craft research bank]]. The catalog exists because ten generated openers failed in a row before the corpus was consulted; derived moves replace invented ones.

**The master rule, stated by every writer in the corpus:** the first sentence carries the payload — the conclusion, the definition, the finding — never preparation for it. Graham's operational form: "just say the most important sentence first." A hook is impossible once the conclusion is already given away, which is why the frame below makes the banned devices unnecessary rather than merely forbidden.

## The master frame: the abstract

Gwern's house rule, mandatory on his essays: the page opens with a summary block — background, finding, conclusion — that gives everything away. "All essays should begin with an abstract." The consequences do the work: nothing downstream carries persuasive load; anecdotes demoted below the abstract read as illustration instead of bait; a question below an abstract can't hook because its answer sits above it. His pages then layer by resolution (abstract → headers → margin notes → footnotes → appendices), so the abstract is "the second-coarsest zoom level, not an entrance." An optional metadata line (status · certainty · importance) states epistemic confidence once, structurally, instead of hedging sentence by sentence. Fit here: the abstract is the whole–part–whole opening whole done as structure, and the metadata line is the confidence dial with the mechanics removed.

## First-sentence moves

The moves the corpus actually shows, each with a verified specimen. Genre notes map to this vault's page types.

1. **Copular definition.** The term is the grammatical subject; "X is a Y that does Z." — "Spaced repetition is a learning method based on computing optimum intervals…" (Woźniak). The payload is sentence one; nothing is withheld. *Fits: concept pages, glossary-shaped notes — the default for this vault.*

2. **Dated checkable fact.** An object in the world, a date, a magnitude. — "GPT-3, announced by OpenAI in May 2020, is the largest neural network ever trained, by over an order of magnitude." (Gwern). Sets the contract as claims-and-evidence. *Fits: analysis pages, project write-ups.*

3. **Thesis as plain causal fact.** The finding stated cold, no framing. — "One reason programmers dislike meetings so much is that they're on a different type of schedule from other people." (Graham). *Fits: concept pages whose claim is causal.*

4. **Attributed folklore.** A sweeping claim opened as someone else's assertion, sourced, then examined. — "Statistical folklore asserts that 'everything is correlated'…" (Gwern). The repair for any census-shaped opener: assert that the claim is asserted. *Fits: pages examining a common belief.*

5. **Opposing position at full strength, then flat dissent.** The rejected view stated generously in its own vocabulary; a new paragraph says "I argue that this is not an effective solution." (Gwern). The reveal-flip with the theatre removed — nothing withheld, the count of reasons announced. *Fits: argument pages.*

6. **Retell the object intact.** The story or claim under examination summarized fairly before it is touched, the motivating defect arriving as a trailing clause — "…but the tank story is usually never sourced." (Gwern). *Fits: debunkings, reviews.*

7. **The debt.** The page exists because a prior claim is outstanding — "I have called [him] 'hubristic'… So I ought to explain what he did wrong." (Gwern). Reason-to-exist without any bid for attention. *Fits: follow-ups, personal notes extending an earlier position.*

8. **Definitional stack.** The working terms defined before any argument — "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep…" (Naval). Vocabulary handed over, not a feeling. *Fits: framework pages.*

9. **Unhedged absolute, scope repaired next.** The claim at full strength, the boundary in the following fragment — "Nobody works eighty hours a week. Not eighty real, productive hours." (Naval). Confidence handled by sequence, never by in-sentence hedges. *Fits: doctrine lines anywhere; the condensed pages already speak this way.*

10. **Epistemic preamble.** Genre, status, and reader contract declared before content — "These are notes to myself. Your frame of reference… may vary." (Naval). *Fits: personal notes and door pages; the Personal section's intro already uses it.*

11. **Own practice, first person.** A flat report of what the author does — "I try to write using ordinary words and simple sentences." (Graham). Cannot be false for the reader; the general principle gets earned afterward. *Fits: personal register.*

12. **Real question, answered immediately.** The question that generated the piece, discharged within a sentence — "…what would the intersection look like? I decided to find out by making it." (Graham). Distinct from a rhetorical question: it converts to provenance instead of hanging. *Fits: research write-ups.*

13. **Scope fence.** What the piece is not about, named warmly, then the subject by subtraction — "beautiful websites come in all sizes… This talk isn't about any of those." (Cegłowski). Pre-empts the wrong frame instead of pre-empting boredom. *Fits: pages whose title invites a misreading.*

14. **Occasion plus deliverable.** Why the piece exists and what shape it takes, in one sentence, then delivery — "People have asked me for advice on writing nonfiction online, so here are some tips:" (Alexander). *Fits: workflow and how-to pages.*

The corpus also holds two moves used by admired writers that stay off this vault's menu: the concrete-example stack (Alexander's fourteen examples — his examples are propositions, never scenes, but the family is close enough to the struck scene-opener to skip), and the literary gambit of Gwern's own early pages, which his later practice abandoned for the abstract — his stated reason being durability.

## Use

Pick the move before drafting, by genre; under an abstract, the first body sentence needs no threshold work at all and simply starts the explanation. When none of the fourteen fits, the fallback is the master rule alone: say the most important sentence first.

Openers are presented as several catalog-derived options for the owner's pick — different moves, each written out in full, side by side. One candidate at a time is a funnel (ruled 2026-08-11: "i hate being funneled"), and serial rejection is the most expensive way to find a preference that a parallel pick finds in one round.
