# The Generator — Selfhood v2

This file is read before the first pass of a page where the thing handed to the reader is the writer's own: a position he holds, his own thinking written down, a note about his life. It says what goes on the page and who it is for. It says nothing about how the sentences sound. That job moved to [[02 - System/Rewrite Prompt|the rewrite prompt]], which a second head applies to the finished draft, because two days of telling the writer how to sound produced the next fault each time, and the record of that is [[journal/2026-08-22-the-context-problem|The Context Problem]].

It adds to [[02 - System/The Generator|The Generator]], the file that says how every page in this vault is written. The Generator calls what the writer is doing while a page comes out *the act*. The act's two paragraphs are carried below in plain words. The original wording stays in The Generator, and where anything added here seems to disagree with those paragraphs, they win. The file stays marked unproven until a page written under it, and passed through the rewrite prompt, is accepted by the owner.

---

Every page in this vault is something you own — something you lived, tested, or built — and you are handing it to a reader you respect. The opening is that handover: what the thing is, what it's for, what it gives them, said the way you'd show a friend a tool from your own workshop. Ease them in the way a learner skims a chapter before studying it: the wide view before the detail, the whole thing before its parts, a welcome before any work. A value gets introduced with the respect you'd give something you hold. A skill gets introduced by what it lets the reader do. Someone doing that doesn't set the reader up to be caught out, doesn't polish a sentence to be quoted on its own, and doesn't open on what's wrong before the reader has seen the thing — not because those are banned, but because they aren't things a person doing *that* actually says.

---

What these pages add. The thing handed over is a judgment. It is a position held, a line drawn, or something declined. A judgment is no use to the friend until they can see the question it answers, and that question has to be one they are standing in too, on ground they already hold. So the page opens on the question, and the position lands as its answer.

The friend has never read the page. Every word the writer uses is one the friend already owns or one the page has already handed them. A term this vault coined gets said in plain words the first time, and its name comes after. The owner's test, from 2026-08-20: a sentence is finished when you would say it out loud to him without bracing, and when someone who has only read the sentences above it knows what every word refers to. A sentence copied from somewhere else has not been written. It gets composed here from what it means, or it stays out. A familiar word used in a sense the page gives it is a coined term too. If the page means "the reader meets the label before the argument or after it," it says that in those words before it ever says the label arrives.

Nothing on the page is used before the page has given it. A sentence can depend on four things, and the page has to have handed the reader each one before the sentence comes. On a list page each item is read alone. Nothing above an item counts as given except the item's own title. Every name, every "the X", and every pronoun in a blurb is given by the blurb itself or by that title. A sentence that refers to a finding gives the finding. A count is given only with the things counted, so "two of the seven hold" is not written unless the two are named and the reason each holds is said. "The page weighs X" with no result after it is an announcement and is never a blurb on its own. A sentence talks about the world and never about the argument: an election, a fight, a debt, a school day, not reasons, claims, premises, evidence, what holds, what the page finds. "Two things that happen together" and "a gap between two groups" are placeholders for things the page has not named; the sentence names them or goes. A blurb about a trend carries no dates.

What the words point at. Every noun, and every "it," "one," and "they," points at something the page has already introduced.

What the words mean here. A word is used in the sense the reader already has, or the page has said the sense it means. A familiar word in a sense the page gave it is a coined term, and it gets said plainly first.

Why two things are treated as the same. If a sentence says one thing is a case of another, or works like another, the page has already shown why, in enough steps that the reader made the connection before the sentence states it. The page says what the first thing does, in its parts. Then it says what the second thing does, in the same parts. The reader sees the two lists match. Only then does the page say they are the same.

Why the thing is on the page at all. The reader knows what question a new thing is answering before the page uses it.

The test for a sentence is one question. What does the reader have to already hold for this sentence to make sense? Every item in the answer is above the sentence, on the page, said outright. A missing item goes in first, as its own sentences, never as a clause hung on the sentence that needs it. The writer checks against the page above, never against his own head, because in his head everything is already connected.

Who the reader came for. On a positions page the reader came for the positions and not for the person holding them. The page says once, at the top, whose positions these are, and after that every position stands as a claim with nothing in front of it and no I. A sentence about the author, what he wants, what he has met, what he is, is a sentence the reader did not come for. The title names the thing and never the holder. A position is a side taken on a question the reader has in front of him, a page, a law, a practice. A general truth that no page puts a question to is a reason, not a position. Stated on its own it is the lecture. It goes inside the line it is the reason for, after the position, or it gets a page of its own where the question is asked. In the content step every fact is sorted into one of the two, and reasons never get a section of their own. (Struck 2026-08-23: "a people is a set of values rather than a bloodline" as the first line of the positions page, for the fourth time.)

What the writer stands on. He stands on what he knows and on what the owner has said on the record: the interviews, the journals, the rulings, and the wiki pages the position rests on. A page that already exists about the subject is not material. It is a prior attempt. It is closed before he writes and opened afterward only to check a fact. A page written with the old page open comes out at the old page's length, in the old page's order, with a joint wherever two of its pieces meet. That is how an assembled page gets made.

What he does after the handover. The person showing a friend a tool does not stop at what it is and what it is for. He shows it working on one real case. He shows where it breaks, or where people get it wrong. He says what follows from it for the friend. When the friend is about to object, he answers the objection, after the fact that raised it and never before. Then he comes back to where they started, with the friend now able to use the thing. He never names the move he is making. He does not say "here is an example," or "the same thing happens with a face." He tells the example, with the people in it and what happened, and the friend sees it is the same thing before he says so. When the page turns to a new thing, he says what it is and why it is here before he uses it. The close stands on the same ground as the opening, with the question now answerable. [[personal/2026-08-11-the-two-egos|The Two Egos]] does each of these in turn. It opens on a rule the site used to run under, shows what the rule cost, says where the writer had it wrong, says what follows, and ends with the rule reversed. It is the nearest model on disk. Its sentences are the model for the sentences, for the reason in the next paragraph.

---

Carried from the locked file, in plain words:

That stance holds for the whole page, not an opening stapled to a body of conclusions.

While it runs, the writer stays inside the thing he's handing over. The opening is as long as it takes to hand it over — what it is, what holding it costs, what it buys — and then it stops. The thing is described from the first sentence; the name this vault gives it comes once the reader could have supplied it themselves. A few easy sentences followed by a line polished to be quoted on its own is still a line polished to be quoted.

What that sounds like, in the owner's words on 2026-08-13 (15:11–15:14): ease in; a simple starting story; bird's-eye, something easy to look at; stop trying to do too much too quickly. Even seven plain words are a big step if they carry the page's whole claim at once. Live models, three pages whose openings the owner accepted: [[wiki/Concepts/Selfhood and the Ledger|Selfhood and the Ledger]], [[wiki/Concepts/Good Faith|Good Faith]] (the opening labelled A was the accepted one), [[wiki/Dimensions/Mindset/Confidence Calibration|Confidence Calibration]] (the opening labelled B).

## How a page under this file is made

0. The writer reads [[02 - System/Readers|Readers]] and finds the reader for this kind of page. That is the only picture of the reader he may use.
   0a. The opening gives the reader the thing before anything about the page: what the thing is, and what people claim for it, in words the reader brought with them. No count of the parts, and no "the X" that points at a section, until that section has been given, because a reader cannot hold a reference to something that does not exist yet on the page. (Struck 2026-08-23 on a DEI opening that named "the two kinds" and "the ten reasons" before saying what a DEI program is.)
1. If a page about the subject already exists, it is reduced to facts. One line per fact, in the writer's words, none of the page's sentences. Then the page is closed. The research bank, the interview, and every other source are closed too. While he drafts, the only things open are the fact list, the ledger, and the draft so far.
2. The question is written down in one sentence. It is the question the friend would ask. If the writer cannot write it, there is no page yet.
   2a. Before any paragraph, the writer puts an outline to the owner: the sections, what each carries, in what register, and what stays out. The whole at the top of the outline is written as what the reader already knows about the subject and what the thing is; the counts and the kinds belong to the parts, where they get introduced. Each whole in the outline is written as what the reader sees at that point, an idea, never as "what this page is" or "what the next part is about"; an outline whose wholes are notes about the page produces a page that announces itself. The outline is shaped whole, part, whole: its first section is a whole, the wide view of the thing before any part of it, and its last section is a whole again, advanced, with the question the opening raised now answerable. Between them come the parts, and a whole can come between parts whenever the reader needs to see the thing again before the next part. Any run is legal so long as it starts and ends on a whole: whole, part, whole; whole, part, part, whole; whole, part, whole, part, whole. Each section in the outline says which it is, a whole or a part, so the shape can be judged before a paragraph exists (ruled 2026-08-22; the shape itself is in [[02 - System/Writing Standards|Writing Standards]] §2). Outline first is the default for every page, and the owner can tell the writer to skip it for a page (ruled 2026-08-22: "this should be the standard workflow for all pages. outline first then writing", and the same evening that a hard rule here was too hard, since he may want to break it once the workflow is routine).
   2a-ii. The logic of a page lives in its layout, not in its words. The claim being tested is the opening. Each defense or reason is a section with its own heading. The order of the sections is the order of the argument. The close says what is left standing. When the layout carries that, the prose does not name it: "premise," "conclusion," "inference," "the defense," "the argument," "the claim" are words for the shape, and the shape is already on the page, so the sentence says the thing instead. (His word, 2026-08-23: "logic jargon should be implied within the formatting and outline + layout itself rather than explicit.")
   2b. For every paragraph that brings in a new thing, the writer first writes the chain. The chain is the list of what the reader has to hold, in order, each link something they can check from the link before it. The first link is something the page has already given them. The conclusion is the last link. The paragraph walks the chain in that order, so the reader reaches the conclusion before the writer says it.
3. The writer answers the question to the owner, in the conversation, one paragraph at a time, and never writes the whole page in one go. After each paragraph he runs the holdings ledger, `scripts/holdings.py`, on the draft as written. The ledger lists every definite reference, name, and pronoun the paragraph used and whether the page gave it above. It also lists the sentence lengths in the paragraph, so a run of short flat sentences shows up as a row of small numbers. That row is a description for the writer's eye, never a target. It is built from the text that came out, never from a plan. The next paragraph may use only what the ledger holds. A paragraph with a reference the page did not give is written again, from its question. A blurb is a page. It goes through the ledger before it goes to the owner, including when it is drafted in a reply.
4. When the draft is whole, it goes through the rewrite pass: a fresh head that has only the draft and [[02 - System/Rewrite Prompt|the rewrite prompt]] in front of it rewrites the page into plain, natural English and keeps every fact. The writer does not do this pass on his own draft in the same context. Then the rewritten page goes to a cold read ([[02 - System/Cold Read|Cold Read]]): a separate reader whose only context is the draft text. Every paragraph it cannot resolve is written again, in the page's own register and at the sentence length the owner would use out loud. A gap closed in shorter, plainer sentences flattens the page; three rounds of that produced a page he called "too plain" (2026-08-22). The check never sets the register. The cold read is temporary, by the owner's ruling of 2026-08-22; it stays until drafts stop failing it.
5. The draft is saved without rewriting. The gate in [[02 - System/Writing Standards|Writing Standards]] §3 deletes and swaps. Nothing else changes.
6. The old page is opened to check facts. A wrong fact is corrected. No sentence is replaced.

The order is the mechanism. Step 1 leaves no sentence to carry and no length to inherit. Step 3 gives the draft the shape of an explanation, because an answer to a person has that shape, and it puts the inventory of what the reader holds outside the writer's head, rebuilt from the text after each paragraph. Step 4 is the check the writer cannot do himself, because a gap is invisible from inside the head that made it. The record behind this is [[01 - Workbench/context-complaint-history-2026-08-22|the context complaint history]]: two hundred and eighteen instances in six weeks, and the only fixes that held were the ones that replaced the writer's judgment of what the reader knows with an inventory outside him.

## Why this file exists

The Generator — Selfhood, the first version, tried to carry the register as well as the content: plain words, natural sentences, no punch, no lecture, no announcement. On 2026-08-22 and 23 every one of those was added as a paragraph after a strike, and every one was followed within the hour by the next form of the same fault. The owner's words: the stupid thing in different languages. His finding, the same day: a plugin that rewrites Claude's replies into plain English does it with one sentence given to a different model after the message is finished, and that is the only kind of fix the two-day record shows holding. So the register is a second pass now, in its own file, and this file is only about what goes on the page. Everything else in the first version is carried here unchanged.

Standing law, carried from the locked file: never answer a strike with a detector. A strike is the owner rejecting a line, in his own words. A detector is a pattern-match, a mandatory checklist, or a threshold written to catch the rejected line. It catches that line, and the next failure walks past it.
