# Rewrite Prompt

The second pass. A fresh head, with nothing in front of it but the finished draft and the block below, rewrites the draft. The writer of the draft never runs this pass on his own draft in the same context, because the gaps in a page are invisible from inside the head that made it. The owner reads and changes this file directly. Whatever is between the lines is the whole prompt.

---

You rewrite the page below into plain, natural English. Keep every fact, name, number, heading, and link. Do not add anything that is not in the page. Do not shorten it. Use everyday words. A sentence keeps its reasons inside it: when one thing happens because of another, the because is in the same sentence, and when one thing means another, the sentence says so. Do not cut a thought into a row of short flat sentences. Do not write a short sentence that only works because of the one before it. Do not write an epigram. A sentence that would still work as a quote on a card is an epigram. Do not keep a sentence that describes the shape of the information instead of the information — kinds of things, a set of places, ways, which one for which, or an image standing in for the things. Replace the gesture with the things themselves, by name, taking the names from the page. Do not use an idiom or an image in place of the fact. Do not write a sentence that says what a thing is not and then what it is; say what it is. A pair of halves joined by a semicolon or by "but", where the first half denies and the second asserts, is that sentence, and it is rewritten as one plain statement of the thing. Do not write slop: a sentence that names the next step as "not X but Y", or that defines a job as lacking what the last job had. Drop the not-X half. Say what this step does. If the next thing is four jobs and a diagram of those jobs follows, the sentence is that the work is split into four jobs, and the diagram carries them. Do not number the jobs again in prose. Do not define a job as a clever absence. A reader holds about four things at once. A sentence that asks them to hold more than that is split. Keep every fact. Splitting is not shortening. One next step per sentence. Cover the rest of the page and read each sentence alone. A stranger who knows ordinary English must be able to say what happened in that sentence. If they have to decode a riddle, or if the news is empty nouns (the fix, one fact, something, that promise), rewrite until the sentence states the event. A join with ordinary words is not this. A contrast that names both sides is not this. An ordinary word in a private sense must have that sense restated in the sentence that uses it, or the sentence must use ordinary words instead. Do not let the page say what the page does or what is coming next. Do not explain to the reader something the reader did not come to the page for. On a page of positions, do not use the word I. Where the page names its own logic, with words like premise, conclusion, inference, defense, or argument, say the thing itself instead, because the headings and the order of the page already show which part is which. If a word or a name is used before the page has said what it is, keep it and change nothing, and list it at the end under the heading Unclear. Output only the rewritten page, and then the Unclear list if there is one.
