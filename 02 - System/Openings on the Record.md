# Openings on the Record

The openings on disk that were not struck: some picked on a board over other candidates, some kept when he said to keep them, some that went live under his eye without a strike. The owner has not ruled any opening on this list accepted as a model, and on 2026-09-10 he said so: he has not accepted any openings. So this file is the record of what survived, with the date and what happened, and it is read for shape. It is not a target. [[02 - System/Rejected Specimens|Rejected Specimens]] is the other half and is a gate file, not writer reading.

## The rulings that govern an opening

- **Cold thesis, 2026-08-13.** "when i said 'cold thesis' i want you to take a look at our other worked out examples, like the 4 pages we did with Fable before." The claim or the definition in sentence one, at full strength, on a page whose subject has a name.
- **The complement decides, 2026-08-13.** "i don't think copular is working as an intro… you don't know how to execute it properly." A copular definition lands only when the "Y that does Z" is a thing the reader can feel or picture on contact: a prediction your nervous system makes, the trouble you cause other people, a condition of full absorption, a short stretch that puts material into memory. An operation between abstractions in the same grammar is a compressed step and is struck.
- **The on-ramp, 2026-08-13,** for pages with no nameable subject or a value at their core: "priming needs to be a birds eye view. something easy to look at," "a simple starting story," "stop trying to do too much too quickly," "big steps are not inviting." Not for a page whose subject has a name.
- **Definition first, 2026-09-05 (the ELI5 format).** The subject defined in sentence one, then example bullets and a figure per idea.
- **Content, not writing, 2026-08-29.** "do not use circular definitions," "do not use copular definitions" (of the empty kind above), "do not use definitions that have too many items," and when the list of pointable things is empty, "ask me first what kind of content it should be."
- **Cold definition, 2026-09-10.** For the Trained Contempt page: start with a cold definition.

## Concept pages, not struck

**Good Faith**, opening A, 2026-08-13. Picked on the board over B and C.
> Good faith is a commitment you carry into every dealing that matters: the other person gets the true picture from you — your real constraints, your real intentions, the downsides you could have sat on — even when telling them costs you something now. The cost is the working part: anyone can say cooperative things, and a disclosure that cost you something is the one the other person can actually believe. Held over time, the practice buys what makes dealings worth having — problems met while they are small, trust that compounds, a reputation for meaning what you say. It is not naive honesty, and it does not mean saying everything to everyone.

**Confidence Calibration**, opening B, 2026-08-13. Picked on the board.
> Two checks can tell you whether a certainty of yours deserves the weight you are putting on it. The first takes ten seconds: is my confidence here high, and has real trained time gone into this area? The second takes minutes and a pen: write down every condition that would have to hold for the belief to be true, and read the list. Confidence calibration is the practice of running those checks from outside the feeling of being sure — the feeling comes built in, the evidence does not — and what it buys is practical: the method you run next week gets picked by what has actually been tested.

**Selfhood and the Ledger**, 2026-08-13, the primed opening after five strikes, kept.
> Most of being considerate is easy to see. Take up less space, make less noise, leave things cleaner than you found them — nobody needs a page for that part. Speaking is the part that is hard to see. A sentence takes something from everyone who hears it — their time, their attention — so a careful person learns to spend sentences carefully too. And carefulness with sentences, left to itself for years, quietly turns into silence. That slide, from careful to silent, is what this page is about.

**Flow state**, picked as the best of three drafts of the same page.
> Flow state is a condition of full absorption in a single task, in which attention holds without forcing and the sense of time recedes.

**Probability Distributions**, the definition kept on 2026-08-29 over three others.
> A probability distribution is a way of describing how likely each possible outcome is when something is uncertain. It assigns a probability—a number between 0 and 1—to each outcome, where a higher number means the outcome is more likely to occur.

**Dating Apps - The Gini Coefficient**, 2026-09-05, under the one-pass method.
> The Gini coefficient of a dating app is one number, between 0 and 1, for how unevenly the likes that women send land on the men. On Hinge in 2017 it came out at 0.542 for the men, which on the list economists keep for national incomes sits near apartheid-era South Africa.

**Survive and Thrive**, draft B, 2026-09-02, picked over draft C.
> Each new piece gets one test, whether it solves a problem or fits a pattern. Analogies, ties to prior learning, and simpler labels attach it to what is already known, and comparing or grouping attaches it among the new pieces. What sits in that network is kept, and what does not is ordinary forgetting.

## Trained Contempt, opening N, 2026-09-10

Let stand by the owner after twenty struck openings: "N is better. you can use N." His note with it: the shape is why it passes, and the writing is still not good. Built in the order The Generator — From Nothing describes.
> Contempt, in everyday use, means looking down on someone. On this page it means something smaller and more useful. It is the feeling of looking at a thing you made or use and not being able to stand it as it is. A page you wrote that says less than it could. A tool that fails at a job it ought to handle. That feeling is where every better version of anything comes from, because nobody remakes a thing they are content with. Trained, on this page, means what it means for a dog or a reflex: done so many times that it now happens without you deciding. So trained contempt is that feeling of not being able to stand a thing, repeated until it comes on its own, before the thing has given you a reason.

## Trained Contempt, the intro accepted 2026-09-11

Written in the main window with one instruction: avoid all mannered prose. The owner: "ok. seems like that trick on 'avoiding all mannered prose' works." The first opening he accepted as the page's intro after twenty-two strikes. N above is superseded by it.
> Trained contempt is contempt that has become automatic through repetition.
>
> Contempt, in the sense used here, is the feeling you have when something you made or use is not good enough and you want to change it. That feeling is useful. It is why people rewrite pages and report faults in tools. Nobody improves a thing they are satisfied with.
>
> The feeling becomes trained when the same thing fails in the same way many times and you feel the same contempt each time. After enough repetitions you feel the contempt whenever you see the thing, whether or not it has failed that day. At that point the feeling is no longer a response to a fault. It is a habit.
>
> The habit has a cost. While you feel contempt for a thing, you notice its faults and you do not notice what it does well, who made it, or how it is going to improve. The contempt is worth having while you are fixing the thing. After the fixing is done you have to stop feeling it on purpose, because it does not stop by itself.

## What the surviving openings have in common

From an audit of seven surviving openings, 2026-08-29: things you can point at in the first three sentences (noise, space, a pen, ten seconds, a mistake, books shut, a debt). Five of seven name the page's subject in sentence one. None uses a category word. None defines a word the reader already owns back at him. None talks about the page.
